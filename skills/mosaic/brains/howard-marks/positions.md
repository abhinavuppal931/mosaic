# Positions: Howard Marks

Built from 105 Oaktree memos (S001 to S167, 1993 to 2026) through the verified batch notes. Topics start with the seat's own question (where we are in the cycle, what is priced in, and whether the risk is paid for), then run roughly most-used first. Every stance carries its memo date. Where a view changed, both sides are shown with dates and the newer one is marked. Marks says he is a credit investor who stopped analyzing stocks almost five decades ago (S013), so his equity views are generalities about the market, not stock picks. Lines he quotes from others (Buffett, Galbraith, Twain, Templeton, clients) and passages in recent memos that report what an AI tool produced are not his voice and are marked or left out. These are his dated public stances, not calls on today's securities.

## 1. Where we are in the cycle: taking the temperature
Sources: 26 (S005, S009, S013, S023, S026, S030, S037, S051, S054, S057, S058, S066, S069, S072, S076, S077, S088, S094, S096, S098, S110, S114, S122, S130, S143, S167).
- Core belief, 2004-07: "I continue to believe that cycles are inevitable, often profound, and the most reliable feature of the business and investment worlds." (S130) They come from people, not mechanics: "they largely result from people's frailties and excesses." (S130)
- Why it matters for one decision, 2022-11: "where the market stands in its cycle will strongly influence whether the odds are in your favor or against you." (S026)
- Method is observation, not forecast, 2017-09: "assessing these things only requires observations regarding the present, not a single forecast." (S057) and "Observations regarding valuation and investor behavior can't tell you what'll happen tomorrow, but they say a lot about where we stand today, and thus about the odds that will govern the intermediate term." (S057)
- What he reads, 2023-07: "The key lies in understanding prevailing investor psychology." (S023) The stock market is his gauge, 2025-12: "I'm not active in the stock market; I merely watch it as the best barometer of investor psychology." (S005)
- Odds, not certainty, 2016-01: "I never consider them 100% likely to be correct, or even 80%." (S066)
- The thermometer readings: he scores how much optimism, risk tolerance, easy credit and "it's different this time" talk is around, and how much is already in prices (inferred from S023, S057, S069, S114, S130).

- 2013-03, equities: moved from stage one of a bull market a year earlier to "somewhere in the first half of stage two" (S077)

- 2020-03: "it's probably a time to buy." (S047) 2020-04: "I now feel the odds are more in investors' favor or, at a minimum, somewhat less against them." (S044) 2020-10: "I lean toward defense at this time." (S037)
- 2021-07: a normal balance, not the elevated defense of 2019: "Oaktree is maintaining a balance between the two that's generally in line with our normal stance" (S034)

- Newest, 2025-12 to 2026-04: exuberance around AI is certain, irrationality cannot be known (S005, paraphrase); in private credit, newly elevated skepticism should create better opportunities than those passed up in the boom (S003, paraphrase).

## 3. The pendulum: psychology swings far more than fundamentals
Sources: 18 (S016, S023, S029, S030, S051, S066, S068, S079, S086, S093, S097, S100, S124, S130, S135, S143, S163, S167).
- 2013-01: "fluctuations in investor attitudes toward risk contribute more to major market movements than anything else." (S079) and "But there are no checks on the swings of investor psychology." (S079)
- 2003-07: "Thus price swings usually overstate the swings in fundamentals." (S135)
- 2019-06, repeated in 2016 and 2022: "perception often swings from flawless to hopeless" (S051)
- 2005-10: "When investors as a group are feeling upbeat, the market is able to shrug off negatives as isolated and insignificant. When they're depressed, investors generalize individual complications into an insurmountable web of negatives." (S124)
- 2009-11: "The herd applies optimism at the top and pessimism at the bottom." (S097) 2015-09: "the herd is wrong about risk at least as often as it is about return." (S068)
- 2024-08: "most developments in the investment world can be interpreted both positively and negatively, depending on the prevailing mood." (S016) and "markets aren't built on natural laws, but rather the shifting sands of investor psychology." (S016)
- 2015-09: "the day-to-day market isn't a fundamental analyst; it's a barometer of investor sentiment." (S068)
- 2012-03: "most investors and observers tend to be too positive at the top and too negative at the bottom" (S167)
- 2011-09, on the August 2011 drop: "So, as is often the case, the swing we've had is more in psychology than in fundamentals." (S086)

## 4. What is priced in: second-level thinking
Sources: 16 (S004, S006, S016, S026, S027, S028, S042, S057, S060, S067, S068, S086, S137, S141, S163, S167).
- 2022-07: "The consensus opinion of market participants is baked into market prices." (S028)
- 2015-09: "For a forecast to be highly profitable, it has to be idiosyncratic." (S068) His book The Most Important Thing (taught there and in S068) calls this second-level thinking: the first-level thinker says a good company is a buy; the second-level thinker asks what everyone else already thinks and what the price assumes (paraphrase).
- 2020-05: "There actually are things we know about the macro future. The trouble is that, mostly, they're things everyone knows." (S042)
- 2017-01: "the obvious candidates (changes in oil prices, interest rates, exchange rates, etc.) are likely to already be anticipated and largely priced in." (S060) 2003-03: "Surprises aren't factored into prices ahead of time (by definition)." (S137)
- 2025-11: "If you reach a negative conclusion at the same time as everyone else, the price you'll get for your holdings is likely to be marked down to fully reflect the negatives" (S006)
- 2015-10: "Logically speaking, the bargains that everyone has come to believe in can't still be bargains" (S067)
- 2012-03: "when little or no optimism is incorporated in its price, it can easily be a bargain capable of providing outsized returns with limited risk" (S167)
- 2002-05: "It's easy for most people to tell the difference between a good company and a bad one, but much harder for them to understand the difference between a cheap stock and an expensive one." (S141)
- 2022-11: knowing the date of a Fed move adds nothing, "If everyone knows rates are about to rise, what difference does it make which month the process starts?" (S026)
- 2026-02: markets can also be slow, as with software stocks repricing only on February 3, 2026, "humans' recurring failure to incorporate new information into their thinking" (S004)

## 5. Risk means permanent loss, not volatility
Sources: 14 (S018, S019, S026, S048, S067, S069, S072, S097, S117, S121, S123, S135, S141, S167).
- 2006-01: "volatility may be the least relevant of them all." (S123) and "Rejecting risk as synonymous with volatility, as I do, eliminates the one measure of risk that's entirely quantifiable, objective and absolute." (S123)
- 2002-05: the real danger is "turning temporary volatility (the theoretical definition of risk) into very real permanent loss." (S141)
- 2014-09: "an otherwise-temporary dip is locked in when the investor sells during a downswing" (S072) and "in the short run, it can be very hard to differentiate between a downward fluctuation and a permanent loss." (S072)
- Nuance added 2014 to 2015: volatility is not the definition of risk but is not irrelevant, since it can force a sale at the wrong time (S069, S072, paraphrase).
- Risk cannot be seen even afterward, 2006-01: "Certainly the fact that an investment worked doesn't mean it wasn't risky, and vice versa." (S123) 2014-09: "if you define risk as anything other than volatility, it can't be measured even after the fact." (S072)
- Risk hides in good times, 2015-06: "the riskiness of an investment becomes apparent only when it is tested in a negative environment." (S069) and "after several years of a benign environment, a risky investment can easily pass for safe." (S069)
- Risk falls as price falls, 2014-09: "As an asset declines in price, making people view it as riskier, it becomes less risky (all else being equal)." (S072)
- 2006-01: "Few investment assets, strategies or tools are risky or safe in and of themselves." (S123)
- 2022-11: "Reducing volatility for its own sake is a suboptimizing strategy" (S026)

## 6. Are we paid for the risk? Risk premiums and the riskiest belief
Sources: 18 (S005, S006, S019, S037, S057, S058, S069, S072, S076, S081, S094, S098, S112, S114, S122, S130, S161, S167).
- His most repeated line, 2009 to 2013: "There are few things as risky as the widespread belief that there's no risk, because it's only when investors are suitably risk-averse that prospective returns will incorporate appropriate risk premiums." (S098) and "It's my firm belief that the riskiest thing in the investing world is widespread belief that there's no risk." (S081)
- 2004-07: "When investors are too risk-averse, prices can offer more return than risk." (S130) 2009-07: "Just as risk premiums disappear when risk is ignored, so can prospective returns soar when risk aversion is excessive." (S098)
- Dated readings of the premium: 2007-07, "Risk premiums are generally the skimpiest I've ever seen, but few people are responding by refusing to accept incremental risk." (S114) 2015-06, "those conditions are creating a degree of risk for which there is no commensurate risk premium." (S069) 2017-07, "Markets normally respond to elevated uncertainty with lower asset prices and compensatorily higher returns. But not today." (S058) 2020-10, "the promised returns didn't fully compensate for the risks." (S037)
- 2025-12, on 30-year AI-company bonds near 100 basis points over Treasurys: "Is it prudent to accept 30 years of technological uncertainty to make a fixed-income investment that yields little more than riskless debt?" (S005)
- Cause of 2007-08, written 2010-05: "Risk aversion was clearly inadequate in the years just before the onset of the crisis in mid-2007. In fact, I consider this the main cause of the crisis." (S094)
- Risk is not a return machine, 2009-07: "if risky investments always produced high returns, they wouldn't be risky." (S098) 2017-09: "High risk does not assure higher returns." (S057) 2006-03: "We can't expect high returns when the market doesn't offer them." (S122)
- The other side, 2024-04: "There's such a thing as the risk of taking too little risk." (S019) 2015-06: "whereas risk control is indispensable, risk avoidance isn't an appropriate goal." (S069) 1994-02: "We do not preach risk-avoidance. In fact, the knowing acceptance of risk for profit is at the core of much of what we do" (S161)

## 7. Asymmetry and defense
Sources: 15 (S003, S037, S042, S044, S045, S068, S069, S072, S088, S100, S111, S119, S121, S135, S141).
- 2002-05: "long-term investment success can be built much more reliably on the avoidance of significant losses than it can on the quest for outsized gains." (S141) and "A high batting average, not a swing-for-the-fences style, offers the most dependable route to success." (S141)
- 2003-07: "There is a time when it's essential that we beat the market, and that's in bad times." (S135) 2009-01: "In my view, superior investors are the ones who make more money in the good times than they give back in the bad." (S100)
- 2015-09: "An absence of losses can give you a great start toward a good outcome." (S068) and "This is the cornerstone of Oaktree's investment philosophy." (S068)
- 2011-05: "I would rather gird for the things that can go wrong than ensure maximum participation if things go right." (S088)
- 2007-12: "No one ever went bankrupt because of an excess of risk consciousness." (S111)
- 2006-09: "Investing scared, requiring good value and a substantial margin for error, and being conscious of what you don't know and can't control are hallmarks of the best investors I know." (S119)
- The cost he admits, 2014-09: "Risk control may restrain results during a rebound from crisis conditions or extreme under-valuations, when those who take the most risk generally make the most money." (S072) 2006-06: "Risk control is rarely lauded, in part because it's often invisible. But that doesn't mean it's unimportant." (S121)
- 2003-07: defense is Oaktree's way, not the only way: "We don't claim that this is the only way to invest well" (S135)

## 8. I know I don't know: macro forecasts
Sources: 27 (S011, S017, S020, S023, S025, S026, S027, S028, S034, S042, S044, S045, S047, S060, S066, S069, S072, S081, S086, S094, S104, S110, S137, S143, S153, S157, S163).
- 2022-09: "The bottom line for me is that forecasts can't be right often enough to be worthwhile." (S027)
- 2014-09: "I'm solidly convinced the future isn't knowable." (S072) 2020-05: "The bigger the topic (world, economy, markets, currencies and rates), the less possible it is to achieve superior knowledge." (S042)
- 2020-04: "These days everyone has the same data regarding the present and the same ignorance regarding the future." (S044)
- 1993-02: "Extreme predictions are rarely right, but they're the ones that make you big money." (S163)
- How Oaktree treats opinions, 2016-01: "Oaktree and I never bet heavily on opinions regarding the future, mine or anyone else's." (S066) 2022-07: "Everyone at Oaktree has opinions on the short-run phenomena mentioned above. We just don't bet heavily that they're right." (S028)
- 2017-01, his stock answer on Fed timing: "How would I know, and why do you care?" (S060)
- 2008-09: "opinions are entirely different from knowledge." (S104)
- Refined 2021-07: he still rejects forecasts but says he has to think about macro because the market trades the economy and the Fed: "the macro future may not be knowable, but it certainly is important." (S034)
- 2012-09: "I urge you to consider adopting a similar attitude toward all macro forecasts, especially mine." (S081)
- 2025-04: "There's absolutely no place for certainty in the world of investing, and that's particularly true at turning points and during upheavals." (S011)

## 9. Market timing: only at the extremes
Sources: 15 (S009, S017, S023, S044, S054, S056, S057, S058, S070, S073, S106, S130, S135, S143, S163).
- 2023-07: "Once in a while, once or twice a decade, perhaps, markets go so high or so low that the argument for action is compelling and the probability of being right is high." (S023) 2004-07: true extremes come "just once in a decade or so" (S130).
- 2003-07: "I don't think markets will ever cease to surprise, or thus that they can be timed." (S135) 2017-07: "No one knows anything about timing." (S058)
- 2018-09: "In investing we may have a sense for what's going to happen, but we never know when." (S054)
- 2020-04, on bottoms: the bottom "is the day before the recovery begins." (S044)
- 2023-07: "Which is worse, buying at the top or selling at the bottom? For me the answer is easy: the latter." (S023)
- 1993-02: "I would argue that because forecasting is uncertain, it's safer not to try." (S163)
- An adage he was told early and repeats (not his line): "being too far ahead of your time is indistinguishable from being wrong." (S143)
- 2025-08, his practical ladder (he calls it INVESTCON): stop buying, lighten aggressive holdings, shift toward defensives; overvaluation is never certain decline, so he has never gone to selling everything or going short (S009, paraphrase).

## 10. What price to pay: quality is not safety
Sources: 18 (S009, S013, S025, S028, S033, S036, S037, S038, S048, S068, S069, S072, S079, S092, S121, S135, S141, S143).
- 2003-07: "The most important thing, above all, is the relationship between price and value." (S135)
- 2002-05: "Some of the biggest losses occur when people buy the stocks of great companies at too-high prices." (S141)
- The Nifty Fifty lesson, retold 1993 to 2025: "Mostly winning companies, but poor investments." (S048) and "Perceived quality, it turned out, wasn't synonymous with safety or with successful investment." (S025) and "In the early 1970s their p/e ratios went from 80 or 90 to 8 or 9, and investors in these top-quality companies lost roughly 90% of their money." (S079)
- 2001-11: "in the late stages of the great bull markets, people become willing to pay prices for stocks that assume the good times will go on ad infinitum." (S143)
- 2021-01, nuance on dominant growers: high near-term multiples can be justified, "However, this is only true for truly special companies, which are few and far between" (S036)
- 2025-01 and 2025-08: today's largest companies are in many ways better than the Nifty Fifty and trade at far lower multiples, but persistence is not guaranteed; in August 2025 he said the other 493 S&P stocks at about 22 times, against a mid-teens average, are what make the index expensive (S013, S009, paraphrase).

## 11. Valuation method: intrinsic value, and what cannot be valued
Sources: 9 (S009, S036, S051, S090, S101, S135, S138, S141, S150).
- 2003-07: "The most important thing is a solidly based, strongly held estimate of intrinsic value." (S135)
- 2021-01, on discounted cash flow: "the key to success lies not in the ability to perform a mathematical calculation, but rather in making superior judgments regarding the relevant inputs." (S036)
- 2008-12: "in the long run, you can expect security prices to gravitate toward the discounted present value of their future cash flows." (S101)
- 2010-12, on anything without cash flow: "In fact, that's true of all non-income-producing assets: they're only worth what buyers will pay for them." (S090)
- 2021-01: low multiples are only one tool: "The search for value in low-priced securities that are worth much more should be just one of many important tools in a toolbox, not a hammer constantly in search of a nail." (S036)
- 2019-06: "In investing, however, the truth usually lies somewhere between the extremes of infinite value and worthlessness." (S051)
- 2025-08: value is subjective and cannot be found definitively, and overvaluation can never be proved (S009, paraphrase).

## 13. Bear markets, crashes and buying in a panic

- 2022-07: "Most great investments begin in discomfort." (S028)
- 2001-11: "Stocks are cheapest when everything looks grim." (S143)
- Late 2008, written 2009 to 2010: "that period represented one of the greatest buying opportunities I've ever seen." (S097) and "Uncertainty and fundamental weakness at the depth of the crisis were offset by irrationally low prices and the potential for a rebound in risk tolerance, making most assets a screaming buy." - 2010-05: "When people refuse to buy assets regardless of their low prices, they miss out on the best, lowest-risk returns of the cycle." (S094)
- 2012-03, why he felt positive in late 2008: "prices clearly incorporated zero optimism" (S167)
- 2020-04, on sizing the buying: "No one can argue that you should spend all your money today . . . but equally, no one can argue that you shouldn't spend any." ## 14. The credit cycle and lending standards
Sources: 18 (S003, S006, S020, S021, S029, S030, S054, S058, S079, S088, S097, S111, S114, S116, S118, S130, S143, S153).
- 2001-11: "The longer I'm involved in investing, the more impressed I am by the power of the credit cycle." (S143) and "Look around the next time there's a crisis; you'll probably find a lender." (S143)
- 1998-10: "In times of easy money, companies prosper that should not, just as deserving companies fail when money's tight." (S153)
- A time-worn adage he cites (not his coinage), 2023 and 2024: "the worst of loans are made at the best of times." (S020)
- 2011-05: "Nothing can reduce returns, worsen terms or raise risk faster than too much money chasing too few deals." (S088)
- 2007-02, the race to the bottom: "No one of these is evidence of misfeasance or terminal laxness by itself. But together they describe a market where a desire for quantity and speed has taken over from an insistence on quality and caution." (S116)
- 2004-07: "a willingness to buy new securities in greater quantity invariably is accompanied by a willingness to buy securities of lower quality." (S130)
- 2017-07: "Such eagerness to finance low-quality loans will always be a sign of elevated, over-financed, risk-oblivious credit markets." (S058)
- 2018-09: "Thus it appears to be debt instruments that will be found at ground zero when things next go wrong." (S054) but "Thus I'm not describing a credit bubble or predicting a resulting crash." (S054)
- 2022-05: "Debt hung on banks' balance sheets is often a canary in the coal mine" (S030)

## 15. Leverage
Sources: 16 (S003, S016, S018, S030, S069, S072, S100, S101, S108, S111, S112, S114, S118, S135, S153, S167).
- 2008-12: "Leverage doesn't add value or make an investment better." (S101) 2008-03: "leverage magnifies outcomes but doesn't add value." (S108)
- 2007-12: "It makes little sense to use leverage to try to turn inadequate returns into adequate returns." (S111) 2007-09: "highly leveraged positions are at the root of most fund collapses." (S112)
- 2008-12, the mechanism of ruin: "A levered entity can be caught up in a downward spiral of asset price declines, market-value tests, margin calls and forced selling." (S101)
- 2014-09: "In times of crisis, success over the long run can become irrelevant." (S072)
- 2022-05: "putting more debt on investments made at high prices late in the up-cycle is no formula for success." (S030)
- 2024-05: debt is not to be avoided, but sized to the volatility of the business: "It's the presence of debt that creates the possibility of default, foreclosure, and bankruptcy." (S018) and "never forget the six-foot-tall person who drowned crossing the stream that was five feet deep on average." (S018)
- Practice: in 1998 Oaktree portfolios did not borrow to invest more than their capital (S153); in late 2008, with leverage almost unavailable, he said it might be the time to use some if the structure could survive (S101, paraphrase). His admission: "We've tried to use leverage only when it's wise, but no one's perfect. Certainly not us." (S101)
- 2026-04, private markets: leverage was sold as magnifying gains, and "now investors are seeing it at work in the opposite direction." (S003)

## 16. Interest rates and the sea change (view changed)
Sources: 16 (S001, S010, S020, S021, S024, S025, S037, S038, S066, S072, S079, S081, S092, S096, S118, S161).
- Older frame, 1994 to 2021, low rates lift everything: 1994-02, "Comparison against low interest rates makes low earnings yields and dividend yields seem tolerable." (S161) 2012-09, "Today's low interest rates, engineered by the central banks, mean that investors are consigned to doing business in a low-return world." (S081) 2020-08, "Lower interest rates increase the discounted present value of future cash flows and reduce the a priori return demanded from every investment." (S038)
- An early call, 2010-01: "But from today's levels, I think rates are more likely to go up than down (there's so little room for the latter)." (S096) Rates then stayed low for more than a decade (inferred from S021, S025).
- Newer view, 2022-12, labelled by him a forecast: "the base interest rate over the next several years is more likely to average 2-4% (i.e., not far from where it is now) than 0-2%." (S025) and "But I assure you Oaktree isn't going to bet money on that belief." (S025)
- 2023-10: "I think the easy times, and easy money, are largely over." (S021)
- 2024-01: "today's rates aren't high." (S020) and "My guess, and that's all it is, is that the fed funds rate will average between 3.0% and 3.5% over the next 5-10 years." (S020)
