# Principles: Howard Marks

Built from 105 Oaktree memos, February 1993 to November 2026 (see sources.md). Decision rules, most-supported first, weighted toward this seat's job: cycle position, what is priced in, and whether the risk is paid for. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Lines he quotes from others (Buffett, Templeton, Galbraith, his son Andrew, clients) and AI tools' output are left out or paraphrased.

## 1. Price decides whether an investment is good; no asset is good or bad without its price.

- "It's not what you buy, it's what you pay that counts." (S013)
- "There's no asset so good that it can't become overpriced and thus dangerous, and there are few assets so bad that they can't get cheap enough to be a bargain." (S013)
- Supporting sources: 16 (S001, S009, S013, S048, S054, S069, S079, S090, S092, S097, S117, S129, S135, S139, S141, S151). Confidence: high.
- Newest form (November 2026): "At the end of the day, intelligent investing is largely about price." (S001)

## 2. You can't know where you're going, but you must know where you are in the cycle.

- "We may not know where we're going, but we sure as heck ought to know where we stand." (S057)
- "where we are in the cycle plays a very strong role in figuring out where we'll go next." (S029)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S025, S029, S057, S097, S111, S114, S122, S143). Confidence: high.

## 3. Be cautious when others are carefree and aggressive when they flee.

- "When most investors are driven to drop their prudence by an excess of confidence, we should be terrified." (S076)
- "When others are aggressive, we should be worried, but when others are worried, we can be confident. That's the essence of contrarianism" (S112)
- Supporting sources: 12 (S054, S058, S076, S079, S088, S094, S104, S112, S130, S141, S153, S119). Confidence: high.
- He often cites Buffett's line that the less prudent others are, the more prudent we should be; that wording is Buffett's (S030, S069, S116).

## 4. Risk means permanent loss, not volatility; refuse to buy when the price is too high for the fundamentals.

- "All you have to do is refuse to buy if the price is too high given the fundamentals." (S069)
- "We can ride out volatility, but we never get a chance to undo a permanent loss." (S069)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S003, S026, S069, S072, S092, S123, S121). Confidence: high.

## 5. Ask what is priced in; an event matters only against what was expected.

- "it's not whether the event is simply positive or not, but how the event compares with what was expected." (S026)
- "The possibility - or even the fact - that a negative event lies ahead isn't in itself a reason to reduce risk" (S028)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S016, S026, S028, S029, S047, S060, S066, S086, S167). Confidence: high.
- Corollary on falls: "If it was overvalued on the 19th, rather than being undervalued today, after the decline, it could just be less overvalued." (S047)

## 6. Have macro opinions if you like, but never bet heavily on them.

- "it's okay to have opinions on the macro; it's just not okay to bet clients' money on them." (S020)
- "It's one thing to have an opinion, but something very different to assume it's right and bet heavily on it." (S034)
- Supporting sources: 12 (S020, S023, S025, S026, S034, S037, S042, S060, S081, S143, S157, S163). Confidence: high.
- Oaktree tenet, unchanged for decades: it does not base decisions on macro forecasts and is not a market timer (S023, S034).

## 7. Act strongly only at extremes; in the middle, don't try to be clever.

- "when conditions are moderate or indistinct, we don't bet heavily." (S060)
- "when there's nothing clever to do, the mistake lies in trying to be clever." (S076)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S023, S030, S060, S068, S076, S130). Confidence: high.
- His record of big calls: five in about 50 years (January 2000, 2004 to 2007, late 2008, March 2012, March 2020), each when markets were crazily high or deeply depressed (S023).
- View changed (July 2021, newer): he says his upbringing made him a knee-jerk skeptic and that "The old me likely would have latched onto today's high valuations and instances of risky behavior to warn of a bubble and the subsequent correction." (S034) The newer Marks does not cut exposure on such predictions alone.

## 8. Never all in, never all out; calibrate along a continuum and never say "get out" or "it's time".

- "Investing is not black or white, in or out, risky or safe." (S057)
- "no one should go all-in without acknowledging that they face the risk of ruin if things go badly." (S005)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S004, S005, S009, S044, S054, S056, S057, S058). Confidence: high.
- Even at INVESTCON 5 (August 2025) he stops short of the extremes: "it's rarely wise to go to those extremes. I know I never have." (S009)

## 9. Buy when it's cheap, buy more if it gets cheaper and the thesis holds, and never wait for the bottom.

- "If it's cheap, we buy. If it gets cheaper and we conclude the thesis is still intact, we buy more." (S023)
- "if something's cheap - based on the relationship between price and intrinsic value - you should buy, and if it cheapens further, you should buy more." (S044)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S023, S042, S044, S047, S056, S106, S110). Confidence: high.
- Scale in: decide how much you want invested by the bottom and spend part of it now (S044, S047); keep some cash, since nobody knows how bad things get (S047).

## 10. Selling at the bottom is the cardinal sin; sell for deteriorated fundamentals or a full price, not for comfort.

- "This is why I describe selling at the bottom as the cardinal sin in investing." (S023)
- "the valid reasons to sell are principally because you feel fundamentals have deteriorated or because the price has risen enough." (S068)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S023, S036, S047, S068, S092). Confidence: high.
- View changed (January 2021, newer): after talks with Andrew he says his bias toward selling as prices rose was wrong for great compounders: "cutting back a holding with great potential can be a life-altering mistake." (S036)

## 11. Turn cautious too soon rather than too late; early is not the same as wrong.

- "I think it's better to turn cautious too soon (and thus perhaps underperform for a while) rather than too late, after the downslide has begun, making it hard to trim risk, achieve exits and cut losses." (S058)
- "That memo may have been too early, but it wasn't wrong." (S079)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S058, S079, S143, S057). Confidence: high.
- He admits the cost: "a single word suffices to describe my recent caution regarding the stock market: wrong." (S151, January 2000, before the bubble burst)

## 12. Don't reach for return when safe returns are low.

- "there is one course of action, one classic mistake, that I most strongly feel is wrong: reaching for return." (S058)
- "When safe investments appear unlikely to provide the returns we need, we shouldn't rush to riskier investments to get them." (S122)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S058, S079, S088, S111, S122, S161). Confidence: high.

## 13. Risk is not paid for automatically; demand the premium and doubt high returns with low risk.

- "You shouldn't expect to make money without bearing risk, but you shouldn't expect to make money just for taking risk." (S019)
- "Dependably high returns from risky investments are an oxymoron." (S130)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S019, S109, S118, S123, S124, S130, S141, S161). Confidence: high.
- "Investors should demand return premiums, but they shouldn't count on them." (S124)

## 14. Survive the bad day: build margin for error and never depend on everything going right.

- "Never forget the six-foot-tall man who drowned crossing the stream that was five feet deep on average." (S117)
- "Every portfolio or investing entity must be examined to make sure it will be able to survive that bad day" (S111)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S024, S026, S097, S101, S111, S117, S124, S141, S153). Confidence: high.
- "We must always expect that something will go wrong and build in margin for error." (S153)

## 15. Leverage magnifies; it adds no value, so size it to the asset's risk and never use it to turn low returns into high ones.

- "Leverage doesn't make an investment better; it merely magnifies the gains and losses." (S097)
- "it can be wise to use leverage to take advantage of high offered returns and excessive risk premiums, but it's unwise to use it to try to turn low offered returns into high ones" (S101)
- Supporting sources: 11 (S018, S020, S097, S100, S101, S117, S118, S129, S135, S153, S114). Confidence: high.
- "investors should usually use less than the maximum available." (S018)

## 16. Eschew certainty; keep confidence in proportion to the evidence.

- "Eschewing certainty can keep you out of trouble. I strongly recommend doing so." (S017)
- "The real expert's confidence is reason-based and proportional to the weight of the evidence." (S042)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S001, S017, S042, S060, S067, S069, S076, S082, S137). Confidence: high.
- Avoid the absolute words will, won't, has to, can't, always and never about the macro future (S017, S027, S066).

## 17. Don't require certainty to act; doing nothing is also a decision.

- "if we insist on achieving certainty or even confidence as a precondition for action, we'll be frozen into inaction." (S011)
- "The right response isn't paralysis but (a) calibrated humility regarding strategy selection, position sizing, and risk-taking and (b) a willingness to act in the presence of uncertainty." (S001)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S011, S081, S104, S110). Confidence: high.
- "The decision to not act, to leave a portfolio unchanged, should be scrutinized as critically as a decision to make changes." (S011)

## 18. When others are terrified and prices have collapsed, step up.

- "when unfavorable developments are raining down, that's often the best time to step up." (S011)
- "the big and relatively easy money in investing is made when prices are low, pessimism is widespread and investors are fleeing from risk." (S054)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S011, S029, S044, S051, S054, S077, S104, S151). Confidence: high.
- After Lehman (2008) he reasoned that most of the time the world doesn't end, so act as if it won't (S011, S023, S104).

## 19. Judge a decision by its quality at the time, not by its outcome.

- "the quality of a decision cannot be determined from the outcome alone." (S026)
- "The fact that something worked doesn't mean it was the result of a correct decision, and the fact that something failed doesn't mean the decision was wrong." (S067)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S026, S056, S067, S068, S074, S117, S138). Confidence: high.

## 20. To beat the market you must be different, accept the risk of being wrong and of looking wrong.

- "It should be clear that you can't hope to earn superior returns if you're unwilling to bear the risk of sub-par results." (S028)
- "you need the strength to diverge from the herd, stand by your convictions, and maintain positions until events prove them right." (S073)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S028, S042, S073, S079, S119). Confidence: high.

## 21. Be contrarian only with a reason, never by reflex.

- "the mass of investors aren't wrong all the time, or wrong so dependably that it's always right to do the opposite of what they do." (S028)
- "you should not aim for contrarianism for its own sake, but only when the reasons are good and the actions of the crowd look particularly foolish." (S119)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S028, S068, S119). Confidence: high.

## 22. Treat "this time it's different" and "no price too high" as alarms, but don't miss real change.

- "When you see or hear words like these, you should go on high alert." (S167)
- "failing to recognize when things actually are different is something that stands between the average investor and superiority." (S009)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S005, S009, S013, S037, S051, S114, S167). Confidence: high.
- View changed: Templeton allowed that things really are different about 20% of the time; Marks said in June 2022 that things change more often than that now (S029), and in October 2023 that the share may be higher given technology (S021, newer). He still adds that belief in a difference is what makes it not different (S005).

## 23. Don't extrapolate; strong past returns borrow from the future.

- "An above-trend year has borrowed from the remaining potential." (S077)
- "high realized returns must never be confused with great opportunities to invest more." (S122)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S077, S092, S122, S124, S135, S141, S143, S167). Confidence: high.
- "Ignoring cycles and extrapolating trends is one of the most dangerous things an investor can do." (S135)

## 24. Cheap can get cheaper and dear can get dearer; overheated doesn't mean down tomorrow.

- "An undervalued asset can remain cheap, or even get cheaper, for a long time, just as an overvalued asset can become more overvalued, and then extremely overvalued, and then crazily overvalued." (S009)
- "An overheated environment doesn't mean the market's going down tomorrow, just as an excess of risk aversion doesn't signal it's the absolute bottom." (S122)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S009, S122, S151, S167). Confidence: high.

## 25. Watch the supply of capital: too much money chasing too few deals means low returns and rising risk.

- "Participating in a field that everyone's throwing money at is a formula for disaster." (S143)
- "when capital is in oversupply, it is inevitable that risk aversion dries up, gullibility expands, and investment standards are relaxed." (S058)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S003, S030, S054, S058, S114, S116, S129, S143, S151). Confidence: high.
- He endorses the banking adage that the worst loans are made in the best of times (S006, S030, S114).

## 26. Skepticism cuts both ways: too good to be true at the top, too bad to be true at the bottom.

- "Skepticism calls for pessimism when optimism is excessive. But it also calls for optimism when pessimism is excessive." (S094)
- "Thus, to benefit, we must be skeptical of the optimism that thrives at the top, and skeptical of the pessimism that prevails at the bottom." (S097)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S023, S029, S094, S097, S109). Confidence: high.
- "Skepticism and pessimism aren't synonymous." (S023)

## 27. Favor limiting losses over full participation; invest scared.

- "investors should favor strategies, managers and approaches that emphasize limiting losses in declines above ensuring full participation in gains. You simply can't have it both ways." (S054)
- "Investing scared will result in making fewer mistakes (although perhaps at the price of failing to take maximum advantage of bull markets)." (S042)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S042, S054, S088, S112, S135, S121). Confidence: high.

## 28. Keep risk control on all the time, like fire insurance, and make it every decision maker's job.

- "Risk control is unnecessary in times when losses don't occur, but that doesn't mean it's wrong to have it." (S069)
- "Risk control isn't an action so much as it is a mindset." (S082)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S069, S072, S082, S098). Confidence: high.
- "whereas risk control is indispensable, risk avoidance isn't an appropriate goal." (S072)

## 29. Psychology swings far more than fundamentals; treat the daily price as mood, not information.

- "Recognize that psychology swings much more than fundamentals, and usually in the wrong direction or at the wrong time." (S026)
- "Fundamentals, the outlook for an economy, company or asset, don't change much from day to day." (S016)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S016, S026, S066, S068, S088). Confidence: high.
- Use the moods: sell to the market when it is eager at any price, buy from it when it desperately wants out (S016, his reading of Graham's Mr. Market).

## 30. A real technology is not the same as a good investment.

- "However, that's not the same as saying AI investments are on the bargain counter or even fairly priced." (S004)
- "I don't think anyone would disagree that it's one thing to innovate and change the world and another thing entirely to make money." (S151)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S004, S005, S051, S151). Confidence: high.
- His size rule for AI (December 2025): "A moderate position, applied with selectivity and prudence, seems like the best approach." (S005)
