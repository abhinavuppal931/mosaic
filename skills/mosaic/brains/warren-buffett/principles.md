# Principles: Warren Buffett

Built from the Berkshire Hathaway letters (1977 to 2024, S003 to S050), the Owner's Manual (S001), the Buffett Partnership letters (1957 to 1969, S051 to S078), lectures, op-eds, the FCIC interview, CNBC interviews and four annual-meeting transcripts (1998, 2008, 2019, 2025). Decision rules, most-supported first, weighted to business quality and owner economics. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## 1. Do not forecast the market, rates or the economy; price the business instead of timing it.

- "We do not have, never have had, and never will have an opinion about where the stock market, interest rates, or business activity will be a year from now." (S014)
- "I don't try and time stock prices, I try to price stock prices" (S093)
- Supporting sources: 24 (S004, S006, S012, S013, S014, S020, S022, S028, S032, S033, S038, S042, S051, S056, S059, S077, S079, S080, S085, S087, S093, S116, S126, S134). Confidence: high.
- He has never passed up an attractive purchase because of the macro or political picture (S033), and asks why anyone would scrap an informed decision for an uninformed guess (S020).

## 2. Stay inside your circle of competence and say no to everything else without regret.

- "What counts for most people in investing is not how much they know, but rather how realistically they define what they don't know." (S018)
- "Passing an opportunity which I don't understand - even if someone else is perceptive enough to analyze it and get paid well for doing it - doesn't bother me." (S078)
- Supporting sources: 20 (S010, S015, S018, S019, S022, S029, S033, S039, S046, S064, S069, S078, S079, S080, S093, S105, S116, S122, S130, S134). Confidence: high.
- He accepts missing the biggest winners as the price of a high batting average (S130).

## 3. Buy a wonderful business at a fair price, or a piece of one, rather than a fair business at a wonderful price.

- "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price." (S015)
- "It's better to have a partial interest in the Hope Diamond than to own all of a rhinestone." (S034)
- Supporting sources: 16 (S004, S005, S007, S013, S014, S015, S016, S018, S027, S032, S033, S034, S035, S040, S043, S126). Confidence: high.
- How his view changed: the partnership (1957 to 1969) bought cheap fair businesses; from the 1989 letter on he says he took 20 years to learn this (S013, S015). Newer: quality first. He also prefers a non-controlling stake in a wonderful business to all of a marginal one (S040).

## 4. Pay a price well below a conservative value; a great business bought too dear is a bad investment, and no idea is good at any price.

- "Of course, a business with terrific economics can be a bad investment if the price paid is excessive." (S032)
- "You can turn any investment into a bad deal by paying too much. What you can’t do is turn any investment into a good deal by paying little" (S130)
- "Never count on making a good sale." (S059)
- Supporting sources: 16 (S006, S008, S009, S016, S018, S032, S034, S035, S056, S059, S062, S064, S080, S082, S126, S130). Confidence: high.
- A silly price can wipe out a decade of retained earnings or business progress (S006, S008). He does nothing once "regardless of price" enters a sentence (S126, paraphrase).

## 5. Never use leverage to own stocks; smart people do not need it and others should not use it.

- "borrowed money has no place in the investor's tool kit" (S034)
- "if you're smart you don't need it and if you're dumb you shouldn't be using it" (S083)
- Supporting sources: 16 (S001, S006, S025, S030, S034, S037, S048, S079, S080, S083, S090, S093, S097, S122, S130, S134). Confidence: high.
- How his view changed: the partnership borrowed against workouts, capped at 25% of net worth, and called borrowing against generals very dangerous (S056, S062, 1962 to 1964). Newer (S034, 2015; S130, 2019): no borrowed money in the investor's tool kit at all.

## 6. Welcome falling prices when the business is intact; be fearful when others are greedy and greedy when they are fearful.

- "Be fearful when others are greedy, and be greedy when others are fearful" (S087)
- "if their business experience continues to satisfy us, we welcome lower market prices of stocks we own as an opportunity to acquire even more of a good thing at a better price." (S003)
- Supporting sources: 16 (S003, S008, S012, S016, S020, S023, S024, S026, S028, S031, S033, S036, S085, S087, S097, S116). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S087, S116: the welcome applies only to sound businesses; fear is right for leveraged firms, weak competitors, and any company that is itself losing value.

## 7. Hold a wonderful business indefinitely; do not sell it because it rose, because it grew large in the portfolio, or because a high bid arrived.

- "when we own portions of outstanding businesses with outstanding managements, our favorite holding period is forever." (S014)
- "To suggest that this investor should sell off portions of his most successful investments simply because they have come to dominate his portfolio is akin to suggesting that the Bulls trade Michael Jordan" (S022)
- Supporting sources: 15 (S001, S012, S013, S014, S016, S017, S019, S021, S022, S023, S038, S043, S080, S122, S126). Confidence: high.
- How his view changed: in 1987 and 1990 he said Berkshire would keep its permanent holdings even if they looked significantly overpriced (S012, S015). Newer (S036, 2017; S116, 2016): "we regard any marketable security as available for sale, however unlikely such a sale now seems." (S036), and he sells when the company is not worth what it sells for (S116), as with PetroChina at about his value (S027). Controlled businesses stay off the block unless problems are unending (S044).

## 8. Judge an investment by business results over years, not by its quote.

- "let our marketable equities tell us by their operating results, not by their daily, or even yearly, price quotations, whether our investments are successful." (S013)
- "Games are won by players who focus on the playing field, not by those whose eyes are glued to the scoreboard." (S033)
- Supporting sources: 15 (S003, S009, S011, S013, S017, S027, S033, S041, S052, S054, S076, S097, S105, S116, S134). Confidence: high.
- He judges what you paid as irrelevant; what matters is where the business will be in five or ten years (S116).

## 9. Repurchase stock only below a conservatively calculated intrinsic value, and never to prop the price.

- "Continuing shareholders are hurt unless shares are purchased below intrinsic value." (S031)
- "In repurchase decisions, price is all-important." (S032)
- Supporting sources: 15 (S006, S010, S031, S032, S034, S036, S038, S039, S040, S041, S042, S043, S046, S130, S134). Confidence: high.
- Offsetting dilution or having spare cash is not a reason (S031). He uses this to judge the managers of holdings too (S010, S036).

## 10. Count every real cost; distrust EBITDA, adjusted earnings and stock pay treated as free.

- "If compensation isn't an expense, what is it?" (S035)
- "When Wall Streeters tout EBITDA as a valuation guide, button your wallet." (S033)
- Supporting sources: 15 (S015, S018, S023, S033, S034, S035, S036, S038, S041, S043, S044, S045, S047, S049, S130). Confidence: high.
- He says depreciation is almost always real: in 51 years he never kept businesses competitive while spending less than it (S035).

## 11. Never risk what you have and need for what you do not need; avoid permanent loss of capital.

- "it is insane to risk what you have and need in order to obtain what you don't need." (S037)
- "Never risk permanent loss of capital." (S043)
- Supporting sources: 13 (S001, S013, S015, S034, S037, S043, S053, S059, S069, S079, S105, S118, S126). Confidence: high.
- A small chance of distress or disgrace is not offset by a large chance of extra return (S015); an extra point of return is not worth ruin risk (S126).

## 12. Treat accounting as the start of analysis, not the end; distrust projections, cherry-picked growth rates and footnotes you cannot read.

- "accounting numbers are the beginning, not the end, of business valuation." (S008)
- "unintelligible footnotes usually indicate untrustworthy management." (S049)
- Supporting sources: 12 (S008, S011, S012, S014, S016, S021, S023, S025, S047, S049, S050, S085). Confidence: high.
- "While deals often fail in practice, they never fail in projections" (S008). He never glances at sellers' projections (S021) and calls CEO growth forecasts deceptive and dangerous (S047).

## 13. Align pay with owners: reward what a manager controls, charge for capital, and share the downside.

- "alignment means being a partner in both directions, not just on the upside." (S020)
- "We issue no lottery tickets that carry payoffs unrelated to business performance." (S026)
- Supporting sources: 11 (S011, S017, S020, S022, S023, S026, S044, S048, S083, S093, S122). Confidence: high.
- For rescued financial firms he wants the CEO and spouse to come away with nothing (S083).

## 14. Never depend on the kindness of strangers; keep ample cash at all times.

- "We never want to count on the kindness of strangers in order to meet tomorrow’s obligations." (S028)
- "But I will never risk getting caught short of cash." (S038)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S028, S029, S031, S037, S038, S041, S042, S083, S086, S097). Confidence: high.
- He holds that liquidity in Treasury bills, the only thing he trusts in a freeze (S083).

## 15. Avoid businesses facing rapid change; prefer the certain good result to the hoped-for great one.

- "Severe change and exceptional returns usually don't mix." (S013)
- "If I can't see where they will be ten years from now, I don't want to buy it." (S079)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S013, S019, S022, S029, S033, S069, S079, S105, S116, S122). Confidence: high.
- How his view changed: technology failed his filter outright in 1967 and 1998 (S069, S122); by 2019 he admitted missing Google (S130) and by 2021 Apple was among Berkshire's largest holdings (S040). Inferred from S040, S130: the rule now bars businesses he cannot see ten years out, not technology as a word.

## 16. Pick the business before the manager; a good jockey cannot win on a broken-down nag.

- "when a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for poor fundamental economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact." (S006)
- "Good jockeys will do well on good horses, but not on broken-down nags." (S015)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S006, S011, S015, S026, S027, S039, S080, S083, S116, S126). Confidence: high.
- A business that needs a superstar to produce great results is not a great business (S027).

## 17. Deal only with honest people you like and trust; no deal with a bad person ever works.

- "We've never succeeded in making a good deal with a bad person." (S015)
- "We are understanding about business mistakes; our tolerance for personal misconduct is zero." (S042)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S012, S015, S023, S026, S042, S043, S048, S069, S082, S122). Confidence: high.
- He trusts character over due diligence: with people he knows, he has skipped audits and lease checks (S014, S023, S050).

## 18. Issue stock only when you receive as much intrinsic value as you give; pay cash when you can.

- "We will issue common stock only when we receive as much in business value as we give." (S001)
- "Though it hurts me to say it, when I've issued stock, I've cost you money." (S023)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S001, S008, S018, S019, S023, S025, S029, S036, S046, S105). Confidence: high.
- How his view changed: he issued stock for Dexter and General Re and calls both costly (S047, S049). Newer (S036, 2017): he would rather prep for a colonoscopy than issue Berkshire shares.

## 19. Concentrate when the facts are clear; when an idea qualifies, buy a meaningful amount.

- "We try to avoid buying a little of this or that when we are only lukewarm about the business or its price." (S004)
- "when you see one that qualifies, you should buy a meaningful amount of stock." (S022)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S003, S004, S014, S017, S019, S022, S029, S067, S072, S080). Confidence: high.
- Partnership limit: up to 40% in one idea when facts very probably are right and value is very unlikely to change drastically (S067).

## 20. Never force money into the market; inactivity is often the job.

- "We are not going to buy anything just to buy something." (S122)
- "But occasionally successful investing requires inactivity." (S049)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S010, S019, S023, S037, S045, S049, S080, S122, S130, S134). Confidence: high.
- He finds doing nothing the most difficult task of all (S010), and calls deploying cash on a schedule the dumbest thing in the world (S134).

## 21. Do not pour capital into terrible industries or turnarounds, however cheap.

- "major additional investment in a terrible industry usually is about as rewarding as struggling in quicksand." (S001)
- "the same energies and talent are much better employed in a good business purchased at a fair price than in a poor business purchased at a bargain price." (S005)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S001, S005, S006, S008, S011, S014, S015, S020, S047, S080). Confidence: high.
- He says value is destroyed, not created, by any business that loses money over its life, however high its interim valuation (S047).

## 22. Admit mistakes, correct them fast, and run honest post-mortems.

- "The cardinal sin is delaying the correction of mistakes" (S044)
- "Agonizing over errors is a mistake. But acknowledging and analyzing them can be useful" (S047)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S011, S025, S034, S044, S047, S085, S116, S122, S130). Confidence: high.
- When information contradicts a cherished belief, look at it quickly (S122); he admits being too slow on Tesco and on replacing managers (S116).

## 23. Own productive assets for the long run, not cash, long bonds or gold; never bet against America.

- "Berkshire will never prefer ownership of cash-equivalent assets over the ownership of good businesses, whether controlled or only partially owned." (S044)
- "I have yet to see a time when it made sense to make a long-term bet against America." (S042)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S028, S031, S035, S040, S041, S042, S044, S086, S087). Confidence: high.
- Cash is a reserve and an option, not a holding: clinging to cash or long Treasuries at low yields is a terrible long-run policy (S028).

## 24. Buy only what you would be happy to hold if the market closed for years.

- "If you aren't willing to own a stock for ten years, don't even think about owning it for ten minutes." (S022)
- "Basically, I don't want to buy any stock where if they close the NYSE tomorrow for five years, I won't be happy owning it." (S079)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S019, S022, S034, S079, S093, S105, S122, S126). Confidence: high.
- "Six months is not an investment period." (S105)

## 25. A recent price rise is never a reason to buy; that is speculation.

- "the fact that a given asset has appreciated in the recent past is never a reason to buy it." (S033)
- "Consciously paying more for a stock than its calculated value - in the hope that it can soon be sold for a still-higher price - should be labeled speculation" (S018)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S018, S033, S047, S064, S082, S086, S105, S122). Confidence: high.
- He ignores price action, volume and charts in every decision (S122) and says speculation is most dangerous when it looks easiest (S047).

## 26. Retain earnings only where each dollar becomes more than a dollar of value; otherwise pay it out or buy back stock cheaply.

- "it is our job to select businesses with economic characteristics allowing each dollar of retained earnings to be translated eventually into at least a dollar of market value." (S008)
- "It’s better to return it to shareholders when you cannot create more than a dollar of value with that capital." (S122)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S004, S007, S008, S010, S079, S122, S126). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S011, S122: a CEO's EPS growth that only reflects retained earnings, at lackluster returns on capital, earns no applause.

## 27. If you cannot value businesses, own a low-cost index fund and keep buying it.

- "Most investors, both institutional and individual, will find that the best way to own common stocks is through an index fund that charges minimal fees." (S022)
- "Both large and small investors should stick with low-cost index funds." (S036)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S019, S022, S033, S036, S093, S116, S126). Confidence: high.
- He warns people not to piggyback on what he buys (S116).

## 28. Think for yourself; agreement from the crowd proves nothing.

- "You will not be right simply because a large number of people momentarily agree with you." (S056)
- "A public opinion poll is no substitute for thought." (S064)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S016, S029, S056, S064, S080, S116). Confidence: high.
- He blames mindless imitation of peers for many corporate failures (S080) and avoids holders who trade on media or analyst commentary (S029).

## 29. Inside your circle, decide fast and skip the decimals.

- "if we can’t make a decision in five minutes, we can’t make it in five months." (S126)
- "Using precise numbers is, in fact, foolish; working with a range of possibilities is the better approach." (S047)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S047, S080, S093, S126, S134). Confidence: high.
- "if it isn't easy to come up with the offer price, I don't make an offer" (S093). Be patient waiting for the pitch, then answer in minutes (S134).
