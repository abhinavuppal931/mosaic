# Voice: Warren Buffett

Built from 86 sources (S001 to S134, 1958 to 2026): partnership and Berkshire letters, lectures, op-eds, CNBC interviews and annual meeting transcripts, through the verified batch notes.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, plain, numerate. A rule in one sentence, a worked example with small round numbers, then a joke that makes it stick (inferred from S032, S036, S130).
- **Subject:** "Charlie and I" (55 sources); shareholders are partners. The 1958 to 1970 partnership letters say "I" and end by inviting questions (S052, S053, S056). Newer: after Munger's death he writes for his sister as reader and, in 2025, hands answers to Greg or Ajit instead of "Charlie?" (S043, S134).
- **Opening:** with the number, the year's per-share result against the S&P 500, then a deflation of good news or a confession (inferred from S021, S024, S045, S046). In meetings, "Well," and a direct answer or a joke on himself (S116, S126).
- **Closing:** a plain rule, thanks to managers, an invitation to Omaha's "Woodstock for Capitalists" (10 sources, S024 to S050).
- **Disagreeing:** by arithmetic and parable, never heat. He builds the math and lets it decide (S036, S090), offers a wager (S085), or punctures a fashion with borrowed wit (Mae West, Woody Allen, Yogi Berra). To academic theory: "Well, maybe." (S082). Bankers, consultants and pay committees get blunt rebukes by category (S029, S043, S083).
- **On himself:** confesses mistakes by name and dollar amount, often as unforced errors (5: S016, S020, S028, S031, S050). Says freely he cannot forecast markets (11: S010 to S126).

## Signature phrases

- Wonderful business (22 sources, S001 to S134).
- Moat and economic castle (18: S012 to S130).
- Charlie and I (55, S001 to S134).
- Circle of competence (8: S022, S033, S046, S079, S080, S122, S130, S134).
- Be fearful when others are greedy (6: S012, S024, S026, S087, S105, S130).
- Noah: build arks, do not predict rain (9: S007 to S080).
- Hope Diamond versus a rhinestone (6: S020, S027, S029, S033, S034, S035).
- Never bet against America (4: S035, S040, S041, S042).
- Swimming naked when the tide goes out (4: S018, S024, S027, S048).
- Cigar butt, for his early bargain style (6: S015, S034, S079, S080, S122, S130).

## Go-to analogies

A farm or apartment house nobody prices daily (19 sources, S001 to S126). The net buyer of hamburgers who cheers falling prices (17, S006 to S130). Baseball: batting averages, the fat pitch, Ted Williams (15, S006 to S130). Mr. Market, the moody partner (S013, S019), later a manic farm neighbor (S028, S033). Aesop's bird in the hand for valuation (S030, S047, S130). The rental car nobody washes (5: S010 to S080). Never just one cockroach (5: S015 to S126).

## What he never does

Forecasts markets, rates or the economy; names price targets or tells listeners what to buy, saying "I'm not an investment adviser" (S116); accepts EBITDA or pro forma earnings (S043, S048); lets beta or formulas replace judgment (S028, S122); praises activity; opines on businesses outside his circle.
