# Positions: Warren Buffett

Built from 86 sources (partnership letters 1958 to 1970, Berkshire letters 1978 to 2025, lectures, op-eds, CNBC interviews, the FCIC interview, annual meeting transcripts 1998, 2008, 2019 and 2025, and Thanksgiving messages to 2026) through the verified batch notes. Each stance carries the publication date of its source (the letter for 1988 is dated 1989-02). Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. The seat's own questions (price, value, moat, capital needs, circle of competence, technology) come first; the rest follow by how many sources carry them. These are dated public stances, not calls on today's stocks.

## 1. What price to pay: wonderful business at a fair price
Sources: 17 (S007, S011, S013, S015, S018, S022, S027, S032, S034, S037, S038, S040, S043, S079, S080, S082, S130).
- Older view, 1956 to about 1972: buy statistical bargains below asset value (Dempster, Sanborn, Berkshire itself). He later called this a 20-year detour: "always a quick study, required only 20 years to recognize how important it was to buy good businesses" (S013, 1988-02).
- 1990-02, the turn: "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price." (S015)
- 2015-02 and 2024-02 (newest, same rule, credited to Munger): "Forget what you know about buying fair businesses at wonderful prices; instead, buy wonderful businesses at fair prices." (S034) "add to it wonderful businesses purchased at fair prices and give up buying fair businesses at wonderful prices." (S043)
- A slice of a great business beats all of a costly one: "we would rather buy 10% of Wonderful Business T at X per share than 100% of T at 2X per share." (S007, 1982-02) He paid a full price for Cap Cities for the people (S011, 1986-02).
- Margin of safety still rules: "You don't try and buy businesses worth $83 million for $80 million. You leave yourself an enormous margin." (S082, 1984-05) "If we calculate the value of a common stock to be only slightly higher than its price, we're not interested in buying." (S018, 1993-02)
- Quality forgives a small overpayment if you hold long: "If you are in a wonderful business for a long time, even if you pay a little bit too much going in you will get a wonderful result if you stay in a long time." (S079, 1998-10)
- But price can still ruin it: "You can, of course, pay too much for even the best of businesses." (S022, 1997-02) "On occasion, a ridiculously-high purchase price for a given stock will cause a splendid business to become a poor investment" (S038, 2019-02)
- The gate in practice: in 2017 the sensible-price test "proved a barrier to virtually all deals we reviewed in 2017" (S037, 2018-02). On Precision Castparts: "I was wrong, however, in judging the average amount of future earnings and, consequently, wrong in my calculation of the proper price to pay for the business." (S040, 2021-02)

## 2. Valuation method, intrinsic value and book value
Sources: 16 (S001, S012, S017, S018, S029, S030, S035, S038, S039, S046, S047, S048, S080, S085, S126, S130).
- Value is the discounted cash a business will hand out; ratios are only clues: "have nothing to do with valuation except to the extent they provide clues to the amount and timing of cash flows into and from the business." (S047, 2001-02) In 1992 he described discounting earnings power at about 10 percent (S017, paraphrase).
- A range, not a point: "Charlie and I admit that we feel confident in estimating intrinsic value for only a portion of traded equities and then only when we employ a range of values, rather than some pseudo-precise figure." (S046, 2000-02) "If your range is $300 to $500 and it's selling for $80 you don't need to be more accurate than that." (S080, 1991-04)
- Imprecise by nature: "business value is a soft number" (S012, 1987-02); "Calculations of intrinsic value are far from precise." (S039, 2020-02)
- He looks for an obvious, fat gap: "that’s all I’m looking for is something that’s financially fat" (S126, 2008-05)
- EBITDA is not earnings: "When CEOs or investment bankers tout pre-depreciation figures such as EBITDA as a valuation guide, watch their noses lengthen while they speak." (S035, 2016-02)
- Market level: total market value to GNP "is probably the best single measure of where valuations stand at any given moment" (S085, 2001-12), with about 70 to 80 percent attractive and about 200 percent playing with fire (paraphrase).
- Book value, changed view. 1987-02: a reasonable if conservative proxy for Berkshire's value (S012, paraphrase). 1993-02: low price to book or earnings is "far from determinative as to whether an investor is indeed buying something for what it is worth" (S018). 2019-02 (newest): Berkshire's book value "is a metric that has lost the relevance it once had." (S038) 2019-05: value is not tied to low multiples, so a Berkshire manager buying Amazon was still value investing; he rejects "The idea that value is somehow connected to book value or low price-earnings ratios" (S130).

## 3. Moats and business quality
Sources: 15 (S009, S010, S012, S013, S016, S019, S022, S025, S027, S031, S034, S050, S079, S080, S126).
- The test: "A truly great business must have an enduring moat that protects excellent returns on invested capital." (S027, 2008-02) "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all." (S027)
- The picture he uses: "I want a very valuable castle in the middle and then I want the Duke who is in charge of that castle to be very honest and hard working and able." (S079, 1998-10)
- Low cost and brands are the two moats he names most: "GEICO has a major, sustainable competitive advantage." (S010, 1985-02) "The company's low costs create a moat, an enduring one, that competitors are unable to cross." (S034, 2015-02) "Buy commodities, sell brands has long been a formula for business success." (S031, 2012-02)
- Commodity businesses lose: "In a business selling a commodity-type product, it's impossible to be a lot smarter than your dumbest competitor." (S016, 1991-02) In insurance, only people are a moat: "Anyone can copy anyone else's product." (S050, 2004-02)
- Stability beats change: "the best business returns are usually achieved by companies that are doing something quite similar today to what they were doing five or ten years ago" (S013, 1988-02)
- Widen the moat even at a short-term cost: "But when short-term and long-term conflict, widening the moat must take precedence." (S025, 2006-02) Moats can narrow, as Kodak's did against Fuji: "but they let that moat narrow." (S079)
- Durable enough for weak managers: "you want to buy stock in a business is so good that an idiot can run it, because sooner or later one will" (S126, 2008-05)
- Changed view on goodwill, 1984-02: "My own thinking has changed drastically from 35 years ago when I was taught to favor tangible assets and to shun businesses whose value depended largely upon economic Goodwill." (S009) He had favored asset-heavy bargains; he moved to businesses with high returns on little tangible capital.

## 4. Capital intensity: utilities and railroads
Sources: 7 (S025, S043, S086, S105, S130, S134, S039).
- 2006-02: "You can't expect to earn outsized profits in regulated utilities, but the industry offers owners the opportunity to deploy large sums at fair returns" (S025)
- 2010-03, after BNSF: willing to own capital-heavy businesses at Berkshire's size. "Not great rates of return, but decent rates of return." (S105)
- 2012-02: utilities fail the inflation test because "To earn more, their owners must invest more" (S086)
- 2024-02: "Indeed, compared to most American businesses, railroads eat capital." (S043) Wildfire rulings changed utility economics: "In such jurisdictions, it is difficult to project both earnings and asset values in what was once regarded as among the most stable industries in America." (S043)
- 2025-05 (newest, more negative): "Berkshire Hathaway Energy is worth considerably less money than it was two years ago, based on societal factors." (S134) Some states, "they’re rat poison." (S134)

## 5. Circle of competence
Sources: 11 (S015, S022, S033, S034, S037, S046, S078, S093, S122, S130, S079).
- "The size of that circle is not very important; knowing its boundaries, however, is vital." (S022, 1997-02)
- "If we have a strength, it is in recognizing when we are operating well within our circle of competence and when we are approaching the perimeter." (S046, 2000-02)
- Missing things outside it is fine: "It's no sin to miss a great opportunity outside one's area of competence." (S015, 1990-02)
- Industry yes, company no (pharma): "it was within our circle of confidence to identify the industry as likely to enjoy very high profits over time." (S122, 1998-05) He would buy a basket rather than bet on a pipeline: "it would be hard for me to make a bet on any specific company based on something that was in the pipeline" (S093, 2008-03)
- 2015-02: "Charlie and I have no idea what a great many companies will look like ten years from now." (S034) 2018-02: "I don't understand manufacturing operations as well as I do the activities of real estate brokers, home builders or truck stops." (S037)
- 2019-05 (newest): "we don’t want to try and win at a game we don’t understand." (S130) "In some cases I’ve learned that we, that I’m incompetent, which is actually a plus" (S130)

## 6. Technology and AI
Sources: 13 (S017, S022, S026, S046, S069, S079, S080, S105, S116, S122, S130, S134, S035).
- 1967-01: "I know about as much about semi-conductors or integrated circuits as I do of the mating habits of the chrzaszcz." (S069)
- 1991-04: "we've never owned a share of a technology company." (S080) 1992-02: "We will never develop the competence to spot such businesses early." (S017)
- 1998-10: "I am not going to be able to figure what the moat is going to look like for Oracle, Lotus or Microsoft, ten years from now." (S079)
- 2000-02: "This explains, by the way, why we don’t own stocks of tech companies, even though we share the general view that our society will be transformed by their products and services." (S046)
- 2016-02: GEICO was working with IBM's Watson, but "I don't know anything about tech, obviously." (S116)
- 2019-05 (changed): Apple was the largest holding (see 38), he was open to tech moats, and he admitted missing Google even though GEICO saw its ads work (S130, paraphrase).
- 2025-05 (newest): on AI against a great insurance manager, "I wouldn’t trade everything that’s developed in AI in the next 10 years for Ajit." (S134) He flagged that big tech is no longer asset-light: "There’s more capital intensity going on with the magnificent 7 than there was a few years ago." (S134)

## 7. Buybacks
Sources: 20 (S006, S010, S011, S023, S030, S031, S032, S033, S036, S038, S039, S040, S041, S042, S043, S046, S122, S130, S134, S034).
- 1981-02: buybacks can buy earning power at "a price under 50% of that needed to acquire the same earning power through the negotiated acquisition of another enterprise." (S006)
- 1985-02: "A manager who consistently turns his back on repurchases, when these clearly are in the interests of owners, reveals more than he knows of his motivations." (S010)
- 1998-05, good and bad cases: Coke should nearly always have bought back stock, "there are very few times in that 112 years, if any, when it would not have been smart for Coca-Cola be repurchasing its shares" (S122); option issuers who buy back "you know, they sell low and then they buy high" (S122)
- 2000-02: "Now, repurchases are all the rage, but are all too often made for an unstated and, in our view, ignoble reason: to pump or support the stock price." (S046)
- Berkshire's own, dated. 2011-02: "Furthermore, not a dime of cash has left Berkshire for dividends or share repurchases during the past 40 years." (S030) 2012-02: up to 110 percent of book, and "we have no interest in supporting the stock and that our bids will fade in particularly weak markets." (S031) 2013-03: raised to 120 percent (S032). 2017-02: "To date, repurchasing our shares has proved hard to do." (S036) 2020-02: book-value cap dropped for a value test, "Berkshire will buy back its stock only if a) Charlie and I believe that it is selling for less than it is worth" (S039). 2022-02: $51.7 billion over two years, about 9 percent of shares (S041). 2023-02: "you are listening to either an economic illiterate or a silver-tongued demagogue" (S042), of those who call all buybacks bad. 2024-02: "All stock repurchases should be price-dependent." (S043)
- 2025-05 (newest): no Berkshire buybacks so far that year, with the new excise tax a factor: "there is a tax that was introduced a year or so ago where we pay 1%." (S134)
- Investee buybacks: "The math of repurchases grinds away slowly, but can be powerful over time." (S040, 2021-02) "we will do considerably better in the next 10 years if their stocks do terribly during certain periods and that they buy lots of stock." (S130, 2019-05)

## 8. Cash and the cash pile
Sources: 18 (S012, S023, S024, S029, S031, S033, S034, S037, S038, S041, S043, S044, S045, S049, S050, S087, S130, S134).
- 1999-02: "Cash never makes us happy." (S045) "But it’s better to have the money burning a hole in Berkshire’s pocket than resting comfortably in someone else’s." (S045)
- 2004-02: "It's a painful condition to be in, but not as painful as doing something stupid." (S050) 2005-02: "Berkshire therefore ended the year with $43 billion of cash equivalents, not a happy position." (S024)
- 2008-10, for the public: "Today people who hold cash equivalents feel comfortable. They shouldn't" (S087) 2012-02: "We heard cash is king in late 2008, just when cash should have been deployed rather than held." (S031)
- The floor, 2014-02: "Moreover, we will always maintain supreme financial strength, operating with at least $20 billion of cash equivalents and never incurring material amounts of short-term obligations." (S033) 2015-02: "Cash, though, is to a business as oxygen is to an individual: never thought about when it is present, the only thing in mind when it is absent." (S034)
- 2018-02, $116 billion: "This extraordinary liquidity earns only a pittance and is far beyond the level Charlie and I wish Berkshire to have." (S037) 2019-05: he would rather own an index fund than T-bills, but Berkshire's size and need for speed justify cash (S130, paraphrase).
- 2022-02, $144 billion: "Berkshire's current 80%-or-so position in businesses is a consequence of my failure to find entire companies or small portions thereof" (S041)
- 2025-02: "Despite what some commentators currently view as an extraordinary cash position at Berkshire, the great majority of your money remains in equities." (S044) "Berkshire will never prefer ownership of cash-equivalent assets over the ownership of good businesses, whether controlled or only partially owned." (S044)
- 2025-05 (newest), $335 billion in Treasuries, not a market call and not a gift to his successor: "We have made a lot of money by not wanting to be fully invested at all times." (S134) He said he would rather hold about $50 billion than force $50 billion a year into the market (S134, paraphrase).

## 9. Management and incentives
Sources: 18 (S004, S012, S016, S022, S025, S034, S043, S044, S079, S080, S105, S122, S126, S130, S017, S035, S037, S049).
- 1987-02: "They unfailingly think like owners (the highest compliment we can pay a manager)" (S012) 1991-02: "Our extraordinary returns flow from outstanding operating managers, not fortuitous industry economics." (S016)
- 1991-04: "We have to decide if they're working because they love the business, or because they love money." (S080)
- 1997-02: "Loss of focus is what most worries Charlie and me when we contemplate investing in businesses that in general look outstanding." (S022)
- 2006-02: "It's hard to overemphasize the importance of who is CEO of a company." (S025) 2008-05: "we have to see the passion in their eyes, and if we see that, then we have to behave in a way that that passion remains" (S126)
- Hands off once in: "I don't tell the management anything. I never have." (S105, 2010-03)
- 2015-02: "the ability to fight off the ABCs of business decay, which are arrogance, bureaucracy and complacency." (S034) 2019-05: "And you can’t incentivize the wrong behavior. You’ve got to do something about it when it happens." (S130)
- 2024-02 and 2025-02 (newest): "Sincerity and empathy can easily be faked." (S043) "I’ve observed, however, that a very large portion of business talent is innate with nature swamping nurture." (S044)

## 10. Concentration versus diversification
Sources: 17 (S001, S004, S010, S017, S019, S022, S023, S031, S039, S045, S067, S079, S080, S083, S093, S126, S134).
- 1966-01: "I am willing to concentrate quite heavily in what I believe to be the best investment opportunities recognizing very well that this may cause an occasional very sour year" (S067)
- 1985-02: "With our financial strength we can own large blocks of a few securities that we have thought hard about and bought at attractive prices." (S010)
- 1994-02: "a policy of portfolio concentration may well decrease risk" (S019)
- 1991-04: against spreading thin: "I call that the Noah's Ark approach to investing, have two of everything." (S080) 1998-10: "Very few people have gotten rich on their seventh best idea. But a lot of people have gotten rich with their best idea." (S079)
- For the know-nothing investor the answer flips: "I believe 98%, 99% who invest should extensively diversify and not trade, so that leads them to an index fund type of decision with very low costs." (S079, 1998-10)
- His own money: over 99 percent in Berkshire for decades (S045, S039, S083); "I'm only 99 percent Berkshire myself, so I never go 100 percent" (S093, 2008-03)
- 2025-05 (newest, more concentrated in hindsight): "I thought if we did about five things in our lifetime, we’d end up doing better than if we did 50, and that we never concentrated enough." (S134)

## 11. Market timing and forecasting
Sources: 17 (S018, S023, S024, S034, S036, S046, S052, S079, S085, S087, S093, S097, S105, S116, S122, S126, S134).
- 1959-01: "I make no attempt to forecast the general market, my efforts are devoted to finding undervalued securities." (S052)
- 1993-02: "short-term market forecasts are poison and should be kept locked up in a safe place, away from children and also from grown-ups who behave in the market like children." (S018)
- 2001-12: "I was no good then at forecasting the near-term movements of stock prices, and I'm no good now" (S085)
- He knows only extremes: "when things get very cheap, I know it. When they get very high, I know it" (S093, 2008-03) He acted on valuation, not forecasts: closed the partnership in 1969, trimmed stocks for bonds in 1997 (S023, S122), and regrets talking instead of selling in the 1999 bubble: "I talked when I should have walked." (S024, 2005-02)
- 2009-03: "if I knew the bottom, you know, I wouldn’t have to look up 10-Ks and do all that stuff, I’d just buy the S&P 500 at the bottoms" (S097)
- 2025-05 (newest, same view): "But they spend all their time talking about it because it’s easy to talk about, but it has no value." (S134)

## 12. Size as a limit on returns
Sources: 17 (S002, S012, S017, S020, S021, S027, S029, S034, S036, S043, S045, S069, S082, S126, S130, S134, S048).
- 1984-05: "Size is the anchor of performance." (S082) 1995-02: "A fat wallet, however, is the enemy of superior investment results." (S020)
- 2008-05: small investors can do better: "if you’re working with small amounts of money and you want to put in significant amounts of time and examine thousands of securities, you will find things that are more intelligent to buy than Berkshire." (S126)
- 2019-05: "But you change the 1 million to 100 million, and that 50 goes down like a, like a rock." (S130)
- 2025-11 (newest): "However, a decade or two from now, there will be many companies that have done better than Berkshire; our size takes its toll." (S002)

## 13. Macro, the Fed, deficits and America
Sources: 16 (S010, S024, S033, S034, S038, S042, S050, S079, S083, S089, S097, S105, S122, S134, S035, S037).
- 1998-10: "I don't think about the macro stuff." (S079) 2004-02: "I should note that the cemetery for seers has a huge section set aside for macro forecasters." (S050) Yet he did make a dollar bet in 2002 to 2006 and admitted "macro-economics is a tough game in which few people, Charlie and I included, have demonstrated skill." (S024)
- The Fed in crises, praised: on the 2008 money-market backstop, "if they hadn’t have insured money market accounts and, in effect, commercial paper, you know, you and I would be meeting at McDonald’s this morning." (S097, 2009-03) "I consider them to have done a terrific job during that period" (S083, 2010-05)
- Deficits, changed then hardened. 1985-02: called the deficit dangerous (S010, paraphrase). 2019-02: said he used to preach doom on deficits while the debt rose 400-fold and America still prospered (S038, paraphrase). 2023-02: "Huge and entrenched fiscal deficits have consequences." (S042) 2025-05 (newest): "we’re operating at a fiscal deficit now that is unsustainable." (S134) On tariffs: "trade can be an act of war." (S134)
- America, constant since 1942: "Charlie and I have always considered a bet on ever-rising U.S. prosperity to be very close to a sure thing." (S033, 2014-02) "who has ever benefited during the past 238 years by betting against America?" (S034, 2015-02)

## 14. Index funds and fees
Sources: 16 (S022, S024, S025, S029, S032, S033, S034, S036, S037, S040, S050, S056, S079, S093, S116, S130).
- 1962-01: "I present this data to indicate the Dow as an investment competitor is no pushover" (S056)
- 1997-02: "Seriously, costs matter." (S022) 2004-02: "those index funds that are very low-cost (such as Vanguard's) are investor-friendly by definition and are the best selection for most of those who wish to own equities." (S050)
- 2014-02: "A low-cost S&P 500 index fund will achieve this goal." (S033) 2016-02: "I think the best thing for most of 'em to do is to buy an index" (S116)
- 2017-02 and 2018-02, after the hedge-fund bet, of Jack Bogle: "He is a hero to them and to me." (S036) "Performance comes, performance goes. Fees never falter." (S037)
- Berkshire itself must beat the index or it adds nothing: "Why should they pay us for merely duplicating that result?" (S029, 2010-02)

## 15. Accounting quality
Sources: 16 (S008, S014, S016, S018, S030, S040, S042, S045, S047, S048, S083, S097, S122, S134, S043, S044).
- 1983-02: "the reaction of weak managements to weak operations is often weak accounting." (S008) 1989-02: "It has been far safer to steal large sums with a pen than small sums with a gun." (S014)
- 1991-02: "earnings can be as pliable as putty when a charlatan heads the company reporting them." (S016) 1993-02: "calling a tail a leg does not make it a leg." (S018)
- 1999-02: "Bad accounting drives out good." (S045) 2001-02: depreciation is real, "Anyone who thinks that the annual charges for depreciation don’t reflect a real cost" (S047) 2002-02: "Those who believe that EBITDA is in any way equivalent to true earnings are welcome to pick up the tab." (S048)
- Smooth promised earnings are a warning: "any time a large financial institution starts promising regular earnings increases you're going to have trouble" (S083, 2010-05) "One way or another, you’re going to cheat." (S097, 2009-03)
- 2023-02: "It requires no talent to manipulate numbers: Only a deep desire to deceive is required." (S042) For Berkshire he points owners to operating earnings: "Operating earnings are what count most" (S040, 2021-02)

## 16. Inflation
Sources: 15 (S005, S007, S009, S010, S031, S042, S044, S086, S087, S090, S093, S097, S105, S134, S035).
- 1977-05: "peacetime inflation is a political problem, not an economic problem" (S090) "As a common stockholder you will have more dollars, but you may have no more purchasing power" (S090)
- 1980-02: "a business earning 20% on capital can produce a negative real return for its owners under inflationary conditions not much more severe than presently prevail." (S005) 1982-02: "For inflation acts as a gigantic corporate tapeworm." (S007)
- The best defense is a business that needs little new capital: "During inflation, Goodwill is the gift that keeps giving." (S009, 1984-02) "Farms, real estate, and many businesses such as Coca-Cola, IBM and our own See’s Candy meet that double-barreled test." (S031, 2012-02)
- 2008-03: "the problem with inflation is that it's very easy to ignite it and it's very hard to put it out" (S093)
- 2025-02 and 2025-05 (newest): "Paper money can see its value evaporate if fiscal folly prevails." (S044) "the natural course of government is to make the currency worth less over time." (S134)

- 1998-02: "You pay a very high price in the stock market for a cheery consensus." (S023)

- 2010-05: "We will have other bubbles in the future, I mean, there's no question about that" (S083) 2015-02: "Periodically, financial markets will become divorced from reality, you can count on that." (S034)

## 19. Bonds and credit
Sources: 15 (S010, S012, S013, S029, S031, S037, S040, S048, S049, S050, S076, S078, S086, S122, S126).
- 1969-10 (an unusual moment): "For the first time in my investment lifetime. I now believe there is little choice for the average investor between professionally managed money in stocks and passive investment in bonds." (S076)
- 1985-02: "we dislike the purchase of most long-term bonds under most circumstances" (S010) 1988-02: "Bonds are no better than the currency in which they are denominated" (S013)
- Junk, bought only when hated: about $8 billion in 2002, then in 2004 "Yesterday's weeds are today being priced as flowers." (S050) "These are not, we should emphasize, suitable investments for the general public, because too often these securities live up to their name." (S048)
- 2010-02, on the 2008 credit panic: "We backed this view with some purchases, but I should have done far more." (S029)
- 2012-02: "Right now bonds should come with a warning label." (S031) "Their beta may be zero, but their risk is huge" (S086)
- 2018-02 and 2021-02 (newest): "Often, high-grade bonds in an investment portfolio increase its risk." (S037) "And bonds are not the place to be these days." (S040)

## 20. Executive pay, options and stock-based compensation
Sources: 15 (S002, S011, S018, S020, S023, S024, S025, S036, S038, S049, S050, S083, S122, S126, S130).

## 21. Leverage and margin
Sources: 14 (S001, S013, S016, S034, S037, S038, S056, S079, S080, S083, S097, S126, S015, S134).
- 1962-01: borrowing only against workouts, capped at 25 percent of net worth (S056, paraphrase).
- 1988-02: "Really good businesses usually don't need to borrow." (S013)
- 1991-02: heavy debt fails at the first pothole: "a plan that requires dodging them all is a plan for disaster." (S016) 1991-04: "You really don't need leverage in this world much." (S080)
- 1996-06: "The financial calculus that Charlie and I employ would never permit our trading a good night’s sleep for a shot at a few extra percentage points of return." (S001)
- 1998-10, on LTCM: "If you risk something that is important to you for something that is unimportant to you it just doesn't make sense." (S079)
- 2009-03 and 2010-05: "And leverage is a lot of fun on the way up" (S097) "if you don't have leverage, you don't get into trouble" (S083)
- 2018-02 and 2019-02 (newest, same view): "Our aversion to leverage has dampened our returns over the years. But Charlie and I sleep well." (S037) "Rational people don't risk what they have and need for what they don't have and don't need." (S038)
- The one stated exception: yen debt matched to yen holdings in Japan (S134, 2025-05).

## 22. Hedging, derivatives and currencies

(S044)

## 28. Position sizing

## 35. Growth versus value
Sources: 7 (S015, S018, S029, S047, S130, S013, S122).
- 1993-02: the split is false: "what is investing if it is not the act of seeking value at least sufficient to justify the amount paid?" (S018) He admits he once used the split himself (S018).
- Growth can destroy value (airlines, see 31). "In a finite world, high growth rates must self-destruct." (S015, 1990-02)
- 2001-02: "I would wager you a very significant sum that fewer than 10 of the 200 most profitable companies in 2000 will attain 15% annual growth in earnings-per-share over the next 20 years." (S047)
- 2010-02: autos, aircraft and TV sets grew, yet "But the future then also included competitive dynamics that would decimate almost all of the companies entering those industries." (S029)
- 2019-05 (newest): growth is part of value, but "you can pay too much for a growing brand." (S130)

## 36. Japan
Sources: 6 (S079, S105, S122, S043, S044, S134).
- 1998-05 and 1998-10: cheap on book but poor returns, so no bargain. "it’s extremely difficult to get rich by owning, by being the owner of a business that earns a low return on equity." (S122) "it is very hard to earn a lot as an investor when the business you are in doesn't earn very much money." (S079)
- 2010-03: "Japan is the great mystery of all time." (S105)
- 2024-02 (changed): buying the five trading houses since July 2019, which "follow shareholder-friendly policies that are much superior to those customarily practiced in the U.S." (S043) and whose managers "have been far less aggressive about their own compensation than is typical in the United States." (S043) A self-imposed cap: "it will not purchase shares that will take our holdings beyond 9.9%." (S043)
- 2025-02: "We simply looked at their financial records and were amazed at the low prices of their stocks." (S044) Cost $13.8 billion, value $23.5 billion, funded with yen debt (S044). "Our holdings of the five are for the very long term" (S044)
- 2025-05 (newest): a 50-year hold, cap being relaxed, and he wishes it were larger. "in the next 50 years, and I hope he’s running things in, we won’t give a thought to selling those." (S134) Yen borrowing is an exception, not a policy: "The Japanese situation is different because we intend to stay long in that position, and the funding situation is so cheap." (S134)
