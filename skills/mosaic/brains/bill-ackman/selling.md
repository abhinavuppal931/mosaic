# Selling: Bill Ackman

Built from the verified batch notes on the Pershing Square letters, reports and decks (2014 to 2026), the 2020 hedge letters (S030, S091) and his interviews and podcasts (2014 to 2026). His rules for selling, trimming, adding, unwinding hedges and re-checking a position, most-used first. Two rules changed over time: the activist short (held for years, then ended in 2018) and the bond hedge (short rates until October 2023, then long); both are shown with dates. Pershing Square company text (investment policy, risk tables, statements by board chairs) is not quoted as his words.

## Rules

### 1. Sell when the price reaches value or the return left falls below the hurdle, even for a great business
Sources: 14 (S001, S005, S006, S007, S010, S015, S017, S018, S034, S038, S047, S087, S016, S064).
The default is to hold: "absent excessive overvaluation or a substantially better use of capital, there are few good reasons to sell." (S001) The exception is price. Starbucks went from 19 to 28 times forward earnings and was sold in January 2020: "in January we sold our stake in Starbucks as it approached our estimate of intrinsic value." (S006) Hilton was bought under 23 times and sold in 2026 at about 32 times: "we exited the position earlier this year as we believed HLT’s current valuation made it unlikely that the future share price growth would meet our high returns thresholds." (S010) Alphabet was sold after three to four times: "alphabet got to a price that we felt the go-forward return had dropped below kind of our long-term aspirations" (S087), and "we did so not because we don't think Alphabet's a great business" (S047). A sale is not a verdict on the company: "Despite the sale, we expect that Starbucks will continue to be a good, long-term investment." (S007)

### 2. Sell a good holding when the capital has a clearly better use
Sources: 13 (S004, S008, S012, S013, S026, S027, S035, S038, S041, S081, S091, S017, S005).
"we are always willing to redeploy capital if an investment appreciates to a level that no longer offers sufficient returns relative to other potential opportunities." (S005) Zoetis: "Despite the high quality nature of the business and its strong management team, we sold to redeploy the capital in certain new investments." (S026) In March 2020 he sold Berkshire at a loss to buy Hilton and Lowe's: "The opportunities had expanded dramatically in March of 2020" (S081). He expects to sell recent buys if something better appears: "This may lead us to sell certain of our existing holdings including investments we recently purchased." (S091) Inside the team the test is plain: "I like this better than this, we should sell the idea I've done the work on to buy this." (S041)

### 3. Exit when the catalyst is spent and the transformation is delivered
Sources: 8 (S004, S009, S026, S027, S036, S015, S034, S067).
An activist position ends when the change is done and priced. Canadian Pacific was sold in 2016 about five years in, with the shares up nearly four times (S004). Air Products and Nomad went the same way: "We exited Air Products and Nomad since our last quarterly communication, generating substantial gains for the funds while freeing up capital for new investments." (S027) Lowe's was sold after nearly six years once margins had closed much of the gap: "Exited Lowe’s after a highly successful, nearly six-year holding period, freeing up capital for new opportunities" (S036) He steps off the board the same way, as at Zoetis (S067).

### 4. A falling price is a re-check, not a sale: add if intrinsic value is intact
Sources: 16 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S020, S022, S024, S044, S065, S079, S082, S058, S064, S028, S032, S041).
"While we don’t automatically buy more if the share prices of our holdings decline, it is the rare circumstance where an existing holding’s stock price declines meaningfully and we are not excited to take advantage of the opportunity." (S001) The test is whether value fell with price: "The above would not be true in the event that recent declines in the market prices of our investments correspond with similar declines in their intrinsic values." (S002) After a shock he sends people in to "build our own financial model for the company." (S003) If the thesis holds, the lower price raises conviction: "we remain optimistic about the risk/return profile of these positions to a greater extent than before because of their reduced prices." (S005) He added to Chipotle "when the company’s share price was less than half of today’s market price, and 21% lower than our original cost." (S065) "That doesn't necessarily mean you've made an investment mistake." (S044)

### 5. When management breaks the thesis, leave rather than fight a second war
Sources: 7 (S015, S034, S046, S004, S039, S065, S001).
United Technologies was bought for a breakup and sold when the board chose a stock merger with Raytheon: "For UTX, the announcement of a value-destroying stock merger was the catalyst for our exit." (S015) "we decided that it was more productive to sell our stock at a small profit and focus our efforts on finding new opportunities." (S015) At Valeant the break was conduct: "We believed that Valeant had a strong management team, a good business strategy, and a low-cost and disciplined operating model." (S046) He judges a strategy that depends on trust in management as fragile: "And it was very much a confidence sensitive strategy because they built their business by acquiring pharmaceutical assets" (S039)

### 6. Sell when the business can no longer be predicted
Sources: 6 (S018, S038, S078, S087, S001, S040).
Netflix was sold weeks after purchase in 2022 when the model shifted to advertising: "management commentary and a required shift in the company’s business model toward ad-supported streaming caused us to lose confidence in our ability to predict the company’s future prospects with a sufficient degree of certainty" (S018) "we think the dispersion of outcomes here has widened dramatically." (S078) The rule: new facts force a choice, buy more "or you have to exit because the thesis is broken." (S078) The same reason ended Chipotle in 2025: "the wide range of potential future outcomes led us to sell our remaining shares" (S038)

### 7. Engage before you sell when a core holding breaks
Sources: 7 (S003, S025, S027, S046, S067, S024, S004).
Defensive activism comes before the exit. At Valeant, after a 51% one-day fall, he did not sell: "Pershing Square has recently taken steps to stabilize the company and to protect our investment, including adding two team members to the board, Bill Ackman and Steve Fraidin" (S067) Before adding, he re-underwrote every charge: "In particular, we reviewed all of the short sellers' allegations, the potential political and regulatory risks, the impact of the shutdown of Philidor" (S024) At Chipotle after the 2017 incident: "We are working with the new Chipotle board and management to assist the company in turning around its operations." (S027) Only after the repair did he sell Valeant (S004).

### 8. Take the permanent loss, and cut a small position that eats the firm's attention
Sources: 7 (S004, S008, S013, S039, S001, S041, S055).
He sold Valeant "at a price that may end up looking cheap." (S004) because a small position would not repay "the human resources and substantial mindshare that this investment had and would have continued to consume if we had remained a shareholder." (S004) He closed Herbalife at the same time: "When we sold Valeant, we covered Herbalife. That was the resetting moment for the firm" (S039) He accepts that both later paid: "We sold Valeant and closed out our Herbalife short despite our belief that substantial profits would likely have been realized from their then trading prices." (S008) "sometimes the better part of valor in an investment situation is to move on." (S001) And "you don't need to make it back the same way you lost it." (S041)

### 9. Trim a position that has grown outsized, but keep the core
Sources: 9 (S002, S006, S013, S021, S025, S037, S040, S064, S050).
Size is managed, the tape is not traded: "we do not trade around our long-term holdings and generally only make adjustments in the size of positions to manage concentration risks in the portfolio." (S040) "We sold approximately 25% of our stake in QSR as its strong price appreciation year-to-date, and over the last 12 months, made it a disproportionately large percentage of the portfolio." (S013) Chipotle was cut by a third when it passed 20% of the book (S006). Hilton was trimmed on a rising premium: "We used this as an opportunity to trim our holdings, although we intend to remain a long-term Hilton shareholder." (S064) In 2016 he sold Mondelez forwards near a still-discounted price because the weight had grown (S002).

### 10. Do not sell core holdings on a macro view: hedge instead
Sources: 7 (S006, S011, S030, S040, S079, S018, S032).
"We do not typically sell our core portfolio holdings even if we believe it is highly probable that they will decline in price in the short term, as long as our view of their long-term potential remains largely unchanged." (S040) In February 2020 he considered liquidating everything for the first time and chose CDS instead (S006). His reasons: the companies he helped build, friction, and "We incur a huge amount of tax liabilities because we have big embedded gains in the companies that we own." (S079) Without margin there is no forced sale; in August 2015 the only change was more currency puts (S011).

### 11. Unwind a hedge when its risk-reward worsens, and redeploy at once; keep it while the risk is live
Sources: 9 (S030, S035, S009, S019, S031, S008, S079, S016, S091).
The COVID CDS were sold from March 12, 2020 because the spread had moved from about 50 to 140 basis points, the position neared 40% of capital, and buying stocks had become the better trade: "Furthermore, the deterioration in markets greatly increased the opportunity cost of our owning CDS." (S030) "this hedge could be worth zero and the stock market can go right back up to where it was." (S079) He would have sold faster if size allowed: "Had we had been able to sell our entire CDS position on March 12th, we would likely have done so" (S030) But a hedge is not cashed while the storm is still coming: "If one purchased a large homeowner's policy, one should be similarly reluctant to cancel it in exchange for a substantial payment from the carrier if a large tornado were enroute." (S009) and "We did not sell these hedges at year end because we remained concerned about the risk of higher long-term rates on our portfolio." (S019) A better use of the cash can still win: "Had we not sold the hedge, we could have likely realized more gains based on the increase in rates, largely today, since our sale." (S031)

### 12. Sell or never finish buying when a full position cannot be built
Sources: 5 (S005, S013, S026, S065, S066).
"We sold our shares in S&P as the large stock price increase during our accumulation period prevented us from establishing a meaningful position in the company." (S005) Hilton in 2016 was sold "as its share price appreciated rapidly during our accumulation period." (S065) He does not chase: a rising price stopped the second 2016 position at half size (S026).

### 13. Exit a short when technicals beat fundamentals (older rule, before 2018)
Sources: 6 (S005, S027, S066, S039, S051, S024).
Older view, 2012 to 2017: hold a short through a rally while regulators were still active (S024, S051). By 2017 the shrinking float and Icahn's stake made the stock untradeable on fundamentals, so he capped the loss with puts and then exited: "the shares have become a highly risky short sale in light of the extremely limited free float, and as a result, we have exited this investment." (S005) Newer, from 2018: no public equity shorts at all (see positions, topic 11).

### 14. Never let redemptions or leverage force the sale
Sources: 7 (S024, S039, S044, S047, S011, S080, S018).
"But many people go out of business because as I mentioned before, large move in a price, if investors are redeeming or you have leverage can put you out of business." (S039) In the 2015 drawdown redemptions were nominal: "As a result, we have not been forced to raise cash as the portfolio declined" (S024) "as long as you're not forced to sell at any one period of time" (S044) The cost when it happened was Canadian Pacific, sold to meet redemptions (S039). Permanent capital is his answer to "imagine a world in which all your clients pulled your money at precisely the time that you should be deploying it" (S047)

### 15. Buy back what you sold when fear, not the business, cuts the price
Sources: 8 (S016, S035, S056, S078, S087, S065, S091, S022).
"In March, shortly after our exit, the stock declined substantially along with the broader market, providing an opportunity for us to repurchase our stake in the company at a highly attractive valuation." (S016) Starbucks went out at $86 and back in at $60 (S035). Hilton was sold in 2016 and rebought in late 2018 (S065). Netflix came back in 2026 after it halved: "at 50% off versus where it was, even a few months ago, we thought it was an attractive entry price" (S087)

## Kill criteria this seat would write
Checkable facts, in his style, that end a thesis (inferred from the rules above and the sources named).
- Management or the board takes an action that contradicts the reason we bought, such as a stock-funded merger that destroys the separation case, and replacing them would take a second full campaign (S015, S034).
- Management walks back the strategy we underwrote, or a model shift widens the range of outcomes so we can no longer forecast free cash flow within a reasonable band (S018, S078, S038).
- The operator who was the thesis leaves, and the next team's results miss the plan we underwrote (S038).
- The turnaround fails both of our pre-set outcomes: fundamentals do not improve, and leadership does not change (S064).
- The thesis depends on capital markets access or on trust in management, and that access or trust closes (S004, S039).
- The share price reaches our conservative intrinsic value, or the forward return falls below our long-term hurdle (S006, S010, S087).
- A surprise in capex turns out to be maintenance, not growth, or returns on the new spending fall (S010).
- For a policy or legal catalyst: the path we are paying for is closed; drop that path and keep the position only if another path and growing business value remain (S040, S082).
- For a hedge: the spread or rate has moved so far that the payoff is no longer asymmetric, or policy action has removed the risk (S030, S035).
- For any short: the free float shrinks until the stock trades on squeeze risk rather than value (S005).

## Re-check triggers
- Every quarter: the thesis metrics, same-store sales, margins, unit growth and bookings, against peers and against the multiple (S029, S028).
- Each year: management's public commitments against delivery, with the next annual meeting as the deadline (S005, S027).
- Any large price drop: send people in, rebuild the model, and ask whether intrinsic value fell or only price (S002, S003, S024).
- A CEO change, the departure of a key operator, or a CFO hire in a transformation (S014, S037, S038).
- Entry multiple versus current multiple; a re-rating that uses up the expected return (S034, S038).
- Position size as a share of the portfolio after a run (S013, S040).
- A board seat or deal talks that would restrict trading (S002).
- Elections, administration actions and court rulings for policy catalysts (S065, S082, S036).
- Credit spreads near record tights and the level of implied volatility, which set the price of protection (S030, S063, S064).
- A disruption review of each moat, now led by AI risk (S041, S042, S087).
- Any objection from a colleague, which triggers more diligence before a decision (S080).
- A miss on a name the team knows: "Let's update the file." (S080)

## Selling mistakes he admits, with dates
- 2011: sold Lowe's right after buying on a quick price rise, then watched it fall behind Home Depot (S014).
- 2015: did not sell Valeant in the mid-$200s, near intrinsic value, and became restricted from trading; "In retrospect, this was a very costly mistake." (S002)
- 2016: sold Canadian Pacific for capital and redemptions; held, it would have done far better (S039).
- 2017: sold Valeant "at a price that may end up looking cheap." (S004) and closed Herbalife before both moved his way (S008).
- 2022: sold Netflix within weeks at a 44% loss, then did not buy it back on the rebound: "Probably should have bought it back then, but we didn't." (S087)
- 2023: held the 30-year payer swaptions too long, selling at 33% above cost after they had been 92% above (S009).
- 2024 to 2025: Chipotle after Niccol left: "We did not adequately adjust our assumptions and required returns following these changes" (S038)
