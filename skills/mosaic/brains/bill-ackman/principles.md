# Principles: Bill Ackman

Built from the Pershing Square Holdings annual and interim letters (2014 to 2026), the quarterly investor letters, the 2020 hedge letters, the annual investor presentations (2016 to 2026), the 2012 Canadian Pacific proxy letters, the Valeant testimony, and interviews and podcasts from 2014 to 2026. Decision rules, weighted for this seat: activism and engagement as the catalyst and governance first, then the asymmetric hedges, then the simple, predictable, free-cash-flow quality filter, price, sizing and selling. Within each group, most-supported first. Pershing Square company text (investment policy, principal risk tables, chairman's statements) is paraphrased and labeled, never quoted as his. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## Activism, engagement and governance

### 1. Buy a great business that is underperforming for fixable reasons, then be the catalyst that closes the gap.

- "Often, we are the catalyst for value recognition, using our influential position as a large, or the largest, shareholder often with representation on the board of directors to effectuate necessary changes." (S014)
- "we target companies that have underperformed their potential. We do so by becoming a large shareholder, obtaining board representation or an otherwise influential role, and then by assisting the company in making management, governance, operational and other changes." (S046)
- Supporting sources: 14 (S001, S005, S013, S014, S015, S021, S039, S041, S043, S046, S050, S052, S064, S081). Confidence: high.

### 2. The biggest profits sit where the business is great and management resists change, because sellers assume nothing will change.

- "We have learned from experience that often the greatest opportunities for profit exist at companies that are the most resistant to and threatened by change." (S013)
- "So we get involved in cases where a great business has kind of made a big mistake or you've a company that's kind of lost its way, but it's recoverable." (S039)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S013, S039, S041, S043, S087). Confidence: high.

### 3. Engage privately first; go public, seek seats or run a proxy contest only when the board will not engage.

- "we approached ADP and offered to share privately our due diligence and analysis with the board to see if we could work together to unlock value for all shareholders." (S013)
- "activism is only needed if you can't get into the boardroom." (S047)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S008, S009, S013, S015, S027, S034, S047, S078, S080). Confidence: high.
- Changed over time: proxy contests at Canadian Pacific (2012) and ADP (2017); quiet engagement named Pershing Square 3.0 in March 2022 (S008); **newer (2026):** "I think we have a sufficient amount of influence that we can get to the right answer with dialogue." (S078)

### 4. When a company fails, blame the board; fix the board and the CEO together.

- "When a company fails, most people blame the CEO. I generally blame the board." (S039)
- "nothing less than a fundamental board restructuring and a new CEO will put CP back on track" (S094)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S039, S043, S065, S093, S094). Confidence: high.

### 5. Put the best CEO in charge, preferably one who has already done this turnaround, and have a replacement in mind before you need one.

- "Our first choice is to make sure that we have the best CEOs running the companies we own." (S065)
- "we want to have someone in mind that we could install if we need to replace the team." (S078)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S014, S023, S025, S039, S050, S065, S078, S080, S093). Confidence: high.
- He rates the business above the CEO: "If it's the wrong CEO, we can play a role in recruiting a better one." (S080)

### 6. Judge management on financial results against the same peers over years, not on promises, plans or friendly operating metrics.

- "Absent an accompanying record of improved financial performance, improvements in non-financial operating metrics cannot support a conclusion of sustainable progress." (S094)
- "we are not prepared to ignore what history has demonstrated about management's and the board's inability to deliver sustained performance" (S093)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S024, S092, S093, S094, S087). Confidence: high.
- A change in what a company measures is a warning: "companies that change the standards by which they measure themselves do so only when the old metric shows business deterioration that they would rather not disclose." (S024)

### 7. Incentives drive behavior: read the proxy, pay for controllable per-share results, and want insiders who bought their stock.

- "incentives drive all human behavior and that certainly applies in the business world" (S039)
- "just look at the proxy and see how they're being compensated" (S041)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S006, S008, S013, S026, S039, S041, S081, S084, S093, S046). Confidence: high.
- On directors who did not buy: "no investor would buy 14% of a company and join a board of directors unless it was interested in the creation of long-term shareholder value" (S093)

### 8. Oversee, do not run; stay a constructive, credible long-term owner, because trading around positions costs you the voice that drives change.

- "We view our job as oversight, not day-to-day management." (S065)
- "Were we to constantly trade around our positions, we would have a less credible voice with management and other shareholders when advocating for strategic initiatives and corporate changes which have long-term implications." (S040)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S008, S040, S042, S065, S081). Confidence: high.

### 9. Prefer a business that needs no fixing when one is available at a good price; go passive only when price, quality, management and governance are all right.

- "When the price is right, the business is of sufficient quality, and we have confidence in management and the company's governance, we are willing to be a passive investor." (S046)
- "The best investments are ones where you don't need to join the board and do anything." (S042)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S013, S042, S046, S047). Confidence: high.
- Changed over time: in 2017 he wrote "we generally prefer to allocate our capital to activist situations" (S013); **newer (2026):** of the already well-run business, "So, you always choose that first." (S047)

### 10. Avoid controlled companies and insist on independent boards; protect minority holders.

- "We generally don't like to invest in controlled companies." (S080)
- "We believe in independent boards of directors." (S081)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S032, S038, S066, S080, S081). Confidence: high.
- His chiseled principles add that there is typically no controlling shareholder (paraphrase of the S038 and S066 decks). At the 2025 Howard Hughes proposal he capped his own voting power below 50% and offered a majority-of-the-minority vote (S032).

## Asymmetric hedges

### 11. Hedge only with a variant view and an instrument whose payoff is many times its cost; without both, sit tight.

- "we must have a variant view about the near or intermediate term future" (S063)
- "Without extraordinary opportunities, we sit tight." (S064)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S009, S040, S041, S063, S064, S078, S087). Confidence: high.
- His filter for macro trades: "a high degree of asymmetry and a high confidence level in the predicted outcome" (S040)

### 12. Buy insurance when it is cheap, near record-low prices for the risk, and keep its cost at a low single-digit percent of capital.

- "We structure our hedges using asymmetric investments which at cost typically represent a low, single-digit percentage of our capital." (S019)
- "Because we were able to purchase these instruments at near-all-time tight levels of credit spreads, the risk of loss from this investment was minimal at the time of purchase." (S091)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S003, S019, S030, S035, S055, S079, S091). Confidence: high.

### 13. When an outside shock threatens a long-term portfolio, hedge it rather than sell the businesses.

- "We chose to hedge coronavirus risk rather than sell because we are long-term investor, and we believe that all of our companies would eventually recover" (S030)
- "And the hedging is kind of elegant because if nothing happened, we would have lost very little money." (S079)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S006, S030, S079, S091). Confidence: high.

### 14. Take hedge profits when the payoff is no longer asymmetric or the position has grown too large, and recycle them into great businesses at crisis prices.

- "Our decision to unwind our hedges was driven by the less favorable risk-reward ratio offered by our credit hedges as spreads widened, and the much more favorable risk-reward ratio presented by the then-trading values of companies in which we bought shares." (S006)
- "we have generally reinvested the proceeds in our core holdings at lower valuations during market disruptions." (S009)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S006, S009, S016, S030, S031, S032, S035, S079, S091). Confidence: high.
- He accepts leaving money on the table: "Had we not sold the hedge, we could have likely realized more gains based on the increase in rates, largely today, since our sale." (S031)

### 15. Hedges exist to prevent permanent loss and create liquidity when it is most valuable, not to protect marks; they beat a big cash pile or a permanent hedge.

- "Our principal goal in initiating and maintaining hedges is to reduce the overall risk of a permanent loss of capital, and to create liquidity at times when liquidity is most valuable." (S009)
- "We believe that hedging is a better alternative to keeping funds in cash while one is waiting for opportunities, particularly because high rates of inflation cause the purchasing power of cash to decline rapidly." (S008)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S008, S009, S011, S031, S036, S055, S040). Confidence: high.
- Changed over time: in 2015 he did not hedge market declines and held mainly currency hedges (S011); from 2020 the episodic credit and rate hedges became a standing capability run by a dedicated subset of the team (S009, 2024).

### 16. A hedge pays only if you are right on both the risk and its timing, so expect most hedges to cost money and never promise one for every risk.

- "We have to be right on both the nature of the risk and the timeframe in which we expect it to occur for a hedging strategy to be successful." (S019)
- "we can't promise to identify and execute attractive hedges for all future risks." (S009)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S009, S019, S040, S063). Confidence: high.

## Quality, price and capital

### 17. Own only simple, predictable, free-cash-flow-generative businesses whose cash you can model for ten to twenty years.

- "If you can't build a model of the next, you know, reasonable period of time of a business, 10, 15, 20 years, I don't think you can come up with the value for it." (S080)
- "These investments typically end up in the Don't Know pile." (S001)
- Supporting sources: 12 (S001, S005, S006, S011, S033, S038, S044, S052, S064, S078, S079, S080). Confidence: high.

### 18. Avoid businesses exposed to things you cannot control, and never pair a great business with a weak balance sheet.

- "We vastly prefer businesses that have limited exposure to macroeconomic factors by generally avoiding companies that are highly exposed to commodity prices, material changes in interest rates, and other extrinsic factors we cannot control." (S011)
- "You can have the greatest business in the world, you apply too much financial leverage to it, you can get obviously knocked out." (S041)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S011, S041, S044, S063, S064, S080, S032, S040). Confidence: high.

### 19. Ask about disruption first; it is the hardest and most important question.

- "the most important and most challenging thing to do is determine what's the risk of disruption?" (S042)
- "The most difficult analysis to do as an investor is that, is kind of figuring out how wide is the moat, how much at risk is the business to disruption?" (S039)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S039, S041, S042, S054, S078, S087). Confidence: high.

### 20. Price matters even for the best business: buy at a big discount to a rough value, aiming for about 20% a year.

- "You can buy the best business in the world and if you overpay, you're not going to earn particularly attractive returns." (S039)
- "we said, look, at this price, at 35 times earnings or whatever, it's hard for us to get to a 20 plus percent rate of return, which is our ambition." (S078)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S018, S022, S039, S044, S078, S010). Confidence: high.

### 21. Buy a great business when it is in temporary trouble or hit by a fear you can show is wrong.

- "We have always believed that a good time to buy a great business is when it is in temporary trouble." (S026)
- "as long as you believe it's not going to change the long-term trajectory of the business, that creates an interesting opportunity to buy a stake" (S041)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S004, S010, S020, S026, S041, S064, S080, S082, S087). Confidence: high.

### 22. Buy more when prices fall and intrinsic value holds; never follow the crowd's pro-cyclical selling, and do not sit in cash waiting for a cheaper market.

- "This pattern of reducing equity market exposure as stock markets decline occurs in every market disruption, but it is precisely the opposite of what long-term investors should do." (S018)
- "I wouldn't sit around holding cash because you think the market's expensive." (S078)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S003, S018, S024, S065, S067, S078, S082, S087). Confidence: high.

### 23. Never use margin leverage or borrow against securities, so you are never forced out at the wrong time.

- "While stocks can trade at any price in the short term, because we do not use margin leverage, we will not be forced out of any investment at an inopportune time." (S011)
- "One, you shouldn't borrow money." (S039)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S002, S011, S036, S039, S044, S055, S056). Confidence: high.
- His own exception, admitted: "I talked about it before about that you don't borrow money, but I borrowed money and I borrowed $300 million from JP Morgan in the middle of this mess." (S039)

### 24. Concentrate in your best dozen or so ideas, and size each by the chance of permanent loss against the gain.

- "Our strategy of investment concentration is an important competitive advantage as we allocate capital only to our best ideas." (S009)
- "We size things based on how much we think we can make versus how much we think we can lose." (S054)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S009, S017, S039, S041, S047, S054, S087). Confidence: high.
- His loss budget: "We'll probably be willing to lose 5-6% of our capital in any one investment." (S054)

### 25. Size leveraged, complex, legally uncertain or binary bets small, and demand a much larger reward for them.

- "In light of this greater risk, we require the potential for a materially greater reward if we are successful, and we size the investments appropriately." (S001)
- "In light of the inherent uncertainty of the situation, our combined investment in the two companies represents about 3% of our capital at current market values." (S023)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S003, S004, S021, S023, S066). Confidence: high.

## Selling, shorting and learning

### 26. Sell or trim when the price nears intrinsic value or the forward return falls below the hurdle, and redeploy into something better.

- "While our investment holding periods tend to be very long term by most investors' standards, we sell our investments when they approach our estimates of intrinsic value." (S015)
- "alphabet got to a price that we felt the go-forward return had dropped below kind of our long-term aspirations" (S087)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S002, S005, S006, S007, S010, S015, S038, S047, S087). Confidence: high.

### 27. Exit when the thesis breaks or certainty is lost, take the loss, and do not try to win it back in the same name.

- "We lost our conviction in the certainty of the outcome." (S087)
- "And the beauty of investing is you don't need to make it back the way you lost it. You can just take your loss." (S039)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S015, S018, S034, S038, S039, S041, S078, S087). Confidence: high.

### 28. Do not trade the macro or the tape; low turnover is the sign of good original decisions.

- "We do not typically sell our core portfolio holdings even if we believe it is highly probable that they will decline in price in the short term, as long as our view of their long-term potential remains largely unchanged." (S040)
- "Frenetic investment activity is often the enemy of long-term performance." (S009)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S009, S021, S040, S069, S080). Confidence: high.

### 29. Do not short stock; the payoff is capped and the loss is not. Express a bearish view through capped-loss instruments, or not at all.

- "I never actually liked short selling because it's asymmetry in reverse, right?" (S078)
- "short selling is just inherently treacherous." (S039)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S008, S011, S039, S046, S069, S078, S079). Confidence: high.
- Changed over time: in 2015 he preferred shorting through credit default swaps and set a "ceiling on valuation" test (S001); by March 2022 he had "permanently retired from this line of work" of activist shorting (S008); **newer (2026):** the asymmetry-in-reverse line above.

### 30. Change your mind promptly when facts contradict the thesis, and study and publish your mistakes so you do not repeat them.

- "I consider one of our investment strengths to be our willingness to promptly change our mind when confronted with new information which is inconsistent with our original investment thesis." (S001)
- "our public disclosure of mistakes encourages us to study and learn from them, markedly decreasing the likelihood that they will be repeated." (S009)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S003, S009, S040, S041, S038). Confidence: high.
