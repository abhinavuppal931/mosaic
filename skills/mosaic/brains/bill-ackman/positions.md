# Positions: Bill Ackman

Built from the verified batch notes on the Pershing Square letters, annual and interim reports (S001 to S032, S040, S058, S063 to S065, S082, 2014 to 2026), investor decks (S033 to S038, S055, S056, S066 to S068), the Canadian Pacific proxy letters (S092 to S094, 2012), the Valeant testimony (S046) and his interviews, lecture and podcasts (S039, S041 to S044, S047, S048, S050 to S054, S069 to S072, S077 to S081, S084, S087, S090, S091). The seat's own ground comes first: activism as the catalyst, governance and incentives, asymmetric hedges and shorting, then the quality filter and the rest of the investor questions, most-used first within that order. Each stance carries a date. Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. Pershing Square company text (investment policy, risk tables, statements by board chairs) is not quoted as his words. These are dated public stances, not calls on today's securities.

## 1. Activism and engagement as the catalyst: long-term arbitrage
Sources: 12 (S001, S011, S015, S039, S041, S046, S050, S052, S063, S065, S069, S080).
- 2015-03: The core idea. "I have come to think of our investment approach as akin to a form of long-term arbitrage, where we invest and then work with our portfolio companies to cause the spread between our purchase price and intrinsic value to narrow." (S001)
- 2015-03: The recipe. "Find a great business where there is an opportunity for management, operational, and/or governance improvements. Build a large stake at an attractive price. Work with management and the board to make necessary changes." (S001)
- 2015-08: Two spreads, one he buys and one he creates. "We seek investments that trade at a discount to intrinsic value as is, and an even wider spread as optimized." (S011)
- 2015-03: Why big companies. "We believe that the largest companies offer the most opportunity for corporate change because they are typically held by passive shareholders and are too large to be vulnerable to private equity buyouts." (S001)
- 2019-08: "identify and purchase large stakes in underperforming great businesses at prices which do not reflect anticipated changes to the status quo" (S015) and then "catalyze changes to the governance, management, and strategy of a target company" (S015)
- 2016-04: "we target companies that have underperformed their potential. We do so by becoming a large shareholder, obtaining board representation or an otherwise influential role" (S046)
- 2024-02: "So we get involved in cases where a great business has kind of made a big mistake or you’ve a company that’s kind of lost its way, but it’s recoverable." (S039)
- 2026-09: The seller is the source of the edge. "we can buy from people selling because they believe the situation will never change. And then we can effectuate change. And that's a huge kind of arbitrage." (S041)
- 2014-04 and 2015-05: Activism is long-term, not raiding. "I believe that short-termism is bad. But we're not a short-termer." (S050) and "They’re changes to fundamentally improve a business over many, many years." (S069)
- 2025-03 (newest restatement): "Pershing Square's long-term strategy has been to acquire stakes in high-quality, durable growth companies that have underperformed their potential, which we can improve by catalyzing changes in management, governance, and strategy." (S063)

## 2. The engagement ladder: private first, proxy contest as the fallback
Sources: 11 (S005, S013, S015, S027, S034, S039, S043, S050, S065, S092, S093).
- 2014-04: Start quietly. "We quietly build a stake in the company. And then we work with the management and the board and the shareholders to propose changes that can make a business more valuable." (S050)
- 2017-08: Offer the work privately first. "we approached ADP and offered to share privately our due diligence and analysis with the board to see if we could work together to unlock value for all shareholders." (S013)
- 2017-11: Go public only when refused. "While we strongly preferred to work collaboratively with ADP's board and management to unlock the company's potential, as we have successfully done in nearly all of our prior active investments, they were unwilling to do so." (S027)
- 2020-02: "Our approach to activism varies depending on the situation." (S034) The deck's range runs from behind-the-scenes talks and private letters to a public presentation, board seats and a proxy contest (S034, paraphrase).
- 2012-04: How he frames a contest for other holders. "Proxy contests are about trust, incentives, capabilities, and results." (S093)
- 2024-02: "it’s a bit like running for office. You’ve got to get all the constituents to support you and your ideas." (S039)
- 2024-05: With a small stake, persuasion is the lever. "The only way you can do that with a small position is by advancing your ideas in a public way and getting those ideas adopted from a broad collection of other shareholders." (S043)
- 2017-11 and 2018-03: A lost vote can still win. "It is a quintessential example of how shareholder activism is supposed to work." (S027) and "we believe that the proxy contest was an effective tool to highlight the value-creation opportunity that exists at ADP." (S005)
- 2019-08: Let management take the credit. "boards and management teams generally prefer to take credit for accelerating value creation on their own rather than being perceived as having been pushed to do so by a proactive shareholder" (S015)
- 2017-08: A hostile bid is a different tool. "While we did run a slate in the Allergan situation in 2014, it was in the context of a hostile takeover which we consider to be a fundamentally different type of situation." (S013)

## 3. Board seats, the CEO, and the operator in hand
Sources: 12 (S001, S003, S014, S020, S033, S039, S050, S064, S065, S067, S078, S081).
- 2015-03: "CEOs often tell us that our involvement enables them to accelerate initiatives that they had previously advocated" (S001)
- 2014-04: Have the replacement before the fight. "We bought 14 percent of the railroad and we had in our pocket Hunter Harrison, who's the greatest railroad CEO of all time." (S050)
- 2025-02: Control is not required. "Canadian Pacific, we had 12% of the stock, we replaced the entire board and put in our candidate." (S081)
- 2018-08 and 2019-02: At Lowe's he backed the CEO search rather than a fight; the deck says "Company recruited Marvin Ellison as CEO, our first choice candidate" (S033). Paraphrase of S014 on watching the candidate list first.
- 2024-02: The board seat buys time. "That large owner on the board can help buy the time necessary for management to behave in a longer term way." (S039)
- 2019-03: "our CEOs have found that they can have the best of public ownership along with the benefit of a private owner’s orientation and long-term timeframe." (S065)
- 2016-03: Used defensively when a holding breaks. "First, we put ourselves in a position of influence and gained access to inside information." (S003)
- 2016-04: Leave the board when the work is done, as with Zoetis: "after the company had achieved significant business progress, Pershing Square elected to not re-appoint Doyle for another term as director" (S067)
- 2026-09: Hire someone who has done it before; in venture he bets on the founder, and in public markets a bad CEO can be replaced, which is the edge (S078, paraphrase).
- 2024-08: "one of the measures of a great CEO is the company that he leaves behind." (S020)
- 2025-03 (newer approach): at Howard Hughes the operators are his own staff, "with the addition of Pershing Square team members and our other resources rather than our typical approach of recruiting third-party management teams." (S064)

## 7. Asymmetric hedges: when he buys one
Sources: 17 (S001, S002, S009, S018, S019, S036, S039, S040, S041, S055, S063, S064, S078, S087, S091, S030, S035).
- 2015-03: "our strong preference is for situations where we risk only a modest amount of capital in exchange for a large payoff should the event take place" (S001) and "I typically call these investments mispriced options." (S001)
- 2015-03: The best hedge test. "the best and least costly hedge is one which you would purchase as a standalone investment without regard to its hedging benefits" (S001)
- 2016-01: "While we have never attempted to hedge short-term market movements, we have always looked for ways to inexpensively hedge the risk of dramatic market declines." (S002)
- 2023-08: The process. "Our approach to hedging is first to understand global macro and geopolitical risks, and then to identify asymmetric ways to hedge these risks." (S019)
- 2024-03: "In order for our hedging strategy to be effective, we need to both identify the potential risk and invest in an instrument that offers a sufficient payoff relative to the cost of the hedge." (S009)
- 2025-03 (newest statement of the test): "First, we must have a variant view about the near or intermediate term future, and second, we must be able to purchase an instrument which offers a sufficiently asymmetric payoff if our assessment of the future turns out to be correct." (S064)
- 2023-03: Rare by design. "we have only found macro investments that fit our requirements, namely a high degree of asymmetry and a high confidence level in the predicted outcome, to be episodically available." (S040)
- 2023-08: Sizing. "We structure our hedges using asymmetric investments which at cost typically represent a low, single-digit percentage of our capital." (S019)
- 2020-03: Buy protection when it is cheap. "Because we were able to purchase these instruments at near-all-time tight levels of credit spreads, the risk of loss from this investment was minimal at the time of purchase." (S091)
- 2024-02 deck: "We believe our asymmetric hedging strategy is a superior alternative to a large cash position or a continuous hedging program" (S036)
- 2025-03: No hedge when protection is dear; none in 2024: "we did not identify any meaningful opportunities to execute on these views as prices for these instruments were too high." (S064) and "The benefit of our more episodic approach to asymmetric and macro investing is that we are under no pressure to deploy capital." (S063)
- 2026-09 and 2026-10: "we try to hedge what we call black swan risk." (S041) and "We look for the event that could cause a market crash." (S087)
- 2024-03: His own critique. "In retrospect, we should have invested more and achieved even greater profits without risking materially more capital." (S009)

## 8. What the hedge is for: liquidity to buy, and hedging instead of selling
Sources: 13 (S002, S006, S008, S009, S019, S030, S031, S032, S040, S055, S056, S079, S091).
- 2016-01: "short positions and hedges are the only way to generate cash when markets are plunging." (S002)
- 2022-03: "We do not use hedges to protect our mark-to-market performance from short-term downward volatility." (S008) and the analogy: "We think this approach can be best understood by analogy to over insuring your home in advance of an expected storm" (S008)
- 2022-01: "We invest in hedges not to protect the funds from a short-term mark-to-market loss, but rather because they can become a large source of" (S031) liquidity when stocks are cheap.
- 2023-08: "Profits from successful hedges can often be redeployed opportunistically because when the hedged risks emerge, common stock prices usually decline substantially." (S019)
- 2020-04: Hedge rather than liquidate. "After a careful review of the portfolio, we concluded that a hedging strategy was more consistent with our long-term ownership philosophy, and would likely lead to a better long-term outcome than selling off all of our assets." (S006)
- 2020-04: "And the hedging is kind of elegant because if nothing happened, we would have lost very little money." (S079)
- 2024-03: "Our hedging program has enabled to us to be comfortable remaining fully invested even during periods of market turmoil." (S009)
- 2025-01: The record in three bear markets is that hedge gains were put into "the purchase of additional investments in our portfolio companies at bear market valuations" (S032).
- 2023-03: "approximately $5.3 billion in total hedging proceeds to date versus a cost of $446 million" (S040)

## 9. Interest rates and rate hedges
Sources: 16 (S001, S007, S008, S009, S019, S031, S039, S040, S041, S056, S069, S071, S084, S087, S090, S082).
- 2015-03 (base policy): pick companies where rate moves are not material to the thesis, and leave rate forecasts alone (S001, paraphrase).
- 2015-05: "Well the overall level of interest rates is about as low as it can get." (S069)
- 2020-12 to 2022, the rate hedge: "We expected a combination of aggressive fiscal and monetary policy, vaccines, and the reopening of the economy would lead to full employment and heightened inflation" (S056). Recalled in 2026: "betting that rates would rise. And we made a couple billion dollars there, almost $3 billion." (S041)
- 2022-03: "In light of our continuing concerns about the impact of rising rates on equity markets and the market’s underestimation of this risk" (S008)
- 2023-03: How rates hit stocks. "higher required investment returns from investors lowered the price that investors were prepared to pay for financial assets, causing stock prices to decline." (S040)
- 2023-08: "We believe that long-term interest rates can continue to rise substantially from current levels." (S019) He built a 5.5% long-run 30-year yield from inflation, the real short rate and a term premium (S019, paraphrase).
- 2023-10 (view flips): "In October, we believed that interest rates were unlikely to rise further and we sold our 30-year USD interest-rate swaptions" (S009). By January 2024: "at 5% I thought that it had gone far enough" (S090) and "We actually reversed our position and instead of being short, the bond market, we went long, the five year and the one year" (S090)
- 2024-02: Rates set the hurdle for a stock's earnings yield. "Compare it to what you can earn lending your money to the government, 4%, that’s a very attractive going in yield." (S039)
- 2026-10 (newest): "I think that it makes rates persistently high." (S084) and "if rates are at five or at six, they're still going to spend the money" (S084). He doubts hikes slow AI and war spending: "I don't think there's going to be fewer dollars spent because rates are get pushed up." (S087)

## 10. Credit, bonds and credit default swaps
Sources: 12 (S001, S030, S039, S040, S041, S055, S069, S071, S087, S091, S035, S002).
- 2015-03: CDS beat shorting stock. "We find the purchase of CDS to be a far superior means to implement a short sale when compared with shorting stock because of the modest risk incurred versus the potential reward." (S001)
- 2005 to 2009, bond insurers and AAA credits: "we made the investment asymmetric in our favor, meaning put up a small amount of money, if it works, we make a fortune." (S039) The deck tallies about $64 million in and $1.1 billion out on bond insurer CDS (S055).
- 2015-05: "I don’t like fixed income as a category, particularly at today’s interest rates." (S069)
- 2020-03: Bought index CDS when spreads sat near record tights, after high yield spreads near their lowest ever (S030, paraphrase).
- 2023-03: "we have always been long-term skeptics of even highly-rated financial institutions" (S040) and on deposits after SVB, "We need a larger deposit guarantee regime, and we need it soon." (S040)
- 2023-10: "a lot of commercial real estate investors they’re gonna have a very challenging period." (S071)
- 2026-10 (newest, private credit): "anytime you have very, very large, levered private market, I think there's risk to the system" (S087) and "And that's what creates the credit cycle." (S087)

## 11. Shorting
Sources: 18 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S008, S011, S039, S046, S050, S051, S065, S069, S070, S078, S079, S090, S026, S019).
- Older view, 2014 to 2017: shorts are a research-funded public good. "Our favorite short opportunities are companies that are highly leveraged, need access to capital to survive, require substantial management judgment in the determination of their reported earnings, and have fundamentally bad business models." (S001) and "A ceiling on valuation is what we deem to be the equivalent of a margin of safety for long investments." (S001)
- 2014-07: "the shorts position that we've taken allowed us to, enabled us to afford to invest the money we've spent researching the company." (S051) and "I'll take it to the end of the Earth." (S051)
- 2016-12: "We remain short Herbalife because we believe its intrinsic value is zero." (S026)
- 2015-05: Doubt sets in. "I think short selling is an inherently healthy thing for markets." (S069) but "I would have to think very, very hard before another public short." (S069)
- 2018-03: Right on the business, wrong on the stock: "we underestimated Herbalife’s ability to access debt capital and use financial engineering" (S005)
- 2019-03 to 2020-04 (turn): "While we have made one short sale investment in the last few years, we have no current intention of shorting stocks in the future." (S065) and "we're done with public short selling. It's just not our thing anymore." (S079) His name for the cost: "I called it Return on Invested Brain Damage." (S079)
- 2022-03: "we have permanently retired from this line of work." (S008)
- Newer view, 2024 to 2026: "We no longer short companies." (S039), "short selling is just inherently treacherous." (S039), "we don’t short stocks anymore, as you know." (S070) and the stone tablet now holds "an aversion to short selling." (S078) Downside views are expressed through capped-loss instruments such as CDS and puts instead (inferred from S001, S039, S066).

## 14. What to buy: simple, predictable, free-cash-flow-generative quality
Sources: 16 (S001, S003, S011, S013, S024, S039, S040, S044, S052, S054, S064, S065, S078, S038, S009, S041).
- 2015-03: "we purchase simple, predictable, free-cash-flow generative businesses that have sustainable competitive advantages due to brand power, unique assets, long-term contractual arrangements, or other factors." (S001)
- 2019-03: "Our portfolio today represents the results of our strategy of investing in simple, predictable, free-cash-flow-generative businesses which are protected by large competitive moats." (S065)
- 2017-08 and 2017-12: The activist version is the same business that slipped. "ADP is a classic Pershing Square investment. It is a simple, predictable, free-cash-flow generative business that has underperformed its potential." (S013) and "I invest in what I describe as simple, predictable, free cash flow, general business, and we look for a business like that, that have lost their way." (S052)
- 2014: "The best businesses are the ones where they don't require a lot of capital to be reinvested in the company." (S044) and "You also want businesses that are not particularly sensitive to outside factors, so-called extrinsic factors that you can't control." (S044)
- 2015-02: "I'm a big believer in concentration. But it's not just analysis that protects you, it's the nature of the things you invest in." (S054) and "You have to really understand what the moats are." (S054)
- Royalty and annuity models, 2016 to 2023: "QSR’s franchised business model is best described as a capital-light, high-growth annuity." (S003) and UMG as "a rapidly growing royalty on greater global consumption and monetization of music" (S040)
- 2015-03: If cash flows cannot be predicted within a reasonable band, the idea goes in the Don't Know pile and is not bought (S001, paraphrase).
- 2023-03: "Investment selection is our most important risk mitigation strategy in an uncertain world." (S040)
- 2024-02: "And very few businesses that you can have a really high degree of certainty about." (S039)
- Changed emphasis, 2026-09 (newer): he says business quality matters far more to him than early in his career, when he bought cheap stocks (S041, paraphrase); the 2025 wording is "we principally seek to invest in high-quality growth companies with low volatility business models." (S064)

## 15. What price to pay and how he values
Sources: 15 (S001, S002, S005, S008, S010, S011, S021, S039, S064, S068, S036, S040, S065, S022, S042).
- 2015-03: "We believe the value of a business is equal to the present value of the cash the business generates for its owner over its lifespan." (S001) and "We buy these businesses at deep discounts to our estimate of intrinsic value giving us a margin of safety against a permanent impairment of capital." (S001)
- 2016-01: "While stocks can trade at any price in the short term, it is rare for companies to trade at material discounts to intrinsic value for extended periods." (S002)
- 2015-08: Free cash flow yield on the deal. "By financing the acquisition with leverage of four times Debt / EBITDA, at our $10.50 purchase price, we paid approximately eight times levered free cash flow." (S011)
