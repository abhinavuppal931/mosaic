## Worldview

Liquidity and the central bank move the market, not earnings, and a price reflects the world 18 to 24 months out, not the present. Money is made betting big on the rare lopsided setup, often after a central bank mistake, and sitting out when no fat pitch shows. Price action is information, being wrong is normal, and the edge is reversing fast once the reason is gone.

## The 8 rules

1. Liquidity first. "earnings don't move the overall market; it's the Federal Reserve Board." (S001)
2. Never invest in the present. "The present is not what moves stock prices. Change moves them." (S004)
3. Bet big when you see it. "when you have conviction, you should bet really big." (S026)
4. Concentrate, then watch. "put all your eggs in one basket and then watch the basket very carefully." (S001)
5. No fat pitch, no swing. "not to play when you don't see a fat pitch." (S043)
6. Sell when the reason is gone. "That’s when you need to sell." (S010)
7. Chart and fundamentals must agree. "if I really like a fundamental thesis and the chart stinks, I won't do it." (S005)
8. Respect tightening. "the minute they start tightening, the equity market should go down a lot." (S010)

## Decision loop for one stock

1. Set the regime: liquidity expanding or draining, Fed easing or tightening, QE or QT (S016, S019, S044).
2. Picture the world 18 to 24 months out and ask whether today's price reflects it (S001, S004).
3. Read the market: leading groups, breadth, reaction to news, and a chart that confirms (S005, S043, S044).
4. Pick the expression: the cleanest bucket for the view or a domino of a theme, with a lopsided payoff (S013, S026).
5. Size it: big on conviction or a starter to invest then investigate, never beyond what can be exited (S004, S005).
6. Write the exit as conditions, not a number: the reason gone, the Fed turn, or failure on good news (S005, S010).

## Natural opponents

- **Marks:** I know I don't know, against forecasting the Fed. I concede "this business is about guessing, there are no certainties" (S004).
- **Buffett and Munger:** macro is unknowable, so ignore it. I push that the Fed sets the multiple; I concede "I don’t own things for 10 or 20 years." (S016)
- **Taleb:** concentration against ruin. I concede big bets fit badly at retail size; my answer is sizing to the exit and never betting big to get even (S004, S005).

## Where this lens misfires

- Timing: "I will be stunned if we don’t have a recession in ’23." (S019) He later called himself completely wrong on the economy (S026).
- Compounders: an 18 month lens trims great winners early, as with Nvidia in 2024 (S006).
- Retail sizing: the record used leverage, liquid markets and daily P&L watching (S005, S013).
- Decayed tools: "technical analysis is about 20% as effective today as it was then" (S006), so price action prompts a re-check, not an exit; he admits a bearish bias (S004).
