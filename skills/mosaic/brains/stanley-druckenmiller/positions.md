# Positions: Stanley Druckenmiller

Built from 16 spoken sources (talks, interviews and podcasts, S001 to S048, 2009 to 2026) through the verified batch notes. He has published no letters or books, so every stance is spoken and carries the date of the talk. S010 was recorded in May 2021 and published in 2024, so its stances are dated 2021-05. Topics start with the seat's own question (liquidity, the Fed and the world 18 to 24 months out), then run most-used first. Where a view changed, both are shown with dates and the newer one is marked. These are his dated public stances, not calls on today's securities.

## 1. The Fed and liquidity move the market, not earnings
Sources: 12 (S001, S004, S005, S009, S010, S013, S016, S019, S026, S027, S044, S048).
- 2015-01: The founding lesson from his first mentor. "earnings don't move the overall market; it's the Federal Reserve Board." (S001) His biggest money came from central bank errors: "about 80 percent of the big, big money we made was in bear markets and equities because crazy things were going on in response to what I would call central bank mistakes" (S001)
- 2009: "if you have the money supply growing a lot faster than industrial production, the stock market is generally going to go up." (S005)
- 2019-06: "When the government buys bonds, bonds go down and stocks go up." (S044) and "But liquidity is a moving animal." (S044)
- 2014-07 and 2015-04: Judges policy as a bet, not an outcome. "What I am sure is they’re making a bad bet, a bad risk/reward." (S027) and "as someone who lives risk reward every day, the risk reward of going too early, to me, is much more favorable than going too late" (S009)
- Older view, 2014 to 2018: urged hikes for years. Changed in December 2018 to 2019-06: with financial conditions tight and global growth slowing, "The Fed shouldn’t hike for now." (S044) Rule kept: "I would sneak one in every time financial conditions allowed." (S044)
- 2021-05: "Monetary policy is absolutely insane." (S010) 2022-09: "We come up with this ridiculous theory of transitory." (S019)
- 2024-05: On the December 2023 pivot, "I feel like they fumbled on the five-yard line with the game on the line." (S016) He wants forward guidance gone: "When you need to raise rates, raise rates. When you need to cut them, cut them." (S016)
- 2024-11: "I'm a little worried that the Fed has declared victory too early." (S026)
- Newest, 2026-02: US economy strong and getting stronger, Fed unlikely to hike, and he was optimistic about Bessent at Treasury working with Warsh at the Fed (S006, paraphrase).

## 2. Liquidity gauges and financial conditions as the entry and exit signal
Sources: 8 (S005, S013, S016, S019, S026, S043, S044, S047).
- 2009: Went with the charts in commodities in 2008 despite a bearish economy because of the liquidity coming. "I just saw so much liquidity that was going to be provided worldwide that I was willing to go with the charts." (S005)
- 2021-02: Measures money against the economy. "since 2018, M2 in the US has grown 25 percent more than nominal GDP." (S013)
- 2022-09: Tracks net liquidity, including Treasury cash drawdowns and the petroleum reserve release. "if you look at the liquidity situation that has driven this, we’re going to go from all this QE to QT, we’re following an asset bubble." (S019)
- 2023-04: The Fed reversing months of balance sheet shrinkage in four days after Silicon Valley Bank unnerved him (S043, paraphrase).
- 2024-05: Exited his leveraged two-year Treasury position when conditions loosened after the pivot. "financial conditions, which is one of the things that put me in them in the first place were turning" (S016)
- 2024-11: A 50bp cut with tight credit spreads, gold at highs and strong equities worried him (S026, paraphrase); see topic 1.

## 3. Time horizon: never invest in the present
Sources: 8 (S001, S004, S006, S009, S013, S026, S044, S047).
- 2015-01: "you have to visualize the situation 18 months from now, and whatever that is, that's where the price will be, not where it is today." (S001)
- 2022-05: "Do not, do not invest in the present. The present is not what moves stock prices. Change moves them." (S004) and "tell me how people are going to think differently in 18 or 24 months about the situation than they're thinking now." (S004)
- 2023-06: On shorts, "I just try and look at the current situation and then I try and think of a situation 12 to 18 months from now based on my forecast" (S047)
- 2024-11: "Always try and envision the situation as you see it in 18 to 24 months" (S026)
- 2026-02: "Most trades I put on, I think in terms of 18 months to three years, that’s how long I think they’re probably gonna evolve." (S006) But: "I’ve put on a three year trade that five days later I’m out of, and I’ve reversed." (S006)

## 4. Market timing: a bad setup can run for years
Sources: 7 (S001, S004, S019, S026, S027, S044, S047).
- 2014-07: With an easy Fed, stay at the party until the rate moves. "You don’t have to move till they raise rates." (S027)
- 2015-01: "every bone in my body said this is a bad risk reward, but I can't figure out how it's going to end." (S001) and "I don't know when it's going to happen." (S001)
- 2019-06: Political cycle timing from his first boss, buy two years before the general election and sell on it (S019, quoting his first boss in 1976, paraphrase).
- 2023-06: A delay does not change the odds. "I haven't changed mine at all the fact that it hasn't happened yet doesn't change the probability" (S047)
- 2024-11: On the fiscal reckoning, "My best guess would be a failed auction, but honestly, it could be six months, it could be six years." (S026) He will not trade it short term: "As a practitioner, it's something I can't be obsessed with on a three to six month basis." (S026)

## 5. Interest rates and bonds
Sources: 12 (S001, S004, S005, S006, S013, S016, S020, S026, S043, S044, S048, S027).
- 1981, told 2015-01: put 50 percent of new capital into 30-year Treasuries at 14 percent because Volcker would not let inflation run (S001).
- 2009: "I am salivating to short the bond market in major size." (S005) and "I don’t want to be six months early and trash myself, but I’m salivating." (S005)
- 2018-05 and 2021-02: Long rates are the price that was taken away. "The central bank has bastardized, probably, the most important price in the world, which is the cost of money, particularly at the long end." (S013)
- 2019-06: Bought Treasuries on one-way logic. "I wasn’t sure rates were going to go down, what I was sure, is they weren’t going to go up." (S044)
- 2022-05: "the 10 year Treasury has sort of been the most important price in the world." (S004)
- 2023-04: In the hard landing camp but not betting big on bonds. "I better be right on my hard landing if I want to own fixed income." (S043)
- 2023-11: "I made a lot of money this year betting on bonds going down because of the debt and had nothing do with the economy." (S048)
- 2024-11: Short about a quarter of NAV, entered the day the Fed cut. "I'm like 25% NAV short, 10-year equivalent." (S026) On how high the 10-year can go: "It can go to nominal GDP." (S026)
- Newest, 2026-02: Short bonds as the hedge for a book long risk assets. "because we’re long all these risk assets I just mentioned, we’re short bonds." (S006)

## 6. Inflation
Sources: 9 (S001, S004, S010, S013, S016, S019, S020, S026, S044).
- 2015-01: Did not know which way it ends, but "there is nothing more deflationary than creating a phony asset bubble, having a bunch of investors plow into it and then having it pop." (S001)
- 2018-05 and 2019-06: Rejects the 2 percent target. "I just don’t understand the 2% inflation obsession." (S044) and "over the last 700 years inflation has averaged barely over 1% and interest rates have averaged just under 6%" (S020)
- 2021-02: His aim is inflation against the policymakers' view. "my overriding aim is inflation relative to what the policy makers think." (S013)
- 2021-05: The equity risk is "Without a doubt: inflation strong enough that the Fed responds to it." (S010)
- 2022-05: Two undefeated records. "once inflation gets above 5%, it's never come down unless Fed funds have gotten above the CPI." (S004) and "it's never been tamed without a recession." (S004)
- 2022-09: "inflation rotates." (S019) and "You don’t cure inflation with an inflationary act." (S019)
- 2024-05: "I’m open-minded to say under a Trump administration, inflation being 6 percent sometime in 2025." (S016)
- 2024-11: Right on inflation falling in 2022 to 2023, wrong on the economy (S026, see topic 9).

## 7. Cycles and bubbles: every bust follows a bubble
Sources: 10 (S001, S004, S006, S010, S019, S020, S027, S044, S047, S009).
- 2014-07: "anybody who says they are not a bubble, I just don’t agree with it." (S027)
- 2015-04: Private tech valuations "I think it's all part of a product of zero interest rates, but I don't think it's outrageous the way it was in 2000." (S009)
- 2018-05: "the three most pernicious deflationary periods of the past century did not start because inflation was too close to zero. They were preceded by asset bubbles." (S020) and "Who knows how many corporate zombies are out there because free money is keeping them alive?" (S020)
- 2019-06: "every bust I had ever seen was preceded by an asset bubble generally set up by too loose policy." (S044)
- 2022-05: The bubble burst in good companies. "A lot of very good companies have been de-rated 60, 70% without a whole lot of change in fundamentals." (S004)
- 2022-09 and 2023-06: "the worst economies happen post asset bubbles" (S019) and "every time you've had a significant asset bubble economic trouble way ahead" (S047)
- Newest, 2026-02: Narrative bubbles are his top fear and he put the cycle late. "They’re all preceded by asset bubbles." (S006) and "Maybe 8th inning." (S006)

## 8. Bubble warning signs: IPO and credit quality
Sources: 4 (S001, S009, S027, S044).
- 2014-07: "In ’99, 83 percent of IPOs went public had never earned a dime. Today that’s 80 percent." (S027) and "70 percent of debt issued is a B rating or worse." (S027)
- 2015-01: "Today 71 percent of the debt that's been issued in the last two years is B rated." (S001)
- 2015-04: "I think the risk of a credit bubble is extremely high, and if it's not addressed pretty soon, things could look difficult three or four years down the road." (S009)
- 2019-06: "5.7 trillion buybacks, financial engineering, versus 2.2 trillion in capital expenditures." (S044)

## 9. Recession, bear markets and reading the economy
Sources: 10 (S004, S009, S019, S026, S027, S043, S044, S047, S048, S006).
- 2015-04: A stock market explosion predicts recovery. "whenever I've seen a stock market explode on record volume and record breadth and move to that degree, like day follows night, 6 to 12 months down the road, you're out of recession, and you're into a full-blown recovery." (S009)
- 2019-06: "By far the best economic predictor I’ve ever met is the inside of the stock market." (S044) and "I’ve never seen a recession before corporate profits peaked." (S044)
- 2022-05: "we're six months into a bear market that has some room to run." (S004) and "we've never had a soft landing after inflation has gotten above 4.5%." (S004)
- 2022-09: "I will be stunned if we don’t have a recession in ’23." (S019)
- 2023-06: "I could see corporate profits down 20 to 30 percent" (S047)
- Newer view, 2023-11 and 2024-11: the recession call was wrong. "If anything, I was wrong on the economy." (S048) and "I was very confident that inflation was going to come down, which I was right on, but I was worried about the economy, which I was completely wrong on." (S026)
- 2024-11: Narrow leadership is a necessary condition for a bear market. "But it's early, but it's a yellow light, it's not a red light." (S026)
- 2026-02: "Why in the world are we using a lagging indicator to predict the economy?" (S006)

## 10. The dollar and currencies
Sources: 8 (S001, S004, S005, S006, S009, S013, S043, S044).
- 2009: "I call it the curse of the reserve currency" (S005)
- 2015-01 and 2015-04: Big euro short on the cost gap between Germany and the periphery (S001, paraphrase). "I've never seen a major currency trend last less than two years" (S009) and "honestly, I can find no correlation between the level and direction of the dollar and the economy" (S009)
- 2021-02: "I have a very, very short dollar position." (S013)
- 2022-05: "I will be surprised if sometime in the next six months, I'm not short the dollar." (S004)
- 2023-04: "So the only space I have any risk on right now is in the US dollar." (S043) He calls missing the 2022 dollar rally "probably the biggest miss in my career" (S043)
- Newest, 2026-02: bearish. "foreigners are way, way overloaded in dollars" (S006)

## 11. Fiscal policy, government debt and entitlements
Sources: 7 (S001, S016, S019, S020, S026, S047, S048).
Kept for how it moves bonds, not for politics.
- 2015-01: "I would freeze all the entitlement payments right now because they've already taken such a tremendous share away from the rest of our population." (S001)
- 2022-09: "by 2027, the interest expense alone on the debt eats all health care spending." (S019)
- 2023-06: A long-run worry, not a trade. "I don't think the entitlement situation is something you should be trading on in the next three to six months" (S047)
- 2023-11: "Now we have a monster bigger than entitlements. It's called interest expense." (S048) and on the Treasury's 2021 funding, "the risk/reward of issuing debt at 1.1 percent in the ten-year is off the charts." (S048)
- 2024-05: "why we’re spending like we’re still in the Great Depression is beyond me" (S016)

## 12. The hurdle rate and capital allocation
Sources: 4 (S020, S044, S048, S009).
- 2015-04: "there's good debt growth, and there's bad debt growth. Good debt growth is when you borrow money, and it goes into the real economy." (S009)
- 2018-05: "Of all the interventions by the not-so-invisible hand, not allowing the market to set the hurdle rate for investment is the one I see with the highest costs." (S020)
- 2019-06: "you need a hurdle rate for investment, and if you don’t have a hurdle rate for investment, bad things happen." (S044)
- Newest, 2023-11: "We now have a hurdle rate for investment in this country." (S048) and "instead of funding a bunch of nonsense and bubble stuff, the capital gets more properly allocated." (S048)

## 13. Concentration versus diversification
Sources: 8 (S001, S004, S005, S009, S010, S013, S026, S043).
Unchanged across 2009 to 2024.
- 2009: "I think diversification is really overrated." (S005)
- 2015-01: "I think diversification and all the stuff they're teaching at business school today is probably the most misguided concept everywhere." (S001) and "only maybe one or two times a year do you see something that really, really excites you." (S001)
- 2021-02: "put all your eggs in one basket and watch the basket very carefully." (S013)
- 2021-05: "It is to make large concentrated bets where they have a lot of conviction." (S010) and "my portfolio is only going to have 15-20, I’m never going to buy something that doesn’t have a great chart and fundamentals." (S010)
- 2024-11: "Concentration, not to be afraid of concentration, that's a big reason for my success." (S026)

## 14. Position sizing: the 70 to 80 percent of the game
Sources: 9 (S001, S004, S005, S006, S019, S026, S027, S043, S044).
- 2009: "Don't bet big unless a) you love your thesis, and b) you're hot." (S005) and "I always sized them relative to the market, not to the capital of the fund." (S005)
- 2014-07: "It’s making a lot of money when you’re right and minimizing it when you’re wrong." (S027)
- 2019-06: "I like to make very large bets, very contrary to when the ducks are lined up and I can analyze it." (S044)
- 2022-05: "sizing is probably 70 or 80% of the equation. It's not whether you're right or wrong, it's how much you make when you're right, and how much you lose when you're wrong." (S004)
- 2022-09: Sizes on payoff to cost. "if they didn’t devalue in the next six months, my fund was going to lose 50 basis points; if they did devalue, I was going to make 2,000 basis points." (S019)
- 2023-04: "when you really see the ball, swing really big. And when you don't see the ball, don't swing." (S043)
- Changed with age: 2014-07 "I just don’t have the guts that I used to." (S027); 2022-05 "I'm just not playing anything as aggressively as I used to." (S004); newest, 2026-02, "I was a much better portfolio manager then because back then I had courage." (S006)

## 15. Leverage and gross exposure
Sources: 5 (S004, S005, S013, S016, S026).
- 2009: Younger, he ran 150 to 200 percent in a currency, 300 percent in 10-year bond equivalents, rarely over 100 percent net long or 50 percent net short equities, gross rarely above 4 to 1 (S005, paraphrase). "if I really, really believed something, I'd have no problem putting 100% of the fund in it." (S005)
- 2021-02: 50, 60 or 70 percent in one asset class (S013, paraphrase).
- 2022-05: Macro positions take the leverage, equities much less. "or some of the time in macro positions, I'll have 200-300%. Believe me, they're not getting stale." (S004)
- 2024-05: A massive leveraged two-year Treasury long, exited when financial conditions turned (S016, paraphrase).

## 16. Cash and sitting out
Sources: 7 (S004, S005, S019, S027, S043, S044, S013).
- 2009: "we're playing a low gross, because I can't figure it out." (S005)
- 2014-07: Sat out 2006 after a strong 2005. "’06 I stunk. I had made some money but I left the party." (S027)
- 2019-06: "when there are white caps on the bay, the pro don’t play." (S044)
- 2021-02: "If you have the latitude to say, I'm just not going to play, it’s too complicated, you don’t play in them." (S013)
- 2022-05: "I'm waiting for a fat pitch. I'm not shooting any at pins, to use a golfing analogy." (S004)
- 2022-09: "But I don’t think you need to short. Just sidestep it." (S019)
- 2023-04: Not in cash; he runs a near-neutral book instead. "I'm too much of a junkie. I'm always doing something." (S043) and "our equities were about 3% net short, which is nothing" (S043)

## 17. Charts, price action and technical analysis
Sources: 7 (S004, S005, S006, S010, S026, S043, S044).
- Older view, 2009: "I will never invest just on a chart." (S005) and "But if I really like a fundamental thesis and the chart stinks, I won't do it." (S005) and "if all the news is great and the stocks not acting well, get out" (S005)
- 2021-05: "right now, I have a triple screen to buy, hold or sell a security." (S010)
- 2023-04: "the technical provides a discipline on the fundamental and the fundamental provides a discipline on the technical." (S043)
- Changed, 2022-05 to 2026-02: the tools decayed as others adopted them. "price versus news is a very weakened tool versus 20 years ago." (S004) and "price action is not what it used to be 20 or 30 years ago" (S043). Newest, 2026-02: "technical analysis is about 20% as effective today as it was then because no one was using it." (S006) He still uses macro data for timing: "We do use macro data for entries and exits" (S006)

## 18. Valuation and the price to pay
Sources: 9 (S004, S006, S009, S010, S013, S019, S026, S048, S001).
- 2015-04: Valuation is not the point when he trades a transmission mechanism. "Over valuation doesn't concern me at all. I'm not even opining on the stock market. I'm opining on the transmission mechanism, the stock market to the economy" (S009)
- 2021-05: "Amazon at $3200 is not a bubble stock." (S010) and on SaaS at 10 to 25 times sales, "I think if you hold these names for 3-4 years, they could easily grow into their valuations." (S010)
- 2022-09: Multiples move with real rates. "That’s because five-years yielded 15 percent when I started Duquesne, so real rates were high. That’s why we were at 8 times depressed earnings." (S019)
- 2023-11: "In a pre-QE world, 15 was about normal. When I got in the business, it was 8." (S048)
- 2024-11: "Stuff like Apple selling 25 or 30 times earnings, it's certainly not growing at 25 or 30%." (S026)
- Newest, 2026-02: "We’re not undervalued; we’re toward the top of the valuation range historically." (S006) Teva re-rated from about 6 to about 12 times earnings on change (S006, paraphrase).

## 19. Long-run equity returns
Sources: 4 (S019, S043, S048, S006).
- 2022-09: "the Dow won’t be much higher in ten years than it is today" (S019) and "you can have a period of 15, 20 years, 10 years where the market doesn’t go anywhere." (S019)
- 2023-04: "I wouldn't be surprised if the stock market was not higher in 10 years." (S043)
- 2023-11: "It is just not going to be like surfing with a hurricane behind your back." (S048)
- Newest, 2026-02: economy strong but valuations near the top of the range (S006); see topic 18.

## 20. Technology and AI
Sources: 8 (S006, S009, S010, S013, S016, S026, S044, S047).
- 2019-06: "My portfolio has basically been long disrupters and short disrupted for like three or four years now." (S044)
- 2021-02 and 2021-05: Cloud is not 2000. "I think the comparisons with 2000 are ridiculous." (S013) and "I'd say we're in the third or fourth inning." (S013)
- 2023-06: "I think AI is real it's probably it could be as transformative as the Internet" (S047)
- 2024-05: "So AI might be a little overhyped now but under-hyped long term." (S016)
- 2024-11: "the reason our exposure is really neither long nor short is how to play it." (S026) and "I don't see it as a winner-take-all model." (S026)
- Newest, 2026-02: AI stopped driving his book in fall 2025 (S006, paraphrase). On AI job losses, the consensus is arrogance: "anybody who believes that with conviction suffers from arrogance and not an open mind." (S006)

## 21. Big tech and growth versus value
Sources: 6 (S004, S005, S006, S010, S013, S026).
- 2009: "I'm not just a value guy. I need charts, I need catalysts." (S005)
- 2021-02: "they're not overvalued, they're GARP names, and they're currently out of favor." (S013)
- 2021-05: On Google, "their core search business is literally the best business I’ve ever seen." (S010)
- 2022-05: "I have a bias toward growth stocks." (S004) and "they've gotten too cheap for me for them to be my shorts anymore." (S004) In cyclicals he plays mean reversion: "when companies are losing money, capacity is always going to shrink and their margins are gonna look better in three years." (S004)
- 2021-02: Rising inflation is the risk to growth stocks: "if we get, say 4-5 percent inflation in the US a couple of years out and bond yields ride precipitously, that's very negative historically for growth stocks relative to other stocks." (S013)

## 22. Gold

## 23. Crypto
Sources: 6 (S004, S006, S010, S013, S019, S044).

## 24. Commodities: oil and copper
Sources: 6 (S004, S005, S006, S009, S013, S016).
- 2015-04: Expected oil to beat the forward curve because 50 dollar oil cuts supply by early 2016 (S009, paraphrase).
- 2021-02: "The longer the Fed tries and keep rates suppressed so they'll have stimulus in the pipeline, the more I win on my commodities." (S013)
- 2022-05: Held energy on an 18-month view, ignoring the pain-trade talk. "I don't care about pain trade this or that." (S004)
- 2024-05: Copper on five to six years of supply and demand. "takes about 12 years, greenfield to produce copper" (S016)
- Newest, 2026-02: "It’s not a genius trade; it’s a big consensus trade." (S006)

## 25. Shorting
Sources: 7 (S001, S004, S019, S043, S044, S047, S026).
- 2015-01: Stretched valuations alone did not make him short. "I haven't committed. I'm not net short equities." (S001)
- 2022-05: "any great short seller will tell you they made 90% their money on the long side. The math is with you." (S004)
- 2022-09: "I’m not advocating going short." (S019)
- 2023-04: Prefers old economy shorts such as the Russell 2000 (S043, paraphrase).
- 2023-06: "if you're dead wrong on a short you can lose 10 times your money" (S047) and "I'm not sure I've made money in shorts" (S047)

## 26. Hedging, the matrix and risk control
Sources: 6 (S004, S005, S006, S013, S043, S044).
- 2009: "my risk management system has always been what I've described, that knowledge, and then my gut, and watching my daily PnL." (S005) and "To me, these models are not only okay, they can get you in huge trouble by causing you a false sense of security" (S005)
- 2021-02: "I've found it’s better to have a matrix." (S013) and "I've always used my P&L because I found all of those risk models, they're great until complete chaos happens." (S013)
- 2022-05: "let me call it a matrix of short fixed income, short stocks" (S004)
- 2023-04: "my P&L doesn't move generally more than 30 or 40 basis points a day." (S043)
- Newest, 2026-02: "But we create a matrix, and the bonds are helpful in both ways." (S006) On curve trades: "Very rarely do you win on both sides." (S006)

## 27. Contrarianism and crowded trades
Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S006).
- 2009: "contrarian thinking, just to be contrarian, can be a death trap, particularly in bonds and currencies, because economic fundamentals are what they are." (S005) and "the crowd makes money 80% of the time." (S005)
- 2026-02: "I don’t care if a trade is crowded if I think the thesis is right and the trend is with me." (S006)

## 28. Holding period and turnover
Sources: 6 (S006, S010, S016, S026, S047, S009).
- 2015-04: "my style of investing, I can change my positions in 24 hours" (S009)
