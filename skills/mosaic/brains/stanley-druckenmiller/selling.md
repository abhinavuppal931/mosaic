# Selling: Stanley Druckenmiller

Built from 16 spoken sources (S001 to S048, 2009 to 2026) through the verified batch notes. His rules for selling, trimming, adding and re-checking a position, most-used first. His method is a trader's: the thesis, the price action and the macro backdrop are checked against each other every day, and a position can be reversed in days. Where he changed his mind, both views are dated and the newer one is marked.

## Rules

### 1. Sell when the reason you bought is gone, whatever the P&L
Sources: 7 (S001, S004, S006, S010, S026, S043, S005).
"I’ve also never hung onto a security if the reason I bought it has changed. That’s when you need to sell." (S010) and "If the reason I bought a stock is no longer the case, I don't care what I paid for it." (S026) On a big macro bet: "when you're betting the ranch and the circumstances change, you have to change, and that's how I've always managed money." (S001) Profit or loss does not enter it: "Do the work, and if it doesn’t work out, doesn’t matter if it’s a profit or loss" (S006)

### 2. No stop losses; a falling price means re-check, not sell
Sources: 3 (S005, S010, S026).
"I’ve never used the stop loss. Not once. It’s the dumbest concept I’ve ever heard." (S010) and "I have never used a stop loss in my career." (S005) "If it’s going down, it definitely means you should reevaluate your thesis, but it doesn’t mean you should sell it." (S010) He fights the urge to sell bottoms: "I have wanted to sell every bottom so bad." (S005)

### 3. Cost basis is irrelevant; once out, out; buy back higher if needed
Sources: 2 (S010, S026).
"that stock doesn’t know whether you have a loss or a gain." (S010) and "I just don't care what I paid for a stock. It's absolutely irrelevant in terms of my investment process going forward." (S026) "Once I'm out, I'm out." (S026) and "I'm perfectly willing to buy something back higher than I sold it." (S026)

### 4. Invest, then investigate: add if the work confirms, cut fast if not
Sources: 5 (S004, S006, S016, S026, S043).
Buy a meaningful starter on intuition that fits the macro matrix, then let two to three weeks of work decide (S004). "If I think we made a mistake, I'll sell it. If I don't think we made a mistake, we'll add to it if we have to." (S026) and "if it turns out I was wrong, after they analyze it, I get out. I don't like to wait around." (S043) Adding on confirming news is the other half: after ChatGPT confirmed the Nvidia thesis, "So then I doubled the position." (S006)

### 5. Price action against news is the alarm
Sources: 4 (S004, S005, S043, S006).
"if all the news is great and the stocks not acting well, get out" (S005) and "If I've got a thesis and it's really bullish and it's playing out and the stock's not going anywhere, makes me go back and check the thesis over and over." (S043) His in-head stop: "if it starts not acting well relative to news or other things I'm watching, I'll get out." (S005) Changed, 2022 to 2026: he now trusts this tool less. "price versus news is a very weakened tool versus 20 years ago." (S004) Newest, 2026-02: "technical analysis is about 20% as effective today as it was then because no one was using it." (S006)

### 6. Exit macro positions on the liquidity turn, not on the low
Sources: 4 (S010, S016, S027, S019).
With an easy Fed, stay in: "You don’t have to move till they raise rates." (S027) When it tightens, get out of risk: "the minute they start tightening, the equity market should go down a lot." (S010) He sold his leveraged two-year long at 415 to 430 without getting the low because "financial conditions, which is one of the things that put me in them in the first place were turning" (S016) In 2022, QE turning to QT after a bubble meant "To me the risk-reward owning assets doesn’t make a lot of sense." (S019)

### 7. Do not take a profit to feel good; remember the original thesis
Sources: 3 (S005, S026, S006).
"you can't just make yourself feel good by taking a profit when something goes your way. You have to remember your original game plan, your original thesis" (S005) He breaks this himself. His two-year Treasury short went from 15 to 150 basis points and he took most of it off; yields went to 500. "I regret deeply not holding that position." (S026) Nvidia in 2024: "I violated everything I said in the interview. I couldn’t stand success." (S006)

### 8. Trim into tops; a technician waits for the top
Sources: 2 (S016, S026).
"I'm a technician, so I usually wait for tops." (S026) and "I don't necessarily sell early." (S026) He trims huge winners after a big run even with the long-term thesis intact, and expects to be back: "Well, I will be very surprised if I don’t own Nvidia on and off next 10 years." (S016) He worries about the cost of waiting to re-enter: "It's hard to buy a stock you're looking at at 100." (S026)

### 9. Cut size when cold; never bet big to get even
Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S026).
"when you're cold, the last thing you should do is try and make big bets to get back to even. You should tone yourself down." (S004) and "But don't ever bet big just because you think this is the way to get out of a mistake you've made." (S005) "A lot of times when I'm cold, I'll just start trading really small." (S005) When up, press: "If you're up 20 or 30%, you're playing the house money" (S005) and "January 1 has always been a great resetting of risk for myself." (S005)

### 10. Step out when the environment cannot be read
Sources: 5 (S005, S013, S019, S043, S044).
"when there are white caps on the bay, the pro don’t play." (S044) After the 2019 tariff tweet he went from 93 percent long to flat: "I don’t want to play in this environment." (S044) "But I don’t think you need to short. Just sidestep it." (S019) and "it's one of the most important things to do is not to play when you don't see a fat pitch." (S043)

### 11. Kill stale positions and anything without conviction
Sources: 2 (S004, S010).
"most people get into much trouble is when they have stale longs or stale shorts." (S004) and "There’s no reason to hang on to any security where you don’t have great conviction." (S010) A big position keeps itself fresh: "If you have a big massive position, it has your attention." (S010)

### 12. Size to the exit, so you can always get out
Sources: 2 (S005, S013).
"I would never get so big in something that I thought I couldn't get out where it would cost the fund maybe 1-2% in execution cost alone to get out." (S005) He prefers markets where he can reverse: "currencies and bonds trade 24 hours a day and they’re very liquid and you can change your mind" (S013) The rupiah, where he broke this, froze him (S005).

### 13. Do not sell because a trade is crowded or painful
Sources: 2 (S004, S006).
"I don’t care if a trade is crowded if I think the thesis is right and the trend is with me." (S006) On energy in 2022: "I don't care about pain trade this or that." (S004) He sells when the 18-month picture weakens: "We think these companies are still cheap relative to what we see a year or two out." (S004)

### 14. Do not chase a rising price, and do not confuse a bull market with skill
Sources: 3 (S001, S010, S047).
"when a security goes up, every bone in your body wants to buy more of it." (S010) and "And you cannot get crazy when it’s going up." (S010) His first boss's warning, "the higher they go, the cheaper they look." (S010, quoting his first boss) In a bubble, "don't get emotional don't get crazy" (S047). His 2000 buyback broke this: "I was just an emotional basket case and couldn't help myself." (S001)

### 15. Daily P&L is the risk system
Sources: 3 (S005, S013, S043).
"I've always used my P&L because I found all of those risk models, they're great until complete chaos happens." (S013) When P&L behaves oddly against what the matrix should do, he re-checks the positions (S005, S013, paraphrase). "my P&L doesn't move generally more than 30 or 40 basis points a day." (S043)

## Selling mistakes he admits, with dates
- 1999-03: covered internet shorts at a 600 to 800 million loss in weeks (S005, S047).
- 2000-01 to 2000-03: sold all tech correctly, then bought about 6 billion back near the top on envy and lost 2 to 3 billion (S001, S004, S010).
- 2006: left the party after a strong 2005 (S027).
- 2021 to 2022: took most of a one-way two-year short off at 150 basis points; yields went to 500 (S026).
- 2022: missed the dollar rally (S043).
- 2023: right on bonds and earnings, too little in equities (S048).
- 2024-03: sold Nvidia near 800 after saying in 2023 he would own it two or three years; it went to about 1,400 (S006, S047).

## Kill criteria this seat would write
Checkable facts that would end a thesis, in his terms (inferred from S004, S005, S010, S016, S019, S026, S043, S044 unless noted).
- The driver named at entry is no longer true 18 months out, whatever the stock has done since purchase (S010, S026).
- The diligence window after the starter buy (about two to three weeks) fails to confirm the concept (S004, S043).
- Good news lands and the stock does not respond, or falls; after one re-check of the thesis it still does not respond (S005, S043).
- The Fed starts tightening, or financial conditions turn from loose to tight, for a position that rests on liquidity; or turn loose for a position that rests on tightness (S010, S016).
- QE gives way to QT after an asset bubble, for any long in risk assets (S019).
- Corporate profits peak and cyclical leaders (trucking, retail, small caps) roll over against defensives, for a long that needs growth (S044).
- A policy or political shock makes the risk unanalyzable; go flat and re-enter when it can be read (S044).
- The position is too large to exit at a cost under 1 to 2 percent of the fund (S005).
- Conviction has faded to the point that it would not be bought today at this size (S010).

## Re-check triggers
- Every day: P&L against what the matrix should be doing (S005, S013, S043).
- Every night: his chart book across equity groups, commodities, currencies and bonds (S005).
- Every Fed meeting and every shift in forward guidance; each QE or QT step, Treasury cash and reserve drawdowns (S016, S019, S026).
- Financial conditions: credit spreads, gold, equity strength when the Fed eases (S026).
- The inside of the stock market: leading versus lagging industries, breadth and narrowing leadership (S004, S026, S044).
- Corporate profits turning and purchasing manager reports from companies (S044).
- Bubble gauges: share of unprofitable IPOs, share of new debt rated B or worse (S001, S027).
- Currency trends against his two-year minimum life (S009).
- A bullish thesis playing out while the stock stalls (S043).
- January 1: reset risk for the new year (S005).
- After a losing streak: size down, or take a full break (S004, S005).
