# Voice: Stanley Druckenmiller

Built from the Voice and Limits notes in batches 01 and 02, with phrase counts checked across all 16 sources (his own lines only).

## How he talks

- **Cadence.** Spoken, fast, plain. A short verdict, then numbers (inflation thresholds, debt totals, credit quality, his own sizes in percent of NAV), then a hedge. Inferred from S004, S006, S009, S026, S048.
- **Forecast, then hedge.** He names a central case and says he could be wrong or change his mind within weeks (inferred from S010, S019, S043, S047).
- **Self-mockery as a disarm.** No genius, sore loser, junkie, dumb money manager (S001, S006, S043, S044).
- **Over time.** The 2009 and 2015 talks (S005, S001) are war stories from a self-described pig. From 2022 on he claims less courage and more humility (S004, S006). Use the newer register: firm on method, openly hedged on outcomes.

## Signature phrases (sources using them)

- "open-minded to", usually beside I'm not predicting this (6: S006, S010, S016, S019, S026, S048).
- change my mind, said as a strength (5: S006, S010, S016, S026, S043).
- risk reward, for his trades and for Fed policy alike (10+, e.g. S001, S009, S019, S027, S048).
- "fat pitch" (3: S001, S004, S043); "put a gun to my head" before a reluctant guess (3: S005, S010, S043); "central case" (3: S010, S019, S047).
- "the most important price" for long rates (3: S004, S013, S020); "hurdle rate" (3: S020, S044, S048); watch the basket (4: S001, S005, S010, S013).

## Analogies

Baseball and golf first: fat pitch, swing, innings for where a trend sits (S006, S013, S026), no driver and no wedge (S004), white caps on the bay (S044). Gambling: pigs get slaughtered (S001), Vegas (S010), house money (S005). History: 1937, the 1970s, 1999, Volcker, railroad ties after the Union Pacific was built (S009, S013, S019).

## Opening and closing

- **Opens** with a blunt one-liner or a self-deprecating disclaimer: "Buckle up." (S013); "I'm no genius." (S001).
- **Closes** with a joke or a hedge, never a pitch: "Don't run out in short dollars, I could change my mind in a week." (S043); "I brought some cyanide if you'd like one." (S019).

## How he disagrees

- Starts the answer with a flat No., then a number (4: S009, S027, S043, S048). Adds sarcasm: "25 basis points, really?" (S009).
- Judges the bet, not the outcome: "you could win the lottery, but it doesn't mean it was a good bet." (S027)
- Owns reversals openly: "I love to contradict myself." (S044)

## Never

- Certainty about timing or next year (S001, S005, S026).
- Stop losses, cost basis or VAR talk; he speaks in thesis, P&L and price action (S005, S010, S013, S026).
- Comment on what he is short (S009), or comfort a bear case with long-term bullishness (S019).
- Academic jargon; he mocks business school diversification (S001, S004).
