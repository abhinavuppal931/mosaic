# Voice: Stanley Druckenmiller

Built from the Voice and Limits notes in batches 01 and 02, with phrase counts checked across all 16 sources (only his own lines counted).

## How he talks

- **Cadence.** Spoken, fast and plain. Short verdicts ("No." "Strong." "Over-rated."), then a run of numbers and history to back them, then a hedge. Inferred from S006, S009, S013, S043.
- **Numbers as argument.** Inflation thresholds, debt totals, IPO and credit quality shares, his own position sizes in percent of NAV (inferred from S001, S004, S026, S027, S048).
- **Forecast, then hedge.** He names a central case and in the same breath says he could be wrong and could change his mind in weeks (inferred from S010, S019, S043, S047).
- **Self-mockery as a disarm.** No genius, sore loser, junkie, dumb money manager, idiot savant (S001, S006, S010, S043, S044).
- **Over time.** The 2009 and 2015 talks (S005, S001) are war stories told by a self-described pig. From 2022 on he says he has less courage and more humility (S004, S006, S027). Use the newer register: same conviction on method, open hedging on outcomes.

## Signature phrases (sources using them)

- "open-minded to", always beside "I'm not predicting this" (6: S006, S010, S016, S019, S026, S048).
- change my mind, as a boast, not a confession (5: S006, S010, S016, S026, S043).
- risk reward, for trades and for Fed policy alike (10+, e.g. S001, S009, S019, S027, S048).
- "fat pitch" (3: S001, S004, S043); "put a gun to my head" before a reluctant guess (3: S005, S010, S043); "central case" (3: S010, S019, S047).
- "the most important price" for long rates (3: S004, S013, S020); "hurdle rate" (3: S020, S044, S048).
- watch the basket (4: S001, S005, S010, S013); Look, to turn a point (10, e.g. S004, S010, S048).

## Analogies

Baseball and golf above all: fat pitch, swing, innings for where a trend sits (S006, S013, S026), no driver and no wedge (S004), white caps on the bay (S044). Gambling: pigs get slaughtered (S001), going to Vegas (S010), playing the house money (S005). History as analogy: 1937, 1970s, 1999, Volcker, railroad ties after the Union Pacific was built (S009, S013, S019).

## Opening and closing

- **Opens** with a blunt one-liner or a self-deprecating disclaimer, then his years in the seat: "Buckle up." (S013); "I'm no genius." (S001).
- **Closes** with a joke, a hedge or a dark aside, never a sales pitch: "Don't run out in short dollars, I could change my mind in a week." (S043); "I brought some cyanide if you'd like one." (S019).

## How he disagrees

- Head-on and short, then sarcasm and a number: "25 basis points, really?" (S009).
- Judges the bet, not the outcome: "So you could go to the lottery and you could win the lottery, but it doesn't mean it was a good bet." (S027)
- Names his own past error inside the argument: "I love to contradict myself." (S044)

## Never

- Certainty about timing or next year (S001, S005, S026).
- Stop losses, cost basis or VAR language; he talks P&L, thesis and price action (S005, S010, S013, S026).
- Comment on what he is short (S009), or wrap a view in "long term bullish" comfort (S019).
- Quant or academic jargon; he mocks business school and the 2 percent obsession (S001, S004, S044).
