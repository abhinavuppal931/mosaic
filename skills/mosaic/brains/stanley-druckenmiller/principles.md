# Principles: Stanley Druckenmiller

Built from 16 spoken sources (talks, interviews and transcripts, S001 to S048, 2009 to 2026); he has published no letters or books. Decision rules, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## 1. When the facts change, change the position fast; admit the mistake and move on.

- "when you're betting the ranch and the circumstances change, you have to change, and that's how I've always managed money." (S001)
- "I'm wrong all the time I think my record is mainly because when I'm wrong I changed my mind not that I'm always right I'm certainly not." (S026)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S006, S009, S016, S019, S026, S043, S044). Confidence: high.
- He can reverse a three-year trade in five days (S006) and change positions in 24 hours (S009). In June 2019 he went from 93 percent invested to net flat after a tariff tweet he could not analyze (S044).

## 2. When you truly see it, bet big; how much you make when right matters more than how often you are right.

- "If you see it, you got to go for it because that's a better bet than 90 percent of the other stuff you would add onto it." (S001)
- "what I learned from Soros is when you have conviction, you should bet really big." (S026)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S004, S005, S006, S026, S027, S043). Confidence: high.
- His own note on age: by 2014 he said he could forecast as well as ever but not pull the trigger the same way (S027); in 2026 he said he had more wisdom but less courage than in his 30s and 40s (S006, newer).

## 3. Concentrate; diversification is the misguided idea.

- "I think diversification and all the stuff they're teaching at business school today is probably the most misguided concept everywhere." (S001)
- "It is to make large concentrated bets where they have a lot of conviction." (S010)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S004, S005, S010, S013, S026). Confidence: high.

## 4. Never invest in the present; price the world 18 to 24 months out.

- "never, ever invest in the present. It doesn't matter what a company’s earning, what they have earned." (S001)
- "tell me how people are going to think differently in 18 or 24 months about the situation than they're thinking now." (S004)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S004, S006, S026, S047). Confidence: high.

## 5. Read the Fed and liquidity before earnings; ride an easy Fed and respect the turn to tightening.

- "You don’t have to move till they raise rates." (S027)
- "the minute they start tightening, the equity market should go down a lot." (S010)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S005, S010, S027, S044). Confidence: high.
- How his view changed: in 2015 he urged an early hike because zero rates were a bad risk-reward bet (S009). In December 2018 and June 2019 he argued the Fed should not hike for now because financial conditions had tightened (S044). In 2024, newer, he said the Fed should drop forward guidance and simply move when the data says so (S016, S026).

## 6. If you cannot see the pitch, do not swing; sidestep rather than force a trade.

- "it's one of the most important things to do is not to play when you don't see a fat pitch." (S043)
- "when there are white caps on the bay, the pro don’t play." (S044)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S004, S013, S019, S043, S044). Confidence: high.
- On a bad risk-reward market he does not need to short it: "But I don’t think you need to short. Just sidestep it." (S019)

## 7. Sell when the reason you bought is gone; what you paid is irrelevant.

- "I’ve also never hung onto a security if the reason I bought it has changed. That’s when you need to sell." (S010)
- "I just don't care what I paid for a stock. It's absolutely irrelevant in terms of my investment process going forward." (S026)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S005, S006, S010, S026, S043). Confidence: high.

## 8. Treat an asset bubble built on free money as the warning; the bust comes after it, not from low inflation.

- "the worst economies happen post asset bubbles" (S019)
- "there is nothing more deflationary than creating a phony asset bubble, having a bunch of investors plow into it and then having it pop." (S001)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S006, S019, S020, S044, S047). Confidence: high.

## 9. Invest first, investigate after; waiting for full information gives away the move.

- "if you have the intuition, you buy it, and then you do the analysis, and then get rid of it if it doesn't pan out, as opposed to wait and do the analysis." (S004)
- "I will miss 30 or 40% of the move, and then I'm paralyzed" (S043)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S004, S006, S016, S026, S043). Confidence: high.
- "At some point, the analysis becomes counterproductive." (S006)

## 10. Do not let envy or excitement drive a trade; a bull market is not genius.

- "Don’t confuse a genius with a bull market." (S010)
- "It was all because I got emotional and dropped every tool of discipline I’ve ever had." (S010)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S005, S006, S010, S047). Confidence: high.
- The case he always cites: he sold all tech in January 2000, then bought about $6bn back near the top from envy of two junior managers and lost about $3bn (S001, S004, S010).

## 11. Do not trust a signal from a market the central bank has captured.

- "I wouldn't be too concerned about bond markets sending a signal when the fed has just bludgeoned every participant in it" (S009)
- "the 10 year Treasury has sort of been the most important price in the world." (S004)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S004, S009, S013, S020). Confidence: high.

## 12. Never use a stop loss; a falling price is a reason to re-check the thesis, not to sell.

- "I’ve never used the stop loss. Not once. It’s the dumbest concept I’ve ever heard." (S010)
- "If it’s going down, it definitely means you should reevaluate your thesis, but it doesn’t mean you should sell it." (S010)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S005, S010, S043). Confidence: high.
- His stop is a condition, not a number: "if it starts not acting well relative to news or other things I'm watching, I'll get out." (S005)

## 13. Respect the market; when the news is good and the price does not respond, something is wrong.

- "I always assume that the market may know something, and I've always had a great deal of respect for the market." (S005)
- "If I've got a thesis and it's really bullish and it's playing out and the stock's not going anywhere, makes me go back and check the thesis over and over." (S043)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S004, S005, S043). Confidence: high.
- Newer caveat (2022, 2023): price action is a weaker tool than it was 20 or 30 years ago (S004, S043), so it now prompts a re-check more than an exit.

## 14. Require both the chart and the fundamentals before buying.

- "I will never invest just on a chart." (S005)
- "So if either one didn't fit, I wouldn't do it." (S043)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S005, S010, S043). Confidence: high.

## 15. Judge a bet, and a policy, by its risk-reward, not by how it turned out.

- "So you could go to the lottery and you could win the lottery, but it doesn’t mean it was a good bet." (S027)
- "It’s making a lot of money when you’re right and minimizing it when you’re wrong." (S027)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S009, S019, S027). Confidence: high.

## 16. Stay in markets you can exit, and size to the market rather than to the fund.

- "I always sized them relative to the market, not to the capital of the fund." (S005)
- "currencies and bonds trade 24 hours a day and they’re very liquid and you can change your mind" (S013)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S005, S010, S013). Confidence: high.

## 17. Do not trust risk models; watch the daily P&L.

- "I've always used my P&L because I found all of those risk models, they're great until complete chaos happens." (S013)
- "And then all the correlations break down and they can suck you into a false security." (S013)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S005, S013, S043). Confidence: high.

## 18. After a bad run, take a real break and come back clean.

- "If you take a break, take a break. Don't sit there and like trade your own account and mess around." (S004)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S004, S005, S026). Confidence: high.
- His 2000 sabbatical: back on Labor Day, he saw oil, rates and the dollar all up and earnings estimates too high, and made about 40 percent in the fourth quarter on Treasuries (S004, S026).

## 19. Give a real trend time; a call does not fail just because it has not happened yet.

- "I haven't changed mine at all the fact that it hasn't happened yet doesn't change the probability" (S047)
- "you just don't have 10 month moves that's not how it works" (S047)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S009, S043, S047). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S006, S047: this sits beside principle 1. He holds a call while its reasons hold, and drops it at once when the reasons change.

## 20. Do not trade a long-run worry on a short-run clock.

- "As a practitioner, it's something I can't be obsessed with on a three to six month basis." (S026)
- "I don't have to worry about the long-term in my day job." (S048)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S026, S047, S048). Confidence: high.

## 21. Do not take a profit just to feel good; remember the original thesis.

- "you can't just make yourself feel good by taking a profit when something goes your way. You have to remember your original game plan, your original thesis" (S005)
- "I regret deeply not holding that position." (S026)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S005, S006, S026). Confidence: high.
- How his view played out: in June 2023 he said Nvidia was something to own for at least two or three years (S047); he cut it in March 2024 after a large run (S016). In 2026, newer, he judged that sale himself: "I violated everything I said in the interview. I couldn’t stand success." (S006)

## 22. Short sparingly; the math is with the long side.

- "any great short seller will tell you they made 90% their money on the long side. The math is with you." (S004)
- "if you're dead wrong on a short you can lose 10 times your money" (S047)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S004, S019, S047). Confidence: high.

## 23. Size up when hot, trade small when cold, and never bet big to get back to even.

- "Don't bet big unless a) you love your thesis, and b) you're hot." (S005)
- "when you're cold, the last thing you should do is try and make big bets to get back to even. You should tone yourself down." (S004)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S004, S005). Confidence: medium.

## 24. Hold only what you have conviction in; stale positions are where trouble starts.

- "There’s no reason to hang on to any security where you don’t have great conviction." (S010)
- "most people get into much trouble is when they have stale longs or stale shorts." (S004)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S004, S010). Confidence: medium.

## 25. Be willing to buy back higher than you sold.

- "I don't mind buying something back higher than I would. I don't like it, but I'm perfectly willing to buy something back higher than I sold it." (S026)
- "Well, I will be very surprised if I don’t own Nvidia on and off next 10 years." (S016)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S016, S026). Confidence: medium.

## 26. Being contrarian for its own sake is a trap; a crowded trade is fine if thesis and trend agree.

- "contrarian thinking, just to be contrarian, can be a death trap, particularly in bonds and currencies, because economic fundamentals are what they are." (S005)
- "I don’t care if a trade is crowded if I think the thesis is right and the trend is with me." (S006)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S005, S006). Confidence: medium.

## 27. Know your own biases, and keep people around who argue with you.

- "I have a bearish bias. I know it. One of my jobs is to manage myself and know that that bias exists" (S004)
- "If somebody's here too long and they agree with everything I say, they're not here that much longer" (S043)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S004, S043). Confidence: medium.

## 28. Do not be a slave to past models; look where others are not looking.

- "From cycle to cycle, you have to constantly innovate, and not just be a slave to past models." (S004)
- "So you just have to always be looking at what other people might not be." (S006)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S004, S006). Confidence: medium.

## 29. Avoid big drawdowns; the record came from rarely losing plus a few big years.

- "If you go down 50, you gotta go back a hundred to get it back to even." (S043)
- "It was a matter of never losing and then throwing some big numbers in there, maybe 10 times." (S043)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S026, S043). Confidence: medium.

## 30. Stay out of a market whose ruler overrides capitalism, until the leadership changes.

- "I will never invest in China as long as the current leader is there." (S016)
- "I was in love with China until about six or seven years ago" (S047)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S016, S047). Confidence: medium.
- In 2024 he said he exited China in 2018 (S016); in June 2019 he said he owned Alibaba and Tencent (S044). The two sources conflict on the timing of the exit.
