# Frameworks: Stanley Druckenmiller

Built from 16 spoken sources (talks, interviews and transcripts, S001 to S048, 2009 to 2026); he has published no letters or books, so every framework rests on what he said aloud. Most-used first. Scoring lines are guidance drawn from his own numbers and thresholds, not a scorecard he published. Dated stances are his public record, not calls on today's securities.

## 1. Liquidity and the Fed move the market *(unnamed; second lesson from his first mentor)*

- **What it is:** the overall market is driven by central bank policy and the flow of liquidity, not by earnings. He reads money growth against the real economy, the direction of the central bank balance sheet, and financial conditions, and treats a turn in any of them as the switch for risk.
- **Parts:** (1) money supply growth against industrial production or nominal GDP (since 2018, M2 grew 25 percent more than nominal GDP, S013); (2) balance sheet direction: QE is risk on, QT is risk off (S044), including Treasury cash and Strategic Petroleum Reserve drawdowns that delay the shrinkage (S019); (3) the policy rate: with an easy Fed you do not have to leave until they raise rates (S027), and once tightening starts equities should fall a lot (S010); (4) financial conditions as entry and exit: he bought a leveraged two-year Treasury position when conditions were tight and inflation falling, and sold once the late 2023 pivot loosened them (S016).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** first, before any single name. Decide whether liquidity is expanding or draining over the next 18 months, then decide how much equity risk to carry at all.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = money growing well ahead of output, the Fed easing or on hold with no inflation pressure, conditions loosening; 5 = Fed on hold, liquidity flat, signals mixed; 0 = buying risk as QE turns to QT right after an asset bubble (his 2022 setup, S019) or as the Fed starts tightening into high valuations.
- **Quote:** "earnings don't move the overall market; it's the Federal Reserve Board." (S001)
- **Also:** "if you have the money supply growing a lot faster than industrial production, the stock market is generally going to go up." (S005)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S005, S010, S013, S016, S019, S027, S044).

## 2. Put all your eggs in one basket and watch the basket

- **What it is:** superior returns come from a few large, high-conviction bets, not diversification. Only one or two times a year does something truly excite him; then he sizes it big and watches it closely. A big position is its own risk control because it holds his attention.
- **Parts:** (1) no more than 15 to 20 equity names (S010); (2) one or two great ideas a year (S001), three or four big winners a year for any investor (S013); (3) nothing in between: either a big bet or no bet; (4) daily watching of the position and the P&L.
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** sizing, and the question of whether a position deserves to exist at all. A name held only for diversification is a candidate to cut.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = an idea strong enough to be one of the year's few big bets, with thesis and trend agreeing; 5 = a good idea that would be filler at a small weight; 0 = a position held for balance or comfort, with no conviction behind it.
- **Quote:** "put all your eggs in one basket and then watch the basket very carefully." (S001)
- **Also:** "completely contrary to what they teach in business school, which is, if you're highly diversified, you have less risk than if you're highly concentrated. I don't believe that at all." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S004, S005, S010, S013, S026, S044).

## 3. Bubble, then bust *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** the worst economies and the worst deflations follow asset bubbles, usually set up by too loose policy, not by inflation being too low. Free money breeds egregious behavior, and the damage arrives when liquidity is withdrawn.
- **Parts:** (1) a long stretch of near-zero or manipulated rates; (2) a broad asset bubble that people justify with a narrative; (3) the policy turn (QE to QT, rate hikes); (4) the bust, which he expects to be long and not a six-month affair.
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** before buying into a hot theme late in an easy-money cycle, and when deciding whether a sharp fall after a bubble is a buying chance or the first leg.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = buying after the bubble has deflated and policy has turned easy again; 5 = valuations high but liquidity still flowing; 0 = buying a narrative bubble late as policy tightens. He put the 2026 narrative bubble at about the 8th inning (S006) and said in 2022 that six-month bear markets after bubbles do not exist (S004).
- **Quote:** "every bust I had ever seen was preceded by an asset bubble generally set up by too loose policy." (S044)
- **Also:** "six months bear markets preceded by asset bubbles don't exist, historically." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S004, S006, S019, S020, S044, S047).

## 4. Central bank mistake playbook *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** about 80 percent of his biggest gains came in equity bear markets caused by central bank mistakes. He judges policy the way he judges a trade, as a risk-reward bet, and positions for the consequences of a bad one.
- **Parts:** (1) the blind test: hide the fed funds rate, look only at the economic data, and ask where rates should be (in late 2003 his analysts guessed 3 to 6 percent against an actual 1 percent, S001); (2) strong nominal growth plus very low rates is a warning even before the mechanism shows (2003 to 2005 housing, S009); (3) judge the Fed by the bet, not the outcome (S027); (4) watch for early victory declarations (the December 2023 pivot, S016; the 2024 cut with tight spreads and gold at highs, S026).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** any time the thesis depends on the Fed's path, and for market-level timing of when to carry or shed equity risk.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the decision is positioned for the consequence of a clear policy error, with a visible mechanism; 5 = a policy error is visible but the mechanism and timing are not; 0 = the decision needs the policy mistake to last forever.
- **Quote:** "about 80 percent of the big, big money we made was in bear markets and equities because crazy things were going on in response to what I would call central bank mistakes" (S001)
- **Also:** "as someone who lives risk reward every day, the risk reward of going too early, to me, is much more favorable than going too late" (S009)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S009, S016, S019, S026, S027).

## 5. Batting average versus slugging percentage (sizing is the game)

- **What it is:** the lesson he took from Soros. Being right matters less than how much you make when right and how little you lose when wrong. When conviction is high, size far beyond comfort.
- **Parts:** (1) conviction level; (2) asymmetry of payoff (framework 6); (3) leverage by asset class: in his younger years 150 to 200 percent in a currency, 300 percent in 10-year bond equivalents, rarely over 100 percent net long or 50 percent net short equities, gross rarely above 4 to 1 (S005); in 2022, 200 to 300 percent in macro positions versus 15 to 20 percent of assets in an equity (S004).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** after the thesis is set, to decide whether this is a starter, a normal position or the big bet.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = high conviction, hot hand, one-way payoff, sized to matter; 5 = right idea at a token size; 0 = big size on low conviction, or big size to win back a loss.
- **How his view changed:** 2014: "I just don’t have the guts that I used to." (S027) 2026, newer: "I think I have more wisdom and I have more tools of the trade than I had in my 30s and 40s, and I was a much better portfolio manager then because back then I had courage." (S006)
- **Quote:** "sizing is probably 70 or 80% of the equation. It's not whether you're right or wrong, it's how much you make when you're right, and how much you lose when you're wrong." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S004, S005, S006, S026, S027).

## 6. The one-way bet (asymmetric risk-reward)

- **What it is:** size on the ratio of payoff to cost, not on the odds of being right. The British pound in 1992 is the template: a small, known cost if wrong and a huge gain if right.
- **Parts:** (1) define the cost if wrong (forward points of about half a percent on the pound, S005); (2) define the gain if right; (3) when the ratio is lopsided, press: he started at $1.5bn, proposed 100 percent of the fund, and Soros said 200 percent (S001, S026); (4) look for the second-order trades (framework 24).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to compare a stock idea against other ways to express the same view, and to check whether the downside is capped or open-ended.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = downside small and capped, upside many times larger (the pound at about 40 to 1, S019); 5 = roughly 2 or 3 to 1; 0 = symmetric payoff or open-ended loss, like a short that can lose ten times the stake (S047).
- **Quote:** "if they didn’t devalue in the next six months, my fund was going to lose 50 basis points; if they did devalue, I was going to make 2,000 basis points." (S019)
- **Also:** "So you could go to the lottery and you could win the lottery, but it doesn’t mean it was a good bet." (S027)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S005, S009, S019, S026, S027).

## 7. Time horizon and trend length *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** he trades trends that run 18 months to three years, sometimes one year, sometimes five. Major currency trends last at least two years. He is not a 10 to 20 year holder.
- **Parts:** (1) expected life of the trend; (2) where in the trend you are; (3) willingness to reverse fast if the reason changes (a three-year trade can be gone in five days, S006). A political cycle rule he credits to his first boss in 1976: buy the market two years before the general election and sell on the election (S019).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to check that the thesis fits an 18 month to 3 year window and that the trend is early or middle, not late.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = thesis plays out over 18 months to three years and the trend is young; 5 = trend mature but intact; 0 = the case needs a 10 to 20 year hold to work, or depends on next week's catalyst.
- **Quote:** "Most trades I put on, I think in terms of 18 months to three years, that’s how long I think they’re probably gonna evolve." (S006)
- **Also:** "I've never seen a major currency trend last less than two years" (S009)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S009, S016, S026, S043, S047).

## 8. The menu of asset buckets *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** he wants bonds, currencies, commodities, credit and equities all available, so the big bet goes wherever the best one sits, and he never has to play where he has no edge.
- **Parts:** (1) five or six buckets; (2) three benefits (S013): the buckets point you in the right direction, 24-hour liquid markets let you change your mind, and the latitude lets you not play; (3) credit only every eight years, in a debacle (S013).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** ask whether a stock is the best way to express the view, or whether a bond, currency or commodity says it more cleanly. A stock idea competes with every other bucket.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the stock is the cleanest, most liquid expression of the macro view; 5 = a stock works but another bucket fits better; 0 = forcing an equity trade in an area he would rather not play.
- **How his view changed:** 2019: "I made 70% of my money when I was competing in bonds and currencies." (S044), said while macro opportunity was thin. 2026, newer: "Macro has been dead for 10 or 15 years; I don’t think that’s the case anymore." (S006)
- **Quote:** "So historically I deal in five or six asset buckets, which tends to keep me out of trouble in terms of playing in an area where I shouldn't be playing at a particular time." (S043)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S006, S013, S026, S043, S044).

## 9. Never invest in the present (the 18 month rule)

- **What it is:** the first lesson from his first mentor at Pittsburgh National. Ignore what a company earns today or what the central bank is doing today. Picture the situation 18 months out (later 18 to 24, and 12 to 18 for shorts) and ask whether today's price reflects it. Change, not the present, moves prices. This is the seat's question.
- **Parts:** (1) forecast the situation 12 to 24 months out; (2) ask how people will think differently then than now (S004); (3) compare with what prices discount; (4) act on the gap.
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** every buy, add, hold, trim or sell, and especially when current earnings are very good or very bad.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = a clear, different world in 18 to 24 months that the price does not reflect (Teva at about 6 times earnings with a new CEO, re-rated to about 12 times as biosimilars proved out, S006); 5 = the forecast differs from consensus but the price already reflects part of it; 0 = the case rests on what the company earns now.
- **Quote:** "you have to visualize the situation 18 months from now, and whatever that is, that's where the price will be, not where it is today." (S001)
- **Also:** "Do not, do not invest in the present. The present is not what moves stock prices. Change moves them." (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S004, S006, S026, S047).

## 10. Invest, then investigate (the Soros rule)

- **What it is:** if an idea appeals intuitively and fits the macro picture, take a meaningful position first and do the diligence afterward, adding if it pans out and selling if not. Waiting for full analysis lets others discover the story; 60 to 70 percent of the move can happen while you study (S004).
- **Parts:** (1) intuition plus fit with the macro view; (2) buy a meaningful but not earth-shaking position (S026); (3) two to three weeks of diligence (S004); (4) add if right, sell if wrong, regardless of profit or loss (S006).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on a new idea that fits the macro view and where speed matters (Argentine ADRs after the Milei Davos speech, Nvidia after a partner's tip, S016).
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = clear fit with the macro view, starter position on, diligence under way with a set exit if it fails; 5 = right idea but stuck in analysis while the move runs; 0 = a large position bought on intuition with no follow-up work, or held after the work says no.
- **Quote:** "I follow the old Soros rule, invest and then investigate. I bought all of them." (S016)
- **Also:** "if you sit around and analyze a company for four months, and you’re not willing to operate with 15-20% of information, you’ll often miss a big move and then you’re afraid to buy it because it has moved." (S006)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S006, S016, S026, S043).

## 11. The inside of the stock market (macro from the bottom up)

- **What it is:** he does macro bottom up, from companies and industry groups, not from economists or official statistics. Stocks lead fundamentals by 6 to 12 months, and the industries that lead the economy tell him where it is going.
- **Parts:** (1) leading groups (homebuilders, trucking, some retail, the Russell 2000) against lagging ones (capital goods) and defensives (S004, S044); (2) talk to purchasing managers, not CEOs (S044); (3) a market that explodes on record volume and breadth means recovery 6 to 12 months later (S009); (4) he has never seen a recession before corporate profits peaked (S044); (5) narrowing breadth is a yellow light (S026); (6) employment data lags (S006), though he uses macro data for entries and exits.
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to judge the cycle a cyclical stock sits in, and to check whether the market is pricing recovery or recession ahead of the data.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = leading groups and breadth confirm the thesis direction; 5 = mixed leadership; 0 = the thesis relies on lagging statistics while leading groups point the other way.
- **Quote:** "By far the best economic predictor I’ve ever met is the inside of the stock market." (S044)
- **Also:** "we do the macro by a compilation of listening to companies and doing a bottoms up analysis of industries that lead the economy and industries that lag the economy." (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S006, S009, S026, S044).

## 12. Charts plus fundamentals (the triple screen)

- **What it is:** a fundamental thesis must be confirmed by the chart, and the chart keeps him from falling in love with an idea. In 2009 he reviewed 272 charts each night across equity groups, commodities, currencies and bonds; by 2021 he called it a triple screen of fundamentals, technicals and a quantitative tool (S010).
- **Parts:** (1) daily charts predict 8 to 20 days, weekly 8 to 20 weeks, monthly 8 to 20 months (S005); (2) about 75 to 80 percent of ideas start in fundamentals and are verified by the chart (S005); (3) charts using second derivative rate of change often bottom a year to 18 months before fundamentals (S005); (4) he waits for tops as a technician (S026). His one big exception, Brazil rates, he calls a 1 in 100 (S005).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** as the final gate on any buy, and as an alarm on a hold.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = great fundamentals and a great chart; 5 = one is strong, the other neutral; 0 = a fundamental thesis with a chart that stinks (he will not do it).
- **How his view changed:** 2009, the chart was a core tool (S005). 2026, newer: "technical analysis is about 20% as effective today as it was then because no one was using it." (S006) He still uses it as a discipline, not as a trigger on its own (inferred from S006, S010, S043).
- **Quote:** "But if I really like a fundamental thesis and the chart stinks, I won't do it." (S005)
- **Also:** "the technical provides a discipline on the fundamental and the fundamental provides a discipline on the technical." (S043)
- **Sources:** 5 (S005, S006, S010, S026, S043).

## 13. The hurdle rate and the most important price *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** interest rates, above all long rates, should be set by the market so capital faces a real hurdle. When central banks pin rates near zero, signals vanish, zombies survive, and capital flows into financial engineering and bubbles. When rates normalize, capital allocation improves.
- **Parts:** (1) is the long rate market-set or suppressed; (2) does the bond market still send a signal (S009); (3) is cheap money keeping weak firms alive (S020, S044); (4) has a hurdle rate returned (S048).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to discount signals from markets the central bank controls, and to judge whether a company's returns depend on free money.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the business earns well above a market-set cost of capital; 5 = returns adequate only at today's rates; 0 = a business that exists because money is free.
- **Quote:** "you need a hurdle rate for investment, and if you don’t have a hurdle rate for investment, bad things happen." (S044)
- **Also:** "The central bank has bastardized, probably, the most important price in the world, which is the cost of money, particularly at the long end." (S013)
- **Sources:** 5 (S009, S013, S020, S044, S048).

## 14. Bubble gauges: IPO quality, credit quality, good debt and bad debt *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his early warning set for excess. He counts the share of IPOs with no profits, the share of new debt rated B or worse, and whether borrowing funds real investment or financial engineering.
- **Parts:** (1) unprofitable IPOs: 83 percent in 1999, 80 percent in 2014 (S027); (2) debt rated B or worse: about 70 percent of issuance in 2014 and 2015 (S001, S027); (3) corporate credit doubling from $3.5 trillion in 2007 to $7 trillion (S009); (4) buybacks against capex: $5.7 trillion against $2.2 trillion (S044); (5) the IBM case, debt tripled to triple the buyback (S027).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** check whether the company's per-share growth is bought with debt-funded buybacks, and whether the market around it shows 1999-style issuance.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = growth funded by real investment, clean balance sheet, issuance markets calm; 5 = some buyback leverage; 0 = debt-funded buybacks with flat book value in a market where most IPOs lose money.
- **Quote:** "there's good debt growth, and there's bad debt growth. Good debt growth is when you borrow money, and it goes into the real economy." (S009)
- **Also:** "In ’99, 83 percent of IPOs went public had never earned a dime. Today that’s 80 percent." (S027)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S009, S020, S027, S044).

## 15. Price action versus news

- **What it is:** watch how a price reacts to news. Bad news that a stock shrugs off is bullish; great news that fails to lift it is a warning. He always assumes the market may know something.
- **Parts:** (1) note the news; (2) note the reaction; (3) if the reaction disagrees with the news, go back to the thesis.
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on holds and adds, as a check after earnings and macro news.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the stock rallies on bad news and keeps confirming the thesis; 5 = reacts as expected; 0 = all the news is great and the stock is not acting well.
- **How his view changed:** 2009, a rule he acted on (S005). 2022 and 2023, newer: he says the tool has weakened because algorithms and the crowd now use it, so he treats it as a reason to re-check rather than to act alone (S004, S043).
- **Quote:** "if all the news is great and the stocks not acting well, get out" (S005), a rule he says he learned from his first boss at Pittsburgh National.
- **Also:** "price versus news is a very weakened tool versus 20 years ago." (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S005, S006, S043).

## 16. The matrix *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** a set of positions that hang together under one view of inflation and growth relative to what policymakers expect, with each leg hedging the others. He holds it in his head and reads tension in it from the daily P&L.
- **Parts:** (1) the core view, usually inflation against policymaker expectations; (2) legs across buckets; (3) a hedge leg that pays in either outcome. Dated examples: 2021, short long-end Treasuries, long commodities, short the dollar (S013); 2022, short fixed income, short stocks, long oil and copper, short the dollar (S004); 2026, long equities in Japan and Korea, copper and some gold, short the dollar, short bonds as the hedge (S006).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to place one stock inside the wider book and check it does not fight the rest.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the stock fits the matrix and another leg hedges it; 5 = neutral to the matrix; 0 = the stock bets against the book's own core view.
- **Quote:** "my overriding aim is inflation relative to what the policy makers think." (S013)
- **Also:** "But we create a matrix, and the bonds are helpful in both ways." (S006)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S005, S006, S013).

## 17. Inflation rules of thumb *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** historical records he treats as near laws for the inflation and rate path, which then set his view of equities and bonds.
- **Parts:** (1) above 5 percent, inflation has never come down unless fed funds rose above CPI (S004); (2) no soft landing after inflation above 4.5 percent (S004); (3) once lit, inflation spreads through wages (S019); (4) 4 to 5 percent inflation with rising yields hurts growth stocks relative to others (S013); (5) you do not cure inflation with an inflationary act (S019).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on any long-duration growth stock, and whenever inflation runs above the target.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the thesis holds whether or not the Fed has to push real rates positive; 5 = the thesis needs a soft landing; 0 = a long-duration growth stock bought while inflation is above 5 percent and fed funds sit below CPI.
- **His record:** 2022: "I will be stunned if we don’t have a recession in ’23." (S019) 2024, newer: "I was very confident that inflation was going to come down, which I was right on, but I was worried about the economy, which I was completely wrong on." (S026)
- **Quote:** "once inflation gets above 5%, it's never come down unless Fed funds have gotten above the CPI." (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S013, S016, S019).

## 18. Bet on change *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** money is made where something is changing and investors cannot keep up: disrupters against the disrupted, governance reform, new management, where young engineers are going.
- **Parts:** (1) find the change (technology, management, governance, policy); (2) ask whether investors can keep up with it (S006); (3) long the disrupter, short the disrupted (S044); (4) watch where the kids go (AI over crypto in 2022, S006).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to tell a re-rating story from a cheap stock that stays cheap. Japan was the biggest value trap in history (S047) until governance changed (S016).
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = a large change under way that the market has not priced, with a clear driver; 5 = change visible and partly priced; 0 = cheap with no change in sight.
- **Quote:** "My portfolio has basically been long disrupters and short disrupted for like three or four years now." (S044)
- **Also:** "It’s a lot cheaper than here and if you believe in change, there you go." (S016)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S006, S016, S044).

## 19. Fiscal reckoning: watch it, do not trade it on a short clock *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** he tracks the consolidated government debt (counting Fed overnight funding, because that is what the taxpayer owes, S048) and interest expense as a long-term threat to bonds, but he will not trade it on a three to six month horizon.
- **Parts:** (1) consolidated debt and its maturity; (2) interest expense against other spending (S048, S019); (3) the size of the fix (taxes up 40 percent or spending down 36 percent forever, S047); (4) a trigger he cannot time (a failed auction, six months or six years, S026).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** for long-duration assets and bond-sensitive equities over years, not for this quarter's entry.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the decision allows for a fiscal shock in rates without depending on its timing; 5 = ignores it but holds a short horizon; 0 = a short-term trade built on the fiscal story alone.
- **Quote:** "I don't think the entitlement situation is something you should be trading on in the next three to six months" (S047)
- **Also:** "I made a lot of money this year betting on bonds going down because of the debt and had nothing do with the economy." (S048)
- **Sources:** 4 (S019, S026, S047, S048).

## 20. The P&L as the risk system *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** no stop losses, no VAR, no quantitative risk system. He holds the matrix in his head and watches the daily P&L; when it behaves oddly against what he expects, he re-checks.
- **Parts:** (1) know what each position should do on a given day; (2) watch daily P&L (it rarely moves more than 30 or 40 basis points a day, S043); (3) treat strange P&L as an alarm; (4) distrust models, because correlations break down in chaos (S013).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on holds, when a position stops acting the way the thesis says it should.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the position behaves as the thesis predicts; 5 = noisy but explainable; 0 = it keeps acting badly against the news and the book.
- **Quote:** "my risk management system has always been what I've described, that knowledge, and then my gut, and watching my daily PnL." (S005)
- **Also:** "I've always used my P&L because I found all of those risk models, they're great until complete chaos happens." (S013)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S010, S013, S043).

## 21. Valuation set by the rate regime *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** multiples only mean something against real rates and policy. He uses valuation as context for the regime, not as a trigger; when trading the transmission from markets to the economy, overvaluation alone does not bother him.
- **Parts:** (1) the real rate regime (8 times earnings in 1982 with five-year yields at 15 percent, S019); (2) the normal multiple for that regime (about 15 before QE, S048); (3) where today sits in the historical range (toward the top in 2026, S006).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to judge whether a multiple can hold if rates normalize, not to time the entry.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the multiple is reasonable for a market-set rate regime; 5 = rich but supported by liquidity; 0 = the multiple only works with free money and the regime is turning.
- **Quote:** "In a pre-QE world, 15 was about normal. When I got in the business, it was 8." (S048)
- **Also:** "Over valuation doesn't concern me at all. I'm not even opining on the stock market. I'm opining on the transmission mechanism, the stock market to the economy" (S009)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S009, S019, S048).

## 22. Hot and cold, and the January 1 reset

- **What it is:** know whether you are seeing the ball. When hot, turn the dial up and play with house money; when cold, trade small. January 1 resets risk like a new night at the casino. After a bad run, take a real break.
- **Parts:** (1) judge your own streak, and your analysts' (S004); (2) up 20 or 30 percent means you play with house money (S005); (3) down means shrink; (4) a clean break clears the head (the 2000 sabbatical, after which he put on a 350 percent ten-year equivalent Treasury position and made about 40 percent in the fourth quarter, S004, S026).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to set size, given the investor's own recent record.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = big size with a hot hand and a loved thesis; 5 = normal size; 0 = big size while cold or to get back to even.
- **Quote:** "One of my number one jobs, is to know whether I'm hot or cold. And when I'm hot, I'm supposed to turn the dial way up." (S004)
- **Also:** "January 1 has always been a great resetting of risk for myself." (S005)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S005, S026).

## 23. Size to the market, not the fund

- **What it is:** cap a position by what it would cost to get out, not by the fund's capital. Liquidity is what lets him change his mind fast.
- **Parts:** (1) exit cost if wrong (no more than 1 to 2 percent of the fund in execution alone, S005); (2) depth of the market; (3) fund size cap at about $10bn (S005). The cautionary tale is the 1996 rupiah position: so big against the market that he froze and lost over $1bn (S005).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on small and mid caps, emerging markets and any name where the planned size is large against daily volume.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = can exit the full position quickly at low cost; 5 = exit takes days; 0 = position large enough to freeze you if wrong.
- **Quote:** "I would never get so big in something that I thought I couldn't get out where it would cost the fund maybe 1-2% in execution cost alone to get out." (S005)
- **Also:** "currencies and bonds trade 24 hours a day and they’re very liquid and you can change your mind" (S013)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S010, S013).

## 24. Theme and dominoes (concentric circles)

- **What it is:** start from one big theme, then trade the consequences that fall from it. In 1992 he made more in gilts, MATIF bonds and UK equities than in the pound short itself (S005).
- **Parts:** (1) the core theme; (2) first-order trade; (3) the dominoes: rates, equities, related currencies.
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to find the stock that is a second-order winner of a macro theme, often cheaper and less crowded than the obvious one.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the stock is a clear domino of a theme he holds with conviction; 5 = loosely linked; 0 = no link to any theme.
- **Quote:** "You get a theme and then you look at the concentric circles or the dominoes that fall because of a theme." (S026)
- **Sources:** 2 (S005, S026).

## 25. Capacity cycle mean reversion *(unnamed; his first coverage, chemicals)*

- **What it is:** in cyclical industries, losses force capacity out and margins later recover; high profits bring capacity expansions. Buy the money losers, avoid the over-earners. He applied the same supply logic to oil in 2015 (S009) and shorted COVID over-earners (S004).
- **Parts:** (1) where the industry sits in its profit cycle; (2) capacity being cut or announced; (3) margins two to three years out.
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on commodity, chemical and other cyclical stocks.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = industry losing money with capacity shrinking; 5 = mid cycle; 0 = over-earning with expansions announced.
- **Quote:** "when companies are losing money, capacity is always going to shrink and their margins are gonna look better in three years." (S004)
- **Sources:** 2 (S004, S009).
