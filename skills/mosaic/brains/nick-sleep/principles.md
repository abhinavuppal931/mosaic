# Principles: Nick Sleep

Built from the Nomad Investment Partnership letters (S001 to S024, 2002 to 2013) and the IGY Foundation pieces (S026 to S031, 2021). Decision rules, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## 1. Do not sell a great compounder early; that error costs more than any bankruptcy.

- "the biggest error an investor can make is the sale of a Wal-Mart or a Microsoft in the early stages of the company's growth." (S012)
- "our greatest error was the sale of Stagecoach (which has risen ever since sold), not the purchase of Conseco!" (S012)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S007, S009, S012, S013, S016, S017, S026). Confidence: high.
- Note: he sold Stagecoach at about 90p after buying at 14p and watched it pass £2.50 (S013); the fund that sold IBM and Wal-Mart is his standing cautionary tale (S007, S016).

## 2. Judge an investment by its destination over many years, not by the ride.

- "if we could turn U$1 into U$16, does it matter if it takes 18 years or 22 years?" (S009)
- "A rational mind understands that it is the destination that is important" (S011)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S004, S005, S006, S007, S009, S010, S011, S017, S019). Confidence: high.
- Measure on rolling five years at the least; he made partners sign that the fund was unsuitable for shorter horizons (S009, S017, S018).

## 3. Back businesses that share their scale with customers, and prefer earning less for longer to harvesting.

- "far better, Zak argues, to earn less, but for a much longer time." (S022)
- "Scale economics shared incentivises customer reciprocation, and customer reciprocation is a super-factor in business performance." (S015)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S003, S007, S008, S012, S015, S016, S018, S022). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S008, S022: a firm that raises prices once rivals fail, or tilts the split toward shareholders to please Wall Street, is draining its own moat.

## 4. Concentrate in the few things that are knowable; more stocks raise risk rather than lower it.

- "In our opinion, just a few big things in life are knowable. And it is because just a few things are knowable that Nomad has just a few investments." (S016)
- "owning more stocks does not lower risk but raises it!" (S016)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S006, S007, S012, S013, S016, S017, S018). Confidence: high.

## 5. Back owner-oriented, founder-led managements whose character resists short-term temptation.

- "There are only two reasons companies behave well. Because they want to, and because they have to." (S006)
- "the character of a firm, call it the ability to resist locker room temptation, is far more important than first we realised." (S017)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S004, S006, S012, S014, S017, S022, S023). Confidence: high.
- By 2008 almost ninety percent of Nomad sat in firms run by founders or the largest shareholder (S014), an outcome he says he did not plan (S012).

## 6. When the price falls and the thesis holds, buy more, and buy the great businesses on fire sale.

- "For those shares that have fallen we have bought more." (S002)
- "If you did not buy the companies you always wished you had owned when they were on fire sale over the last few years then, when exactly are you going to buy them?" (S022)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S002, S003, S007, S014, S015, S017, S022). Confidence: high.
- In late 2008 the partners were the second and third largest subscribers when Nomad reopened (S015).

## 7. Once you own the right businesses, the work is not acting.

- "the decision not to do something is still an active decision; it is just that the accountants don't capture it." (S022)
- "it requires daily decisions not to sell the shares as well!" (S017)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S016, S017, S022, S023). Confidence: high.
- Changed view: in 2003 to 2005 he sold holdings at full value and recycled the money (S004, S005). From 2009 (newer) he called this inactivity a source of value added (S023).

## 8. Pay well below value; price and capital allocation are the two prime determinants of outcome.

- "The prime determinants of outcome are price (sticking to 50 cents on the dollar) and capital allocation by management." (S005)
- "as prices rise prospective returns go down." (S005)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S006, S016). Confidence: high.
- Later he warned that tight price to value thinking narrows the range of outcomes too much (S013, 2007), so for a compounder he pairs price with destination (principle 2).

## 9. A great business can stay cheap for decades; do not let a high trailing multiple end the analysis.

- "The shares of great companies can therefore be cheap, in some cases, for decades." (S016)
- "The runway ahead for our businesses may be very long indeed." (S023)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S007, S015, S016, S019, S023). Confidence: high.
- He calls price to earnings, price to book and EV to Ebitda heuristics that tell you nothing about value (S007).

## 10. Accept lower margins or free cash flow today when the spending widens the moat.

- "In effect the company had been punished by the markets for being sensible." (S002)
- "You can bank on us being the buyer." (S002)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S002, S007, S009, S011). Confidence: high.
- Applied to Costco's low margins (S007) and Amazon's growth spending and price givebacks (S011).

## 11. Value a business on the free cash flow it will produce over its life, not on reported earnings.

- "a business is worth the free cash flow that it can be expected to generate between now and judgment day, discounted back at a reasonable rate. Period." (S007)
- "This matters not a jot to us, note again: cash flow is unchanged." (S002)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S002, S007, S020). Confidence: high.

## 12. Update the thesis as facts change and try to kill your own companies.

- "The analytical mistake in both cases was to have a static view of a firm formed at the time of purchase, which failed to evolve as the facts changed." (S013)
- "Routinely we try to kill our companies (they can be killed!)" (S022)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S008, S013, S022). Confidence: high.
- In 2005 he discarded two of his own beliefs this way: that a low share price beats a high one, and that high inside ownership is always good (S008).

## 13. Sell when you can no longer value the business with confidence, even at no gain.

- "we are no longer confident of valuing Monsanto with any certainty and the prudent thing to do was to sell our shares for approximately the price we had paid." (S002)
- "the stock's biggest attraction, its apparent cheapness, becomes the investor's Achilles heel" (S014)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S002, S014, S017). Confidence: high.
- Monsanto (a missed indemnity, 2002) and MBIA (unknowable losses and share count, 2008) are the two cases.

## 14. Value is per share: know the share count before you value, and size modestly where dilution is possible.

- "one cannot place a valuation on the business per share without knowing the number of shares outstanding" (S014)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S002, S004, S014). Confidence: high.

## 15. Risk is misanalysing the destination or losing value per share, not price volatility.

- "transient, historic, stock price quotation volatility is not the same as investment risk." (S013)
- "as we define risk: the permanent loss of value per share" (S014)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S013, S014, S016). Confidence: high.

## 16. Never mark emotions to market; ignore quarterly earnings, indices and the news.

- "never mark emotions to market" (S004)
- "next quarter's earnings are worthless after next quarter." (S021)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S004, S012, S014, S015, S020, S021). Confidence: high.

## 17. Good investing is a minority sport: do not own what everyone is chasing, and accept lagging in booms.

- "Good investing is a minority sport, which means that in order to earn returns better than everyone else we need to be doing things different to the crowd." (S011)
- "we don't own what everyone else is chasing" (S014)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S002, S009, S010, S011, S012, S014). Confidence: high.

## 18. Size to conviction; his own record says positions started too small.

- "The logical extension of this line of thought is that Nomad’s portfolio concentration has at times been too low." (S006)
- "we have probably made the mistake of a starting holding that was almost certainly too low." (S012)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S006, S007, S012, S016). Confidence: high.

## 19. Use no leverage, shorting or derivatives.

- "If we ever write to you asking for permission to invest in any of the above, sell your shares." (S005)
- "Rather our results have been achieved the old-fashioned way, through buying securities in reasonable businesses at discounted prices." (S003)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S003, S005, S017, S028). Confidence: high.

## 20. Hold cash when nothing fits, and keep slack, but do not hoard.

- "we are in no hurry to invest the money in companies that do not meet our criteria." (S001)
- "It is, however, an argument for a cash buffer, just in case, a little slack in the system." (S015)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S001, S003, S015). Confidence: high.

## 21. Admit mistakes openly and study them more than successes.

- "Investment mistakes are inevitable and indeed to some extent desirable" (S003)
- "In our original analysis we missed the indemnification" (S002)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S002, S003, S005, S009, S013). Confidence: high.
- Selling to hide an analytical mistake is wrong; being in the wrong stock is an analytical mistake, not a liquidity one (S009).

## 22. Prefer managements that own their mistakes; self-inflicted problems are fixable.

- "what is required is a rational, honest mea-culpa and a low share price." (S013)
- "If the problems are self-inflicted, they are usually within the powers of the firm to fix." (S014)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S013, S014, S020). Confidence: high.

## 23. A low take-out bid is theft; own enough to influence the outcome.

- "being taken out at a low-price amounts to theft." (S008)
- "The best defense is to own enough of the company to influence the outcome." (S005)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S005, S006, S008, S011). Confidence: high.
- His working threshold was more than about 10% of the shares (S005, S011).

## 24. Do not assume a large company must slow down.

- "the fact that a firm is quite large already does not necessarily tell you that its growth rate is set to slow." (S019)
- "although some online retailing firms may be quite large, they may also be quite young." (S019)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S015, S019). Confidence: medium.

## 25. Look for an advantage built from many small things and a cost culture present from the start.

- "an interlocking, self-reinforcing network of small actions may be more successful than one big thing." (S018)
- "Good things follow when you care about the pennies." (S022)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S018, S022). Confidence: medium.
- Changed view: he dismissed this idea for about twenty years before June 2010 (S018).

## 26. Weigh information by how long it stays true, and filter news by what it does to the customer relationship.

- "the information that Zak and I weigh most heavily in thinking about a firm is that which has the longest shelf life" (S021)
- "does any of this make a meaningful difference to the relationship our businesses have with their customers?" (S020)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S020, S021). Confidence: medium.

## 27. Keep investing simple and high level.

- "It is terribly, terribly simple, but it is not easy." (S020)
- "in the long run, the high level is all that matters." (S024)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S013, S016, S020, S024). Confidence: high.
