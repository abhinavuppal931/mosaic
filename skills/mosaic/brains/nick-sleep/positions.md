# Positions: Nick Sleep

Built from the Nomad Investment Partnership letters (S001 to S024, January 2002 to December 2013) and the IGY Foundation pieces (S026 to S031, 2021), through the verified batch notes. Each stance carries the date of the letter it comes from. Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. Topics are ordered by how many sources carry them, with the seat's own question (runway and reinvestment) placed first.

## 1. Runway: how big can it get, and does size mean slowing
Sources: 7 (S003, S012, S016, S019, S020, S022, S023).
- 2002-12: Sized Costco's runway from its densest home market: 21 stores in Washington State, about 2% of the US population, implied room for about 1,000 US stores against 284 open, or about 13 more years at 10% growth (S003).
- 2007-06: A business that can grow from mouse to elephant needs self-funded growth, a moat that widens with size, and a simple building block that scales. "we want a skeletal structure that can support growth from mouse to elephant without too much skeletal re-engineering." (S012)
- 2009-06: Great compounders can look expensive and still be cheap for a very long time. "The shares of great companies can therefore be cheap, in some cases, for decades." (S016)
- 2011-06: Rejects the habit of building a slowdown into every model. "the fact that a firm is quite large already does not necessarily tell you that its growth rate is set to slow." (S019) and "regression to the mean begins the moment the analyst picks up their pen" (S019)
- 2013-06 (newest): His holdings were young when bought (about fifteen years old on average), so the runway is long. "The runway ahead for our businesses may be very long indeed." (S023)

## 2. Holding period and turnover
Sources: 15 (S001, S002, S004, S006, S009, S010, S011, S012, S014, S015, S016, S017, S018, S022, S023).
- 2002-06: Short holding periods elsewhere are the opening. "Rich pickings, we suspect, for the patient." (S002)
- Older view, 2004 to 2006: an expected holding period of about five years, about 260 weeks against 20 weeks for other holders (S006, S011, paraphrase). Three trades a month in 2006 (S010).
- 2008-06: "The average holding period for the US stocks held in Nomad (excluding Berkshire Hathaway) is fifty-one days!" (S014) He expected to hold about twenty-five times longer.
- 2009-12: The second way to invest is to buy one great business and keep deciding not to sell. "it requires daily decisions not to sell the shares as well!" (S017)
- Newer view, 2012-12 to 2013-06: Inactivity is the method. "We have, broadly, the businesses we want in Nomad and see little advantage to fiddling." (S022) and "Our portfolio inaction continues and we are delighted to report that purchase and sale transactions have all but ground to a halt." (S023)
- Changed: from a roughly five-year holding (2004 to 2006) to holding great businesses with no planned exit (2009 onward). The newer view is the one he ended with.

## 3. Concentration versus diversification
Sources: 13 (S001, S002, S003, S006, S007, S009, S011, S012, S013, S016, S017, S018, S020).
- Older view, 2001 to 2004: 18 to 30 holdings, largest under 9%; he then judged this too timid. "The logical extension of this line of thought is that Nomad’s portfolio concentration has at times been too low." (S006)
- 2004-06: Diversification serves the seller of the fund, not the owner. "stocks are not like children. The more stocks you own the less you care about each one individually." (S006)
- 2007-12: Diversification does not touch the real risk. "the only real, long term risk, which is the risk of misanalysing a company's destination." (S013)
- Newer view, 2009-06: Twenty investments, ten about 80%, about 30% in one (Amazon). "In our opinion, just a few big things in life are knowable. And it is because just a few things are knowable that Nomad has just a few investments." (S016) and "owning more stocks does not lower risk but raises it!" (S016)
- 2011-12: "long-term investments in the shares of, for all that matters, ten companies, all paid for with cash." (S020)

## 4. Moats: scale economics shared
Sources: 12 (S003, S007, S008, S011, S012, S014, S015, S016, S018, S020, S021, S022).
- 2002-12: Costco returns purchasing scale to customers through a fixed markup. "This is retail’s version of perpetual motion and has been widely employed by Wal-Mart among others." (S003)
- 2004-12: "Most companies pursue scale efficiencies, but few share them. It’s the sharing that makes the model so powerful." (S007)
- 2008-12: The loop runs through the customer. "Scale economics shared incentivises customer reciprocation, and customer reciprocation is a super-factor in business performance." (S015)
- 2009-06: About sixty percent of the Partnership sat in this model (Costco, Amazon, Berkshire, Carpetright, AirAsia). "It works because it turns size, normally an anchor to growth and returns, into an asset." (S016)
- 2011-12: "the moat surrounding the firm deepens as the firm grows." (S020)
- 2012-12: After rivals fail, lower prices again rather than collect rent. "far better, Zak argues, to earn less, but for a much longer time." (S022)

## 5. Management, founders and incentives
Sources: 11 (S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S012, S014, S015, S017, S022, S023).
- 2002-06: Bad pay plans bend the accounts. "management, plump with stock options and growth in earnings per share related bonuses, were strongly incented to recognise earnings up front" (S002)
- 2003-06: The core questions on character. "what are returns on incremental capital and the longevity of those returns, are management correctly incented to allocate capital appropriately, and what is discounted by prices?" (S004)
- 2004-06: "There are only two reasons companies behave well. Because they want to, and because they have to." (S006) He prefers those that want to.
- 2007-06 and 2008-06: Over eighty-five, then almost ninety, percent in founder or largest-shareholder run firms, an outcome he did not plan. "Almost ninety percent of the portfolio is invested in firms run by founders or the largest shareholder" (S014)
- 2008-12: Judge any pay scheme by its effect on the destination. "the incentives are helpful if they raise the probability of a favourable destination for our investee firms." (S015)
- 2009-12: Character matters more than he first thought. "the character of a firm - call it the ability to resist locker room temptation - is far more important than first we realised." (S017)
- 2013-06: Money is not the main motive of the founders he backs. "The best entrepreneurs we know don't particularly care about the terms of their compensation packages" (S023)

## 6. Index and benchmark
Sources: 11 (S001, S004, S006, S011, S014, S016, S017, S018, S021, S022, S023).
- 2002-01: "Nomad is an absolute return fund and reference here to an index is simply to place our performance, short term as it is, in context." (S001)
- 2006-12: "the index is not our benchmark." (S011) Treating the index as a risk-free home leads to over-diversification and opinions on everything; "it is one, of many, opportunity sets." (S011)
- 2008-06: "Index envy is not a route to outsized returns" (S014)
- 2009-06: For an investor who wants diversification, "Real diversification is offered by index funds at a fraction of the price of active management." (S016)
- 2012-06: "Zak and I spend almost no time thinking about stock market indices." (S021)

## 7. Valuation method
Sources: 8 (S001, S002, S005, S007, S011, S013, S018, S020).
- 2002-06: Cash flow, not reported earnings. "This matters not a jot to us, note again: cash flow is unchanged." (S002)
- 2004-12: "a business is worth the free cash flow that it can be expected to generate between now and judgment day, discounted back at a reasonable rate. Period." (S007) He used about a 10% discount rate and worked out what growth the price implied. On P/E, P/B and EV to Ebitda: "We all know that it does not mean a thing." (S007)
- 2006-12: Splits reported free cash flow from discretionary growth spend; at Amazon the price implied the growth spend had no value (S011).
- Older view, 2003 to 2005: the price to value ratio of the Partnership was the scorecard. "Rest assured, nothing occupies your manager more than reducing the price to value ratio of the Partnership" (S005)
- Newer view, 2007-12: destination analysis replaced a static price to value at purchase. "Destination analysis is consciously central to how we analyse businesses these days." (S013) He warned of "an over-reliance on price to value ratio type analysis, which can encourage a tighter range of outcomes than occurs in reality." (S013)
- 2010-06: "share prices are an aggregate of all possible future worlds, not the actual future" (S018)
- 2011-12: "Almost all valuations are wrong" (S020)

## 8. Learning from mistakes
Sources: 9 (S002, S003, S005, S007, S009, S012, S013, S017, S018).
- 2002-12: "Investment mistakes are inevitable and indeed to some extent desirable" (S003)
- 2003-12: Studies losers more than winners, after Bill Miller, so as not to be flattered by winners (S005).
- 2005-12 and 2007-06: The worst mistakes are sales, not bankruptcies. "our greatest error was the sale of Stagecoach (which has risen ever since sold), not the purchase of Conseco!" (S012)
- 2007-12: Names three traps: denial, anchoring and drift. "mistakes can be put on a price earnings ratio of one and the resultant, conditioned, good behaviour on a ratio of more than one." (S013)
- 2010-06: Changed his mind on "many small things" as a moat after twenty years of dismissing it. "And I think that all this time I may have been wrong." (S018)

## 9. Price to pay
Sources: 8 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S006, S016, S017, S022).
- Older view, 2001 to 2005: buy at about half of value. "Our aim is to make investments at prices we consider to be fifty cents on the dollar of what a typical firm is worth." (S005) and "as prices rise prospective returns go down." (S005)
- 2004-06: Even great businesses need a price. "Those that chase high prices today, leave less gunpowder for the future." (S006)
- Newer view, 2008 to 2012: for a long-runway compounder the growth of value swamps the entry price. "in the end, it did not matter what price he paid particularly, as the growth in underlying value made his purchase one of the best investments of all time." (S015) and "the second way to invest is to buy shares in a great business at a reasonable price and let the business grow." (S017)
- 2012-12: Buy the great businesses in a crash. "If you did not buy the companies you always wished you had owned when they were on fire sale over the last few years then, when exactly are you going to buy them?" (S022)

## 10. Growth versus value
Sources: 6 (S003, S007, S010, S016, S017, S026).
- 2002-12: "Costco is as perfect a growth stock as we have analysed and is available in the stock market at a close to half price." (S003)
- 2004-12: "The debate over growth and value is perennial, and quite unnecessary." (S007) Growth is part of value. The test: what would have stopped you owning K-Mart, and what would have stopped you selling Wal-Mart (S007).
- Older mix, 2002: franchises, asset-backed businesses and deep value workouts, with the losers in the workouts. "It is the first category that contains our current winners and the latter that contains our losers." (S002)
- Newer mix, 2009-06: "around three-quarters of the portfolio is invested in growth businesses" (S016), the rest cigar butts.
- 2021 (looking back): would not sell a great business for a cigar butt. "we were unlikely to sell any of those to fund the purchase of another cigar butt, Philippine cement company, were we?" (S026)

## 11. Emerging markets and Zimbabwe
Sources: 7 (S001, S006, S008, S009, S014, S015, S024).
- 2002-01 to 2004-12: South East Asia was the largest region, 33% to 40% of assets (S001, S007).
- 2005-06: Zimbabwe cement at a fraction of replacement cost. "so much so that a wait of ten years for normalisation would still yield wonderful results." (S008) Sized at a few percent because of confiscation risk.
- 2005-09 speech: In Asia the business elite is patient, but the trading crowd gambles, so troughs get very cheap (S009, paraphrase).
- 2008-06: On China and resources: "we don't own what everyone else is chasing" (S014)
- Newer view, 2013-12: Zimbabwe returned three to eight times in dollars, but he would not repeat it. "we can do better with the compounding businesses these days, and they are much less stressful" (S024)

## 12. Cycles, bubbles and momentum
Sources: 6 (S002, S009, S010, S013, S014, S017).
- 2002-06: "You should therefore be expecting us to do relatively well as the bubble collapses." (S002)
- 2006-06: Read prices as claims about the future. "Prices are a language" (S010) Short holding periods expose the excess: "something is up in the global capital markets when investors’ average holding period for Phelps Dodge stock, a relatively sober copper mining company, is three months." (S010)
- 2006-06: "When there is a frenzy of activity in one area of the market there is very often an anti- bubble of discarded companies." (S010)
- 2008-06: "We can all do momentum investing, but it is emotional investing and I just don't think it is that intelligent, or profitable." (S014)
- 2009-12: "it helps to be outside the bubble to see the bubble." (S017)

## 13. Bear markets and crashes
Sources: 6 (S003, S005, S014, S015, S017, S022).
- 2002-12: "During the late summer and autumn, when investors were at their most depressed, we made several new investments" (S003)
- 2008-06: "When investments go from hated and reasonable to despised and cheap something is normally up in the markets." (S014)
- 2008-12, after a 45% loss in the year: "the rational mind will anchor on the notion that today the birds in the bush are very large indeed." (S015) He reopened the fund and he and Zak were among the largest subscribers.
- 2009-12: Great businesses went on sale in the credit crisis, and he called that the investment opportunity of the period (S017, paraphrase).
- 2011-12: A weak economy helps cost leaders. "the worse it gets for the economy, the better it gets for our firms from a competitive position." (S020)

## 14. Leverage and margin
Sources: 6 (S003, S005, S015, S017, S022, S028).
- 2003-12: Never. "If we ever write to you asking for permission to invest in any of the above, sell your shares." (S005)
- 2008-12: Gearing is the symptom of no slack. He cites "fifty-to-one gearing ratios at the investment banks" (S015)
- 2009-12: Returns of about sixteen percent a year "has been achieved without leverage, derivatives, options, selling short, contracts for difference" (S017)
- 2012-12 and 2021: Prefers equity-funded businesses and a foundation that grew "without recourse to borrowings" (S028, short phrase).

## 15. Shorting, hedging and derivatives
Sources: 5 (S003, S005, S007, S014, S017).
- 2002-12: "Rather our results have been achieved the old-fashioned way, through buying securities in reasonable businesses at discounted prices." (S003)
- 2003-12: "Nomad is not a hedge fund, it is an Investment Partnership" (S005)
- 2008-06: On short sellers attacking holdings: "what the shorts are really testing is the shareholders' conviction" (S014)

## 16. Cash
Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S003, S006, S015).
- 2002-01: Held about 28% cash at the start. "we are in no hurry to invest the money in companies that do not meet our criteria." (S001) but "holding cash does not meet our long-term investment goals" (S001)
- 2002-12: Cut cash from over 20% to about 5% in the autumn 2002 lows (S003).
- 2008-12: Cash carries an option on future bargains. "one could rationally argue that cash is worth more than cash." (S015) and "It is, however, an argument for a cash buffer, just in case, a little slack in the system." (S015)

## 17. Market timing and forecasting
Sources: 5 (S002, S003, S004, S014, S020).
- 2003-06: "we do not have the faintest idea what share prices will do in the short term" (S004)
- 2003-12: "the only event we control is whether we are right, not when we are right." (S005)
- 2008-06: He controls misanalysis of the big things, "not spotting wiggles on the path along the way. Everyone else does wiggle guessing." (S014)

## 18. Macro, the Fed, interest rates and politics
Sources: 3 (S015, S020, S023).
- 2008-12: "I doubt that worrying is the solution to anything particularly: far better planning." (S015)
- 2011-12: Lists macro as noise: "news items, the soap opera of the stock market, macro-economic events, politics, currencies, interest rates" (S020)
- Rates: his only use of rates is the 6% hurdle, set near long bond yields of the time (S023, paraphrase). No recorded view on the Fed.

## 19. Activism, takeovers and minority rights
Sources: 7 (S005, S006, S008, S009, S011, S013, S014).
- 2004-06: "To stand by and watch whilst we are having our wallet lifted makes little sense to us." (S006)
- 2005-06: "being taken out at a low-price amounts to theft." (S008)
- 2006-12: Management buyouts are "insider trading with the takeover rules being used as a bulk purchasing facility" (S011) He aims to own more than 10% in the UK to block low bids (S011).
- 2007-12: Stays invested to influence rather than divest. "being inside a company is far more likely to influence behaviour than being outside." (S013)

## 20. Technology and the internet
Sources: 5 (S007, S011, S012, S015, S019).
- 2004-12: "In our opinion a business such as eBay could be the most valuable in the world." (S007)
- 2006-12: Amazon's growth spend and price cuts create value even when reported free cash flow dips (S011). "there is no guarantee that investment spending will always work." (S011)
- 2008-12: "Amazon has been priced in the market as if it would not grow in the future" (S015)
- 2011-06: "although some online retailing firms may be quite large, they may also be quite young." (S019)
- AI: no recorded position.

## 21. Buybacks and dividends
Sources: 4 (S003, S005, S008, S014).
- 2002-12: Applauds buybacks of debt and shares at depressed prices: "management score highly for gusto, and their equity holders, of which Nomad is one, should applaud such actions." (S003)
- 2005-06: "the correct response to a six-year share price famine is for the company to use its balance sheet to buy back shares at prevailing prices" (S008)
- 2008-06: 55% of holdings were buying back shares or had meaningful insider buying (S014). Dividends: no general position beyond liking Matichon's 9% yield at a trough (S001).

## 22. Share issuance, options and dilution
Sources: 3 (S002, S004, S014).
- 2003-06: "the dripping tap of share issuance is rarely turned off." (S004) He screened for firms with no change in share count over ten years.
- 2008-06: "Dilution risk occurs when companies issue equity at less than the firm is worth." (S014) and "one cannot place a valuation on the business per share without knowing the number of shares outstanding" (S014)

## 23. Reinvestment versus current free cash flow
Sources: 4 (S006, S011, S015, S020).
- 2004-06: Shrinking can be right where returns are poor; ask a firm with poor economics why it wants to grow (S006, paraphrase).
- 2006-12: Willing to give up current free cash flow if the spending widens the moat (S011, paraphrase); the market at the time rewarded high current cash flow instead (S011).
- 2011-12: The test is cash-in and cash-out: Nomad's firms grew revenue over thirty percent a year with return on capital over twice competitors. "Look, son, at the end of the day it is all about cash-in, and cash-out" (S020, quoting a bank CFO)

## 24. Advertising and capitalised investment
Sources: 3 (S009, S016, S030).
- 2005-12: Advertising, R&D and product quality are expensed, so the accounts hide value; capitalise them in your own analysis (S009, paraphrase).
- 2009-06: Quotes Bezos: "Advertising is the price you pay for having an unremarkable product or service" (S016)
- 2021: His firms were "eschewing public relations, advertising, and sometimes, heaven forbid, investor relations" (S030)

## 25. Accounting quality
Sources: 4 (S002, S007, S009, S020).
- 2002-06: "It is important to note that whether a transaction is deemed an operating lease or a sale-type lease, cash -flow is unaffected." (S002) Read the notes to the accounts, where he missed the Monsanto indemnity.
- 2011-12: Where there is no market, value at zero rather than invent a number. "to assign a positive value would be just as arbitrary" (S020)

## 26. Risk and volatility
Sources: 4 (S013, S014, S016, S017).
- 2007-12: "the biggest risk in investing is the risk of misanalysis." (S013) and "transient, historic, stock price quotation volatility is not the same as investment risk." (S013)
- 2008-06: Risk is "the permanent loss of value per share" (S014)
- 2009-12: Concentration makes the fund volatile; he warned partners that permanent impairment of capital is possible (S017, paraphrase).

## 27. Quarterly earnings, news and reporting
Sources: 6 (S001, S007, S012, S018, S020, S021).
- 2002-01: "more regular reports, daily, weekly, monthly or otherwise, are likely to be of little value to you, and may even be counterproductive for us." (S001)
- 2011-12: Filter news by one question: "does any of this make a meaningful difference to the relationship our businesses have with their customers?" (S020)
- 2012-06: "next quarter's earnings are worthless after next quarter." (S021) and "the information that Zak and I weigh most heavily in thinking about a firm is that which has the longest shelf life" (S021)

## 28. Bonds and credit
Sources: 3 (S003, S004, S006).
- 2002-12: Bought bonds at unwarranted discounts when US spreads hit records (S003).
- 2003-06: Bought Lucent 8% convertible preferred at about 35 cents on the dollar and sold near full value (S004). About 10% in distressed corporate bonds in mid 2004 (S006). No later bond holdings recorded.

## 29. Fees and the fund industry
Sources: 6 (S006, S009, S010, S021, S023, S024).
- 2004-06: "at Nomad, no performance means no fees." (S006)
- 2006-06: "the management fee should not be a profits centre" (S010) The fee fell toward ten basis points by 2013 (S024).
- 2013-12: Active management in aggregate "does not add value per se: it only shuffles wealth created elsewhere." (S024)

## 30. Taxes
Sources: 1 (S024).
- 2013-12: A policy view, not a portfolio rule: "if you really want to change industry behaviour, I'll whisper this, tax short term investing." (S024) No recorded position on tax management in his own portfolio.

## 31. Inflation
Sources: 1 (S024).
- 2013-12: Zimbabwe holdings rose three to eight times in dollars through hyperinflation, market suspension and nationalisations (S024, paraphrase). No recorded general position on inflation.

## 32. IPOs
No recorded position. In 2006 he noted a boom in small-company privatisations but gave no rule (S010).
