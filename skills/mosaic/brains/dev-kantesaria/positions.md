# Positions: Dev Kantesaria

Built from 8 sources (S001, S006, S007, S008, S018, S019, S020, S021; July 2019 to December 2024) through the verified batch notes. Each stance carries its source date. Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. The seat's own question (pricing power and the price to pay) comes first, then topics by how many sources carry them. S006 is a reporter's write-up, so its unquoted points are paraphrase of the reporting.

## 1. What price to pay and how to value
Sources: 6 (S006, S007, S008, S018, S019, S021).
- 2019-10: buy at least 30% below the free cash flow yield the business deserves, set against the 10-year Treasury. At the time Moody's traded at a low-4% yield on 2021 estimates, FICO about 3.5%, Visa about 4%, Intuit 4.2%, against a 1.7% risk-free rate, justified by low-teens to mid-teens growth. "Growth like that can make a stock less expensive relatively quickly." (S008)
- 2021-10: "we'll buy them up front at a significant discount, generally 30 to 60 percent below what we think they're worth." (S019) "So we want to see a large gap with the risk-free rate." (S019)
- 2024-12 (newest): precision is not the point. "So this idea of bringing great precision to valuation, I think is a silly exercise because at the end of the day, we're trying to predict the future." (S018) Forward multiples mislead for high organic growers and special cases such as FICO's price increases (S018, S021): "If you look at some of our companies and you look at their forward multiples, they seem expensive, but those multiples are, in my view, inaccurate." (S021)

## 2. Pricing power and inflation
Sources: 6 (S006, S007, S018, S019, S020, S021).
- 2019-07: Moody's as the model: "Moody's has strong pricing power it can consistently raise its prices above the rate of inflation, strong operating leverage its margins increase with higher revenue" (S006)
- 2021-10: pricing power companies such as Moody's and S&P carry inflation-plus pricing (S019, paraphrase).
- 2024-12: "So if inflation goes to 10%, you want a company that can raise its prices 13%." (S018)
- 2024-11 (newest): "We think the best way as an equity investor to protect against risks like inflation or even deflation is to own companies with pricing power." (S021)

## 3. Concentration and position count
Sources: 5 (S006, S007, S008, S019, S021).
- 2019-07 to 2019-10: 8 to 12 names, 1 to 3 new ones a year (S006, S008). "By the time we get out to our 15th-best idea, we're not really happy with the risk/reward proposition." (S008)
- 2021-10: large caps make 8 to 12 workable because he can exit in days. "We're looking for one to three great ideas a year" (S019)
- 2022-12: 8 to 12 is a range, 14 is fine, mid-teens is the ceiling (S007, paraphrase).
- 2024-11 (newest): "we make few decisions, but when we make them, they're big." (S021)

## 4. Cash and market timing
Sources: 6 (S006, S007, S008, S018, S019, S021).
- Older view, 2019-10: "Since 2007 we have averaged around 19% cash, not because we're market timers but because we only put cash to work when great ideas present themselves." (S008)
- 2021-10: cash once up to 20%, now low single digits; "And we know that market timing doesn't work." (S019)
- 2022-12: under 1% cash. "You are catching a falling knife, and you don't need to catch the knife perfectly at the bottom" (S007)
- Newer view, 2024-11 and 2024-12: about 1%, range 0 to 5%. "Over the long term, holding cash is a drag on returns." (S021) "if you own a great business, you should be fully invested." (S021)
- Changed: from waiting in cash for ideas (average 19% to 2019) to staying fully invested in compounders (2021 onward). The newer view is the current one.

## 5. Interest rates and the Fed
Sources: 5 (S006, S007, S008, S018, S019).
- 2019-10: "If we have the prolonged period of low interest rates that we expect, people are going to be willing to pay higher prices for equities and equity multiples should expand." (S008)
- 2021-10: "Equity multiples are dynamic." (S019) The curve to watch: "the one curve that I think people should follow is the S&P 500 earnings yield relative to the 10-year treasury" (S019)
- 2022-12: "the real focus needs to be on where long-term interest rates are going to be and not where interest rates are going to be for the next year or two." (S007)
- 2024-12 (newest), after the 2022 to 2023 rate rise: "The way we look at things is that we're in a low range, and we expect that range to go even lower." (S018)

## 6. Macro, politics and regulation
Sources: 5 (S006, S007, S008, S018, S019).
- 2021-10: "We're not prognosticators on those macroeconomic factors." (S019)
- 2022-12: Fed meetings, inflation prints and elections: "Those are meaningless in the long term." (S007) On regulation: "it doesn't ultimately matter if you own a very high-quality business." (S007)
- 2024-12: no link between GDP growth and stock returns: "there's no direct correlation between those two." (S018)

## 7. Technology, big tech and AI
Sources: 6 (S007, S008, S018, S019, S020, S021).
- Older view, 2019-10: excluded Google over capital allocation and founder control. "We're looking for predictable and efficient uses of free cash flow, and we just don't see that in Google" (S008)
- 2021-10: called the purism an error and owned Amazon. "But it's been, I think, an error for us to be such purists about some of these other characteristics of these companies when the business models have been so good." (S019)
- 2022-12: Amazon kept as an outlier for its optionality. "Amazon is an outlier for its capital intensity" (S007)
- Newer view, 2024-11 and 2024-12: no big tech; AI helps them for a few years, then commoditizes and pushes them into capex. "We owned a little bit of Amazon along the way." (S021) "it's our view that AI becomes commoditized, and then it becomes difficult for these companies to monetize their AI offerings." (S018) "It doesn't have the predictability that we like to see." (S021) He expects AI to lift his own holdings' margins for about five years (S021) and chose ASML as the most predictable AI exposure (S021).
- Changed: big tech went from excluded (2019) to a regretted omission with one holding (2021 to 2022) to excluded again on AI capital intensity (2024, newest).

## 8. Healthcare, biotech and high R&D
Sources: 4 (S007, S008, S018, S019).
- 2019-10: "That lack of long-term visibility makes the risk/reward proposition uninvestable for us." (S008)
- 2021-10: "People assume that Valley Forge is full of health care names. It turns out that Valley Forge has no health care." (S019)
- 2022-12: "they are not predictable enough for our standards" (S007)

## 9. Management, incentives and capital allocation
Sources: 6 (S007, S008, S018, S019, S020, S021).
- 2019-10: "Whenever management changes we worry primarily about whether there's going to be a fundamental shift in capital allocation." (S008)
- 2022-12: management matters less in mature businesses. "I think they're less important for the mature businesses that we like to invest in." (S007)
- 2024-12: dual-class shares, pet projects and acquisition sprees are red flags (S018, S021, paraphrase). "So my preference is a company that holds virtually no cash because it keeps them from making mistakes." (S018)

## 10. Buybacks and dividends
Sources: 4 (S007, S008, S018, S020).
- 2019-10: steady repurchases, not timed ones. "We don't think management should try to time the market quite as much as it does in buying back stock." (S008)
- 2024-12 (newest): buybacks ahead of dividends, which are tax-inefficient. "buying back stock remains the most efficient use of capital." (S018)

## 11. Stock-based compensation
Sources: 2 (S007, S019).
- 2021-10: "We actually calculate our free cash flow yields for our company after the cost of buying back stock that is issued in terms of compensation." (S019)
- 2022-12: "We also run our free cash flow models, netting out the cost of compensation." (S007)

## 12. Holding period and turnover
Sources: 5 (S006, S007, S018, S019, S021).
- 2019-07: 10-plus year horizon (S006, paraphrase).
- 2022-12: 10 to 30 year view. "The 10 businesses we own today are the same 10 businesses that we owned in January of 2020" (S007)
- 2024-12: "But I don't need a traded share price every day." (S018)

## 13. Index funds and active management
Sources: 4 (S007, S018, S019, S021).
- 2022-12: "you can go out and buy an S&P 500 index fund and that will be a better outcome for you than trying your hand at active investing." (S007)
- 2024-12: "for 99% of people, an index fund is a beautiful thing." (S018) "it is my belief that the vast majority of the public equity management industry is simply random statistical noise minus fees." (S018)

## 14. Bubbles, IPOs and SPACs
Sources: 3 (S007, S020, S021).
- 2022-12: "this sort of cryptocurrency, SPAC, IPO period over the last couple of years was just another speculative bubble." (S007) "I think there is this idea that if a company goes down 80% it has to bounce back, and that's not the case." (S007)
- 2023-05 and 2024-11: of roughly 2,000 new listings, none matched FICO's model (S020, S021, paraphrase).

## 15. Gold, crypto and commodities
Sources: 4 (S007, S008, S018, S019).
- 2019-10: "We also don't view cryptocurrencies as a credible threat." (S008), speaking of Visa and Mastercard.
- 2022-12: "We know that gold bars can't produce free cash flow and the same thing to cryptocurrencies." (S007)
- 2024-12: "I wish there were easier ways to make money. I wish I could trade gold bars. I wish I could predict where the price of oil was going to go." (S018)

## 16. Foreign stocks, emerging markets and China
Sources: 4 (S007, S008, S018, S019).
- 2019-10: "We much prefer to get our international exposure through U.S. multinationals rather than buying foreign companies directly." (S008)
- 2022-12: "our foreign investments have been the most work with the least amount of gain." (S007)
- 2024-12: of India and emerging markets: "the business models are almost entirely commoditized." (S018)

## 17. Shorting, hedging and derivatives
Sources: 3 (S006, S008, S019).
- Older view, 2019-07: "the fund may short opportunistically," (S006)
- 2019-10: "we don't employ any of the complicated and usually ineffective financial engineering you often see used by our hedge-fund peers." (S008)
- Newer view, 2021-10: five shorts in 14 years, one in March 2020 on a medical information edge. "Shorting is almost always a bad idea." (S019) Deep in-the-money LEAPs twice (eBay, Nike): "And it was a lot of work and risk for not much gain." (S019)

## 18. Leverage
Sources: 2 (S006, S019).
- 2021-10: "we don't use leverage at our at the fund level, although we have the ability to do so." (S019) Excess company debt is a sell reason (S019).

## 19. Cycles, crashes and bear markets
Sources: 5 (S007, S008, S018, S019, S021).
- 2019-10: down about half as much as the S&P 500 in 2008. "Companies that generate predictable and growing free cash flow usually have a natural floor to their share prices." (S008)
- 2022-12: of the COVID cyclical rebound: "we know how the story ends it will end badly" (S007) "So we're playing offence right now when others are fearful." (S007)
- 2024-12: "we take comfort in owning companies and industries where we know that the volume drop is temporary." (S018)

## 20. Growth versus value
Sources: 3 (S007, S018, S019).
- 2021-10: "I would say that 98% of our peers in value investing focus on cigar butt stocks." (S019)
- 2022-12: rejects the split; quality needs both volume and price growth (S007, paraphrase).

## 21. Activism
Sources: 2 (S018, S019).
- 2021-10: "But no, we prefer private conversations for now." (S019)
- 2024-12: wants to shake boards that lower quality through deals. "That's an unforced error." (S018)

## 22. Private markets and the public equity foundation
Sources: 2 (S001, S021).
- 2024-11: "I think allocators gravitate towards private equity, private credit, because there's not a mark to market for these funds." (S021) "But I believe that any great portfolio's foundation has to start with a public equity foundation." (S021)

## 23. Learning from mistakes
Sources: 4 (S007, S008, S018, S019).
- 2024-12: "In the second category, generally, the mistakes of omission have far higher magnitude than the type 1 mistakes." (S018) "So you have to be very careful about your mistakes." (S018)

## No recorded position
Bonds and credit as an asset class for his own book, taxes beyond tax efficiency of low turnover and buybacks (S018, S019), and specific sizing rules beyond a roughly 10% position built in days (Monster, S008).
