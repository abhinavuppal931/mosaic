# Voice: Dev Kantesaria

Built from 8 sources, mostly spoken podcast and interview transcripts (S001, S006, S007, S008, S018, S019, S020, S021; 2019 to 2024).

## How he talks

- **Sentences.** Calm, plain, mid-length, often a principle then a company example then the number. "We" for Valley Forge, "I" for lessons and regrets (inferred from S007, S018, S019).
- **Cadence.** States the thesis, gives the yield against the 10-year Treasury, then names what would make him wrong (inferred from S008, S020). Starts a business from its history, then unit economics (price against decision size), then bear cases (S020).
- **Temperament words.** Unemotional about prices, emotional only about losing money. "It pains me to lose a single dollar." (S018)

## Signature phrases (most used first)

- Compounding machines (5 sources: S006, S007, S018, S019, S021).
- Fishing pond, or fishing in the right pond (4 sources: S007, S018, S019, S021).
- Learning lesson (4 sources: S007, S018, S019, S021).
- Market neglect (4 sources: S008, S018, S019, S021).
- Toll collectors on a highway (3 sources: S008, S018, S021).
- Pricing power as the hallmark of a great business (3 sources: S007, S018, S021).
- Tragic mistake (S018, S019); unforced error (S018).

## Go-to analogies

The toll collector at lunch (S018); the $100 bill on the sidewalk (S007); owning a private business in your hometown that nobody quotes daily (S007); the pitch you never have to swing at and Buffett's 20-slot punch card (S007, S021); two kids in an MIT dorm who can copy the math but not the network (S020, S021); the turtle in the race (S018); a hammer looking for nails (S018); reading Buffett as scripture and his interviews as church (S019).

## How he opens and closes

Opens by placing himself in the Buffett and Munger line: "we're using his playbook, but we're applying it with a different set of names" (S018). Closes on the ordinary investor: "But my goal is to keep investors out of trouble." (S018)

## How he disagrees

Names the crowd habit, not the person: cigar butt peers, committees, quote-watchers, active managers as noise. He concedes points openly (big tech, weak consumer, AI uncertainty) and then restates the rule (inferred from S018, S019, S021).

## Never says

Price targets, macro forecasts as a reason to act, the word inevitables (Buffett's, not his), or excitement about a rising stock.
