# Selling: Dev Kantesaria

Built from 8 sources (S006, S007, S008, S018, S019, S020, S021; 2019 to 2024) through the verified batch notes. His rules for selling, trimming, adding and re-checking. One gap: his fullest answer on selling in S007 sits behind a paywall and is not captured.

## Rules

### 1. Sell when business quality falls, not when the price does.
Lower quality, worse competitive dynamics, misuse of free cash flow, acquisitions, or too much company debt are his exit reasons. "if our company were to take on an excessive amount of debt, that could be another reason that we exit a position." (S019) Monster in 2019 is the worked case: competition cut future pricing power, so he sold an excellent business (S008).
Sources: 3 (S008, S018, S019).

### 2. An acquisition spree is a sell signal.
Asked directly whether acquisitions would make him sell, he said yes: "capital allocation is absolutely something that can ruin a great business." (S018) He worries most when a new CEO arrives: "Whenever management changes we worry primarily about whether there's going to be a fundamental shift in capital allocation." (S008)
Sources: 3 (S007, S008, S018).

### 3. At full value, holding must be justified; the older rule was to exit in full.
Older view, 2019-10: "we exit a position in its entirety when it reaches our estimate of intrinsic value." (S008)
Newer view, 2021-10: "But when something reaches 100% of our intrinsic value, we have to justify, you know, why they are still in the portfolio." (S019) By 2024 he would not sell a compounder because its multiple drifted a few turns, say 26 to 29 times, since value growth overwhelms it (S018, paraphrase). No partial trims are recorded.
Sources: 3 (S008, S018, S019).

### 4. The easiest sale is a swap into something better.
"I would say for us, the easiest decision is for exiting company is when we find something that we like better." (S019) Equifax was sold for FICO (S008). In sell-offs he upgrades: "it can be an opportunity to trade out of a lower-quality name for a higher-quality name." (S007)
Sources: 3 (S007, S008, S019).

### 5. A falling price on an intact thesis is a reason to add.
"if there are large dips that we believe are unjustified we generally look to buy more." (S008) "We might buy another 13, 14, 15 times along the way when the market is not properly assessing intrinsic value." (S021) Cyclical volume drops count too: "It often gives us a buying opportunity to buy more into these businesses" (S018)
Sources: 5 (S007, S008, S018, S020, S021).

### 6. Do not read flat or volatile prices as a signal.
Moody's went flat from 2015 to 2017 and he held (S019, paraphrase). "Near-term share price volatility doesn't bother us at all." (S020) "But the idea of looking at your portfolio day-to-day, week-to-week is a very silly notion." (S007)
Sources: 3 (S007, S019, S020).

### 7. Low turnover is an outcome of quality, not complacency.
"So the lack of turnover is really nothing other than a natural outcome of how we think about the business quality" (S007) "We think about our companies every day, even the ones that we've held for 15 or 17 years, about subtle changes to business quality, both up or down." (S018)
Sources: 3 (S007, S018, S019).

### 8. No emotional attachment, either way.
"There's no emotional attachment." (S018) "We don't get happy when the stock price goes up. We don't get sad when it goes down." (S019)
Sources: 3 (S007, S018, S019).

### 9. Selling too early is accepted as the price of discipline.
Names he sold, such as Monster and Nike, often kept rising, which he reads as proof the pond is right, not that the sale was wrong. "So for us, it's confirmation that we're still fishing in the right pond." (S019) He accepts that lines in the sand cause misses (S007).
Sources: 2 (S007, S019).

### 10. Large-cap liquidity keeps the exit cheap.
"We can sell out of a position entirely in a couple of days or a few days and switch into another position." (S019)
Sources: 1 (S019).

## Kill criteria this seat would write
- The company announces or begins a growth plan built on acquisitions outside its core franchise (S007, S018).
- Price increases slow to inflation or below, or are used to mask falling volume (S008, S018).
- A credible rival takes share without a cost penalty for the customer, for example issuers getting equal pricing on bonds without a Moody's or S&P rating, or lenders and securitizers accepting another score at no spread (S008, S020).
- Net debt rises to an excessive level relative to free cash flow (S019).
- Free cash flow after stock compensation stops growing while reported free cash flow rises (inferred from S007, S019).
- New CEO shifts capital allocation away from buybacks toward deals or pet projects (S008, S018).
- He can no longer say the company stays dominant for 10 more years (S018).

## Re-check triggers
- Every quarter: update intrinsic value and buy price on owned and shortlist names (S019).
- Each earnings release, filing and analyst day on the 15 to 20 shortlist names (S007, S008).
- A management change (S008).
- Any acquisition announcement (S007, S018).
- A regulatory, legal or antitrust ruling, such as the FHFA score decision or the Durbin Amendment (S007, S020).
- A 40% to 50% drop on a scare: check whether it changes 10-year economics, and buy if not (S021).
- A position reaching 100% of intrinsic value (S019).
- A new technology threat, such as AI credit models or AI unemployment hitting the consumer (S020, S021).

## Admitted selling mistakes
- 2021-10: sold Monster and Nike, both kept rising (S019).
- 2021-10 and 2024-12: the larger cost was never buying big tech, an omission rather than a bad sale (S019, S018).
