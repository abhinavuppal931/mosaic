# Principles: Dev Kantesaria

Built from 8 sources (S001, S006, S007, S008, S018, S019, S020, S021; 2019 to 2024) through the verified batch notes. Decision rules in plain sentences, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## 1. Never trade business quality for a cheap price.
"You should never sacrifice business quality." (S007) "we don't believe in buying a low-quality business and fixing it up." (S019) Cigar butts rarely improve: "These companies generally lack organic growth, and their business quality rarely improves, even in the hands of strong management," (S006)
Sources: 4 (S006, S007, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

## 2. Do not time the market; there is no bad time to buy a compounding machine.
"there really is no bad time to buy a compounding machine." (S019) "For us, market timing has been a poor way to generate returns over the long term." (S018)
Sources: 4 (S006, S007, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

## 3. Capital allocation can veto an otherwise great business.
"capital allocation is absolutely something that can ruin a great business." (S018) "Many companies are prone to making defensive acquisitions and so we penalise the companies for those acquisitions." (S007)
Sources: 6 (S007, S008, S018, S019, S020, S021). Confidence: high.

## 4. Own only what is predictable for at least 10 years; if it can fall 90%, it cannot be held concentrated.
"If you invest in companies that could go down 90% in value, you can't run a concentrated portfolio with that lack of predictability." (S007)
Sources: 4 (S007, S008, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

## 5. Count stock compensation as a real cost; measure free cash flow after it.
"We also run our free cash flow models, netting out the cost of compensation." (S007) "When you're giving out a large amount of restricted stock and then claiming that you have a large buyback, you're essentially running in place." (S019)
Sources: 2 (S007, S019). Confidence: medium.

## 6. Know your buy price at all times, so you can act in days when a great business goes on sale.
"But no, we know at any given moment what price we want to buy our favorite companies at." (S019) "There are a handful of situations that can arise in a given year where a great business goes on sale." (S008)
Sources: 5 (S007, S008, S018, S019, S021). Confidence: high.

## 7. Buy at a wide discount to value, but do not chase precision.
"we'll buy them up front at a significant discount, generally 30 to 60 percent below what we think they're worth." (S019) "if you buy a great compounding machine, over the long term, that type of intrinsic value growth overcomes whether you've overpaid by 10 or 15 percent" (S018)
Sources: 5 (S006, S007, S008, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

## 8. Avoid high R&D, commodity-linked, rate-linked and capital-intensive businesses.
"we avoid things that have high R&D risk." (S019) "That lack of long-term visibility makes the risk/reward proposition uninvestable for us." (S008)
Sources: 4 (S007, S008, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

## 9. Pricing power is the best hedge against inflation, deflation and macro.
"We think the best way as an equity investor to protect against risks like inflation or even deflation is to own companies with pricing power." (S021)
Sources: 4 (S007, S018, S019, S021). Confidence: high.

## 10. Never feel forced to buy; a pass or a side burner is a fine answer.
"you never have to swing at a pitch." (S007) "we are perfectly happy putting something on the side burner." (S007)
Sources: 1 (S007). Confidence: low.

## 11. Set lines in the sand for buying and selling, and accept the misses they cause.
"you have to have a specific line in the sand for buying and selling" (S007)
Sources: 2 (S007, S019). Confidence: medium.

## 12. Prefer businesses that do not need a star manager, and use management meetings only for facts.
"We want to own businesses as Warren Buffett says that your idiot nephew could run." (S007) "Meeting management is a double-edged sword." (S007)
Sources: 2 (S007, S008). Confidence: medium.

## 13. Do not underwrite optionality or sum-of-the-parts; value the core you can predict.
"we don't add it to any of our modelling or expectations." (S007) "we don't really believe in some of the parts at our firm," (S020)
Sources: 2 (S007, S020). Confidence: medium.

## 14. Every company has a bear case; name it before you buy.
"There's always a bear case for any company." (S020)
Sources: 2 (S008, S020; inferred from his habit of naming what would make him wrong in S008). Confidence: medium.

## 15. Doing nothing is usually right once you own the right businesses.
"the main learning lesson is that sometimes the best thing to do is to do nothing." (S007) "If there's a lot of turnover in your portfolio, either you're speculating or maybe your decision making isn't of the highest quality." (S019)
Sources: 3 (S007, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

## 16. Decide in a small team, not a committee.
"I don't believe in having an army of analysts that meet every Monday to discuss ideas." (S019) "There's also a lot of group decision making involved, and I'm not a fan of that." (S008)
Sources: 3 (S007, S008, S019). Confidence: high.

## 17. Prefer moats from industry structure or technology, not from scale.
"TSMC's advantage today comes from really scale. And that is not a advantage that we prefer." (S021) In 2022 he said none of the 50 pond names wins by size or volume (S007).
Sources: 2 (S007, S021). Confidence: medium.

## 18. Use common stock and no leverage; skip financial engineering.
"We will generally default to common stock positions." (S019) "we don't use leverage at our at the fund level, although we have the ability to do so." (S019)
Sources: 3 (S006, S008, S019). Confidence: high.
