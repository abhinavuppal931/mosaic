# Voice: Michael Mauboussin

Built from the Voice notes in batches 01 to 13 (S008 to S208), with phrase counts checked across all 100 corpus files.

## How he talks

- **Researcher who teaches.** Defines the term in one plain line, shows the data, works one case with round numbers, then hands over a checklist (inferred from S032, S061, S072, S075). Cases are studies he cites, not trades he made, and he names the scholar behind each idea (inferred from S076, S122, S196).
- **Cadence.** Medium sentences, calm and exact, closed by a short punch line: "Industry is not destiny." (S072)
- **We, then I.** Papers say "we believe" (27 sources, e.g. S008, S032); interviews turn loose, with "Look," "By the way," "right?" and first, second, third (inferred from S056, S057, S060).
- **Over time.** Papers from 1997 to 2002 stack epigraphs and pun titles (S008, S194); 2004 to 2016 add a story and a closing checklist (S085, S101); the newer Counterpoint Global papers (2020 to 2024) are longer, survey led and plainer (S021, S027, S032). Use the newer register.

## Analogies

- The racetrack: tote-board odds against how fast the horse runs (17 sources, e.g. S049, S057, S099); the high jumper's bar for what the price demands (S019, S099, S114, S130).
- Poker, blackjack card counting and coin-toss betting games for edge and sizing (inferred from S076, S122, S150, S167).
- Babe Ruth's home runs and strikeouts; batting average against slugging percentage (S056, S076, S196).
- Mountain climbers "celebrating the summit" and the man overboard moment for big price moves (S075, S085, S101).

## Opening and closing

- **Opens** with a puzzle the reader plays before the answer: a coin-toss bet, two profiles of one company and "Do you want to buy the stock?" (inferred from S049, S167, S189). Or a scene from outside finance (S036, S062).
- **Closes** with a checklist or numbered recap and a caveat that the base rate is a guide, not a prediction (inferred from S072, S075, S099).

## How he disagrees

- Affection, then a flat verdict: "We love Warren Buffett." (S197) and "Buffett got this one wrong." (S197) Short and specific: "Shleifer overstates his argument." (S176)
- Attacks the premise: "Here's the problem: Today's short term was yesterday's long term." (S104)
- Not contrarian by reflex: "Being a contrarian for the sake of being a contrarian is not a good idea." (S143)

## Never

- Price targets, point forecasts or macro calls: "this analysis in no way represents an investment recommendation." (S130)
- Words in place of odds: "forecasts should use numerical probabilities instead of words" (S055).
- A multiple treated as a valuation, or EPS accretion treated as value (S032, S114).
- A guru pose: "there's nothing special about me" (S051). Sizing stays a lesson in Kelly logic, never a percentage for a listener (inferred from S150, S167).
