# Selling: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from the six verified batch notes over 56 sources (papers 2012 to 2026, testimonies 2009 and 2011, interviews and transcripts 2010 to 2025). Taleb gives no price-based sell rules and no stock calls. His exits are structural: cut what can ruin you, cap the loss in advance, and re-check payoff shape, leverage and hidden short options rather than the quote. Most-used first. Where a rule is read across from policy or risk papers to one position, it says "inferred".

## Rules

### 1. Cut an exposure that can ruin you now; do not wait for evidence
Sources: 7 (S001, S003, S017, S029, S037, S047, S048).
Evidence of a rare event arrives after it. "By definition, evidence follows and never precedes rare impactful events." (S017) and "In the case of ruin, by the time evidence comes it will by definition be too late to avoid it." (S003) So act while it is cheap. "If you must panic, panic early." (S037) "When it is not expensive to panic, in any situation, it must be done." (S048) "if you realize that something is fragile, immediately, do something about it." (S047) Applied to finance by him: "Like in finance and anything. You get out now." (S037) Wanting more data is the delay fallacy (S017).

### 2. Cap the loss in advance; the stop on the whole book matters most
Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S004, S035).
Set the maximum loss before the event, at portfolio level, then be agnostic about the rest. "In a nutshell, we hardwire the curtailments on loss but otherwise assume maximal uncertainty about the returns." (S004) "We note that the stop loss plays a larger role in determining the stochastic properties than the portfolio composition." (S004) The cap must be enforceable through insurance, safe assets or an executed stop, since "parametric VaR is known to be ineffective as a risk control method on its own." (S004) A stop that is not executed accurately changes the result (S004, paraphrase).

### 3. If you cannot bound the loss, change the payoff or get out
Sources: 6 (S001, S013, S029, S035, S044, S011).
You cannot fix the distribution, only your exposure to it. "it is not possible to change the distribution; but it is possible to change the payoff" (S035) "So the idea is to exit the fourth quadrant." (S035) "Or, alternatively, try to move your exposure type to make it third-quadrant style" (S029) When the loss cannot be bounded: "Better stay home." (S001) If small changes in an estimated tail move the answer a lot, the position is concave and should be cut or hedged (inferred from S011, S013).

### 4. Leverage or debt that cannot survive a forecast error is a reason to exit
Sources: 6 (S029, S031, S035, S053, S054, S055).
"If you are leveraged, errors blow you up; if you are not, you can enjoy life." (S029) The holder who cannot survive a wrong forecast should not hold the position (S031, paraphrase): "The one who borrows will go bust if he makes a mistake." (S031) Re-check any holding by its debt: "Name a company that is successful and let's look at its debt history" (S054) Leverage is the hard number he trusts over model risk figures (S055).

### 5. A falling price is not the trigger; fragility is
Sources: 4 (S001, S038, S046, S054).
He gives no price-triggered sell rule; the checks are payoff shape, leverage and hidden short-volatility exposure (S001). With protection in place, falls are for buying. "And I like to buy after dips" (S038) and the hedge "allows you to buy when nobody has dry powder." (S038) He judges a holding by its stress history: "like a stock that bounces back, goes through hell and comes back, generally have given us information about how much heat they can sustain." (S054) Headlines about volatility do not change the strategy (S046).

### 6. A big move outside the model means refit the tails fatter, not explain it away
Sources: 5 (S001, S011, S013, S016, S032).
"If we see a 20 sigma event, we can rule out that the distribution is thin-tailed." (S032) "any future parameter uncertainty should make tails fatter, not thinner." (S001) "Accordingly, the future needs to be dealt with as having thicker tails than what was measured in the past." (S011) "The higher the estimation variance, the lower the true exponent." (S013) After a shock, re-run any risk figure with an added layer of error and see how fast the tail moves (inferred from S011, S016).

### 7. Steady returns and calm periods are a reason to look for the hidden tail, not to add
Sources: 8 (S001, S007, S018, S029, S035, S037, S051, S055).
"In general, the data will show thinner tail than it should." (S001) "Do not confuse absence of volatility with absence of risks." (S029) "Steady returns (continuous ratification) comes along with hiding tail risks." (S051) Risk grows with visible success: "It is a representative of risks that keep increasing in the absence of losses, until the explosive blowup." (S007) He was on the receiving end of this check himself in 1987: "You made too much money, you've got to be taking risk." (S037) A fall in reported VaR is not a fall in risk (S055, paraphrase).

### 8. Run the perturbation test: losses that accelerate mean exit or hedge
Sources: 4 (S002, S032, S037, S041).
Shock the key input at several sizes. "Should the exposure show negative asymmetry (worse at -20% than it improves at -10%), we deem that their risk increases in the tails." (S002) Fannie Mae was the case: loss grew about 20 times as rates moved 200 basis points, so "They're sitting on a barrel of dynamite" (S037) Harm that "accelerates with intensity, up to the point of breaking" is the fragile signal (S032). Treat a rate or financing input as uncertain, not fixed (S041, paraphrase).

### 9. Judge the hedge with the insured; never drop insurance for its cost
Sources: 5 (S001, S021, S037, S046, S047).
"One should never calculate the cost of insurance without offsetting it with returns generated from packages than one would not have purchased otherwise." (S021) "you've got to look at insurance versus the insured." (S037) When stocks rally and the hedge bleeds, the package still did its job: "the stock market rallied, which means that not only did the hedge do okay, but it wasn't needed." (S046) Skip insurance only if you can sleep with a total loss, which he says most cannot (S047, paraphrase).

### 10. A hedge that depends on correlation or continuous trading is not a hedge
Sources: 4 (S004, S020, S037, S051).
"So there was a dependence on correlation." (S037), said of a client firm that went out of business. "Correlations are unstable in an unstable way" (S004) "Under the class of fat tailed distributions, increasing the frequency of the hedges does not guarantee reduction of risk." (S020) Prefer protection already bought and static over one you must keep adjusting (S020, paraphrase).

### 11. Remove fragility first: via negativa before adding anything
Sources: 4 (S003, S037, S047, S029).
"The first thing to be antifragile is first, you have to eliminate fragilities." (S037) "It is often better to remove structure or allow natural variation to take place rather than to add something additional to the system." (S003) His advice is what to avoid: "You can live longer if you avoid death, get better if you avoid bankruptcy, and become prosperous if you avoid blowups in the fourth quadrant." (S029)

### 12. Re-check the people: no skin in the game, discount them
Sources: 5 (S007, S029, S036, S047, S053).
"The solution is to ask, and only take into account, what the predictor has done" (S007) "any business where you're judged by your peers and not by some contact with reality is gonna rot eventually" (S036) A manager on a bonus with no clawback is betting on hidden risks: "Is optimal to make series of bonuses betting on hidden risks in the Fourth Quadrant, then blow up and write a thank you letter." (S029) Own-money investors keep tail insurance; paid managers drop it (S047, paraphrase).

### 13. Size and stacking: cut when risks add up or the position gets big
Sources: 5 (S001, S003, S032, S037, S053).
"No risky exposure can be analyzed in isolation: risks accumulate." (S032) With correlated sources, "the total exposure needs to be reduced." (S003) "As connectivity increases the risk of extinction increases dramatically and nonlinearly" (S003) Size itself costs you in a squeeze: "So you need to be small, as I said, and you need to be diversified." (S053)

### 14. Change the action when the test fails, keep the process
Sources: 2 (S047, S048).
Bitcoin was his case: he owned some, tested it as currency, inflation hedge and refuge, exited and later went short. "So I realized quickly that I made a mistake with Bitcoin." (S047) "They don't realize that I'm not selling a recipe. I'm selling a process." (S047)

### 15. Selling too early or too late: survival outranks timing
Sources: 2 (S001, S038).
He does not discuss taking profits early or late. The point he makes is that a blowup cancels the record: "anything that will eventually go bust will lose all past profits." (S001) Selling to cash has its own cost: "The risk of having a lot of cash is that if the market rallies" (S038)

## Kill criteria this seat would write
Checkable facts that would end a thesis, in his terms (inferred from S001, S002, S007, S029, S031, S035, S037, S047, S053, S054, S055 unless noted).
- A single plausible event can take the position, or the fund, to zero or force a sale at the bottom (the uncle point) (S038, S003).
- Loss accelerates under the perturbation test: a 20% shock hurts much more than twice a 10% shock (S002, S037).
- Leverage or debt rises to where a forecast error, not just a bad outcome, wipes out equity; debt is refinanced at rates above what growth can carry (S031, S046, S053).
- Income turns out to come from selling tails: steady earnings with a hidden short option, covered writes or short volatility (S007, S037).
- The hedge depends on a correlation, on continuous rebalancing, or on a proxy such as a VIX product rather than real tail options (S020, S021, S037).
- A long-dated, low-yield asset holds no upside and all the rate downside, as at SVB (S047).
- Management has upside without downside, cuts redundancy for margin, or is paid annual bonuses on risks that take years to show (S035, S053, S055).
- A zero-yield asset with no residual value shows any path to an absorbing barrier (S006).
- The thesis needs a point forecast of a fat-tailed variable to be right (S017).

## Re-check triggers
- Any move beyond four or five standard deviations in a model: refit the tails fatter, distrust the model, not the move (S001, S032).
- Long calm spells or unusually smooth returns: look for the hidden tail and the rising exposure behind them (S007, S029, S051).
- Changes in leverage, debt maturity or interest rates, including rates moving through 4% for heavy debtors (S031, S046, S047).
- Rising connectivity or correlation across holdings, or a hedge that has started to depend on correlation (S003, S037).
- A risk figure built from a past sample: re-run with error on the error before trusting it (S011, S016).
- Bonus, ownership or clawback changes at managers and funds you rely on (S007, S036, S055).
- Not news flow: he does not change a hedging strategy because volatility headlines pile up (S046).

## Selling mistakes he admitted, with dates
- Before 2021, admitted 2023: bitcoin. He owned it and "wrongly thought bitcoin would be a bulwark against the distortions of this monetary policy." (S048) He exited after testing it and later went short (S047).
- No other selling mistake is on record in these sources. His other admitted errors are about ideas, not exits: "I made some mistakes in Fooled by Randomness in my thinking about mass hysteria." (S038)
