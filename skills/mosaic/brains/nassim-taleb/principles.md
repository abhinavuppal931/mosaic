# Principles: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from 56 free sources, 2008 to 2026 (technical papers, Edge essays, congressional testimony, lectures, interviews, podcast transcripts and INCERTO and Substack posts). The Incerto books are copyrighted and not quoted. Decision rules, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.


## 1. Avoid leverage; debt turns a forecast error into ruin, while equity lets you survive it.

- "If you are leveraged, errors blow you up; if you are not, you can enjoy life." (S029)
- "I'm not against credit. I'm against leverage." (S031)
- Supporting sources: 11 (S001, S029, S031, S035, S045, S046, S047, S053, S054, S055, S056). Confidence: high.
- He allows credit tied to a completed commercial transaction and debt for emergencies (S031); his target is leverage, including leverage used to cure leverage (S045, S055).

## 2. Survive first: take any risk you like, but never one that can end the game.

- "Take all the risks you want, but make sure you're there tomorrow." (S036)
- "my work is first about avoiding tail risk. Basically, if you want to do well, you must first survive." (S047)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S001, S003, S009, S029, S032, S036, S038, S047, S048). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S001, S003, S036: on any decision, the ruin check comes before return, valuation or conviction, and a failed ruin check is not offset by a high expected return.

## 3. Trust only advice from people with skin in the game; ask what they hold, not what they think.

- "The solution is to ask, and only take into account, what the predictor has done" (S007)
- "you should never tell people what they should be doing. You should tell them what you're doing" (S036)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S001, S007, S025, S036, S037, S038, S047, S051, S054). Confidence: high.
- He applies it to himself: he hesitates to talk about investments he does not have (S054), and says forecasters who claim actionable skill should be rich (S001, S025).

## 4. Distrust steady returns and low volatility; they often mean a short option is hidden in the tail.

- "Do not confuse absence of volatility with absence of risks." (S029)
- "Companies that have steady income are short an option somewhere." (S037)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S001, S007, S029, S035, S037, S046, S051, S055, S018). Confidence: high.
- Funds with impeccable records were the ones that went bust in 2008 to 2009 (S001), and the best pre-crisis track records were the most vulnerable (S025).

## 5. No upside without downside: do not back managers who keep the gains and pass on the losses.

- "No incentives without disincentives: capitalism is about rewards and punishments, not just rewards." (S045)
- "Removing the agency problem on the part of bank managers and staff who have upside and no downside." (S035)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S007, S031, S035, S036, S039, S045, S053, S055, S029). Confidence: high.
- Citi's Robert Rubin, by his account about 120 million dollars of pay over ten years and kept after 2008, is his standing example (S036, S053, S055).

## 6. Do not size or approve a position with Sharpe, VaR, standard deviation, beta or correlation.

- "Beta, Sharpe Ratio and other common hackneyed financial metrics are uninformative." (S001)
- "VaR is charlatanism because it tries to estimate something that is scientifically impossible to estimate, namely the risk of rare events. It gives people a misleading sense of precision." (S055)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S001, S002, S004, S011, S015, S029, S035, S051, S055). Confidence: high.
- Use hard measures instead: leverage, the maximum loss, and how losses accelerate under bigger shocks (S002, S055).

## 7. Judge by payoff, not by being right; it is fine to be wrong often if losses are small and gains large.

- "Life is about payoffs, not forecasting, and the difference increases in Extremistan." (S001)
- "One can be wrong very frequently if the cost is low, so long as one is convex to payoff" (S001)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S005, S007, S011, S030, S035, S044, S054). Confidence: high.
- A good binary hit rate can be a reverse indicator when payoffs are skewed (S005); Morgan Stanley called the credit crisis and was still badly hurt because it missed the size (S035).

## 8. Do not act on point forecasts in fat-tailed domains; identify who is fragile instead.

- "Forecasting single variables in fat-tailed domains is in violation of both common sense and probability theory." (S017)
- "I can't predict black swans, but I can predict who is vulnerable to black swans." (S053)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S017, S029, S035, S046, S053, S054, S047). Confidence: high.
- He refuses to name the trigger or the date (S046, S053); the work is on the structure of the exposure.

## 9. Never use an average or a sample mean to size a fat-tailed risk; size to the extremes.

- "Never cross a river that is 4 feet deep on average" (S017)
- "For fat-tailed variables, the mean is almost entirely determined by extremes." (S033)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S008, S014, S017, S018, S032, S033, S047). Confidence: high.

## 10. Remove fragility before seeking upside: subtract first, keep slack, do not optimize.

- "The first thing to be antifragile is first, you have to eliminate fragilities." (S037)
- "Avoid Optimization, Learn to Love Redundancy." (S029)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S003, S029, S035, S037, S045, S047, S053, S054). Confidence: high.
- If you realize something is fragile, act on it at once (S047).

## 11. More uncertainty calls for less exposure and earlier action, not for waiting on more data.

- "More uncertainty in a system makes precautionary decisions more obvious." (S017)
- "If you must panic, panic early." (S037)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S003, S017, S029, S036, S037, S048, S054). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S003, S017: doubt about a model widens the left tail, so a user who says we cannot know the downside has argued for a smaller position, not a larger one.

## 12. Change the exposure, not the forecast: cap the loss so the tail cannot reach you.

- "it is not possible to change the distribution; but it is possible to change the payoff" (S035)
- "focus on changing payoffs to clip tail exposures" (S013)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S002, S004, S013, S029, S035, S044). Confidence: high.

## 13. A clean record so far is not evidence of safety when ruin is possible.

- "Lack of ruin due to several or even many trials does not indicate safety from ruin in the next one." (S003)
- "On the 1001st day, the turkey has a surprise." (S029)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S003, S014, S029, S033, S035, S050). Confidence: high.
- A mitigation that worked is not proof it was unneeded (S050).

## 14. Prefer firms financed by equity, with slack, over levered and optimized firms with slightly higher earnings.

- "The one who borrows will go bust if he makes a mistake. The one who issues equity will fluctuate but will be able to survive a forecast error." (S031)
- "a company that can withstand storms can be inferior to one that is fragile to the next slight downturn or rise in interest rates" (S051)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S029, S031, S051, S053, S054, S055, S047). Confidence: high.
- He says public listing pushes firms to cut redundancy (S053), and blames SVB's failure on long bonds with no upside, not on credit (S047).

## 15. Prefer what has survived a long time over what is new.

- "Things that have worked for a long time are preferable" (S029)
- "an old technology has a huge survival advantage over new technology" (S036)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S006, S029, S031, S035, S036, S049). Confidence: high.
- Limit he states: "It doesn't work for all domains." (S036)

## 16. Analyze every risk together with all the others; risks add up and correlations are unstable.

- "No risky exposure can be analyzed in isolation: risks accumulate." (S001)
- "Correlations are unstable in an unstable way" (S004)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S003, S004, S032, S037, S051). Confidence: high.
- A client who bought S&P puts and sold German index puts to fund them was ruined when the correlation broke (S037).

## 17. Never sell uncapped insurance or far out of the money options where catastrophe is possible.

- "you should never write an uncapped insurance contract if there is a risk of catastrophe." (S001)
- "They were correct in the prediction, but went bust nevertheless." (S001)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S007, S021, S030, S032, S037). Confidence: high.
- Niederhoffer sold options for about 5 cents and had to buy them back at up to 38 to 40 dollars, which bankrupted Refco (S001, S021, S037).

## 18. Judge a hedge together with the portfolio it protects, never on its own cost.

- "One should never calculate the cost of insurance without offsetting it with returns generated from packages than one would not have purchased otherwise." (S021)
- "You don't look at insurance as a tradable thing." (S046)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S021, S037, S038, S046, S047). Confidence: high.
- The hedge must pay in the real tail: "It is an error to mistake the VIX for tail events." (S021)

## 19. Diversify broadly and equally rather than optimize, unless the bet is a deliberate small convex one.

- "Likewise, portfolio diversification needs to be larger, much larger than anticipated." (S035)
- "The 1/n heuristic mitigates the risk of suffering ruin due to an error in the model; there is no single asset whose failure can bring down the ship." (S003)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S003, S015, S030, S035, S047). Confidence: high.
- His tension, 2023: "So if you buy into a lot of companies, you lose that skewed attribute." (S047) Inferred from S030, S047: diversify the core broadly, and keep the convex sleeve as many small positions rather than an index.

## 20. Assume tails are fatter than measured; blame the model, not the world, for a 10-sigma event.

- "Accordingly, the future needs to be dealt with as having thicker tails than what was measured in the past." (S011)
- "if someone tells you there is a ten-sigma event, it is much more likely that they have the wrong distribution than it is that you really have ten-sigma event." (S032)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S011, S013, S016, S022, S032). Confidence: high.

## 21. Hardwire a maximum loss for the whole book and stay agnostic about everything else.

- "In a nutshell, we hardwire the curtailments on loss but otherwise assume maximal uncertainty about the returns." (S004)
- "We note that the stop loss plays a larger role in determining the stochastic properties than the portfolio composition." (S001)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S002, S004, S035, S038). Confidence: high.
- His caveat: a stop is only as good as its execution in a gap, so a floor built by structure (numeraire share or bought protection) is sturdier (S004).

## 22. Value cash flow, not an exit promise; an asset with no yield and any chance of hitting zero is worth zero.

- "for me an investment was something that generates cash flow." (S037)
- "Earnings-free assets with no residual value are problematic." (S006)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S006, S037, S047, S048). Confidence: high.
- How his view changed: before 2021 he owned bitcoin and hoped it would guard against monetary distortion: "I wrongly thought bitcoin would be a bulwark against the distortions of this monetary policy." (S048) Newer view (2021 to 2023): worth zero, and he went short (S006, S047).

## 23. Ordinary investors should mind their own business and not trade or use options.

- "unless you're a trader, don't trade." (S037)
- "I don't recommend individual investors use options." (S038)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S037, S038, S045, S055). Confidence: high.
- "Citizens should experience anxiety about their own businesses (which they control), not their investments (which they do not control)." (S045)

## 24. Do not demand certainty before protecting against a severe, asymmetric downside.

- "When in doubt, use what protection you can." (S052)
- "You need evidence of safety, not evidence of lack of safety" (S029)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S024, S029, S052, S017). Confidence: high.

## 25. Hold a valuation view loosely in either direction; protection matters more than whether the price looks high.

- "If the market delivers a crazy valuation, it can deliver a crazy valuation in any direction." (S038)
- "you don't have a tail-hedge protection, then your returns are virtually going to be zero" (S038)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S038, S046, S047). Confidence: high.
- He likes to buy after dips (S038), and the point of protection is to buy when nobody has dry powder (S038).

## 26. Keep the protection protocol fixed through the news cycle; discipline beats cleverness.

- "We didn't change strategy for a second. And we will not change strategy." (S046)
- "in more than two decades, I never saw him once deviate a micro-inch from a given protocol." (S051)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S046, S047, S051). Confidence: high.
- He says Universa's 2023 positioning was identical to 2013 (S047).

## 27. Change the action when the evidence changes, but keep the process.

- "They don't realize that I'm not selling a recipe. I'm selling a process." (S047)
- "So I realized quickly that I made a mistake with Bitcoin." (S047)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S037, S047, S048). Confidence: high.
- He also changed on the covid vaccine, from wait and see to taking it after about a billion doses (S037); and he admits errors on mass hysteria in Fooled by Randomness (S038).

## 28. Antifragility is local; never use it to excuse a tail risk.

- "So just don't take the idea too far. It's very local." (S047)
- "Antifragility is something that likes volatility and likes variation, likes turmoil, likes stress, up to a point." (S031)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S009, S031, S047). Confidence: high.
- "I know, but what kills you doesn't make you stronger." (S037)

## 29. Be prudent where you are fragile and aggressive where you are robust.

- "They're typically aggressive where they should be prudent and prudent where they should be aggressive." (S053)
- Supporting sources: 1 (S053). Confidence: low.
- Inferred from S004, S038, S053: this is the barbell stated as behaviour, so the same idea has wider support through the barbell framework.
