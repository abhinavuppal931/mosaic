# Positions: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from the six verified batch notes over 56 sources (technical papers 2012 to 2026, Edge essays, two congressional testimonies, interviews and podcast transcripts 2010 to 2025, Medium and Substack posts to 2026). Each stance carries the date of its source. Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. Topics run from the seat's own question (can this blow us up, and do we gain or lose from disorder) to the general ones, most-sourced first within each group. He gives no stock calls and no price targets; nothing here is one.

Date notes: S032 is filed as 2009 but the lecture was given in January 2017. S054 carries a 2013 header but mentions Ebola and ISIS, so it is likely 2014 or later.

## 1. Risk of ruin, ergodicity and survival first
Sources: 10 (S001, S003, S005, S006, S009, S017, S032, S036, S038, S047).
- 2014-10: A small chance of ruin taken repeatedly is certain ruin. "we must consider any genuine risk of total ruin as if it were inevitable." (S003) and "Lack of ruin due to several or even many trials does not indicate safety from ruin in the next one." (S003)
- 2017 lecture (S032): "If we visibly incur a tiny risk of ruin, but have a frequent exposure, it will go to probability one over time." (S032) and "if one of us goes to the casino and on day 28 is ruined, there is no day 29." (S032)
- 2018-04: The uncle point, a forced sale, cuts you off from the market's average. "The market can have a positive expected return, and you have a negative expected return." (S038)
- 2019-03: "Take all the risks you want, but make sure you're there tomorrow." (S036)
- 2020-01: "Ergodicity in this context means that your analysis for ensemble probability translates into time probability. If it doesn’t, ignore ensemble probability altogether." (S001)
- 2020-07: Cost-benefit math fails where there is an absorbing barrier. "you cannot use naive B-school costs-benefit analyses for Russian roulette, because of the presence of an absorption barrier" (S017)
- 2023-04 (newest): "my work is first about avoiding tail risk. Basically, if you want to do well, you must first survive." (S047)
- Scope: the strict ruin rule is built for systemic risk. For one person's own farm, "whether or not he does so is generally a matter of individual preferences" (S003), though he still calls the individual fragile.

## 2. Payoff over probability: convex versus concave
Sources: 11 (S001, S005, S009, S010, S013, S029, S030, S035, S037, S044, S047).
- 2008-09: "it is the exposure (or payoff) that creates the complexity" (S029)
- 2012: "We have no control over X, some control over F(X)." (S044)
- 2012-10: When the tail cannot be estimated, change the payoff. "focus on changing payoffs to clip tail exposures" (S013)
- 2012-12: "Critically, convex payoffs benefit from uncertainty and disorder." (S030)
- 2019-07: "You do not eat forecasts, but P/L" (S005)
- 2020-01: "One can be wrong very frequently if the cost is low, so long as one is convex to payoff" (S001) and "Life is about payoffs, not forecasting, and the difference increases in Extremistan." (S001)
- 2022-09: "The presence of concavity in the tails of the distribution implies a silent risk." (S009)
- 2023-04: A convex bet still needs a positive expectation. "the number one condition, is to have positive expected return." (S047)
- 2023-09 (newest): The test in one line. "if the market goes down 10 percent, you make a million dollars; if it goes down 20 percent, you make 10 million dollars, you have convexity." (S037)

## 3. Fragility detection instead of event prediction
Sources: 7 (S002, S032, S037, S046, S053, S054, S055).
- 2009-09: Fannie Mae, warned in 2007: "Fannie Mae, when I look at its risks, seems to be sitting on a barrel of dynamite, vulnerable to the slightest hiccup." (S055)
- 2010-08: "I can't predict black swans, but I can predict who is vulnerable to black swans." (S053)
- 2012-08: Stress the input both ways and look for asymmetry. "Should the exposure show negative asymmetry (worse at -20% than it improves at -10%), we deem that their risk increases in the tails." (S002)
- 2013 (header date): "we know which companies are likely to go bust, so we can measure fragility." (S054)
- 2017 lecture: "It has to have harm that accelerates with intensity, up to the point of breaking." (S032)
- 2023-09: The dose matters more than the average. "The danger is in the dose. It's non-linearity." (S037)
- 2025-07 (newest): "it's foolish to say what color truck is going to break a fragile bridge, okay? But it is not foolish to say this bridge is fragile, it's going to break one day, don't cross it" (S046)

## 4. Leverage and debt
Sources: 10 (S001, S029, S031, S035, S039, S045, S053, S054, S055, S056).
- 2008-09: "If you are leveraged, errors blow you up; if you are not, you can enjoy life." (S029)
- 2009-04: "Such systems survive thanks to slack and redundancy; adding debt produces wild and dangerous gyrations and leaves no room for error." (S045) and "Using leverage to cure the problems of too much leverage is not homeopathy, it is denial." (S045)
- 2009-09: Leverage is a measure he trusts. "leverage is a robust measures (like the temperature, it does not change with your model), while VaR is not." (S055)
- 2010-08: "anything that has high degree of debt requires the more that you have, the more precision you need in your forecasting." (S053)
- 2011-07: "Leveraging in finance has the effect of increasing concavity to model error." (S035)
- 2013-03: Debt versus equity, the two brothers. "The one who borrows will go bust if he makes a mistake. The one who issues equity will fluctuate but will be able to survive a forecast error." (S031) and "I'm not against credit. I'm against leverage." (S031)
- 2013 (header date): "You cannot predict the event, but you can say that company with debt for example cannot sustain the stress that the company without debt would be able to go through." (S054)
- 2020-01 (newest on portfolios): "Adding leverage makes blowups certain in the long run" (S001)

## 5. Hedging, tail insurance and options
Sources: 11 (S001, S005, S020, S021, S022, S023, S037, S038, S046, S047, S051).
- 2014-01: Insurance is what let him own stocks at all. "Many investors (such as this author) would have stayed out of the market, or would not have added funds to the market, without such an insurance." (S021) He rejects VIX products as tail cover: "It is an error to mistake the VIX for tail events." (S021)
- 2014-05: Dynamic hedging fails on gaps. "In dynamic heding, missing a single hedge, or encountering a single gap (a tail event) can be disastrous" (S020)
- 2016-09: "Options far from the money (i.e. concerning tail events) increase in value with uncertainty on the variance of the distribution, as they are convex to the standard deviation." (S022)
- 2018-04: The payout is not the main point. "It's because it allows you to buy when nobody has dry powder." (S038) For individuals: "I don't recommend individual investors use options." (S038)
- 2019-08: "This shows how models claiming overpricing of tails are grossly misspecified." (S023)
- 2020-01: "One should never calculate the cost of insurance without offsetting it with returns generated from packages than one would not have purchased otherwise." (S001)
- 2021-05: "the output is simple to the outsider, the process is hard seen from the inside." (S051)
- 2023-04: "eliminating your tail risk allow you to make any mistake you want for your investments." (S047)
- 2023-09: "you've got to look at insurance versus the insured." (S037)
- 2025-07 (newest): "You don't look at insurance as a tradable thing." (S046)

## 6. The barbell, cash and the numéraire
Sources: 7 (S001, S002, S004, S021, S035, S038, S053).
- 2010-08: As an illustration of redundancy, not advice: "I'm investing 15%. I have 85% in cash." (S053)
- 2011-07: "allocating a high portion of a portfolio to T-Bills (or equivalent)" (S035)
- 2014-01: "A barbell is defined as a bimodal investment strategy" (S021)
- 2014-12: "maximal certainty/low risk on one side, maximum uncertainty on the other" (S004) and, with 80% in safe numéraire assets, "the risk of losing more than 20% is zero independent from all possible models of returns" (S004)
- 2018-04: "to have a smaller amount allocated to the most volatile things, rather than a larger amount allocated to medium-volatile things" (S038) Cash alone has a cost: "The risk of having a lot of cash is that if the market rallies" (S038)
- 2020-01 (newest): The safe side is a numéraire, not just cash. "A numéraire is defined as the unit to which all other units relate." (S001)

## 7. Conventional risk metrics: Sharpe, beta, VaR, standard deviation, correlation
Sources: 10 (S001, S002, S004, S011, S015, S029, S035, S051, S055, S007).
- 2008-09: "Conventional metrics based on type 1 randomness don't work." (S029)
- 2009-09: "VaR is charlatanism because it tries to estimate something that is scientifically impossible to estimate, namely the risk of rare events. It gives people a misleading sense of precision." (S055)
- 2011-07: "one has 99% probability of not exceeding a $1 million loss, but should such a loss occur, it can be $10 million or $100 million." (S035)
- 2012-09: "the Gaussian and its siblings (along with the measures such as STD, correlation, etc.) should be completely abandoned, along with any attempt to measure small probabilities" (S011)
- 2013-08: The coherent-risk literature is like "risk managing an airplane flight by spending resources making sure the pilot uses proper grammar when communicating with the flight attendants" (S007)
- 2014-12: "Correlations are unstable in an unstable way" (S004)
- 2018-02: "distributions deliver an unstable and uninformative second moment. Mean deviation proves far more robust there." (S015)
- 2020-01: "Beta, Sharpe Ratio and other common hackneyed financial metrics are uninformative." (S001) On the Sharpe ratio in 2008: "Yet it is still used because people can be suckers for numbers." (S001)
- 2021-05 (newest): "correlations are, if anything, not fixed, changing randomly." (S051)

## 8. Forecasting, point estimates and the forecaster's record
Sources: 10 (S001, S005, S007, S017, S025, S029, S031, S035, S053, S054).
- 2011-07: "predicting financial risks has only worked on computers so far (not in the real world)" (S035)
- 2013-03: "My view of the future is you don't have to be right, you have to have a dominant strategy to act as if you were pessimistic." (S031)
- 2013 (header date): "forecasting makes you fragile because those who forecast develop overconfidence about the future and start to engage in debt and other risky activities." (S054)
- 2019-07: "A binary forecasting record is likely to be a reverse indicator under some classes of distributions or deeper uncertainty." (S005)
- 2020-01: Direction and exposure are different things: "the market is more likely to go up, but should it go down it will fall much much more" (S001) and "if they claim to be so good at forecasting, and their forecasts are actionable and related to reality, why aren’t they so rich?" (S001)
- 2020-07: "Forecasting single variables in fat-tailed domains is in violation of both common sense and probability theory." (S017)
- 2023-01 (newest): "decision makers are usually not well equipped to deal with probabilistic information" (S025)

## 9. Concentration versus diversification
Sources: 8 (S001, S003, S015, S030, S035, S037, S047, S051).
- 2011-07: "Likewise, portfolio diversification needs to be larger, much larger than anticipated." (S035)
- 2012-12: "A large exposure to a single trial has lower expected return than a portfolio of small trials." (S030)
- 2014-10: "The 1/n heuristic mitigates the risk of suffering ruin due to an error in the model; there is no single asset whose failure can bring down the ship." (S003)
- 2018-02: "Single stocks have kappas between .3 and .7" (S015), so "one simply needs broader portfolios." (S015)
- 2020-01: "Markowitz portfolio allocation is never the best solution." (S001) and "MPT fails in both risk control and real-world investment optimization." (S001) He blames mean-variance 60/40 thinking for US state pensions falling from about 100% funded in 2001 to about 74% in 2019 (S001).
- 2021-05: Portfolio theory and risk parity "requires correlations between assets to be both known and nonrandom." (S051)
- 2023-04: For a skewed bet, too much breadth kills the skew. "So if you buy into a lot of companies, you lose that skewed attribute." (S047)
- 2023-09 (newest): "international diversification was no longer diversification." (S037)

## 10. Position sizing and loss limits
Sources: 6 (S001, S004, S032, S037, S053, S038).
- 2010-08: "So you need to be small, as I said, and you need to be diversified." (S053)
- 2014-12: "Operators are less concerned with portfolio variations than with the drawdown they may face over a time window." (S004) and "We note that the stop loss plays a larger role in determining the stochastic properties than the portfolio composition." (S004)
- 2017 lecture: "No risky exposure can be analyzed in isolation: risks accumulate." (S032)
- 2018-04: Not stated as a rule, but his own short is sized to stay small: "It's nothing sizable, just enough to make me wake up." (S038)
- 2020-01: Kelly-style sizing over variance optimization. "In practice one needs the ratio of expected profit to worst-case return dynamically adjusted to avoid ruin." (S001) He notes Kelly still needs a hard worst case and some knowledge of the mean (S001).
- 2023-09 (newest): Many small units beat one big one. "one reactor is vastly more dangerous than 10 small ones" (S037)

## 11. Track records, backtests and steady returns
Sources: 9 (S001, S007, S018, S025, S029, S035, S042, S051, S055).
- 2008-09: "Do not confuse absence of volatility with absence of risks." (S029)
- 2009-09: The blow-up pattern is "producing steady profits for a long time, collecting bonuses, then losing everything in a single blowup." (S055)
- 2011-07: "left tailed distributions will (1) overestimate the mean; (2) underestimate the variance and the risk." (S035)
- 2013-08: "And companies that have hidden risks tend to outperform others in small samples, their executives see higher compensation." (S007)
- 2016-03: For backtests and studies, "one needs a p-value of at least one order of magnitude smaller." (S042)
- 2020-01: "funds with impeccable track records were those that went bust during the 2008-2009 rout" (S001)
- 2020-04: A record with no big loss yet hides a tail: "Empirical distributions have their in-sample maxima as natural censoring." (S018)
- 2021-05: "Steady returns (continuous ratification) comes along with hiding tail risks." (S051)
- 2023-01 (newest): "trading records are not like points in soccer games, particularly when they can hide tail risks." (S025)

## 12. Selling options, short volatility and steady income
Sources: 6 (S001, S007, S018, S030, S037, S051).
- 2012-12: "in the real world, betting against long shots does not pay" (S030)
- 2013-08: "Many fund managers beat the odds by selling tails, say covered writes, by which one can increase the probability of gains but possibly lower the expectation." (S007)
- 2020-01: A firm that sold volatility in 2017 forecast correctly and still went bust. "They were correct in the prediction, but went bust nevertheless." (S001)
- 2020-04: Track records misstate "the misestimation of the true P/L of strategy with long or short volatility profile, to the pricing of options with remote strike prices" (S018)
- 2023-09 (newest): "Companies that have steady income are short an option somewhere." (S037) and "There's nothing people like more than steady income." (S037)

## 13. Banks and financial firms
Sources: 9 (S001, S007, S029, S032, S037, S046, S047, S051, S055).
- 2008-09: "Only fools (such as Banks) optimize, not realizing that a simple model error can blow through their capital (as it just did)." (S029)
- 2009-09: "Data shows that banks routinely lose everything earned in their past history in single blowups" (S055)
- 2017 lecture: "This is why, outside a crisis, the banks seem to make large profits. Then once in a while they lose everything and more and have to be bailed out by the taxpayer." (S032)
- 2021-05: "Banks lost more money in two episodes, 1982 and 2008, than they made in the history of banking" (S051)
- 2023-04: On SVB, a duration loss, not a credit loss: "So these banks were very fragile" (S047)
- 2023-09: "they blow up on the risk that bring them a tiny amount of money" (S037) He ranks hedge funds above banks because "Hedge funds have skin in the game." (S037)
- 2025-07 (newest): Banks in 2007 were net short volatility, which told him they would break without naming the trigger (S046, paraphrase).

## 14. Management, incentives and skin in the game
Sources: 11 (S001, S007, S029, S031, S035, S036, S045, S047, S051, S053, S055).
- 2009-04: "No incentives without disincentives: capitalism is about rewards and punishments, not just rewards." (S045)
- 2010-08: "One of my rules is that we should not let the manager of a nuclear plant get an incentive bonus." (S053)
- 2011-07: "Removing the agency problem on the part of bank managers and staff who have upside and no downside." (S035)
- 2013-03: "When you're a banker and you have the upside but no downside, what are you going to do?" (S031)
- 2013-08: "Counter to public perception, corporate managers are not entrepreneurs." (S007) and "Money managers do not have enough skin in the game unless they are so heavily invested in their funds that they can end up in a net negative form the event." (S007)
- 2019-03: "you cannot walk away from risks you've created for others. You should own your risks." (S036)
- 2021-05: Owners grasp tail risk faster than MBAs: "people who have skin in the game, that is, self-made successful people with their own money at risk (say a retired textile importer or a former shopping center developer), get it right away." (S051)
- 2023-04 (newest): Own-money investors buy tail insurance; paid managers skip it. "And then you have the second category, people are paid to invest." (S047)

## 15. Stock-based compensation and bonuses
Sources: 5 (S007, S035, S036, S045, S055).
No recorded position on stock-based compensation as such. On bonuses:
- 2009-04: "Bonuses do not accommodate the hidden risks of blow-ups." (S045)
- 2009-09: "This is an aberrant case of capitalism for the profits and socialism for the losses." (S055)

## 16. Corporate slack, redundancy and over-optimization
Sources: 7 (S017, S029, S035, S037, S045, S051, S053).

- 2009-04: "The complex economy is already a form of leverage: the leverage of efficiency." (S045)

## 17. Crashes, bear markets and calm periods
Sources: 8 (S001, S003, S014, S029, S032, S033, S046, S054).
- 2008-09: "Close to 99% of the variations, over the span of 20 years, will be represented in 1 single day" (S029) and "Most of histories of Black Swan prone events is going to be Black Swan free!" (S029)
- 2013: "the average violence observed in the past underestimates the true statistical average by at least half." (S033)
- 2013 (header date): Suppressing small shocks builds the big one. "If you take anything organic and you try to control its variability, you'll end up with less variability than you started with but the system would become more fragile." (S054)
- 2014-10: The great moderation "deceived those relying on historical statistical evidence." (S003)
- 2015-05: "one single extreme observation can make it jump" (S014)
- 2017 lecture: "Black swans are not more frequent, they are more consequential." (S032)
- 2020-01: The 1987 crash alone was about 80% of S&P 500 kurtosis from 1952 to 2008 (S001).
- 2025-07 (newest): Turbulence today is perception, not structure. "I don't think that we are experiencing the real volatility compared to what could have happened" (S046)

## 19. Interest rates and the Fed
Sources: 6 (S046, S047, S048, S053, S054, S056).
- 2010-08: He blamed Bernanke, Summers and Geithner: "They crashed the plane." (S053)
- 2013 (header date): "I am much more worried about things that have been fuelled by low interest rates, like the U.S." (S054)
- 2023-01: "Lowering rates creates asset bubbles without necessarily helping the economy." (S048) and "what we need is to return to a normal economic life, with interest rates of 4 to 5%." (S048)
- 2023-04: "So the root of everything is interest rates at zero, which ironically created Bitcoin." (S047) and "you can't solve the debt problem by putting interest rates at zero for a long time." (S047)
- 2025-07 (newest): Normal rates are now the danger for the indebted: "at north of 4% interest rates, 4 to 5% interest rate, this is significant. It means you can have a snowballing effect." (S046) Consistent with 2023: he wants normal rates and says the debt cannot carry them.

## 25. Crypto and bitcoin (changed)

## 29. Lindy: prefer what has lasted
