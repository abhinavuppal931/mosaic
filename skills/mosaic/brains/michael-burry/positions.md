# Positions: Michael Burry

Built from the verified batch notes on his Silicon Investor posts (S006, 1997 to 2000), MSN MoneyCentral journal and Value Investors Club write-ups (S031 to S042, 2000 to 2002), Scion fund letters and memos (S001, S020 to S027, 2001 to 2008), the FCIC interview, op-ed, lecture and speech (S002, S028 to S030, 2010 to 2012), the 2019 GameStop letters and Bloomberg interview (S003, S004), the 2025 Michael Lewis podcast (S005) and his Cassandra Unchained posts (S007 to S019, S043 to S046, 2025 to 2026). Each stance carries a date. Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. The seat's own question (what the crowd is missing, and how bad the downside is) comes first; the rest run most-used first. These are dated public stances, not calls on today's securities.

## 1. What the crowd is missing: the contrarian stance
Sources: 9 (S001, S006, S021, S022, S023, S024, S027, S029, S030).
- 1999: Asked the question no one in a bull market asks. "Why can't this market conceivably crash from these levels and not recover for 20 years? I guess I am just a bit of a contrarian." (S006)
- 2001-04: "while capital raising may be a popularity contest, intelligent investment is quite the opposite." (S021) At a 2001 symposium he said risk is ill-conceived investment, not volatility, and ate lunch alone while the low-volatility speaker drew the crowd (S021, paraphrase).
- 2001-07: Being alone and right is the edge. "I would argue that if I am the only one that thinks in this manner, and if I am correct, then my understanding becomes a competitive advantage that makes the subject even more relevant." (S022)
- 2002-01: "The real opportunities in any market of common stocks will occur when it is the investors who carry the pessimism." (S024)
- 2006-10: The crowd's appetite moves, his does not. "It all comes back to the market's tolerance for risk, which this year has risen to unprecedented heights while mine has remained constant." (S001)
- 2008-04: "Yet I simply don't recall too many people agreeing with me back then." (S027)
- 2011-04 and 2012-06: "there is nothing wrong with breaking from the social norm to ensure good outcomes." (S029) and "you should be aware of the social proof that operates there." (S030)

## 2. Cycles, bubbles and manias
Sources: 12 (S001, S003, S005, S006, S024, S027, S028, S029, S037, S040, S041, S042).
- 1999: "Buy and hold becomes mantra at the end of a bull market." (S006)
- 2001-05: The 2000 to 2001 fall was a bubble ending, not a cycle. "Indeed, what we are seeing now is not an era-defining downturn, but simply the expiration of an era-defining asset bubble." (S041)
- 2001: "Of course, most people don’t recognize bubbles until they’ve burst, while precious few seem quite capable of recognizing asset bubbles even while they are still intact." (S041)
- 2001-06: Took a property chairman selling at the top as a signal: "we're in the 9th year of a 7 year boom" (S037, quoting the Wellsford chairman).
- 2002-01: "fantastic undervaluation ought to be expected as a reaction to fantastic overvaluation." (S024) and "It is my belief that one constant in the stock market is human nature." (S024)
- 2006-10: "If this is not a peak in a dramatic debt-fueled economic boom, well, it certainly looks like one." (S001) Record margins in nearly every sector at once were part of the warning (S001, paraphrase).
- 2010-04: "there are these mass delusions and everybody wants to do it, then there's no stopping it unless it just doesn't exist." (S028)
- 2011-04: The top of an asset comes at the loosest credit. "The point at which the provision of credit was most lax would mark the point of maximal price in the asset." (S029)
- 2019-09: "Like most bubbles, the longer it goes on, the worse the crash will be." (S003)
- 2025-12 (newest): Uses net investment against GDP to date a build-out mania; in past manias the stock peak came before capex was halfway done. "you can look at net investment, which is capital expenditures less depreciation over time." (S005)

## 3. What price to pay, and how he values
Sources: 15 (S006, S021, S022, S023, S024, S031, S032, S033, S034, S035, S037, S038, S039, S041, S042).
- 1999: Quality is easy, price is hard. "finding Buffett companies now isn't so hard. Finding them at reasonable prices is dang near impossible." (S006) and "figure out your buy price and wait." (S006)
- 2000-08 to 2001-03: Enterprise value and free cash flow, not earnings ratios. "I usually focus on free cash flow and enterprise value (market capitalization less cash plus debt)." (S038) and "I tend to ignore price-earnings ratios. Return on equity is deceptive and dangerous." (S038) Maintenance capex proxy: "Luckily, there is already a ballpark number for the amount going to maintenance, it's called depreciation." (S039)
- 2000 to 2002, his own numbers: Huttig at 3.1 times EBITDA and about 5.1 times free cash flow (S032); GTSI at an honest PE under 5 with no debt (S031); Quipp near 6 times average free cash flow (S035); Bachoco at 2 times free cash flow (S033); Wellsford at 50 to 61 cents on the dollar of net asset value (S037).
- 2001-07: "fully aware that wonderful businesses make wonderful investments only at wonderful prices" (S022)
- 2001: His fair value hurdle. "What is fair value? I use an annual 10% return to shareholders after dilution, slings and arrows." (S041)
- 2002-01: "not because the prices are too high, but because the prices are so low that a potential acquirer proposing them would be laughed out of the boardroom." (S024)
- 2002: "In my world, it is primarily the financial buyer’s perspective that is meaningful, even if the strategic value to a corporate buyer might be somewhat higher." (S042)

## 4. Margin of safety and permanent loss
Sources: 8 (S020, S022, S024, S036, S037, S038, S039, S041).
- 2001-01: "maximizing the upside means first and foremost minimizing the downside." (S020) and "lost dollars are simply harder to replace than gained dollars are to lose." (S020)
- 2001-03: "All my stock picking is 100% based on the concept of a margin of safety" (S038) and "Specific, known catalysts are not necessary. Sheer, outrageous value is enough." (S038)
- 2000-08: "But I like it when my margin of safety does not require a calculator." (S039)
- 2001-07: "The emphasis will always be placed first on preventing the permanent loss of capital, and good results should follow." (S022)
- 2001: "I truly only fear permanent and absolute capital loss." (S041)

## 5. Volatility is not risk
Sources: 7 (S020, S021, S022, S023, S027, S030, S038).
- 2001-01: "I do not view volatility as being in any manner a measure of risk" (S020)
- 2001-04: "A wildly fluctuating dollar selling for 40 or 50 or 60 cents will always remain more attractive, and far less risky." (S021)
- 2001-07: "risk is minimized not through the alchemy of volatility calculus but rather through respectful business evaluation." (S022)
- 2001-10: "I will always choose the dollar bill carrying a wildly fluctuating discount rather than the dollar bill selling for a quite stable premium." (S023)
- 2008-04: "calculating risk is about the most brainless brainy endeavor one can imagine." (S027)
- 2012-06: "Mainstream economists and finance practitioners, please check your premises." (S030)

## 6. Market timing and having a market view (changed)
Sources: 9 (S001, S003, S005, S020, S022, S024, S039, S040, S041).
- Older view, 2000 to 2002: no market view. "The prudent view, in my opinion, is no view." (S020) and "I continue to believe the prudent view is no market view." (S041) "I certainly have proven no ability to pick tops, and I do not anticipate attempting such a feat in the future." (S022)
- 2000-08: Early, but rarely by much on single stocks. "History is on my side, I am only rarely more than six months early on a stock." (S039)
- 2001-03: "Capitulation will be defined by a loss of interest in capitulation." (S040)
- 2006-10: Named a peak in the debt boom (S001), while admitting the subprime short could take more than a year to pay.
- 2019-09: "However, I just don't know what the timeline will be." (S003)
- Newer view, 2025-12: He now states a market-level view but still refuses a date. "In most situations like this, there's no good way to time it. And shorting it is just a high-risk endeavor." (S005)
- Changed: from no market view (2000 to 2002) to explicit market-level warnings (2006, 2019, 2025). Constant across all of them: no date, and size the bet so being early is survivable (inferred from S003, S005, S025).

## 7. Shorting (changed)
Sources: 10 (S001, S005, S024, S025, S026, S028, S029, S040, S041, S042).
- 2001 to 2002, MSN journal: shorted GE, Krispy Kreme, Kohl's, Adobe, Standard Pacific and Magma (S040, S041). "Krispy Kreme trades in a manner decoupled from any reasonable fundamental valuation, much like the internet stocks of 1999-2000." (S040)
- 2002-01: Special situations only. "I short a stock for the Fund when there is some temporary, manipulated, or misunderstood phenomenon that has caused the stock to rise to an egregious valuation." (S024) and "the short selling field has become awfully crowded" (S024)
- 2002: "Investors examining stocks with high short interest are simply speculating if they cannot understand and refute the best arguments of the short sellers." (S042)
- 2006-10: "I do not like the math of shorting stocks, nor the risks." (S001) and "It is simply a better use of time, over the long run, to focus on finding great longs." (S001)
- 2010-04: Shorting through credit instead. "I don't like to short equities. I'm not generally a short guy" (S028) After WorldCom: "So it hit me, that's the way to short." (S028)
- Newer view, 2025-12: For a bubble he uses long-dated, far out-of-the-money puts rather than stock shorts, because timing is unknowable. "I thought two years would be enough, yes." (S005)
- Changed: stock shorts as special situations (2001 to 2002) gave way to capped-loss credit protection (2005 to 2008) and then long-dated puts (2025). The newer instruments cap the downside of being early (inferred from S005, S029).

## 8. Hedging, options and derivatives (changed)
Sources: 8 (S001, S003, S005, S020, S022, S024, S028, S039).
- Older view, 2000 to 2002: "my firm opinion is that the best hedge is buying an appropriately safe and cheap stock." (S020) and "One hedges when one is unsure. I do not seek out investments of which I am unsure." (S022) "It will never be my purpose to sell stocks short as part of a risk management program, contemporaneously defined." (S024)
- 2006-10: "Our experience with equity index puts has not been a good one, especially now that indices are surpassing or approaching all-time highs." (S001)
- 2010-04: On derivatives as a system, even while having profited from them: "But I really believe that we were better off without the derivatives." (S028) and "There is an argument to be made that you shouldn't allow what I did." (S028)
- 2019-09: Says derivatives used by passive funds to match flows will be impossible to unwind in a panic (S003, paraphrase).
- Newer view, 2025-12: "it would be very hard to be in long stocks in the United States and protect yourself." (S005) He closed the fund and kept the positions for himself. Press reports of his 13F put exposure counted notional value, two orders of magnitude above the premium paid (S005, paraphrase).

## 9. Concentration versus diversification
Sources: 6 (S001, S020, S022, S023, S038, S039).
- 2001-01: "Concentrated portfolios... are entirely about stock picking." (S020) Typically 15 to 25 stocks or fewer (S020).
- 2001-03: "I like to hold 12 to 18 stocks diversified among various depressed industries, and tend to be fully invested." (S038)
- 2001-10: Diversify the drivers of value, not the count. "I seek to minimize the correlation between the intrinsic values of the various securities held in the portfolio." (S023) and "Including speculative or overpriced stocks in the portfolio simply to diversify against the impact of an array of possible external shocks is simply irrational given the relative odds involved." (S023)
- 2006-10: "A Scion portfolio will be a concentrated portfolio, though, and I have generally thought that in any market environment I should be able to spot the handful of investments that will make all the difference." (S001)

## 10. Position sizing
Sources: 7 (S024, S025, S027, S028, S029, S039, S041).
- 2000-09: "One of the tenets of my philosophy is not to let single positions ruin a portfolio." (S039)
- 2001: Trimmed American Physicians from large to average once the no-brainer price was gone (S041).
- 2002-01: "In retrospect, one might argue I ought to have rushed to take larger positions at the time." (S024)
- 2006-11: Budgeted the worst case of the mortgage short at about six percent of assets a year in carry (S025).

- 2010-04 and 2011-04: Big notional, capped loss. "And credit derivatives were within our purview but it didn't feel good to make it too big within the fund." (S028) Peak short about $8.4 billion notional with a maximum loss under $100 million (S029).

## 11. Leverage and margin
Sources: 6 (S001, S020, S022, S027, S029, S040).
- 2001-01: "the Fund neither wrote calls on its positions nor borrowed exorbitant sums of money to enhance returns." (S020)
- 2001-07: Keeps a little cash instead of borrowing. "My preference is to hold a portfolio of 15-25 securities long while holding a small cash position in order that I may take advantage of particularly valuable opportunities without leveraging the Fund or rashly selling another position." (S022)
- 2001-04: Margin calls create the floor-breaking moves in small stocks. "Illiquid stocks falling beneath 5 often fall much further because of margin calls that kick in in the 3-5 price range." (S040)
- 2006-10: Prices were set by leveraged buyers "rather than what is rational for a cash account long investor to pay." (S001)
- 2006 letter, repeated 2011-04: "It is simply a tragedy of fate that ever-lower returns encourage ever-increasing leverage, with only one possible ultimate outcome." (S029)
- 2008-04: On carry trades sold to institutions: "portfolio strategies that implode every ten years or so" (S027)

## 16. Interest rates
Sources: 4 (S005, S028, S029, S041).
- 2001: Rich stocks carry rate risk. "investment in a stock with a price/cash earnings multiple of 25 will be much more sensitive to interest rates than investment in a stock with a price/cash earnings multiple of 5." (S041) Low rates also push savers to chase yield (S041, paraphrase).
- 2010-04: Rates at forty-year lows started the easy-credit chain (S028, paraphrase).
- 2011-04: On quantitative easing: "This is in fact a Pyrrhic gamble, as we continue to debase our currency." (S029)
- 2025-12: Against further cuts; puts the neutral rate near 4%. "None of this is costless." (S005)

## 19. Government debt
Sources: 3 (S005, S029, S030).
- 2011-04: "Our country’s math is scary big, but what’s even more scary is that it simply does not work." (S029)
- 2012-06: Expects another great recession and US debt above 200% of GDP within a generation, possibly both. "I think they’re ignoring reflexivity, and I think you face both." (S030) He shorted Portugal, Italy, Greece and Spain in 2006 (S030).
- 2025-12 (newest): Will not bet on the date of a debt or reserve-currency crisis. "I always kind of put it in terms of waiting for Castro to die. It's not a strategy." (S005)

## 20. Bear markets and crashes
Sources: 7 (S005, S021, S022, S040, S041, S042, S006).
- 2001-04: "All stocks, including technology stocks, must find a floor in terms of fundamental value and expected return to the stockholder before they find an era-defining floor in price." (S021)
- 2001-04: "If the market begins the first multi-decade sideways run of the new century (there were two such runs last century, both times after extreme valuation bubbles), then the surest way to profit will be to buy stocks of incontrovertible value." (S040)
- 2001-07: Expects his fund to do relatively best in bear markets and lag in bull markets (S022, paraphrase).
- 2001: "but never during the April lows did I find stocks, generally speaking, go on sale." (S041)
- 2002-06: "Investors should simply be wary of the risks in stocks, and should not delude themselves into thinking the market could not fall another 50%." (S042)
- 2025-12 (newest): Expects a long bear market like 2000. "I think the stock market could be in for a number of bad years." (S005)

## 21. Index funds and passive investing
Sources: 4 (S003, S005, S024, S029).
- 2002-01: Disagreed with Bogle's efficient-market view; Bogle had mocked him in Forbes (S024, paraphrase). By 2011 he recalled Vanguard's attorneys threatening his early website over criticism of index funds (S029, paraphrase).
- 2019-09: "And now passive investing has removed price discovery from the equity markets." (S003) and "The theater keeps getting more crowded, but the exit door is the same as it always was." - 2025-12 (newest): With passive over half the money, there is no ignored pocket to hide in. "Now I think the whole thing's just going to come down." (S005)

## 23. Stock-based compensation
Sources: 5 (S004, S005, S021, S022, S040).
- 2001-04: The tax test. "conservative investors ought to assume that if the IRS can tax something, then it is a real profit. And if they allow one to deduct something, then it is a real cost." - 2025-12 (newest): "you can look at how much our company is buying back to offset that dilution. And you can just take that amount and deduct it from cash flow." (S005)

## 26. Buybacks and dividends
Sources: 8 (S004, S022, S033, S035, S037, S039, S040, S041).
- 2001-04: "Whenever a company has an opportunity to purchase $1 dollar of intrinsic value for 50 cents, it should do so." (S040) The reverse is waste: "They’ve been buying companies with their 60-cent dollars, i.e., their shares, and that is just nonsensical and wasteful." (S040)
- 2001-06: Calls himself "a strong advocate of share buybacks in undervalued securities" (S037).

- 2019-08: "Depending on the timing and quality of execution, such a repurchase would increase earnings per share dramatically far more than any other possible action on a per share basis." (S004) and "lack of faith in management's capital allocation is the default conclusion." (S004)

## 27. Growth versus value, and quality at a price
Sources: 8 (S005, S006, S021, S023, S027, S038, S039, S041).
- 1999: Apple as a value stock: "Subtract out the cash and it was recently trading at about 10 times earnings." (S006)
- 2000-08: Most value lies far out. "To look at it another way, just 5.7% of the company's intrinsic value is represented by its earnings over the next three years." (S039)
- 2001-03: "I try to buy shares of unpopular companies when they look like road kill, and sell them when they've been polished up a bit." (S038)
- 2001-04: Sorts businesses into shrinking, stable and growing intrinsic value, preferring the growing franchise at a big discount (S021).
