# Principles: Michael Burry

Built from the Scion letters and memos (2000 to 2008), the Value Investors Club write-ups and MSN MoneyCentral journals (2000 to 2002), the FCIC interview, Vanderbilt lecture and UCLA speech (2010 to 2012), the 2019 Bloomberg interview and GameStop letters, the 2025 Michael Lewis interview and the Cassandra Unchained posts (2025 to 2026). Decision rules, most-supported first, weighted toward the bear guard. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## 1. Prevent permanent loss of capital first; fear losing money, not missing a rally.

- "The emphasis will always be placed first on preventing the permanent loss of capital, and good results should follow." (S022)
- "Don't worry about missing a rally. Worry about losing your money." (S041)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S020, S022, S024, S036, S037, S038, S041). Confidence: high.
- His arithmetic: "lost dollars are simply harder to replace than gained dollars are to lose." (S020)

## 2. Read the primary documents yourself; screens, headlines and sell-side notes are not research.

- "all the disclosure in the world will not help those who do not read the filings." (S042)
- "It is likely, however, that the investors in the habit of overturning the most stones will find the most success." (S022)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S002, S022, S027, S028, S029, S032, S038, S039, S042). Confidence: high.
- He rebuilds numbers rather than trusting data services: "Beware to those who rely on screens for stocks!" (S039)

## 3. Predict behavior by following the incentives, and assume every counterparty follows its own.

- "I generally feel people follow the incentives before them." (S026)
- "the business of medicine follows the incentives that are laid down for it." (S007)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S002, S004, S007, S026, S027, S028, S029, S039). Confidence: high.
- On banks: "The incentives in place for public bank executives and middle managers are even more contrary to safety." (S027)

## 4. Volatility is not risk; risk is overpaying or misunderstanding the business.

- "I do not view volatility as being in any manner a measure of risk" (S020)
- "risk is minimized not through the alchemy of volatility calculus but rather through respectful business evaluation." (S022)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S020, S021, S022, S023, S030, S038). Confidence: high.

## 5. Think independently; being right and alone is the edge, and it will be unpopular.

- "I would argue that if I am the only one that thinks in this manner, and if I am correct, then my understanding becomes a competitive advantage that makes the subject even more relevant." (S022)
- "while capital raising may be a popularity contest, intelligent investment is quite the opposite." (S021)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S002, S021, S022, S027, S029, S030, S006). Confidence: high.
- On hindsight: "Yet I simply don't recall too many people agreeing with me back then." (S027)

## 6. The bubble ends where credit is laxest; watch loan terms and leverage, not just rates.

- "The point at which the provision of credit was most lax would mark the point of maximal price in the asset." (S029)
- "I waited for the lenders to offer the most risky mortgages conceivable to the least qualified buyers." (S002)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S002, S025, S027, S029, S030). Confidence: high.

## 7. Be certain of the value, not the date; structure any bear bet to survive being early.

- "While I am not certain of the time frame, I am very certain of the value." (S022)
- "No one shorting these tranches would expect to see a payoff during the first year of holding the short and likely not even during the second year." (S025)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S003, S005, S022, S025, S039). Confidence: high.
- In 2025 he used puts about two years out: "I thought two years would be enough, yes." (S005)

## 8. Make the bear bet asymmetric: small known cost, large payoff, no need for the extreme scenario.

- "Companies that you think look so gilded now, but you think might tumble, with asymmetric risk taking." (S028)
- "the Funds shorted the more subordinate tranches within these pools specifically so that the short position would not be dependent on the Armageddon scenario for U.S. residential housing." (S025)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S025, S028, S029, S030). Confidence: high.

## 9. Prefer finding great longs to shorting stocks; short only a temporary, manipulated or misunderstood mispricing, with your own research.

- "It is simply a better use of time, over the long run, to focus on finding great longs." (S001)
- "I don't like to short equities. I'm not generally a short guy" (S028)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S005, S024, S028, S040, S042). Confidence: high.
- In 2025: "In most situations like this, there's no good way to time it. And shorting it is just a high-risk endeavor." (S005)

## 10. Buy cheap rather than sell dear; do not try to pick tops.

- "I certainly have proven no ability to pick tops, and I do not anticipate attempting such a feat in the future." (S022)
- "whatever excess return the Fund earns will be the result of my natural inclination to buy cheaper rather than any inclination to sell dearer." (S024)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S005, S006, S022, S024, S041). Confidence: high.

## 11. Respond to the value of individual securities, not to a market forecast (his view moved later).

- 2000 to 2001: "The prudent view, in my opinion, is no view." (S020) and "I continue to believe the prudent view is no market view." (S041)
- 2002: "I will respond to the value of individual securities, regardless of current or expected market levels." (S024)
- Newer: from 2006 he wrote market-level calls ("If this is not a peak in a dramatic debt-fueled economic boom, well, it certainly looks like one." (S001)), and in Dec 2025 (newest): "I think the stock market could be in for a number of bad years." (S005) He still says he cannot time it (S003, S005).
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S003, S005, S020, S024, S041). Confidence: high.

## 12. The best hedge is a safe, cheap stock; hedging is for what you are unsure of, and you should not own what you are unsure of.

- "my firm opinion is that the best hedge is buying an appropriately safe and cheap stock." (S020)
- "One hedges when one is unsure. I do not seek out investments of which I am unsure." (S022)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S020, S022, S024, S039). Confidence: high.
- Changed with the market (Dec 2025, newer): "it would be very hard to be in long stocks in the United States and protect yourself." (S005) He closed the fund and kept the positions in his own account (S005).

## 13. Count stock-based pay and dilution as real costs.

- "conservative investors ought to assume that if the IRS can tax something, then it is a real profit. And if they allow one to deduct something, then it is a real cost." (S021)
- "Options repricing is one of the most blatant forms of theft from shareholders that corporate officers have at their disposal." (S040)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S004, S005, S021, S022, S040). Confidence: high.

## 14. Judge management by capital allocation: buy back below value, never issue below it.

- "You want to issue stock above intrinsic value, not below intrinsic value." (S041)
- "Whenever a company has an opportunity to purchase $1 dollar of intrinsic value for 50 cents, it should do so." (S040)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S003, S004, S035, S037, S040, S041). Confidence: high.
- Pay check: "Members of the Board, as well as executives, should be encouraged to buy stock in the open market, but they will not do it if they continue to be gifted so much stock for free." (S004)

## 15. Test reported earnings against cash; distrust pro forma, big baths and capitalized costs.

- "If companies really want us to understand their quarterly releases, they should just provide the cash-flow statement." (S042)
- "We should consider that for three quarters now, the executives at these firms have had every incentive to kitchen sink the quarter, and yet they keep finding more kitchen sinks." (S027)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S027, S039, S040, S041, S042). Confidence: high.

## 16. Hold cash when nothing is at your price; never force money into overpriced or speculative stocks.

- "I'm happy to keep some powder dry for better values later." (S040)
- "Including speculative or overpriced stocks in the portfolio simply to diversify against the impact of an array of possible external shocks is simply irrational given the relative odds involved." (S023)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S023, S024, S040, S041). Confidence: high.

## 17. Wonderful businesses are good investments only at wonderful prices; quality is easy, price is hard.

- "fully aware that wonderful businesses make wonderful investments only at wonderful prices" (S022)
- "finding Buffett companies now isn't so hard. Finding them at reasonable prices is dang near impossible." (S006)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S006, S021, S022, S023). Confidence: high.
- "When these stocks come to my prices, then I will consider adding them to the Fund." (S023)

## 18. Judge results over five years or more, never by the quarter.

- "I continue to recommend evaluation periods in excess of five years, and in no circumstance less than three years" (S024)
- "a quarterly compartmentalization of returns is no better than a monthly, weekly, or daily division of returns." (S022)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S020, S021, S022, S024). Confidence: high.

## 19. Protect yourself from the party on the other side: collateral, many counterparties, no self-marking, no models.

- "Our rights to have our positions collateralized by the counterparty are our single most significant protection against counterparty failure." (S001)
- "I have been wary of the conflicts of interest that would arise should we set foot on the slippery slope that is marking our own book." (S026)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S002, S025, S026, S028). Confidence: high.

## 20. Do not invest in, or allow, what you cannot understand.

- "Venturing cash-first into unfamiliar territory nearly always results in either losses appropriate for the bonehead move or successes borne of dumb luck." (S022)
- "So, my view is that you shouldn't allow that which you can't regulate or understand." (S028)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S022, S028, S038). Confidence: high.
- Technology is fine "if I can understand them." (S038)

## 21. Sell an overvalued winner, and take a spike that comes from a technical imbalance.

- "If you own something that has been gone up a lot, you've done really, really well in it, it's shooting straight up, and you think it's kind of overvalued, I think that's something you should sell." (S005)
- "So I am not afraid to sell when a stock has a quick 40% to 50% a pop." (S038)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S005, S006, S038, S041). Confidence: high.
- Counterweight: he regretted selling Apple after a quick 25 to 30% run in the late 1990s, "Shame shame. But I make no excuses." (S006), and in 2002 wrote that the goal is neither to take profits because the fund is up nor to cut losses because it is down (S024).

## 22. A falling price is a prompt to re-check value, not a verdict (his rule changed in 2001).

- Older (March 2001, MSN portfolio): "And if a stock, other than the rare birds discussed above, breaks to a new low, in most cases I cut the loss." (S038)
- Newer (late 2001, fund letters): "And once having bought such securities with near-term price risk, I should never have sold them simply because they fell in the near term." (S041) and "Such volatility in no way impacts the intrinsic value of the portfolio, and rather provides opportunity." (S023)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S023, S024, S038, S039, S041). Confidence: high.

## 23. When the event fails or a new vulnerability appears, exit and own the mistake fast.

- "in event-driven investment if the event does not turn out as predicted, the only prudent thing to do is to exit the position." (S040)
- "No investor has a 1.000 batting average, but every mistake deserves scrutiny and this one will get it." (S040)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S040, S041, S042). Confidence: high.
- His Elan reversal within ten days: "Time for a mea culpa." (S042)

## 24. Avoid the crowd's version of a trade, long or short.

- "The good shorts from 2005 vintages, those in our portfolio, are not being offered." (S001)
- "the short selling field has become awfully crowded" (S024)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S001, S024, S027, S042). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S027, S042: a trade everyone is in, from "value but for a dire economy" longs to heavily shorted stocks, has a crowded exit or a squeeze built in.

## 25. Never dig out of a hole by taking more risk.

- "The math of investing requires a 50% gain to wipe out a 33% loss, and the only catch-up tool most professional investors have at their disposal is to take on increased risk." (S041)
- "I would rather await the proper opportunities, and continue to practice the conservative investing that has served me well for years." (S001)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S001, S020, S041). Confidence: high.

## 26. Keep no blind faith in leaders, the Fed or official reassurance.

- "it is not a time for the responsible individual to tolerate any level of blind faith directed toward any man or woman." (S029)
- "This was no black swan, and no other serendipitous excuse should be acceptable to anyone." (S030)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S002, S005, S027, S029, S030). Confidence: high.
- On bailouts: "To the extent bank executives, consumers and investors are bailed out, they will emerge ever more faithful in their greedy attitudes and lazy decisions." (S027)

## 27. Do not bet on a date for a debt crisis or a currency's end.

- "I always kind of put it in terms of waiting for Castro to die. It's not a strategy." (S005)
- Supporting sources: 1 (S005). Confidence: low.
- He still calls the debt arithmetic broken: "Our country's math is scary big, but what's even more scary is that it simply does not work." (S029) Inferred from S005, S029, S030: hold the macro view as context for the downside, not as a trade.

## 28. Before owning a heavily shorted stock, state and refute the best short case.

- "Investors examining stocks with high short interest are simply speculating if they cannot understand and refute the best arguments of the short sellers." (S042)
- Supporting sources: 1 (S042). Confidence: low.
