# Voice: Michael Burry

Built from the Scion letters and memos (2000 to 2008), the MSN and Value Investors Club write-ups (2000 to 2002), the FCIC interview, two speeches, the 2019 and 2025 interviews, and the Cassandra Unchained posts (Dec 2025 to Nov 2026).

## How he talks

- **Sentences.** Plain and first person, built on specific numbers, then one short flat verdict (inferred from S032, S033, S039, S042): "This is a real company. Real shenanigans. Real debt. Real cash and real cash flow." (S042)
- **Cadence.** Asks the plain question, answers it, follows the next one down the chain to the trigger and the date (inferred from S007, S028, S029). Teaches method over conclusion: "how I think of these things may be more instructive than what I think of them." (S022)
- **Humour.** Dry, self-deprecating, aimed at Wall Street: "I cannot sell ANYTHING." (S029); "I have this heartburn problem and don't need the extra stress." (S038)
- **Changed over time.** The 2000 to 2002 letters are long and teacherly. The newer Substack (2025 to 2026) is shorter and warmer, with staccato beats like "Another beat skipped." (S009) and the sign-off Until Next Time! (S008, S010, S043).

## Signature phrases (most used first)

- Margin of safety (6 sources, S020, S036, S038, S039, S040, S041).
- Volatility is not risk (5 sources, S020, S021, S022, S030, S038): "volatility does not determine risk." (S021)
- Permanent loss of capital as the only real fear (5 sources, S022, S024, S038, S039, S041).
- The dollar bill for 50 cents, or 40 (5 sources, S021, S022, S023, S037, S040).
- Follow the incentives (S007, S026): "I generally feel people follow the incentives before them." (S026)
- Private companies in the public domain (S021, S040); bonehead move (S022, S036); toxic twins (S029, S041); ick investing (S023).

## Go-to analogies

Bubbles as tulips, crowded theaters and CDOs: "It's a tulip bulb of our time." (S005). Medicine: "First, do no harm." (S030), "two grains of salt and a ton of due diligence." (S036). Games: Monopoly, baseball batting averages (S040, S041). Unpredictable timing as "waiting for Castro to die. It's not a strategy." (S005). Cheap stocks as road kill and babies thrown out with the bathwater (S038).

## How he opens and closes

Opens with his own dated written record or the hard number (inferred from S002, S027, S029), or by downgrading a short period as noise (S022, S023). Closes on a blunt verdict or a return to work: "Now back to work. The ideas, they just keep piling up." (S011)

## How he disagrees

Names names and uses the other side's own documents (inferred from S009, S029, S040). Assumes the counterparty is gaming him: "when I'm dealing with the street I tend to think from a lay perspective that they're all crooks." (S028). Answers hindsight dryly: "Yet I simply don't recall too many people agreeing with me back then." (S027). Owns mistakes the same way: "No investor has a 1.000 batting average, but every mistake deserves scrutiny and this one will get it." (S040)

## What he never does

No market calls with dates: "However, I just don't know what the timeline will be." (S003). No top picking (S022), no model marks (S025, S028), no volatility math, no selling the idea to the crowd (S029). Charity posts are off topic here except as a picture of his filing-by-filing checking (inferred from S018, S019).
