# Frameworks: Michael Burry

Built from the Scion Value Fund and Scion Capital letters and memos (2000 to 2008), the Value Investors Club write-ups and MSN MoneyCentral journals (2000 to 2002), the FCIC interview, Vanderbilt lecture and UCLA speech (2010 to 2012), the 2019 Bloomberg and GameStop letters, the 2025 Michael Lewis interview, and the Cassandra Unchained posts (2025 to 2026). Most-used first, weighted toward the bear guard's job: what the crowd is missing and what the downside looks like. Scoring lines are guidance drawn from his own numbers and thresholds, not a scorecard he published.

## 1. Margin of safety: minimize the downside first *(unnamed in his words; he credits Graham and Dodd)*

- **What it is:** the base of every pick. Upside takes care of itself only after the permanent loss of capital has been made unlikely. Losses compound against you, so a lost dollar is harder to replace than a gained dollar is to lose.
- **Parts:** (1) estimate what the business is worth on conservative numbers; (2) ask what you lose in the worst plausible case (asset floor, replacement value, private-market value); (3) buy only at a large discount; (4) no catalyst needed, because "Sheer, outrageous value is enough." (S038); (5) remember the breakeven math: a 33.3% loss needs a 50% gain, a 50% loss needs 100% (S020, S041).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** first, on every buy, add or hold. For the bear guard, it is the question behind every other one: if the bull case is wrong, where does the price stop?
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = a floor you can name in hard assets, cash or a buyer's price, sitting near the current price, so the worst case is small; "I like it when my margin of safety does not require a calculator." (S039). 5 = value is real but rests on earnings holding up. 0 = no floor at all: the price rests on a story, a multiple, or what the next buyer will pay.
- **Quote:** "maximizing the upside means first and foremost minimizing the downside." (S020) Also: "All my stock picking is 100% based on the concept of a margin of safety" (S038)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S006, S020, S021, S022, S024, S036, S037, S038, S039, S040, S041).

## 2. Do the work: primary filings and a chain of plain questions *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his research method. Ask a plain question, answer it from primary documents (10-Ks, prospectus supplements, servicer reports, regulatory filings, the other side's own studies), and let each answer raise the next question. He trusts filings over headlines, screens and sell-side research.
- **Parts:** (1) start from something that looks odd or cheap; (2) read ten or more years of filings; (3) rebuild the numbers yourself, because screens and data services get spin-offs, finance arms and one-offs wrong (S032, S039); (4) follow the chain until it ends in a mechanism and, ideally, a date; (5) build the case from the counterparty's own documents (S009).
- **Worked chain (2002 to 2005):** homebuilders looked cheap but were flattered by land gains, so he passed; that led to how housing was financed, then mortgage insurer filings, then the pools, then credit derivatives (S028, S029).
- **When to use it:** whenever the bull case rests on what "everyone knows". The bear guard asks whether anyone at the table has read the filings that would disprove the story.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the downside case is built from primary filings and pool-level or segment-level data the crowd has not read. 5 = built from reported numbers taken at face value. 0 = built from headlines, screens or a management narrative; "superficial analysis with readily available data is, well, too superficial." (S032)
- **Quote:** "Importantly, because subprime mortgages were being turned into securities, there were mandatory regulatory filings, and this is how I educated myself." (S029) Also: "all the disclosure in the world will not help those who do not read the filings." (S042)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S007, S009, S022, S025, S027, S028, S029, S030, S032, S042). The same habit runs his charity vetting by Form 990 (S018, S019); off-topic, kept thin.

## 3. Follow the incentives *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** predict how people and industries will behave by asking how they are paid. A boom built on a subsidy, a fee or an originate-and-sell model ends when that incentive changes or runs out of buyers.
- **Parts:** (1) map who is paid for what (volume, assets under management, reported earnings, options); (2) find where pay rewards quantity over quality or short-term over long-term; (3) look for agency risk, moral hazard and adverse selection (S028); (4) predict where the boom ends; (5) assume dealers, managers and counterparties act on their own incentives, not yours (S026).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on any company whose growth depends on a policy, a reimbursement rule, a fee model, or managers paid on assets or adjusted earnings (REIT managers paid on assets prefer to buy and borrow, S039; executives at banks, S027).
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = you can say who is paid to keep the story going and what happens when they stop. 5 = incentives are mixed but not toxic. 0 = growth depends on a party paid for volume with no stake in the outcome; "Increasingly, lenders concerned themselves more with the quantity of mortgages they sold than with their quality." (S002)
- **Quote:** "I generally feel people follow the incentives before them." (S026) Also: "Too much of the debate in health care comes from a place of passionate personal anecdote. Much of it is predictable by following the incentives." (S007)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S004, S007, S009, S026, S027, S028, S029, S030, S039, S041).

## 4. Peak credit marks the peak price ("credit extension by instrument")

- **What it is:** his bubble-timing lens. Once rates stop falling, an asset price can keep rising only if lenders loosen the type and quality of the loan. Track the loan terms, not the rate. The point of laxest credit is the top.
- **Parts:** (1) compare asset prices with incomes or cash flows; (2) when the gap widens and rates have bottomed, ask what is financing it; (3) track product migration in the filings (interest-only, stated income, option ARMs, second liens; interest-only share of subprime pools went from 10% to over 40% in a year, S029); (4) wait for the last-stage signal: the riskiest loans offered to the least qualified buyers (S002); (5) find the clock (teaser resets 20 to 24 months after issue, S025).
- **When to use it:** on banks, lenders, homebuilders, real estate, and any sector whose sales depend on easy credit; and at market level whenever leverage is chasing falling returns.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = you can show the credit terms that support today's price and the date they reset or tighten. 5 = credit is loose but no mechanism forces a break. 0 = the bull case ignores how buyers are financed.
- **Quote:** "The point at which the provision of credit was most lax would mark the point of maximal price in the asset." (S029) Also: "Critically, Interest rates alone would no longer determine affordability." (S029)
- **Related view:** "It is simply a tragedy of fate that ever-lower returns encourage ever-increasing leverage, with only one possible ultimate outcome." (S029)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S025, S027, S028, S029, S030, S041, S042).

## 5. Bubble tells *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** a set of checks he uses to say a market or theme has left value behind. He is clear he cannot time it.
- **Parts:** (1) slogans at the top: "Buy and hold becomes mantra at the end of a bull market." (S006); (2) the best companies still overpriced after the first fall (S021); (3) a stock that "seems to go up simply because it is going up." (S041); (4) capex mania test: in prior manias the stock peak came before capex was halfway done, measured as capital expenditures less depreciation against GDP (S005, Dec 2025); (5) announced capex lifts market value by a multiple of the spend (S005); (6) the billionaires-to-revenue ratio, which he offers as a cute tell, not a model (S005); (7) record margins across most sectors at once (S001); (8) Graham's law that fantastic overvaluation is followed by fantastic undervaluation (S024).
- **When to use it:** on any popular theme stock or market-level question, and especially when the bull case says this time the spending will pay.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = several tells present and the thesis needs the theme to keep running. 5 = one tell, with earnings that cover the price. 0 = none present. Use it to size the downside, not to pick a date.
- **Quote:** "Of course, most people don't recognize bubbles until they've burst, while precious few seem quite capable of recognizing asset bubbles even while they are still intact." (S041) On AI (Dec 2025): "you can look at net investment, which is capital expenditures less depreciation over time." (S005)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S005, S006, S021, S024, S027, S041).

## 6. The dollar bill selling for 50 cents, and volatility is not risk *(his phrase for the first half)*

- **What it is:** the long side of his contrarian lens. Risk is overpaying or misjudging the business, not price swings. A wildly swinging discount beats a stable premium, and volatility is the chance to buy at 40 cents.
- **Parts:** (1) value the business; (2) buy near 50 cents on the dollar, ideally 40; (3) treat price falls without a fall in intrinsic value as opportunity (S023); (4) reject beta and volatility math as a risk measure (S020, S022, S030).
- **When to use it:** when a stock has fallen hard and the crowd calls it risky; the bear guard checks whether value fell too, or only price.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = price at or under about 50 to 60 cents of a conservative value with a visible floor (Wellsford "at as little as 50 cents on the dollar (and at most 61 cents on the dollar)", S037). 5 = about 70 to 80 cents. 0 = at or above value, however calm the chart.
- **Quote:** "I have previously written that I strive to discover the proverbial dollar bill selling for 50 cents, preferably with enough volatility such that I have the opportunity to buy at 40 cents or less." (S022) Also: "I will always choose the dollar bill carrying a wildly fluctuating discount rather than the dollar bill selling for a quite stable premium." (S023)
- **Sources:** 8 (S020, S021, S022, S023, S024, S030, S037, S038).

## 7. The asymmetric credit short *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** how he bets against a bubble without taking unlimited risk: buy protection (credit default swaps) on the weakest slice of the structure, so a small, known premium buys a large payoff if the slice fails. Born after WorldCom went from investment grade to bankrupt: "So it hit me, that's the way to short." (S028)
- **Parts:** (1) pick the thin, subordinated slice (BBB and BBB- tranches of 2005 subprime pools) so the bet "would not be dependent on the Armageddon scenario" (S025); (2) select on shared predictive traits, not for diversification (S025); (3) avoid later deals with extra credit support and pools with interest rate swap protection (S025, S026); (4) prefer standardized, tradable contracts over bespoke ones (S029); (5) state the worst-case cost of carry up front (about six percent of assets a year, fully amortized in three years, S025); (6) know the maximum loss (peak short $8.4 billion notional, maximum loss under $100 million, S029, S030).
- **When to use it:** when the bear case is strong but the timing is not; a bear thesis should look for the instrument with capped loss and long tenor before considering a plain short.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = loss is capped and small versus the payoff, the trigger is contractual, and the bet does not need the extreme scenario. 5 = capped loss but the payoff needs a broad collapse. 0 = open-ended loss (a naked stock short in a crowded name).
- **Quote:** "the Funds shorted the more subordinate tranches within these pools specifically so that the short position would not be dependent on the Armageddon scenario for U.S. residential housing." (S025)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S025, S026, S027, S028, S029, S030).

## 8. Capital allocation is the verdict on management *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** judge management by what it does with cash and shares. Buying back stock below intrinsic value creates value; issuing stock below it, or buying companies with cheap stock, destroys it.
- **Parts:** (1) compare price with cash per share and intrinsic value; (2) check buybacks against value, not against a habit; (3) check whether shares are issued above or below value (S041); (4) watch acquisitions, especially stock-financed and ego-driven ones (S036, S041); (5) check whether directors buy stock in the open market or are gifted it (S004).
- **When to use it:** on any cash-rich cheap stock, any serial acquirer, and any thesis that needs management to do the right thing.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = buying back hard below value, insiders buying in the market. 5 = neutral. 0 = issuing below value, stock-financed deals at depressed prices, big pay grants while the stock falls; at GameStop in 2019, cash above market cap and heavy short interest meant "lack of faith in management's capital allocation is the default conclusion." (S004)
- **Quote:** "Whenever a company has an opportunity to purchase $1 dollar of intrinsic value for 50 cents, it should do so." (S040) Also: "VCLK is acquiring competitor ClickAgents with stock. This seems like the bonehead move of the century." (S036)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S004, S035, S036, S037, S039, S040, S041).

## 9. Enterprise value and true free cash flow *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his valuation core. Price the whole enterprise (market cap plus debt less cash) against free cash flow he rebuilds himself; ignore P/E and distrust ROE.
- **Parts:** (1) EV = market cap less cash plus debt (S038); (2) free cash flow = operating cash flow less depreciation as a maintenance proxy (S039); (3) strip out captive finance arm debt from industrial EV (Paccar, S039); (4) include off-balance-sheet items; (5) accept different ratios by industry and cycle; (6) check peak-cycle cash flow for cyclicals, since past downturns cut Paccar's cash flow 15% to 70% (S039).
- **When to use it:** on every valuation, and to test whether a "cheap" P/E survives once debt, dilution and capex are counted.
- **Scoring guidance (his own examples):** clearly cheap at about 2x to 5x free cash flow or 1.6x to 3.3x EV/EBITDA (Bachoco, Huttig, Quipp, S032, S033, S035), or 3/4 of trailing free cash flow at the extreme (S023). Fair value is a 10% annual return to shareholders after dilution (S041). Expensive when the multiple needs decades of growth.
- **Quote:** "I usually focus on free cash flow and enterprise value (market capitalization less cash plus debt)." (S038) Also: "I tend to ignore price-earnings ratios. Return on equity is deceptive and dangerous." (S038)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S023, S031, S032, S033, S035, S038, S039).

## 10. Forced sellers and "ick" investing *(ick investing is his term)*

- **What it is:** price falls that come from who is selling, not from the business. Spin-offs dumped by index holders, post-bankruptcy equity dumped by banks, failed buyouts, tax-loss selling, window dressing, delisting, margin calls. The crowd's "ick" is his starting point.
- **Parts:** (1) identify the seller and why they must sell; (2) confirm business value is intact ("This introduces price risk but not business risk." S032); (3) expect spin-offs to bottom about a year after the spin (S032); (4) buy when the forced selling ends.
- **When to use it:** the mirror check for the bear guard. Before calling a falling stock broken, ask whether the fall is flow or fundamentals.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = an identifiable price-insensitive seller and an intact business. 5 = some forced selling but also real deterioration. 0 = the selling is informed (insiders, lenders) and the business is shrinking.
- **Quote:** "Ick investing means taking a special analytical interest in stocks that inspire a first reaction of" (S023) Also: "the market delights in throwing babies out with the bathwater." (S038)
- **Sources:** 8 (S021, S023, S032, S034, S035, S036, S041, S042).

## 11. Accounting quality red flags *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** reported earnings are a claim to be tested. He hunts the gap between reported profit and cash.
- **Parts:** (1) pro forma profit versus the cash flow statement (S042); (2) days sales outstanding jumping (55.6 to 97.6 at one short, S042); (3) interest income or gain-on-sale inside operating profit (S039, S042); (4) big-bath charges that erase earlier profits, like Cisco's inventory write-off (S040, S041); (5) keep goodwill visible because "Goodwill amortization hides mistakes." (S040); (6) capitalized costs and accounting leverage (his Elan reversal, S042); (7) financial statements too opaque to value (banks in 2008, S027).
- **When to use it:** on every bull case built on adjusted earnings, and on any company with acquisitions, financing arms or long receivables.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = cash earnings match reported earnings and the filings are plain. 5 = some adjustments, explained. 0 = profit depends on pro forma items, capitalized costs or a write-off that resets the base.
- **Quote:** "If companies really want us to understand their quarterly releases, they should just provide the cash-flow statement." (S042) On banks in 2008: "the quality of financial statements is every bit as inscrutable as those of Enron circa 2001." (S027)
- **Sources:** 7 (S005, S021, S027, S039, S040, S041, S042).

## 12. Special-situation short selling *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his rule for when a stock short is worth its bad math: only when a temporary, manipulated or misunderstood phenomenon has pushed the price to an egregious level, with his own research, in a lightly shorted name. Never as a portfolio hedge.
- **Parts:** (1) name the phenomenon (hype, lock-ups, accounting); (2) check short interest is light; (3) set a target cover price (Magma shorted near 30, target about 9, S042); (4) use buy stops so a squeeze cannot take the shirt (S040); (5) cover when the rule of thumb is met (Standard Pacific back to book, S040).
- **IPO checklist (Magma, Dec 2001):** no profits, aggressive accounting changes, lock-up expiry covering most shares, venture exit, 36x revenue against 3x to 6x for peers (S041).
- **When to use it:** before any short or put. The bear guard prefers to say avoid or sell over short, as he does.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = a named, temporary phenomenon, a dated catalyst (lock-up, reset), light short interest, defined cover. 5 = overvalued but no catalyst. 0 = crowded, popular short with open-ended loss.
- **Quote:** "I short a stock for the Fund when there is some temporary, manipulated, or misunderstood phenomenon that has caused the stock to rise to an egregious valuation." (S024) Also: "I do not like the math of shorting stocks, nor the risks." (S001)
- **Dated stance:** by his account in Dec 2025, his Palantir position was long-dated puts costing about $10 million, not the $1 billion reported, on a stock he thought worth far less than its price (S005).
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S005, S024, S028, S040, S041, S042).

## 13. Concentrated portfolio, uncorrelated values, cash when nothing is cheap *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** hold a short list (15 to 25 in the fund, 12 to 18 in his MSN portfolio) across unrelated businesses, and let cash build when there is nothing at his price.
- **Parts:** (1) concentrate where value is greatest; (2) minimize the correlation of intrinsic values, not of prices (S023); (3) never add speculative names to diversify (S023); (4) keep a small cash reserve so a new idea does not force a sale (S022); (5) let cash run high when needed (about 40% in late 2001, S024; "quite a bit" in 2006, S001).
- **When to use it:** on sizing and on portfolio-level questions; the bear guard checks whether several holdings fail on the same shock.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the position's value does not depend on the same driver as the rest of the book, and there is cash for the next opportunity. 5 = some overlap. 0 = several positions share one macro or credit driver.
- **Quote:** "I seek to minimize the correlation between the intrinsic values of the various securities held in the portfolio." (S023)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S020, S022, S023, S024, S038, S039).

## 14. Government over-reach, private over-reaction *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** read legislation, regulation and central bank policy to find what drives a private-market boom, and expect the boom to end when the policy edge closes. The biggest consequences are the unforeseen ones.
- **Parts:** (1) find the rule or rate that makes the business boom (Medicare reimbursement gap for rehab hospitals in his 1994 paper, S007; easy money and homeownership policy in 2003 to 2006, S028, S029); (2) ask how dependent the sector is on that rule; (3) expect over-reaction in the private market; (4) expect bailouts and intervention to distort exits (he sold credit positions by early 2008 on fear of intervention, S002).
- **When to use it:** on any company or sector whose margins come from a subsidy, a reimbursement rate, a rate regime or a regulator's forbearance.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = margins survive without the policy. 5 = some dependence, with time to adapt. 0 = the business exists because of a policy differential that can close.
- **Quote:** "my focus was on the interplay between the actions of our government, and the over-reactions of the private market." (S029) From 1994: "The problem is it will not last." (S007)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S007, S027, S028, S029, S030).

## 15. Certain of value, uncertain of time *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** he expects to be early and builds that in. The value case can be firm while the date is unknown, so the structure of the bet must survive the wait.
- **Parts:** (1) separate the value call from the timing call; (2) use a clock where one exists (mortgage resets: "maximum stress during the latter half of 2007", S025); (3) where no clock exists, use long tenor (two-year puts, S005) or skip the bet; (4) expect no payoff in year one and maybe not year two (S025); (5) never bet on a specific date for a debt crisis.
- **When to use it:** on any bear case. The question is not only "is it overvalued" but "can the position survive being right two years early".
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = a dated trigger and a position that can be held through it. 5 = no trigger but limited carry. 0 = the bear case needs a fall soon and the position bleeds while waiting.
- **Quote:** "While I am not certain of the time frame, I am very certain of the value." (S022) In 2025: "In most situations like this, there's no good way to time it. And shorting it is just a high-risk endeavor." (S005)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S005, S022, S025, S039).

## 16. Stock options and stock-based pay as a real cost ("shell game")

- **What it is:** count employee stock pay as an expense, because the tax authority treats it as one. Measure dilution by what buybacks spend just to keep the share count flat.
- **Parts:** (1) take the tax benefit from option exercises and divide by about 35% to estimate the missing expense (Adobe 2000: $125 million benefit, about $357 million cost, operating income from $408 million to $51 million, S021, S040); (2) dock earnings by the buyback spent to offset dilution (Intel, about one third, S040); (3) in 2025, subtract that buyback spend from cash flow rather than adding stock pay back (S005); (4) treat repricing of options as theft (S040).
- **When to use it:** on any technology or growth stock valued on adjusted earnings or free cash flow that adds back stock pay.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = stock pay small and share count falling without heavy buyback spend. 5 = moderate dilution offset by buybacks below value. 0 = stock pay close to or above reported profit; such firms are "private companies in the public domain" (S040).
- **Quote:** "conservative investors ought to assume that if the IRS can tax something, then it is a real profit. And if they allow one to deduct something, then it is a real cost." (S021) And in 2025: "you can look at how much our company is buying back to offset that dilution. And you can just take that amount and deduct it from cash flow." (S005)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S005, S021, S022, S040, S041).

## 17. Counterparty and mark protection *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** when you bet against the crowd, the party on the other side may not pay or may mark you unfairly. Protection is part of the thesis, not an afterthought.
- **Parts:** (1) spread exposure across many dealers and avoid the weakest (six to nine, avoiding Lehman and Bear, S002, S029); (2) demand daily collateral and valuation (S002, S028); (3) negotiate terms close to equal (S028); (4) do not mark your own book and do not price to models (S025, S026); (5) assume the dealer's interests differ from yours (S026).
- **When to use it:** on any derivative, structured product, private fund or position whose payoff depends on a third party's solvency or marks.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = collateralized daily, diversified counterparties, standard contracts. 5 = one strong counterparty. 0 = payoff depends on an uncollateralized party that is itself exposed to the same risk (he shorted AIG partly because it did not post collateral, S028).
- **Quote:** "The creditworthiness of our counterparties is an integral part of the investment thesis." (S025)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S025, S026, S028, S029).

## 18. Private-market and acquirer value *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** check the price against what a financial buyer would pay for the whole company. He wants prices so low that an acquirer offering them would be laughed out of the boardroom.
- **Parts:** (1) find recent private deals and take-privates as comparables (Springs Industries at 5.2x EV/EBITDA for Pillowtex, S034); (2) use the financial buyer's view, not a strategic premium (S042); (3) treat bids and failed buyouts as evidence of value (S023, S035); (4) use replacement cost as a floor (Carnival's ships, S039).
- **When to use it:** to anchor the downside: if a buyer would pay well above the price, the bear case has a ceiling on its damage.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = price at about half of private-market value. 5 = near it. 0 = price far above anything a financial buyer would pay.
- **Quote:** "In my world, it is primarily the financial buyer's perspective that is meaningful, even if the strategic value to a corporate buyer might be somewhat higher." (S042)
- **Sources:** 6 (S023, S024, S032, S034, S039, S042).

## 19. Technical support entry and the new-low exit *(unnamed; his view changed)*

- **What it is:** an overlay he used in 2000 and early 2001 on top of value work: buy within 10% to 15% of a 52-week low that has held, and cut the loss on a break to a new low, except for rare birds (asset plays, deep net nets, compounders at good prices).
- **Parts (2000 to March 2001):** "I am rarely willing to buy more than 15% above technical support." (S039); "I also generally use broken support as an exit point." (S039); wait for "falling knives to lay motionless on the floor" (S040).
- **Newer view (August 2001 onward, in the fund letters):** he wrote that he should never have sold near-term price-risk names just because they fell, and in January 2002 that the goal is not to cut losses because the fund is down (S041, S024). He averaged down in 2001 instead (S023).
- **When to use it:** as a check on whether a falling price is information. The bear guard should ask whether the break is flow (add) or a thesis break (exit), not apply a mechanical stop.
- **Scoring guidance:** a break to new lows is a prompt to re-check value, not a verdict; it counts against a position only if the re-check finds intrinsic value has fallen.
- **Quote:** "And if a stock, other than the rare birds discussed above, breaks to a new low, in most cases I cut the loss." (S038, March 2001). Newer: "And once having bought such securities with near-term price risk, I should never have sold them simply because they fell in the near term." (S041, 2001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S023, S024, S038, S039, S040, S041).

## 20. Crowded theater, same exit door *(unnamed liquidity test)*

- **What it is:** compare the money that would need to leave with the liquidity of what it owns. Passive funds and institutions crowd into the same names and leave together.
- **Parts:** (1) count index constituents that trade under a set dollar value per day (1,049 of the Russell 2000 under $5 million, 266 of the S&P 500 under $150 million in 2019, S003); (2) compare with assets indexed to them; (3) note derivatives used to match flows that cannot be unwound (S003); (4) for small stocks, expect forced selling and margin calls to push prices well below value (the 5 to 3 effect, S040).
- **When to use it:** on popular index heavyweights, crowded themes and illiquid small caps; it shapes how bad the downside path can get, even when it cannot say when.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = holders are long-term and the stock trades well relative to the money in it. 5 = some crowding. 0 = the stock is held mostly by flows that must sell together.
- **Quote:** "The theater keeps getting more crowded, but the exit door is the same as it always was." (S003) Earlier: "institutional investors are exceptionally good at crowding the exits." (S022)
- **Changed view, with dates:** in 2019 he called passive a bubble but said "I just don't know what the timeline will be." (S003). Newer (Dec 2025): with passive over half of money, "Now I think the whole thing's just going to come down." (S005)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S005, S022, S023, S040).

## 21. Asset floor and sum of the parts *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** value the pieces separately and see what the market charges for the rest. When cash plus stakes plus assets cover the price, the operating business comes free.
- **Parts:** (1) list cash, holdings and saleable assets; (2) adjust each from book to realizable value (Wellsford: three units, each marked up or down, S037); (3) subtract liabilities; (4) compare with market cap; (5) treat any development or growth as upside (Tejon Ranch, S006).
- **When to use it:** on holding companies, real estate, cash-rich tech after a bust, and any bull case that leans on hidden assets; the bear guard checks the haircut on each asset.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = realizable assets at or above the price (ValueClick at cash plus stakes, S036). 5 = assets cover most of the price. 0 = the parts are worth less than the price after realistic haircuts.
- **Quote:** "Therefore I see a margin of safety in the current price even without playing the obvious arbitrage." (S036)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S036, S037, S039, S042).

## 22. Three kinds of business, and where the value sits in time *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** sort a business by whether its inflation-adjusted intrinsic value is shrinking, stable or growing, then ask how much of the value sits in distant years and how sensitive that makes the price to rates.
- **Parts:** (1) classify the business (S021); (2) prefer a long-term franchise bought at a big discount to growing value; (3) note that most value of a growth company sits more than ten years out (only 5.7% in the next three years in his model, S039); (4) richly priced stocks are far more sensitive to interest rates than cheap ones (S041); (5) wonderful businesses are wonderful investments only at wonderful prices (S022).
- **When to use it:** on high-multiple stocks when rates or growth assumptions shift; the bear guard asks what a small change in the distant years does to the price.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = growing value bought cheap, so little depends on distant years. 5 = stable value at a fair price. 0 = shrinking value at a growth multiple, or a 25x cash earnings stock whose case needs low rates.
- **Quote:** "investment in a stock with a price/cash earnings multiple of 25 will be much more sensitive to interest rates than investment in a stock with a price/cash earnings multiple of 5." (S041)
- **Sources:** 4 (S021, S022, S039, S041).

## 23. Refute the best short argument *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** a check for longs in heavily shorted stocks. If you cannot state and answer the short sellers' best case, you are speculating. High short interest is also a squeeze risk he avoids from the short side.
- **Parts:** (1) read the short case; (2) answer it with facts; (3) on the short side, stay away from names the shorts control (AmeriCredit's 75% rally in 12 days, S042); (4) note when the crowd has piled into the same short (S024).
- **When to use it:** on any decision in a stock with high short interest, long or short.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = the bull and bear cases are both written down and the decision answers the strongest bear point. 0 = the bear case has not been stated.
- **Quote:** "Investors examining stocks with high short interest are simply speculating if they cannot understand and refute the best arguments of the short sellers." (S042)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S024, S042).
