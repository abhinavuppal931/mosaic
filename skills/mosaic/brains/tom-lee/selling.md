# Selling: Tom Lee

Built from the verified batch notes on 21 interviews and talks (S001 to S021, 2019-10 to 2026-11). His rules for selling, trimming, adding and re-checking, then the kill criteria and re-check triggers this seat would write. Rules are ordered by how many sources carry them. He is mostly a holder and adder; his sell side is a short list of things that would end a cycle or a theme. Mistakes he admits are dated.

## Rules

1. **A falling price is not a broken thesis; a drawdown with no recession is a buy.** "as long as the economy's holding up, that drawdown is going to be viewed as a buying opportunity." (S001) "if we fall but we don't have a recession that just means everyone just panicked" (S003) "as long as the economy isn't breaking, whatever correction we have will be V-shaped" (S011) Sources: 7 (S001, S002, S003, S009, S011, S020, S021).

2. **Sell into a top only when the top signs line up.** Everyone bullish: "Markets peak when everyone's bullish and it no longer responds to good news" (S001). Excess speculation or a policy shock: "the second source of a negative catalyst is excess speculation" (S018). Models bent to fit the price: "The second is when analysts have to suddenly shift discount rates to a level that removes all risk." (S004) Skeptics still around means no top: "So there's skepticism at these levels. That's not a top." (S018) Sources: 6 (S001, S003, S004, S009, S016, S018).

3. **Watch credit before equities crack.** "to me, I'd be watching credit before I watched equities crack" (S011) A recession needs high-yield spreads near 800 basis points (S003, paraphrase). If credit stress became the central case, he would turn: "Like to me, if that's what was happening, then I wouldn't be positive on markets." (S020) Sources: 5 (S003, S008, S011, S020, S021).

4. **Do not sell the painful decline; you will not get back in.** "once you sell something because it's a painful decline, you're so reluctant to get back in" (S018) "you've got to be tilted long and then buy on the dips and then ignore the noise most of the time, including us." (S017) Sources: 4 (S011, S017, S018, S019).

5. **Trim winners mechanically through rebalancing, and accept the cost.** Granny Shots rebalance to equal weight every quarter, which trims the leaders; he admits Nvidia would have done better untouched and takes the cost for steadier volatility (S007, paraphrase). Timely picks rotate monthly with a technical filter, above the 20 and 200 day averages (S005). Two themes per name keep a position alive when one theme stalls: "a single theme won't always work at every month in a stock market." (S002) Sources: 4 (S002, S005, S007, S018).

6. **Do not wait for the perfect low to add.** "You don't know it's the low until a week later." (S007) "unless you're really a genius, you know, it's buying it here is actually going to be at a better price point" (S014) For savers: "whether that means you make a commitment to buy every month or you buy every time there's a dip, I'd really recommend people dollar cost average" (S012) Sources: 4 (S002, S007, S012, S014).

7. **The theme ends when its engine stops, not when growth slows.** "I'd say the real key is, is AI no longer providing remarkable innovation? And if it doesn't, that's really the end of the cycle." (S010) "it's only a bubble if spending is not driving income growth." (S002) For the US as a whole: "if America becomes, has what we call Dutch disease and no longer seeks to innovate, then another country will take the lead" (S011) Sources: 3 (S002, S010, S011).

8. **Buy when earnings bottom, sell when they peak.** "you got to buy stocks when earnings are bottoming and you sell them when earnings are peaking." (S019) Stocks move first: "since 1920, stocks bottomed 12 months before earnings bottomed." (S005) Late-cycle earnings growth is single digits (S016). Sources: 3 (S005, S016, S019).

9. **Expect a large drawdown, but do not call the top.** "I wouldn't want to call it top either." (S011) He picks one most likely path rather than hedging with scenarios: "I probably wouldn't play that game because it's not clearer than what we're thinking." (S006) Gains pulled into one year raise the drawdown risk for the next (S016, paraphrase). Sources: 3 (S006, S011, S016).

10. **Separate a delay from an impaired earnings trajectory.** "If the trajectory of earnings is impaired, that's different than if you're delaying, you're taking a two-quarter hit because of the tariff uncertainty and stocks might go down on both but one's a huge buying opportunity" (S007) In 2020: "the only reason we would be wrong is if these companies run out of money." (S020) Sources: 2 (S007, S020).

11. **No stops on great theme stocks; a stop only for someone who cannot stand the swings.** "people would have been stopped out of Palantir and Tesla a long time ago" (S009) Theme leaders can idle: "there have been times when Nvidia's done nothing for a year, like literally dead money or Palantir, dead money for years" (S009) For a small crypto starter position: "if they have a stop loss of 10%, they can just hold it and sell it if, if they can't tolerate the volatility." (S019) Sources: 2 (S009, S019).

12. **Drop a name when its data cannot be trusted.** Supermicro left the Granny Shots on audit and compliance trouble after it had paid: "Now there was a drawdown with Supermicro, but it already produced a lot of returns for the granny." (S007) Watch for gamed metrics: "once people know something is a KPI, then people will try to game it." (S017) Sources: 2 (S007, S017).

13. **Stop adding at the target, then wait.** "And now we're done stacking while a bull market is starting." (S015) "the big money is not in the buying and selling, but in the waiting" (S015, quoting Munger). Until then, re-decide each week: "every week, we decide, you know, what is the best use of cash?" (S014) Sources: 2 (S014, S015).

14. **Short-term headwinds do not move a five-year view.** "But it doesn't change where Bitcoin should be five years from now." (S019) "I think it's easier to have conviction on a longer term price than a shorter term" (S019) He judges a long strategy over about six years, not weeks (S014, paraphrase). Sources: 2 (S014, S019).

15. **Conviction or stubbornness: the test is whether the facts changed.** "conviction is basically riding through the volatility and it's not easy to tell the difference until history has already passed" (S001) "stubborn is riding something when and believing in something when all the facts have changed" (S001) Sources: 1 (S001).

## Selling mistakes he admits

- 2023-10: sold Arista after trading in and out: "I probably shouldn't have sold it." (S005)
- 2022 (said 2025-08): his worst call, from underweighting a technician's warning before the drawdown: "I kind of didn't understand how dire his warning was, because, you know, we had the 2022 drawdown." (S018)
- 2025-03: quarterly rebalancing trimmed Nvidia, which he says would have done better left alone; he keeps the rule anyway (S007, paraphrase).
- 2025-10: held to a year-end Bitcoin target that a crypto winter overran (S016, S014); see stories.
- 1999 (said 2025-08): a sell rating that was right but nearly ended his career: "a sell rating on a stock during that era that almost destroyed my career" (S018)

## Kill criteria this seat would write

Inferred from S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S007, S010, S011, S018, unless a line cites one source. Written as checkable facts, in his style.
- High-yield spreads widen toward about 800 basis points over Treasuries, or credit cracks before equities do (S003, S011).
- The Fed tightens into an economy it calls overheated, ending liquidity (S018, S021).
- The theme's spending stops driving income: margins shrink, or debt grows faster than revenue, for the companies doing the spending (S002).
- The theme stops producing remarkable innovation, or new use cases appear with no end market (S010).
- Private investment passes 27% of GDP and rolls over (S006).
- The earnings trajectory is impaired, not delayed: estimates fall for reasons that outlast the shock (S007).
- Margin debt makes a meaningful leap above its prior high while surveys turn broadly bullish (S018, S001).
- An IPO wave in the theme, with analysts cutting discount rates to justify prices (S004, S003).
- The stock now fits only one theme, so one story must carry it (S002, S007).
- A restatement, audit or compliance problem (S007).
- For a crypto holding: price below about half of production cost (S011), or leverage enters the holder's structure (S009).

## Re-check triggers

- Every quarter: the Granny Shots rebalance, and whether each name still sits on two themes (S007, S018); monthly for timely picks, with the 20 and 200 day averages (S005).
- ISM crossing 50 in either direction, and PMIs rising from below 47 (S001, S002, S005).
- AAII bulls minus bears at minus 20 or worse, read over about six months (S007, S008).
- One-month VIX future above the four-month (S007, S008).
- Fed meetings, the start of a cutting cycle, and a change of Fed chair (S006, S011).
- High-yield and CCC spreads after any equity selloff (S003, S006, S021).
- Margin debt and fund-manager positioning (S011, S018).
- IPO lockup expiries in the theme (S011).
- Early December each year, when he sets the next year's view (S016).
- Crypto: a fourth straight down quarter would be a first (S011); Bitcoin's 200-day average (S015).
- Elections, midterms and geopolitical buildups: sell the buildup, buy the invasion (S003, S010).
- Client and institutional surveys, for sentiment and cash on the sidelines (S005, S006).
