# Principles: Tom Lee

Built from the verified notes on 21 sources (interviews and one BitMine chairman's message, 2019 to 2026). Decision rules, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## 1. Size a technology theme by how the young adopt it, not by what incumbent experts say.

- "young people are the ones who change the world." (S001)
- "it was the experts that dismissed the future of personal computing." (S006)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S001, S002, S004, S006, S017, S018, S019, S021). Confidence: high.
- The error he names is to "assume the giants stay giants and all the new entrants are just fighting for crumbs." (S017)

## 2. A theme is not at its top while skeptics outnumber believers; tops come when everyone is bullish.

- "people are not bearish at the tops, they're bullish at the tops, and I don't really find that many bullish people." (S001)
- "So there's skepticism at these levels. That's not a top." (S018)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S004, S009, S010, S013, S016, S018). Confidence: high.
- The flip side: "when something is so strongly held, it's very unlikely to be true." (S016)

## 3. Buy the drawdown while the economy holds up, and do not sell into a painful decline.

- "as long as the economy's holding up, that drawdown is going to be viewed as a buying opportunity." (S001)
- "once you sell something because it's a painful decline, you're so reluctant to get back in" (S018)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S002, S003, S011, S017, S018, S021). Confidence: high.
- His standing instruction: "you've got to be tilted long and then buy on the dips and then ignore the noise most of the time, including us." (S017)

## 4. Do not fight the Fed: a Fed that pulls liquidity ends the bull, and cuts without a recession feed it.

- "monetary policy is really the driver, so you can't fight the Fed." (S004)
- "when a Fed cuts and there is no recession, it is hugely positive for the equity market" (S006)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S003, S004, S006, S018, S020, S021). Confidence: high.
- Even a strong theme needs it: with a tightening Fed, AI alone would not have produced a rising market (S003).

## 5. Invest on themes, not on economic forecasts.

- "thematic approaches surpass cyclical approaches." (S004)
- "I don't know anyone who's made money trying to listen to an economist to bet on risk markets. It's really the wrong way to invest." (S019)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S002, S003, S004, S009, S012, S019). Confidence: high.
- A crowd recession call is a contrary signal: "When everyone says we're in a recession, guess what? It's not a recession." (S009)

## 6. Read credit before equities; an equity panic with calm credit is a buy.

- "If that's yielding more than equities, then equities are cheap." (S008)
- "credit was actually really well behaved whether you looked at Triple C or high yield or spreads or Bond volatility" (S021)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S003, S008, S011, S019, S020, S021). Confidence: high.

## 7. Do not anchor on a precise earnings model; the multiple and the story drive the price.

- "you can't be grounded by a rigid framework" (S009)
- "if I had to live and by the rules of those spreadsheets, I would have been a terrible equity analyst" (S014)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S003, S009, S014, S017, S021). Confidence: high.
- His rule for reading price: "It's important to let the market tell us what signal versus noise, whereas a lot of people think that they can tell the market what to do." (S017)

## 8. Leverage, not volatility, is what destroys a theme holder; fund with equity.

- "the only way these crypto treasuries get into trouble is if they use leverage" (S009)
- "we wanted to keep the capital structure clean and the whole endeavor was funded with equity, no debt, no convertible instruments." (S014)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S001, S002, S004, S009, S013, S014). Confidence: high.
- Same test for the economy: "The stock market crash is usually a debt problem, you know what I mean?" (S009)

## 9. Say when you have not done the work, and stay inside your coverage.

- "I haven't done the work, so I don't know." (S016)
- "we don't want to stretch beyond our capabilities" (S013)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S003, S011, S013, S016, S017, S020). Confidence: high.
- The cost of the line: Fundstrat does not cover commodities, and he said "I kind of regret not recommending gold because it's been so great." (S016)

## 10. Look past the frontier name and the bottleneck to the downstream beneficiary.

- "actually the history shows downstream did better" (S010)
- "So it was better to feed the worker than to supply the worker." (S003)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S003, S010, S011, S014, S015). Confidence: high.
- On AI in November 2026: "how am I going to make money in AI? It may not be through Anthropic or OpenAI." (S010)

## 11. Expect sharp drawdowns inside a bull market and plan for them; one does not end the thesis.

- "they say bull markets take the escalator up and the elevator down so big Corrections like this are more associated with a bull market than a bare Market" (S021)
- "I'm sure there'll be 50% drawdowns because the stock market has more frequent 25% drawdowns." (S016)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S001, S004, S016, S018, S021). Confidence: high.
- His December 2025 outlook built one in ahead of time: "Our outlook actually does call for a drawdown next year." (S001)

## 12. Treat geopolitical and election scares as noise unless they change growth.

- "you sell the buildup, but you buy the invasion" (S003)
- "it's hard to actually make an investment action unless it's World War III, right?" (S006)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S003, S006, S010, S020, S021). Confidence: high.
- Elections change leadership, not the level: "I think it makes a huge difference on what sectors lead" (S021), while for the index "And if we had A-B testing like a true time machine, the market would be in the same place." (S020)

## 13. Hold a new technology theme as a basket and expect most single names to fail.

- "it wasn't so bad if you held it as a basket" (S001)
- "if you bought a basket of internet stocks, 97% went to zero." (S017)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S001, S003, S016, S017). Confidence: high.
- Winners rotate through the cycle: "Some of the loser categories will turn into winners. Some of the winners will turn to losers" (S003)

## 14. Own a stock only if two or more themes carry it.

- "we only add a stock that appears in more than one theme" (S007)
- "a single theme won't always work at every month in a stock market." (S002)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S002, S007, S016, S018). Confidence: high.

## 15. Do not wait for the all-clear; stocks bottom on bad news and the low is only known later.

- "that's why stocks bottom on bad news, because we know the news will be bad for the next 12 months" (S005)
- "You don't know it's the low until a week later." (S007)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S002, S005, S007, S014). Confidence: high.
- At the index level a retest is rare, so the second chance may not come (S002). Once a bottom looks mostly in on time and price, "unless you're really a genius, you know, it's buying it here is actually going to be at a better price point" (S014)

## 16. Do not stop out of great theme stocks; they reprice in jumps after dead spells.

- "people would have been stopped out of Palantir and Tesla a long time ago" (S009)
- "you should really think of it more as you're buying companies, not just buying the stock market, because then you'll have a lot more conviction about what you own." (S018)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S009, S015, S016, S018). Confidence: high.
- Exception for a small holder of a volatile asset (2024-07-22): "if they have a stop loss of 10%, they can just hold it and sell it if, if they can't tolerate the volatility." (S019)

## 17. Let time do the work: hold for a decade, and buying at highs then stops mattering.

- "So as long as you own something for 10 years, you don't have to worry about buying at the highs." (S012)
- "time is probably the biggest friend of anyone who's going to be an investor" (S017)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S012, S015, S017, S019). Confidence: high.
- He recommends dollar-cost averaging, monthly or on dips (S012), and closed his October 2026 message with Munger: "the big money is not in the buying and selling, but in the waiting" (S015)

## 18. Act on an indicator only at an extreme, and check why it fired before trusting it.

- "when there's extremes, so more than a two standard deviation reading, it's signal" (S008)
- "this is an anomalous inversion, just like 98, and it was not an economic signal" (S008)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S004, S008, S018, S021). Confidence: high.
- On the Sahm rule: "unless we're already in a recession it's a it's a bad signal" (S021)

## 19. Treat a big policy move as real, and count the policy backstops under a drawdown.

- "Our stance at Fundstrat is to treat it like a real move." (S006)
- "There are three puts out there. There's the Fed put." (S007)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S006, S007, S012, S021). Confidence: high.

## 20. Do not call a market expensive without pricing its growth and resilience.

- "when someone says they're a value investor and they're saying stocks are expensive, I know they didn't read his book." (S001)
- "history shows that a period of rising interest rates does not mean PE goes down" (S005)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S001, S004, S005, S016). Confidence: high.
- He means Graham's 12 plus two times growth (S001).

## 21. A theme ends when its innovation ends, not when its growth slows.

- "I'd say the real key is, is AI no longer providing remarkable innovation? And if it doesn't, that's really the end of the cycle." (S010)
- "a business cycle will end, and of course, innovation could end" (S011)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S001, S010, S011). Confidence: high.
- A growth recession is not the end: "Cisco fell from $9 to $5. A year later, it was at $18." (S010)

## 22. Hold conviction while the facts hold; change when they change.

- "conviction is basically riding through the volatility and it's not easy to tell the difference until history has already passed" (S001)
- "stubborn is riding something when and believing in something when all the facts have changed" (S001)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S001, S014, S019). Confidence: high.
- Do not buy a bear story that shows up only after a fall: "that narrative is going to disappear the minute the crypto bull market starts and Ethereum starts going up." (S014)

## 23. Size an early, volatile theme small, and let the young go larger.

- "Our original recommendation was 1%. Today, for newer investors, I would recommend two to five." (S012)
- "I don't have a problem with someone is 25% or 30% crypto, but they're young because time is on their side." (S019)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S009, S012, S019). Confidence: high.
- How it moved: Fundstrat's 2017 advice was 1 to 2% (S009, recalled 2025-08-06). Newer: 2% minimum and 5% for many (S019, 2024-07-22); 2 to 5% for newer investors (S012, 2024-12-12).

## 24. Test a narrow-market claim by earnings contribution; one theme carrying the index is normal.

- "if you measure it by net income contribution, it's pretty broad-based." (S003)
- "the entirety of the market's gain this year is being fueled by earnings coming from this one theme actually does not fly in the face of financial history" (S002)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S002, S003, S006). Confidence: high.
- Narrow leadership matters only when investors are fully invested (S006).

## 25. The next call is the one that counts; never take the victory lap.

- "it's our next call that matters, not our previous set of calls." (S002)
- "pride is the sin before the fall when it comes to investing." (S017)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S002, S008, S017). Confidence: high.

## 26. Score yourself on the size of wins, and accept that likely calls still fail.

- "when we're correct, that the amplitude or the benefit of being right offsets the problems for being wrong." (S017)
- "something that has an 80% chance of happening doesn't prevent this being one of those 20% days that it doesn't happen" (S003)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S001, S003, S017). Confidence: high.

## 27. Pick the most likely view and stick to it; a list of risks with no probabilities is a hedge.

- "unless you can assign a cumulative probability to each one and then say what this aggregate represents, then it's just, it's your hedge." (S006)
- "not confuse people with like three or four different views, but just sort of stick to a thesis or a theme" (S018)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S006, S018). Confidence: medium.

## 28. Cut a holding when its data quality breaks, not because its price fell.

- "Now there was a drawdown with Supermicro, but it already produced a lot of returns for the granny." (S007)
- "the only reason we would be wrong is if these companies run out of money." (S020)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S007, S020). Confidence: medium.
- Inferred from S007, S020: Supermicro left the Granny Shots list over auditing and compliance problems after a strong run, while survivable laggards stayed in.
