# Frameworks: Tom Lee

Built from the verified notes on 21 sources (15 interviews, 5 interviews pulled with the fetch tool, and one BitMine chairman's message, 2019 to 2026). Most-used first. Scoring lines are guidance drawn from his own thresholds and numbers, not a scorecard he published. He has been chairman of BitMine, the largest ETH treasury company, since June 30, 2025 (S002), so his crypto frameworks after that date are those of an interested party. Index and coin levels appear only as dated historical stances that show the arithmetic, never as current calls.

---

## 1. Bubble test: who is bullish, what is borrowed, what is being faked *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his main tool for the seat's question, early or late. A theme is not a bubble because prices rose or one theme carries the index. It is late when behavior, money and models all show excess at once.
- **Parts:** (1) Sentiment: tops come when everyone is bullish and good news stops lifting prices (S001, S009). (2) Spending against income: a capex boom is fine while it drives income growth; it is a bubble when margins shrink or debt outruns revenue (S002). (3) Models: analysts forcing discount rates down or inventing terminal multiples to justify the price (S003, S004). (4) Supply: an IPO frenzy and bankers pushing deals (S004, S018). (5) Leverage: margin debt making a meaningful leap above its prior high (S018); he flagged margin debt up 55% year over year in July 2026 as a warning (S011). (6) Use cases: AI becomes a bubble when use cases are invented with no end market (S010). (7) Aftermath: capital raised in a bubble gets misallocated (S021).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** before adding to any theme stock that has run hard, and whenever someone calls the theme a bubble. Run every part; one metric is not enough (S010).
- **Scoring guidance:** early (high tailwind) = skeptics outnumber believers, no IPO wave in the theme, spending matched by income growth, margin debt below its prior high, mainstream multiples (he called Nvidia at 30 times earnings "not crazy" in June 2025, S003). Middle = margin debt rising fast while fund managers stay underweight (his mid 2026 read, S011). Late (low tailwind) = everyone bullish, good news ignored, discount rates cut to remove all risk, PEs of 100 for a mega cap (S004), an IPO rush, use cases made up.
- **Quote:** "it's only a bubble if spending is not driving income growth. So margins should be shrinking or debt should be rising faster than revenue." (S002)
- **Quote:** "you'll know because everyone's having to fake their model to explain why they're still buying the stock." (S003)
- **How his view moved:** in June 2025 he said of AI "the bubble bursts in AI. And I think that's going to happen for sure." (S003). Each later check found it not yet: "I actually think stocks are still cheap." (S016, 2025-10-23) and "So to me, it's hardly a bubble until it's a bubble." (S014, 2026-08-24). Newest (S010, 2026-11-04): the test is whether invented use cases appear. He expects an eventual bust; he has not dated it.
- **Sources:** 11 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S009, S010, S011, S014, S016, S018, S021)

## 2. Granny Shots: the two-theme screen

- **What it is:** Fundstrat's thematic stock list, later an ETF (GRNY, launched October 2024, S016). Named for Rick Barry's underhand free throw: unglamorous names with the highest odds of beating the market (S005, S018).
- **Parts:** (1) Pick about seven multi-decade themes expected to last 10 to 15 years (S002): AI, millennials, the labor shortage, energy security, cybersecurity, Fed cycles, a seasonal or ISM rotation, and others (S004, S007, S016). (2) Keep only large caps (S016). (3) Keep only stocks that sit on two or more themes, so one theme going quiet does not stall the holding (S002, S007, S016, S018). (4) Filter with a quant model, a technical view and a company check (S007); the monthly super grannies also need price above the 20 and 200 day averages (S005). (5) About 30 to 35 names, equal weight, rebalanced quarterly, under 100% turnover a year (S002, S007, S018). (6) Add a new theme only when it is closest to requiring investment; 2026 candidates were sovereign security, longevity and Generation Alpha (S016). (7) Remove a name when data quality breaks, as with Supermicro (S007).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** first, on any buy. Name the themes the stock sits on and count them.
- **Scoring guidance:** 10 = a large cap tied to two or more live themes, each with a decade or more to run, passing the technical filter. 5 = one strong theme only, or a theme that is real but not yet in large caps. 0 = no theme, an expired theme, or audit and compliance trouble. He accepts that equal-weight rebalancing trims winners like Nvidia as the cost of volatility control (S007, paraphrase).
- **Quote:** "it had to be tied to two themes because we figured two sales means that you can catch the wind from two different directions." (S018)
- **Quote:** "thematic investing explained performance better than macro and stock picking." (S003)
- **Sources:** 9 (S002, S003, S004, S005, S007, S008, S016, S018, S020)

## 3. Early or late in the cycle: business-cycle gauges *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** measures he trusts over the age of an expansion or a recession call. Expansions end when the Fed kills them, not of old age.
- **Parts:** (1) ISM: a long run below 50 means business caution and no cycle peak; once above 50 it tends to stay there for years (S002, S012, S016). (2) Private investment as a share of GDP: late cycle only above 27% and rolling over (S006). (3) Employment to population ratio (S008). (4) Earnings growth: late cycle is single digits (S016). (5) The Fed: if it decides the economy is overheated and pulls liquidity, the bull ends (S018, S021).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to place a cyclical theme (industrials, financials, small caps, housing) and to answer anyone who says the cycle is old.
- **Scoring guidance:** early = ISM below 50 for a long stretch, private investment near 25% of GDP (his 2024 reading, S006), double-digit earnings growth, a Fed that is easing. Late = ISM rolled over after years above 50, private investment above 27% and turning down, single-digit earnings growth, a Fed tightening into strength.
- **Quote:** "we've never been late cycle until this number's over 27% and then rolls over." (S006)
- **Quote:** "expansions don't die of old age um they get killed by the FED right" (S021)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S006, S008, S009, S012, S016, S018, S021)

## 4. Generational cycle: cohort peaks and market tops *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** the size of the 20 to 50 year old cohort drives credit, spending and bull markets. Major tops come at a generation's peak; the gaps between generations are the bear markets.
- **Parts:** (1) Mark each generation's peak: greatest 1930, silent 1974, boomers 1999, Gen X 2018, millennials later (S006, S008). (2) Leverage per person rises from age 20 to 50, so that group is the credit impulse (S008). (3) Add the wealth transfer: millennials inherit tens of trillions, which shifts money from credit toward equities and digital assets (S005, S015, S018). (4) Equity returns accelerate into a peak (S006).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to judge whether a secular bull has room, and to pick the themes the largest young cohort will spend on.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong tailwind = the cohort is still growing and its peak is a decade or more away; weak = within a few years of a generational peak, or in a gap between generations.
- **Quote:** "The gaps between the generations are really where the bear markets exist, and the bull market tops coincide with the peak of a generation." (S018)
- **Quote:** "leverage per person rises from age 20 to 50. So that group is really driving the credit impulse, and it's the GDP multiplier of the economy" (S008)
- **How his view moved:** he placed the millennial peak near 2038 (S006, 2024-09-27) and said "the next major top shouldn't be till 2038" (S007, 2025-03-07). Newer (S018, 2025-08-28): millennials peak about 2035 and "I think we are in a new bull market. I think there's a lot to be excited about and 2035 sounds about right." (S018). He notes immigration can move the peak (S006).
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S005, S006, S007, S008, S015, S017, S018)

## 5. The lens of a 20-year-old *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** judge a new technology by how the youngest users adopt it, not by how older decision-makers feel about it. Wall Street sits a generation away from the adopters, so it underestimates penetration and the durability of a megatrend.
- **Parts:** (1) Find adoption by age (cell phones in the 1990s: about 70% of 20-year-olds versus 5% of 60-year-olds, S001). (2) Assume the young keep the habit as they age, and model penetration from there (S004). (3) Note who is dismissing it and why (incumbent bias, S017; experts dismissed the PC, S006). (4) Map assets to generations: boomers hold gold, millennials hold Bitcoin (S020).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** whenever the bear case is that a product is a fad, a toy or too expensive.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = the young already use it heavily and older investors call it a toy; weak = adoption is driven by older incumbents, or the young are not using it.
- **Quote:** "Most investors underestimate the durability of a megatrend because they're anchored in like how the legacy system works for them." (S019)
- **Quote:** "if 100% of like teenagers have a cell phone, by the time they're 60, the penetration rate should be whatever it is." (S004)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S004, S017, S018, S019, S020, S021)

## 6. Sentiment and positioning at extremes *(Fundstrat cornerstones, in part)*

- **What it is:** read sentiment only at statistical extremes, and read it as contrary.
- **Parts:** (1) AAII bulls minus bears at minus 20 or worse, about two standard deviations, read over a six-month window (S007, S008, S016). (2) VIX term structure: one-month future above the four-month means fear has an expiry date (S007, S008). (3) Option skew at rare readings near the end of a panic (S021). (4) Client surveys: a plurality of bears, cash that has not moved back, and the top worries listed (S005, S006). (5) Slice each indicator into ribbons and act only at the edges; a VIX extreme that flips back is the best signal (S018).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to time adds inside a theme you already like, never to pick the theme.
- **Scoring guidance:** buy signal = AAII at minus 20 or worse, VIX curve inverted, clients bearish with cash idle. Caution = broad bullishness and cash fully deployed. He warns about small samples: "now N equals only seven" (S008).
- **Quote:** "When the AAII bulls less bears is extreme negative, so minus 20 percentage points or worse, 100% of the time, it's a buy signal six months out." (S008)
- **Quote:** "So what the markets have now priced in is that the volatility has an expiration date." (S007)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S005, S006, S007, S008, S016, S018, S021)

## 7. Waterfall declines and the impaired or delayed test

- **What it is:** a waterfall decline is a fall of 10% or more in about two weeks; he counts 12 since 1929. Without a recession they recover in a V, in roughly 1.4 to 1.7 times the time of the fall.
- **Parts:** (1) Measure the speed of the fall; faster falls rebound faster (S002, S003, S020). (2) Ask whether earnings power is impaired or only delayed by a quarter or two (S007). (3) Ask whether a second shock is stacked on the first (a Fed fighting inflation into recession, stagflation, austerity plus a trade war, S007). (4) Check credit and the yield curve for a breaking economy (S011).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** whenever a theme stock or the index falls hard and the question is sell or add.
- **Scoring guidance:** add = fast fall, no recession signal in credit, earnings only delayed, no layered shock. Step back = earnings trajectory impaired, credit spreads blowing out, or a second shock on top.
- **Quote:** "If the trajectory of earnings is impaired, that's different than if you're delaying, you're taking a two-quarter hit because of the tariff uncertainty and stocks might go down on both but one's a huge buying opportunity" (S007)
- **Quote:** "stock market recoveries are symmetric to the decline. So the faster you fall, the faster you rebound." (S020)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S003, S007, S009, S011, S020, S021)

## 8. Labor-shortage technology cycle *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his main secular thesis behind tech spend. When population grows faster than the prime-age workforce, labor runs short and companies must automate; each such period produced a parabolic move in tech. On this reading, tech spend is a necessity, not a bubble, unless the multiple is unjustified.
- **Parts:** (1) Prior cycles: 1948 to 1967 and 1991 to 1999 (S001, S005). (2) Current cycle runs to 2035 (S001, S004). (3) Size the shortage in wages that turn into tech spend, mostly hardware, with the largest share to the chip supplier (S004). (4) Implication: tech can grow toward 40 to 50% of global market weight (S004) and the US becomes the world's labor supplier through tech (S005). (5) Own the company that serves the scarce worker as well as the one that replaces it (S003).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to judge whether an automation, chip, robotics or AI name has a secular tailwind beyond the current product cycle.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = the company sells the capital that replaces scarce labor, and its revenue estimates still assume a normal cycle; weak = its model depends on cheap, plentiful labor.
- **Quote:** "whenever the population growth rate grows faster than the prime age workforce, which means you have compounded labor deficit. You've always had a technology cycle." (S001)
- **Quote:** "close to 40 million worker equivalence. And that's $3 trillion of wages." (S004)
- **How his figures moved:** the start date was 2015 (S004, 2024-06-24; S005, 2023-10-20, where he corrected himself from 2018) and 2018 in the newest telling (S001, 2025-12-12). Size: a global shortage of 80 million (S005), about 40 million worker equivalents by 2030 (S004), almost $4 trillion a year in wages by the end of the decade (S019). These are different measures, not one revised number.
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S006, S018, S019)

## 9. Credit leads equity

- **What it is:** equities are the junior piece of the capital structure, so the bond market and credit say first whether a fall is a recession or a panic.
- **Parts:** (1) High-yield spreads: a recession needs spreads near 800 basis points; in April 2025 they widened only 150 to 200, so he put recession odds near 10% against economists at 60% (S003). (2) High yield's yield to worst as a P/E to compare with equities (S008). (3) A negative calendar year for high yield total return has been followed by positive stocks five of five times (S008). (4) CCC, quality spreads and bond volatility as the panic check (S019, S021). (5) The debt service ratio as the consumer leverage gauge (S004).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** in every sharp selloff, before deciding the theme is broken.
- **Scoring guidance:** hold or add = equities panicking while credit is calm; step back = high yield blowing out toward recession levels. He notes false signals when one sector dominates high yield (energy in 2015 and 2016, S008).
- **Quote:** "equities are the junior piece of the capital structure, which means that economics and credit lead what equity should do" (S008)
- **Quote:** "the reason we stayed bullish into the April low was because high yield said the chance of recession is probably 10%, whereas the economists were saying it was 60" (S003)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S004, S008, S011, S019, S020, S021)

## 10. Relabel the business: re-rating when the label changes *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** low multiples often come from boxing a company into a lazy one-line label. When a business systematizes or adopts a new technology, its label and multiple can change.
- **Parts:** (1) Write the one-sentence label the market uses (Apple as a handset or hardware company, S018, S020). (2) Ask whether the economics now match a different sector (Walmart and Costco rerated from staples multiples, S013). (3) Swap labels to test a bubble claim: would you call it a bubble if these were financial institutions? (S001). (4) His live case: financials adopting AI and blockchain cut staff, look like tech and may trade on tech multiples (S001, S002, S005, S016, S018).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** when a stock looks cheap on its sector multiple but its business now runs like a different sector.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = results already look like the new sector but the stock still trades on the old multiple; weak = the new label is a story with no change in margins or headcount.
- **Quote:** "I think people put low multiples because it's like the way people said Apple's a handset company, so they put a 10 multiple on it forever." (S018)
- **Quote:** "financials business results look more like tech companies now, but they still trade like financials." (S016)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S005, S013, S016, S018, S020)

## 11. Base rates and history first *(Fundstrat evidence-based research)*

- **What it is:** the first pillar of his process. Say nothing history, correlation or probability cannot support, then give the base rate before the opinion.
- **Parts:** (1) Find the historical analog and its count (June positive 11 of 11 times after a set pattern, S004; Fed cuts with no recession, seven of seven higher a year later with a 16% median, S006; midterms followed by a 12-month rally every time, S010; PMIs rising from below 47, industrials up 22% on average with a 95% win ratio since 1950, S005). (2) Stocks bottom about 12 months before earnings (S005). (3) Note the dispersion around the average (year three of a bull has wide distributions, S007).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to set odds before arguing the story, and to rebut a claim with the record.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = a long sample with a high hit rate that matches today's setup; weak = small samples or a pattern with wide dispersion.
- **Quote:** "we really don't want to say something unless history can show it or we can show some correlation or probabilities" (S008)
- **Quote:** "When the PMIs are rising from below 47, which is where we are, industrial stocks, again, since 1950, have an average of 22% gain and they have a win ratio of 95%." (S005)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S005, S006, S007, S008, S010, S011)

## 12. Exponential-industry template: wireless and the internet *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his map of how a new technology pays investors, built from covering wireless from the 1990s. The industry can be right and most of its stocks still wrong.
- **Parts:** (1) The industry compounds for decades (wireless about 40% a year, 1990 to 2015, S003). (2) All parts of the ecosystem peak relative to the market about one third into the cycle, rise together and crash together (S003, S016). (3) Nascent technologies waste capital early; a shakeout follows (S016). (4) About ten years in, one or two groups break out and capture the value (towers and Apple in wireless, S003). (5) Growth industries have growth recessions along the way (Cisco 1997, six growth cycles in mobile, S010). (6) The basket still wins even if bought near the peak (internet basket bought in 1999, S001), while most single names fail (S001, S017).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to place an AI, crypto or other new-tech name in its cycle: before the broad peak, in the shakeout, or among the survivors.
- **Scoring guidance:** early = adoption still small, the whole group rising, before the one-third mark (own a basket); late = the broad group has peaked relative to the market and the shakeout has begun; the late chapter pays only in the one or two groups pulling away. He put AI winners at about 10% of AI stocks (S001).
- **Quote:** "But then 10 years into the cycle, two groups broke out of wireless and captured the value." (S003)
- **Quote:** "if you bought the internet, the internet basket in 99 okay so you bought it near the peak and you held it to today you actually still outperformed the S&P 500" (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S006, S010, S016, S017)

## 13. Narrative times multiple *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** price is earnings times a narrative multiple. Over the intermediate term the narrative moves a stock far more than an earnings beat, so time spent perfecting the E is mostly wasted. Great assets reprice in step functions after long dead spells.
- **Parts:** (1) Ask which E, which horizon, and how it is discounted (S001). (2) Ask what narrative sets the multiple and whether it is changing (S003). (3) Expect valuations to be dynamic, not fixed by a model (S016, S017). (4) Expect step moves after dormancy (Nvidia, Palantir, Tesla, S009).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** when the case for or against a theme stock rests on a precise earnings model.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = a narrative that is still widening while earnings keep up; weak = the case needs one exact future earnings number. When he does give an index number, the arithmetic is forward earnings times a multiple: in June 2024, about 12 to 15% a year of earnings growth plus 5% of multiple expansion, which he said could put the S&P near 15,000 by 2030 (S004); in July 2026, about 400 of 2027 earnings at 20 times for 8,000 (S011). These are dated stances, not current calls.
- **Quote:** "it takes a whole lot of E to offset PE" (S003)
- **Quote:** "you should divorce your belief that an economic model explains asset prices." (S014)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S004, S009, S014, S016, S017, S011 for the target arithmetic); 6 counting framework uses only (S001, S003, S009, S014, S016, S017)

## 14. Unkillability: the stress-test premium

- **What it is:** if businesses keep growing earnings through shocks that should have been kill shots, they deserve a higher multiple, not a lower one.
- **Parts:** (1) Count the shocks absorbed: COVID, the supply-chain bullwhip, the fastest inflation, the fastest Fed hikes, tariffs, a war (S001, S002, S005, S012). (2) Check earnings through them (S005, S020). (3) Conclude the multiple should drift up, and that heavy skepticism after such shocks is fuel (S001, S004).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** when a company or the market looks expensive after a run of crises it came through with earnings intact.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = earnings grew through two or more major shocks and investors are still nervous; weak = earnings fell with each shock.
- **Quote:** "that's the unkillability, right? We've thrown a lot of things at businesses." (S005)
- **Quote:** "this is like putting companies to the ultimate stress test. And if they were able to grow earnings or revenues, they're amazing businesses." (S020)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S012, S020)

## 15. Network value and the penetration S-curve *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** for a network asset, value tracks users and activity per user. The drivers change as penetration grows.
- **Parts:** (1) Model value on active users times activity per user; his 2017 Bitcoin work said this explained about 87% to 97% of the move in different periods (S003, S009, S019, S021). (2) Count active users, not holders; a small base means doubling users can quadruple network value (S017). (3) Expect different drivers from 0.5% to 5% penetration than from 5% to 50% (S017). (4) Watch who the incremental buyer is: retail first, institutions once the network is big enough (S017, S004).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on platforms, payment networks and crypto assets where users are the value driver.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = low penetration, active users rising, institutions just starting to arrive; weak = high penetration with activity per user flat.
- **Quote:** "what influences valuation from the 0.5% to 5% penetration is not going to be the same influences when it's going from 5% to 50%" (S017)
- **Quote:** "when you look at growth businesses, current size versus future growth determine upside price." (S017)
- **How his view moved:** in 2019 he said institutions would wait until network value was a trillion and stayed there (S017). Newer: BlackRock's entry "pretty much invalidates the idea that this is just a bunch of people in their basements playing with, you know, digital money." (S004, 2024-06-24), and by October 2026 "It's institutional, unlike the prior waves." (S015)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S004, S009, S017, S019, S021)

## 16. Tokenization and the 1971 moment *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** his crypto theme after 2025. As Wall Street rebuilt finance on the synthetic dollar after 1971, it now rebuilds on blockchains, which he expects to be mostly Ethereum. He says this outweighs stablecoins, and calls it an institutional wave.
- **Parts:** (1) Addressable market: about 200 trillion of global liquid assets, 160 trillion in stocks, bonds and credit; each dollar tokenized creates about a dollar of layer-one value; 10% on chain gives 16 trillion (S015). (2) Layer-one value floor: about a third of value locked (S016). (3) Complementary, not rival, to Bitcoin (S013). (4) Stablecoins as the first killer app and a buyer of Treasuries (S003). (5) Factorization: splitting a stock into tranches or revenue lines (S016). (6) Regulation inviting Wall Street on chain (S002).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on exchanges, banks, custodians, layer-one assets and treasury companies, to size the market they may serve.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = an institution is actually moving assets on chain and the asset or firm earns from the settlement layer; weak = retail-only use, as in the ICO and NFT waves (S015). Treat his Ethereum views with care: he chairs the largest ETH treasury.
- **Quote:** "So Ethereum is experiencing that same 1971 moment where Wall Street's rebuilding everything onto a blockchain, which we think it'll be Ethereum." (S016)
- **Quote:** "So if 10% moves on-chain, that creates a $16 trillion market." (S015)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S003, S013, S014, S015, S016)

## 17. Macro attribution *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** split a return into macro, industry and stock-specific parts before debating the story. Macro plus industry is over 80% of a stock and about 90% in crypto.
- **Parts:** (1) Start with the macro (S014). (2) Size the high-beta theme against the index: if the S&P falls 20, Bitcoin can fall 40 (S016). (3) Expect the speculative theme not to move until the broad market does (S017, S019, S020). (4) Do not blame company actions for a macro decline (S014).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** when a theme stock is falling and the debate is about its narrative.
- **Scoring guidance:** hold = the decline matches its macro and industry beta; re-check = it falls far more than its beta explains.
- **Quote:** "In crypto, when I looked at these cycles, I might say the attribution is 90% macro." (S014)
- **Quote:** "Bitcoin isn't going to go anywhere until the S&P breaks out." (S017)
- **Sources:** 6 (S014, S016, S017, S019, S020, S021)

## 18. Downstream winners *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** the first-wave frontier names and the bottleneck suppliers may not be the ten-year winners. Second-wave, downstream companies that use the cheap new input often do better.
- **Parts:** (1) Find the bottleneck investors crowd into because growth is visible (S011). (2) Ask who uses the input once it is cheap (the second owner of the hotel, Expedia and streaming after the fiber bust, S003). (3) Ask who serves the scarce worker rather than who supplies them (Domino's over Robert Half, S003). (4) Look for downstream stories of a dominant theme (crypto and software downstream of AI, S014, S015).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** when the bull case is "the AI leader" or "the pick and shovel", and when a theme looks crowded.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = a company whose costs fall or whose demand rises because of the theme, still priced as a non-theme stock; weak = a frontier name whose value depends on staying the leader through the shakeout.
- **Quote:** "the long-term winners might actually be second wave companies you know downstream companies" (S010)
- **Quote:** "it was better to feed the worker than to supply the worker." (S003)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S010, S011, S014, S015)

## 19. Human intensity: who gains from AI and blockchain *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** the more human-intensive a business, the more AI or blockchain can lift its margins and its multiple.
- **Parts:** (1) Market value per employee: the Mag 7 above 20 million, restaurants about 25,000 (S012). (2) Revenue per worker rising is the productivity check 1999 lacked (S010). (3) Biggest cost is people: a bank on a blockchain might need a fraction of its staff (S013, S018). (4) Spending must replace real work somewhere (S001).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** to find the non-tech companies with the most to gain from a tech theme.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = low market value per employee, labor the largest cost, and visible adoption; weak = already lean, or AI spend not replacing any work.
- **Quote:** "look at market value of a stock divided by the number of employees, because it's a measure of how human intensive a company is" (S012)
- **Quote:** "it's only justifiable if it's replacing real work somewhere else" (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S010, S012, S013, S018)

## 20. Rates and the P/E band *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** rising yields do not automatically cut multiples. Within a middle band, higher yields go with higher P/Es, because rates raise barriers to entry and pay cash-rich companies interest.
- **Parts:** (1) Below the band, P/E falls as yields fall. (2) In the band, P/E rises with yields. (3) Above it, de-rate (S004, S018). (4) Earnings acceleration can lift P/E while rates rise, as from 1940 to 1967 (S016).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** when the bear case on a theme is "rates are rising".
- **Scoring guidance:** fine = 10-year yield inside the band; caution = yield moving to the top of it. In 2025 he put the de-rating zone at 5 to 6% (S018).
- **Quote:** "history shows that a period of rising interest rates does not mean PE goes down" (S005)
- **How his band moved:** "between 4 and 7%, it is positively correlated. So when interest rates go up, PE rises." (S004, 2024-06-24). Newer: "But between 2% and 6%, higher yields are associated with rising PE." (S018, 2025-08-28), with "I'd say between 5% and 6%, you know, you have to start derating the stock market." (S018)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S005, S016, S018)

## 21. Technicals for turning points *(unnamed)*

## 22. Asset-based valuation and asymmetry for treasury companies *(unnamed)*

- **What it is:** a treasury company is valued on its balance sheet, like Exxon on reserves, not on earnings. He built it for BitMine, so it is his own company's case.
- **Parts:** (1) Start at one times net asset value and add the capitalized staking yield (S009). (2) Add premiums for velocity (how fast asset per share grows), liquidity and scarcity (S009). (3) Set a downside case at the year's low and an upside case from an earlier ETH to BTC ratio, then compare (S009). (4) Raise equity only above NAV; each week choose between buying the asset, buying back stock or holding, by expected return (S014). (5) No leverage (S009, S014).
- **When to use it on a stock decision:** on any company whose value is mostly an asset it holds.
- **Scoring guidance:** strong = asset per share rising, equity issued above NAV, no debt; weak = trading below NAV with leverage or exotic structures.
- **Quote:** "crypto treasuries are just the new Exxon" (S009)
- **Quote:** "So I think we judge really what is the expected future return of our deployment of that capital." (S014)
