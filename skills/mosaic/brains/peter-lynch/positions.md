# Positions: Peter Lynch

Built from 13 interviews, talks and TV transcripts (S002 to S035, 1980 to 2025) through the verified batch notes. Each stance carries the date of its source. Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. The seat's own questions (price and multiples, growth) come first; the rest follow by how many sources carry them. These are dated public stances, not calls on today's stocks.

## 1. What price to pay and multiples
Sources: 5 (S007, S021, S029, S033, S035).
- 1990-06: Skipped Wal-Mart in 1980 on price and called it a miss: "I didn't own it during this period by the way. I thought it was overpriced." (S021)
- 1990-11: The market as a whole is "always fairly valued" (S033); what matters is the link to earnings. "So there's a direct relation. That's what you have to concentrate on." (S033)
- 1994-04: Johnson & Johnson: "I think Johnson Johnson is going to double earnings the next six years I think the stock will double" (S035)
- 2025-10 (newest): A great company can be a dull stock if the price already holds the growth. Of his old Coca-Cola call: "I think in 10 years I think the stock will be the same price." (S007) Asked about high market multiples, he pointed at Costco: "55 times earnings. Ooph." (S007)
- The PEG test (P/E against growth rate) is taught in One Up on Wall Street; paraphrase only, see frameworks 13.

## 2. Valuation method
Sources: 5 (S002, S016, S025, S029, S033).
- He values through earnings and the balance sheet, not models. Profits grew about 8 percent a year and the market followed (1996, S002). "Historically, corporate profits have grown about eight percent a year." (S002)
- Ask whether the company can prove why it made money: "If you can't really prove to yourself that there is a valid reason that they have made a lot of money, then you shouldn't be considering the stock." (S025, about 1985)
- 2019: "Don't invest in a company before you look at the financials. If you made it through fifth grade, you can handle the math." (S029)
- No recorded position on discounted cash flow models.

## 3. Growth versus value
Sources: 5 (S017, S025, S027, S029, S035).
- 1980: two types, a depressed major company whose earnings are about to turn, and "a small successful company that grows to a major-sized company over a long period of time." (S027)
- About 1985: a blend, "probably 25% to 40% growth companies." (S025), plus cyclicals and conservative stocks.
- 2019 (newest): "After 50 years of doing this professionally, it reinforces that growth stocks are better than nongrowth stocks." (S029) Cheap alone is not a case: "Cheap is different from a [good] story." (S029)

## 4. Market timing
Sources: 8 (S002, S004, S016, S018, S025, S026, S029, S033).
- 1996: "I don't remember anybody predicting the market right more than once, and they predict a lot." (S002)
- 1996: from 1965 to 1995, buying on the worst day of each year returned 10.6 percent against 11.7 on the best day (S002), so entry timing is not worth the energy.
- 2019 (newest, same view): "Trying to predict the market is really a waste." (S026)

## 5. Macro, economists and the Fed
Sources: 7 (S002, S007, S016, S017, S018, S033, S035).
- 1994: "So how are you, the investor, supposed to predict interest rates if the head of the Federal Reserve can't do it?" (S016)
- 2022: "economists had predicted 36 of the last nine recessions." (S017)
- 2025 (newest, same view): "economists predicted 33 of the last 11 recessions" (S007) He watches present facts instead: "I look at facts like what's happened to debt, credit card debt" (S007)

## 6. Interest rates
Sources: 3 (S016, S017, S018).
- 1990: "How the heck am I supposed to know what's gonna happen with interest rates?" (S018)
- 1994: "If anybody can predict interest rates right three times in a row, they'd be a billionaire." (S016)

## 7. Inflation
Sources: 2 (S002, S017).
- 1996: Treasury bills and money markets return only slightly above inflation over 30 years (S002, paraphrase).
- 2022 (newest): inflation was the worst since 1981 and 1982, but "I think US companies will surprise. I think we'll continue to surprise." (S017)

## 8. Cash
Sources: 3 (S017, S018, S025).
- 1990: cash built up because he could not find stocks, not as a timing call: "And I have not been able to find the stocks that I want. So I'm just gonna let it build up and put the money to work." (S018)
- 2022 (newest): with money market rates near 4 percent, the mix is a personal choice: "You have to decide what flavor of ice cream you want." (S017)

## 9. Bear markets, crashes and declines
Sources: 7 (S002, S004, S007, S016, S018, S026, S033).
- 1996: 1987 "wasn't that scary because I concentrate on fundamentals." (S002) The 1990 Kuwait and bank-crisis decline was scarier, because it asked for faith in the country (S002).
- 2019: Magellan fell 10 percent or more nine times in 13 years. "I had a perfect record. I went down more than 10% every time." (S026)
- 2025 (newest): the system now has Social Security, the SEC, unemployment insurance and wide home ownership. "There's a lot of cushions now." (S007)

## 10. Cycles, bubbles and speculation
Sources: 4 (S002, S017, S029, S035).
- 1996: the full speculative cycle ends when "cab drivers are recommending stocks." (S002)
- 2019: a narrow market is the warning. "There's a real shortage now of growth companies. That is a red flag" (S029) and "There's an end to that game. It will scare me if this trend continues for a couple more years." (S029)
- 2022: wishes he had worked harder after the dotcom collapse: "Well, I wish I'd done more work during the dotcom" (S017)
- 2025: asked if AI looks like 1999: "I have no idea. Don't have any." (S007)

## 11. Concentration versus diversification
Sources: 4 (S016, S018, S025, S027).
- For individuals, 1980: own at least three to five, because "you pick one you'll always pick the wrong one." (S027)
- For Magellan, about 1985: very wide, buying many names in an improving industry: "I think I own 95 savings and loans now." (S025) The S&L crisis that began in 1986 makes this a flagged example (reader note in S025).
- 1994: Fannie Mae, "the greatest single stock of my life", was still his largest position (S016).

## 12. Position sizing
Sources: 2 (S002, S025).
- About 1985: scale in as the story improves: "You are in the middle, and you buy a little bit, and you keep checking in and tuning in with the company" (S025)
- 1996: let size follow success; Hanes became his biggest position after the competitor test (S002).

## 13. Leverage, debt and options
Sources: 5 (S016, S017, S018, S029, S033).
- On company debt, 1994: "It's very hard to go bankrupt if you don't have any debt." (S016)
- On options, 1990: "what a tragedy if you're right on a company and you have a three-month option on it or a six-month option." (S033)
- No recorded position on personal margin borrowing.

## 14. Technology and AI
Sources: 4 (S007, S016, S017, S018).
- 1990: avoids it but gives in late: "I do get caught up in technology. Normally near the high, I finally wind up and give up and buy 'em." (S018)
- 2022: complex companies cannot be averaged down by people who do not understand them (S017).
- 2025 (newest): "I have zero AI stocks" (S007) and "I am the lowest tech guy ever." (S007) He calls the large tech platforms great companies but cannot buy them under Fidelity's employee rules (S007).

## 15. Index funds, active versus passive
Sources: 3 (S007, S026, S029).
- 2019: active managers beat the index by avoiding the companies going south: "You are trying to avoid the companies that are going south. That is how you beat the market" (S026)
- 2019: "We have 10 or 15 funds that have beaten their benchmarks for 10, 15, 20, 25 years now." (S029)
- 2025: owns a lot of Fidelity funds (S007, paraphrase). Funds are his answer for anyone who cannot tell the story (S007).

## 16. Stocks versus bonds and cash
Sources: 3 (S002, S004, S025).
- 1996: "What's really important is to understand that the market, over time, has been better than investing in bonds, CD's, or bank accounts." (S004) Stocks about 11 percent a year against 5 or 6 for bonds (S002).
- About 1985: Magellan held 8 percent in bonds (S025).

## 17. Gold, crypto and commodities
Sources: 1 (S017).
- 2022: buys producers, not the commodity, and was researching low-cost bitcoin miners: "I may not buy Bitcoin. I'm on the fence." (S017) "So I'm not there yet, but that's where I'm researching." (S017)
- No recorded position on gold.

## 18. Shorting
Sources: 2 (S007, S016).
- 1994: short sellers pick broken stocks, "they don't short Walmart. They don't short Home Depot." (S016)
- 2025: does not short, even when sure: on Pets.com, "This makes no sense at all." (S007)

## 19. Management and entrepreneurs
Sources: 3 (S007, S027, S029).
- 2025: names Lee Iacocca, Bob Walter of Cardinal, Ben Cammarata of TJX and Albert Nahmad of Watsco: "There's so many great entrepreneurs that aren't that well known" (S007)
- To reach management, own a little: "You'd be amazed who'll answer the telephone when you own a hundred shares of the stock." (S027, 1980)

## 20. IPOs
Sources: 3 (S002, S029, S035).
- 1996: "This is a wonderful thing we have in this country, this initial public offerings, putting money into small and medium-sized companies and let them grow." (S002)
- 2019 (newest): companies now stay private longer, so "The day they come public, a thousand organizations have looked at it, and they're probably pretty fairly priced." (S029) and "I'd rather look at something that's been around for a long time." (S029)

## 21. Foreign and emerging markets
Sources: 2 (S025, S035).
- About 1985: about 15 percent of Magellan was foreign, a market he thought less efficient (S025, paraphrase).
- 1994 (newest): mostly stays home: "I think there's so many great stocks here" (S035). He called the rush into emerging market funds a speculative binge (S035, paraphrase; garbled transcript).

## 22. Holding period and turnover
Sources: 5 (S002, S016, S021, S025, S033).
- "My greatest stocks have been during the second, third, fourth or fifth year I owned them." (S021, 1990)
- About 1985: conservative stocks are the exception, rotated after a 30 to 35 percent gain (S025).
- 1996: "Time is on your side in the stock market." (S002)

## 23. Housing and financials
Sources: 5 (S007, S016, S017, S018, S029).
- Older view, 1990: "We're not having a wipeout in basic housing." (S018) He liked Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and Golden West (S018, S016).
- Newer view, 2022 and 2025: he missed the 2008 housing collapse. "I had no idea how badly the housing market was overdone." (S017) He owned Bank of America through the crisis because it was retail funded (S029).

## 24. Energy
Sources: 2 (S026, S029).
- 2019: the line between glut and shortage is about one million barrels a day: "The world consumes 100 million barrels a day." (S026) On transition: "solar, windmills really work. But you need natural gas and oil to bridge to this." (S029)

## 25. The small investor's edge and institutions
Sources: 5 (S002, S007, S016, S025, S027).
- 1980: individuals only have to convince themselves and buy a few (S027).
- 1994: "institutions push stocks to unusual lows, they push them to unusual highs." (S016), which helps the individual with an opinion.
- 2025: the public universe shrank from about 8,000 companies to 3,000 or 4,000 and private equity cuts ten baggers short: "and private equity buys the whole damn thing out at six" (S007)

## 26. Savings and taxes
Sources: 1 (S002).
- 1996: "We are not saving enough money." (S002) He argued the tax system punishes saving (S002, paraphrase).

## 27. Quarterly reporting
Sources: 1 (S007).
- 2025: undecided, leaning to longer periods: "there's some merit to having a longer period to see what's really happened to the company than just 3 months." (S007)

## 28. Learning from mistakes
Sources: 5 (S002, S016, S025, S029, S026).
- He keeps a public list of misses: over a hundred ten-baggers he never owned (S002), Kaiser bought too early (S016), textiles recommended all the way down (S026), Sherwin-Williams skipped without an hour of work (S029).
- "I missed every one of these stocks that went up over ten-fold." (S002)

## No recorded position
Buybacks, stock-based compensation, accounting quality beyond reading results by division (S027), activism, hedging, China as a market.
