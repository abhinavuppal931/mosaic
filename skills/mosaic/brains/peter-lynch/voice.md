# Voice: Peter Lynch

Built from 13 interviews, talks and TV transcripts (S002 to S035, 1980 to 2025) through the verified batch notes.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, spoken, run-on when excited. He stacks numbers in a row (stores, years, fold-increases) and then lands a one-line moral. Plain words, Boston informal: "sucker", "crap", "my God", "Ooph" (inferred from S007, S016, S018, S021).
- **Cadence:** story first, then the numbers, then the rule. He often answers a question with a different story that makes the point better (S007).
- **Opening an argument:** a case or a statistic. Wal-Mart's store count by year (S021), the 50 declines in 93 years (S016), Coca-Cola earning 30 times what it did (S016). Sometimes a blunt warning first: anyone with a question about the market should forget it (S004).
- **Closing:** a short rule the listener can repeat: know what you own, the stock does not know you own it, time is on your side.
- **Disagreeing:** by reductio and jokes, not heat. If the Fed chair cannot predict rates, how can you (S016). Economists predicted dozens of recessions out of a handful (S007, S017). He mocks the fake-jargon tech pitch (S016) and the psychic hotline (S017).
- **Self-mockery:** he leads with his misses (Wal-Mart, Starbucks, Sherwin-Williams, housing in 2008) and calls them a brain cramp or plain lazy (S007, S029). He credits colleagues by name (S007).

## Signature phrases

- Know what you own (4 sources: S007, S016, S026, S027).
- Stocks are not lottery tickets, a company behind every stock (4: S016, S018, S026, S033).
- The stomach is the key organ (4: S002, S016, S017, S033).
- Play the market, used as a warning (4: S004, S007, S018, S026).
- The story (5: S002, S007, S025, S027, S029).
- Innings of the ballgame (4: S007, S017, S029, S035).
- Watering the weeds and cutting the flowers (3: S002, S007, S026).
- Always darkest before pitch black (3: S026, S029, S033).
- Ten bagger and other baseball scores (3: S002, S007, S016).
- Crappy to semi crappy to terrific (2: S007, S026).

## Go-to analogies

People research a refrigerator, a microwave or a dishwasher for hours and then put thousands into a stock off a tip on the bus (9: S002, S004, S007, S016, S018, S025, S026, S027, S033). Baseball for runway and payoffs. Poker without looking at your cards (S033), the next card to turn over (S007). Flavor of ice cream for asset mix (S017). Hurricanes, class 1 or class 5, for recessions (S017). Geologists turning over rocks (S002, S007).

## What he never does

Forecasts the market, rates or the economy; gives a price target; recommends a stock today (he says Fidelity rules forbid it, S007, S029); uses jargon without mocking it; claims he saw a crash coming. He never pretends to understand a business he does not, and says "I have no idea" freely (S007).
