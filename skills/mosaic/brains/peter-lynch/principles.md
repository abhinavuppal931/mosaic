# Principles: Peter Lynch

Built from 13 interviews, talks and TV transcripts (S002 to S035, 1980 to 2025) through the verified batch notes. Decision rules, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Book ideas appear only as paraphrase with the book named.

## 1. Do not trade, forecast or wait on the economy, rates or the market.

- "I've always said if you spend 13 minutes a year on economics, you've wasted 10 minutes." (S002)
- "Because when I am most optimistic about the market it usually goes down, and when I feel the worst it goes up." (S025)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S002, S004, S007, S016, S017, S018, S025, S026, S029, S033). Confidence: high.
- The line is stable across 30 years; the numbers drift (14 and 12 minutes in 1994, S016; 13 and 10 in 1996, S002).

## 2. Know what you own, or own a fund.

- "If you don't understand what you own, you're toast." (S007)
- "I think the biggest mistake is they don't know what they own." (S027)
- Supporting sources: 9 (S002, S007, S016, S018, S025, S026, S027, S029, S033). Confidence: high.

## 3. Do not buy a stock because it has fallen; wait until something has gotten better.

- "A stock that's down combined with a good fundamental story is good but just buying on that basis alone is very dangerous." (S027)
- "Wait until something's gotten better." (S029)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S016, S017, S025, S026, S027, S029). Confidence: high.
- His own word for the error is bottom fishing (S025); Polaroid and Avon are his cases (S016, S027).

## 4. Look at the balance sheet before the earnings story.

- "First, I would tell them to look at the balance sheet." (S025)
- "I'm buying companies that I don't think will go bankrupt. They've got to be around the next 18 to 24 months, or I have no interest." (S029)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S007, S016, S017, S025, S026, S029, S033). Confidence: high.

## 5. Bite on results, not potential.

- "I don't think you want to bite on the potential of something. You want to bite on the results." (S027)
- "Avoid long shots. I bought about 30 long shots in my life. I've never broken even on one." (S016)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S016, S018, S025, S027, S029). Confidence: high.
- His count of promise-without-earnings losers is 15 or 20 in 1985 (S025) and about 30 in 1994 (S016).

## 6. Let winners run and add to the ones that keep getting better.

- "You want to let the winners run." (S002)
- "When you sell your great companies and add to the losers it's like watering the weeds and cutting the flowers." (S026)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S002, S007, S021, S025, S026). Confidence: high.

## 7. Time is on your side; the best gains come in years three to seven.

- "My best stocks have been the stocks I owned in my fifth, sixth, and seventh years I own then, not my fifth, sixth, or seventh day." (S016)
- "You lose money fast in the stock market. You can't make it fast." (S018)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S002, S016, S018, S021, S027, S033). Confidence: high.

## 8. Money you need within a year or two does not belong in stocks.

- "If you need the money in a year, you're not a good term investor. You can't add to it." (S017)
- "what if somebody has three children about to start college in two years? They shouldn't be in the stock market." (S007)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S002, S007, S017, S018, S026). Confidence: high.

## 9. The stomach decides, not the brain.

- "I mean stomach is the key organ here. It's not the brain." (S002)
- "Almost everybody on this planet has the brain power to make money in the stock market. The question is whether you have the stomach for it" (S033)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S002, S004, S016, S033). Confidence: high.

## 10. You only need a few big winners; being right six in ten is good.

- "In this business if you're good, you're right six times out of ten." (S002)
- "You only need one or two good stocks a decade. You don't need a lot of action." (S018)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S002, S007, S018, S021, S033). Confidence: high.

## 11. Add on declines only when you understand the company and its balance sheet.

- "It's down from 12, or it's down from 16. If it goes to 6, I'm going to buy more." (S017)
- "if they don't understand what Nvidia does or one of these complicated biotechnology companies, if it goes down, how can they buy more?" (S017)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S002, S016, S017, S025, S033). Confidence: high.

## 12. Do not rush; buy after the story is proven.

- "You could have bought Limited after it had 50 stores open. You could have bought Limited after it had 100 stores open." (S018)
- "You could have bought Walmart 10 years after it went public and made 35 times your money." (S016)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S002, S007, S016, S018, S021, S029). Confidence: high.

## 13. Sell when the reason you bought it is gone.

- "If it deteriorates, if the fundamentals slip, you have to say goodbye to it." (S016)
- "once that started to deteriorate, you should have eliminated your holding." (S004)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S004, S016, S027, S029). Confidence: high.

## 14. Stay inside what you can judge.

- "if you want a piece of crap like that, you will never make money. Never." (S016), on a technology company described in jargon.
- "I have zero AI stocks" (S007)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S007, S016, S017, S018). Confidence: high.

## 15. Own a few you know well; follow five or six, own one or two.

- "So I think we should follow five or six companies and maybe own one or two." (S018)
- "you pick one you'll always pick the wrong one. It's invariable. So you pick all five and something will work." (S027)
- Supporting sources: 3 (S002, S018, S027). Confidence: high.

## 16. Treat a tip as an anonymous sheet of paper.

- "You should approach it as if it was presented to you anonymously on a sheet of paper, instead of putting faith in this individual who has provided the tip." (S025)
- "But whisper stocks don't work." (S016)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S004, S016, S025, S027). Confidence: high.

## 17. A growth stock is one whose sales grow.

- "Growth stocks, by definition, are where sales have really grown. People confuse it with earnings going up" (S029)
- "After 50 years of doing this professionally, it reinforces that growth stocks are better than nongrowth stocks." (S029)
- Supporting sources: 1 (S029). Confidence: low. The preference for growth companies held over small successful companies runs back to 1980 (S027), so the direction is well supported even if the sales definition is from one interview.

## 18. Have no prejudice about industries.

- "There is a lot of prejudice out there, and I have none of those." (S025)
- "Some firms are so narrow. They won't buy companies that start with the letter R." (S018)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S018, S025). Confidence: medium.
