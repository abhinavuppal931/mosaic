# Stories: Aswath Damodaran

Built from the verified batch notes on 100 sources (blog posts 2012-02 to 2026-11, papers 2005 to 2012). These are the cases he returns to, ordered by how many sources carry them. Losers, sells that came too early and misses count as much as wins. Prices and values are his dated public numbers, not calls on today's stocks.

1. **Tesla, the company he kept underestimating (2013 to 2026).** Valued at $67 against $168 in 2013 (S005), called overvalued through 2018 (S029, S034, S035), then bought in June 2019 after the price halved: "That day may be here, as I put in a limit buy order at $180/share, knowing fully well that, if I do end up as a shareholder, this company will test my patience and sanity." (S041) He sold at $640 in January 2020 when price passed his $427 value and watched it pass $900 (S051, S063, S070), bought again and sold again in early 2023 (S077, S079). Lesson he draws: "Through its lifetime, I have under estimated Tesla's value" (S063), and "I keep returning to the scene of the crime." (S070) Sources: 13 (S005, S029, S034, S035, S041, S051, S063, S070, S071, S077, S079, S082, S091).

2. **Facebook and Meta, overpriced at the IPO, bought after the fall (2012 to 2026).** Valued at about $71 billion before the 2012 IPO and would not buy at the ask (S001); bought at $18 after the collapse: "it became a bargain just three months later, when the price hit $18, making me a buyer of the stock" (S096). Sold at $45 and watched it reach $95 (S012, S017). Bought again in 2018 after Cambridge Analytica on optionality (S033, S061) and doubled up in November 2022: "I made an exception to my rule of not doubling down and doubled my holding of Facebook on November 4, 2022, because its valuation looks compelling" (S069). Lesson: a pariah with a valuable user base can be a buy, and a governance risk is one you price, not one you argue with. Sources: 11 (S001, S012, S017, S033, S061, S067, S068, S069, S082, S087, S096).

3. **Apple, bought and sold around value four times (2012 to 2018).** A long-held, admitted-bias position (S002); sold at $600 in 2012, bought at $440 in 2013, sold at $128 in 2015 when price beat value by about 10%: "That turned out to be a lucky break, because the momentum shifted (as it does in pricing games)" (S012). Back in at around $93 in 2016: "I am an Apple shareholder again." (S020) Sold at $220 on 2018-09-21 when it passed value (S038). Lesson: value investing means trading around value, not holding forever. Sources: 8 (S002, S012, S020, S021, S036, S037, S038, S087).

4. **Nvidia, the big winner he sold in halves (2018 to 2025).** Bought in 2018 after a drawdown of more than 50% when AI was "not even a speck in my imagination" (S072). Sold half near $400 to $450 in summer 2023 and half the rest in 2024, keeping a quarter: "selling half my holdings in the summer of 2023 and another quarter in the summer of 2024" (S084). "Looking back, I am at peace with the decision made in the summer of 2023 to shed half my Nvidia shares, and hold on to half." (S082) Lesson: sell in stages when price runs past value, because value has error and taxes and regret cut both ways. Sources: 6 (S072, S079, S082, S084, S087, S094).

5. **Uber and the Bill Gurley correction (2014 to 2019).** Valued Uber at $6 billion in June 2014 as an urban car service; Gurley's challenge that the story was too small moved his value to $23.4 billion by 2015 (S010, S015, S027). He kept the separation of value and price: "You can believe that Uber has lots of room to grow and believe that it is over valued at $40 billion, at the same time." (S010) Lesson: the market definition drives everything, and a cramped narrative is an error too; it changed how he sizes markets (S062). Sources: 7 (S010, S015, S016, S027, S028, S040, S062).

6. **Twitter, the IPO he priced well and a management bet that failed (2013 to 2022).** Valued about $18 at the IPO and was ready to buy at $10 (S007, S008); bought at $27 and $25 in 2015 as an option on a management fix (S012, S017), then leaned to sell: "Much as I would like to hold on until the stock gets back to $25, I am inclined to sell the stock sooner, unless the narrative changes dramatically." (S017) His 2013 forecast of 2021 revenue was $9.6 billion; actual was $5.1 billion (S065). Lesson: at a young company, management quality shapes how the story evolves (S026). Sources: 9 (S006, S007, S008, S009, S012, S017, S026, S064, S065).

7. **Amazon, great company, rarely a great investment at the price (1998 to 2018).** His 1998 valuation built his young-company method and its forecasts were "horrendously wrong" (S033, S100). "I have valued Amazon about once a year every year over its existence, and I have bought Amazon four times and sold it four times in that period." (S033) Shorted it in September 2018 for the first time in twenty years (S038). Admits: "I will admit, with no shame, that Amazon is a company that I have consistently under estimated" (S037). Sources: 6 (S030, S033, S036, S037, S038, S100).

8. **WeWork and the story built on a person (2019).** Pricing fell from $60 billion to $20 billion in weeks and to $8 billion in the Softbank rescue (S043, S045). He would buy only at single-digit billions as an option. "there is a dark side to story companies and it stems from the fact that value is built on a personality, rather than a business" (S043) Lesson: when the narrator loses trust, price collapses faster than value. Sources: 3 (S043, S044, S045).

9. **The big market delusion (2013 to 2025).** A CNBC analyst in 2013 said Twitter was worth at least $60 because online ads were "really big" with no number, which started his habit of sizing the market first (S013). Dot-com retail in 1999 and cannabis in 2018 (Tilray at over $13 billion on $28 million of revenue) showed the pattern: "it does mean that the companies will be collectively over priced" (S049). He applies it to LLMs in 2025 (S087). Sources: 4 (S013, S026, S049, S087).

10. **Valeant and Volkswagen, buying through scandal (2015 to 2016).** Bought Volkswagen after the emissions scandal because a mature narrative was barely dented (S018). "I did buy Valeant at $32, because my valuation of the stock yielded $45 for the company." (S019) Watched Bill Ackman average down: "when you are very smart, you are used to being right and that breeds hubris" (S018). Lesson: scandal can create a bargain if cash flows are less damaged than price, but do not average down on ego. Sources: 2 (S018, S019).

11. **Kraft Heinz and idol worship (2019).** Investors followed Berkshire and 3G on reputation while flat revenue and shrinking margins were visible. After the drop he set a limit buy at $30: "if it does hit that price, I will be a Kraft Heinz stockholder, notwithstanding the fact that I think its future does not hold promise." (S039) Lesson: pedigree is not a thesis, and a bad business can still be a buy at a low enough price. Sources: 1 (S039).

12. **Alphabet and the limit buy that waits (2018).** Roughly fair at $1,030; he raised his limit buy from $950 to $1,000 after valuing the bets as options: "Perverse though it may sound, I am hoping that there are enough negative surprises in it to cause a price drop that would make my limit buy execute" (S032). Lesson: set the price in advance and let the market come to you. Sources: 1 (S032).

13. **Disney against Netflix (2018 to 2023).** Called Netflix overvalued in 2018 with content as a fixed cost (S031). In 2023: "I agree that Disney is a mess, right now, but I do believe that at current pricing, the odds favor me more with Disney than Netflix, but that is just me!" (S074) Lesson: in a disrupted business, the bet is on adaptability at a price. Sources: 3 (S031, S033, S074).

14. **Zomato and Paytm, Indian IPOs priced past value (2021).** Valued Zomato at about 41 INR against a 72 to 75 INR offer and found no path at 140 INR (S061, S062). Paytm was valued near the offer and fell well below after the IPO, with management talking users rather than take rate (S064). Lesson: big market and platform stories in emerging markets are a joint wager on company, sector and country. Sources: 3 (S061, S062, S064).

15. **Anthropic, valuing the IPO of the year (2026).** Refused to take a side before the prospectus (S095), then valued the equity at $1.475 trillion, near the low end of the offer range: "I estimate a value for the equity of $1.475 trillion, at the lower end of the offer pricing range ($1.5 to $2 trillion)" (S096), revised to $1.45 trillion on leaked numbers (S097). The variable he would watch: "If I were forced to pick one variable around which my valuation revolves the most, it is my estimate of the target revenues for Anthropic" (S097). Sources: 4 (S094, S095, S096, S097).

16. **SpaceX and buying as one bet among many (2026).** Valued at $1.22 trillion against a higher expected IPO price: "it is one big correction away from being fairly priced or even cheap" (S091). By August he stood ready to buy near $100 a share, but only as part of a portfolio (S093). Sources: 2 (S091, S093).

17. **Situational Awareness and Long Term Capital: conviction plus leverage (2026).** An AI fund up about 450% lost more than two thirds in four weeks and was forced to liquidate; LTCM is the older echo (S093, S094). "there is no better bound on over confidence than losing lots of money on what you thought was a sure bet" (S093) Lesson: a plausible vision is not certain enough to fund with heavy borrowing. Sources: 2 (S093, S094).

18. **Dow Chemical and the hidden rate view (2008 paper).** At an 8% cost of capital Dow was undervalued; at a "normalized" 9% it was overvalued. "In effect, your initial conclusion that about Dow Chemical being over valued reflected both your assumptions about the company and your views on interest rates" (S106) Lesson: check how much of an over or undervalued call is a macro view smuggled in. Sources: 1 (S106).

19. **The 2008 crisis and the price of risk (2008 to 2026).** "In a period of a month, the implied equity risk premium rose from 4.20% on September 12 to 6.39% at the close of trading of October 10." (S098) It moved him to monthly estimates and away from mean reversion: "I trace my awakening to the dangers of mean reversion to the 2008 crisis" (S022). Sources: 3 (S022, S089, S098).

20. **The Damodaran Bot (2024 to 2026).** A bot trained on his public work valued companies against his best students (S081). Two years on: "I have seen the Damodaran Bot get much better at story telling in the two years that I have watched it, and I need to up my game." (S088) Lesson: mechanical valuation is replicable; the edge is in the story and judgment. Sources: 2 (S081, S088).
