# Positions: Aswath Damodaran

Built from the verified batch notes on 100 sources (91 blog posts, 2012-02 to 2026-11, and 9 papers, 2005 to 2012). Each stance carries the date of the post or paper it comes from. Where a view changed, both are shown and the newer one is marked. Topics are ordered by how many sources carry them, with the seat's own question (what is it worth, and what does the price assume) placed first. Company stances are dated public records, not calls on today's prices.

## 1. What price to pay: value against price, nothing else
Sources: 22 (S001, S005, S007, S008, S012, S016, S026, S039, S043, S044, S061, S062, S067, S071, S076, S087, S091, S092, S094, S096, S097, S079).
- 2012-02: Facebook at $75 to $100 billion against his value near $71 billion. "Could it be worth $ 100 billion? Sure, but you are fighting the odds as an investor." (S001)
- 2013-10: Price bands, not a yes or no. "at a $6 billion market cap ($10/share), I think it is a very good deal, at $10 billion ($17.5/share), I am indifferent to it, and at $20 billion ($35/share), it is a moon shot." (S007)
- 2013-10: "I do not have a deep rooted aversion to buying young or money losing companies, if the price is right." (S008)
- 2015-08: No sector is off limits. "at the right price, I would buy almost any company, even a risky one with bad corporate governance, and at the wrong price, I would not buy even the very best company." (S012)
- 2021-07: Dismissing a loss maker on PE or price to book is "investing malpractice" (S061); "dismissing Zomato as an investment, just because it does not make money now, or fails to meet some conventional value tests on pricing (PE, Price to Book), is investing malpractice" (S061)
- 2022-11: "I will buy any company, no matter what its faults, at the right price" (S067)
- 2025-12: Highly priced is not the same as overpriced; translate the price into the revenue it needs. "By reframing a pricing in terms of breakeven revenues, profitability and reinvestment, it allows you to consider whether a stock, even if priced at $4 trillion, may still be a good buy." (S087)
- 2026-06 (newest restatement): "At the right price, a money-losing company can be a good investment and at the wrong price, a company with solid and stable profits can be a bad investment." (S092)
- 2026-08: Growth alone does not justify a price. "having your ARR grow 80% a year last year is not even close to being a rationale for why you should buy Anthropic at a $2 trillion pricing" (S094)

## 2. Valuation method: intrinsic value built from a story
Sources: 16 (S010, S015, S028, S044, S047, S057, S063, S064, S068, S069, S079, S090, S091, S100, S102, S103).
- 2014-12: DCF is not conservative by nature; soaring narratives give soaring values (S010, paraphrase).
- 2017-06: "I have long argued that DCF models are much more flexible than most people give them credit for" (S028) and switching method does not remove assumptions: "you cannot escape having to make those assumptions." (S028)
- 2019-09: "the notion that the value of a business is a function of its capacity to generate cash flows is not repealed, just because you have a young, high growth company" (S044)
- 2020-10: "value is a bridge between stories and numbers" (S057)
- 2021-11: Every story has to land in five inputs. "whatever story you tell about Tesla has to show up in five inputs that drive its value" (S063)
- 2021-12: "when investing in young tech companies, you are investing in a story about the company, not an extrapolation of numbers" (S064)
- 2022-11: "every valuation tells a business story, and understanding what the story is and its weakest links is key to good valuation" (S069)
- 2024-02: "intrinsic valuation is the only tool that I have for assessing whether the market is incorporating what I see in a company" (S079)
- 2026-04: "All of these stories are grounded in truth, but they are empty if they remain stories." (S091)
- Limit he names (2019-11): "a DCF is an approach for valuing going concerns" (S047)
- Papers: probabilistic tools complement risk-adjusted value, they do not replace it (S103, 2007); relative valuation hides its assumptions: "The difference is that the assumptions in a relative valuation are implicit and unstated, whereas those in discounted cash flow valuation are explicit." (S102)

## 3. Pricing versus value, and market efficiency
Sources: 14 (S006, S008, S019, S023, S036, S044, S049, S059, S080, S090, S095, S096, S102, S014).
- 2013-09: "Much of what passes for valuation in investment banking and portfolio management is pricing." (S006) He does not believe markets are efficient in the aggregate (S006).
- 2016-05: Using the market to price a stock and then calling the same market wrong is inconsistent. "it strikes me as inconsistent to use the market to get your pricing number (by using multiples and comparable firms) and then argue that the same market misprices the asset in question" (S019)
- 2016-10: "VCs price businesses, not value them!" (S023) and "most investors in public markets (including many who call themselves value investors) are also in the pricing game" (S023)
- 2018-09: Value decides whether; pricing triggers can decide when. "while my primary decision of whether to buy or sell a stock is driven by value judgments, the question of when to do it can be affected by market triggers." (S036)
- 2024-08: "I am also a realist and recognize that I live in a world, where pricing dominates" (S080)
- 2026-03: "I never cease to marvel at markets, where millions of individuals with disparate views and information reach consensus on a price." (S090)
- 2026-10: If you believed price always converges on value, index instead: "you would save yourself the time and aggravation associated with trying to price or value assets by investing in index funds" (S095)

## 4. Uncertainty and being wrong
Sources: 11 (S001, S015, S018, S023, S038, S061, S069, S094, S097, S100, S103).
- 2012-02: "you cannot avoid valuing companies where there is uncertainty." (S001)
- 2016-05: "If you don't like to be wrong, it is best not to value companies in the face of uncertainty." (S018) and "being comfortable with uncertainty may very well be the edge that separates success from failure in investing" (S018)
- 2018-09: "uncertainty is a given, a feature and not a bug" (S038) and "In investing, you have no choice but to make your best estimates and value companies, knowing fully well that you will be wrong, no matter how much information you have and how good your models are." (S038)
- 2021-07: "you don't have to be right to make money, just less wrong than everyone else" (S061)
- 2026-08: "anyone who claims to have conviction that they know what's coming is being either ignorant or arrogant" (S094)
- 2026-11: "I cannot let that concern paralyze me" (S097)

## 5. Multiples and buzzy pricing metrics
Sources: 11 (S001, S016, S019, S049, S061, S068, S079, S087, S095, S101, S102).
- 2012-02: Value per user is a shortcut; "I personally think that the number of users is a distraction." (S001)
- 2019-12: "young companies tend to be priced, not valued, and often on raw metrics (users, subscribers, revenues)" (S049)
- 2022-11: "I don't use PE or EV to EBITDA multiples as my primary stock picking tool" (S068)
- 2024-02: "the PEG ratio is an absolutely abysmal measuring of pricing" (S079)
- 2026-10: Forward multiples on ARR are compared to the wrong base. "many will (mistakenly) compare these forwards earnings multiples to current earnings multiples in the market to make their investment judgment on cheap or expensive" (S095)
- Paper, 2005: "A biased analyst who is allowed to choose the multiple on which the valuation is based and to pick the comparable firms can essentially ensure that almost any value can be justified." (S102)

## 6. Margin of safety (view changed)
Sources: 7 (S003, S018, S019, S020, S044, S077, S080, S097).
- 2012-06: Describes the screening-plus approach of buying only at a 30% to 50% discount to intrinsic value as part of value practice (S003, paraphrase).
- 2016-05: "the margin of safety, at least as practiced by some in the investing community, has become an empty vessel, an excuse for inaction rather than a guide to action in risky times" (S018) and "That is the reason that I have never formally used a MOS in investing." (S019)
- 2016-05: "I am not a diehard believer in the margin of safety, but if I were, I could use this value distribution to create a more flexible version of it" (S020)
- 2019-09: Smaller margin where the upside tail is long. "I would buy Peloton with a smaller margin of safety than a more mature company, because of the potential of significant upside." (S044)
- 2023-11: "the MOS is a blunt instrument that is most useful when you are valuing mature companies" (S077)
- 2024-08: "Advice on concentrating your portfolio and having a margin of safety, both value investing nostrums, may work with the former but not with the latter." (S080)
- Newer view, 2026-11: a trade-off you choose, not an article of faith, defined as buying "where you will buy a stock only if its price is less than your value estimate by a margin (20%, 30%, 40%) that you specify" (S097)

## 7. Market timing (view shifted and shifted back)
Sources: 9 (S022, S055, S060, S073, S086, S089, S090, S036, S085).
- 2016-08: A high CAPE alone does not make US stocks overvalued; it says lower your expectations (S022, paraphrase).
- 2020-06: "I have absolutely no shame in admitting that I am not a market timer" (S055)
- Older view, 2021-01: "ultimately we all time markets, implicitly or explicitly" (S060) and he acted on it: "Going into 2021, I have far more cash in my portfolio than I usually do, and for the first time in a long, long time, I have bought partial protection against a market drop, using derivatives." (S060)
- 2023-08: "I am not a market timer and estimate an equity risk premium primarily because I need it as an input in valuation and corporate finance" (S073)
- Newer view, 2025-10 to 2026-03: "As someone who buys into the notion that market timing is the impossible dream" (S086) and "it is much more difficult to be a successful market timer than it is to be a good stock picker" (S090)
- 2026-03: "In bad news for market timers, none of the equity risk premium approaches does well at forecasting next year's actual return" (S089)

## 8. The Fed and macro
Sources: 7 (S022, S025, S055, S056, S058, S060, S086).
- 2016-08: "central bankers (in my view) have contributed to an environment of low economic growth and higher risk premiums" (S022)
- 2020-06: "analysts and investors over estimate the effect that the Fed has on markets" (S055) and "much of the drop in rates occurred before the Fed's two big moves" (S056)
- 2020-10: Blaming the Fed for value's troubles is "a convenient excuse": "this belief has become a convenient excuse for not looking at the very real problems at the heart of value investing" (S058)
- 2021-01: "you have to belief that the Fed has the power to keep rates low, no matter what happens to the economy, and I don't share that faith" (S060)
- 2025-10 (newest): "I believe that central bankers have neither the power nor the tools to move the economy in significant ways." (S086)

## 9. Interest rates and the equity risk premium
Sources: 9 (S018, S055, S063, S070, S073, S089, S097, S098, S106).
- Paper, 2008: Keep rate views out of company valuations. "As a general rule, it is not a good idea to bring in our idiosyncratic views on interest rates, no matter how well thought on and reasoned they may be, into individual company valuations." (S106)
- Paper, 2008: "A careless analyst will tend to find growth companies to be undervalued in high interest rate scenarios and mature companies to be bargains in low interest rate scenarios." (S106)
- 2016-05: Low rates raised the ERP, so expected stock returns held near 8%: "the equity risk premium has risen to compensate" (S018)
- 2020-06: Low rates cut both ways, lowering the discount rate and signalling lower growth; his model sets perpetual growth at the risk-free rate (S055, paraphrase).
- 2026-03: Historical premiums "move in the wrong direction": "Disconcertingly, they also move in the wrong direction, falling during crises (as historical returns get updates) and rising during good times." (S089)
- 2026-03: Market at start of 2026: richly priced on PE but implied ERP in line with 65 years; "the market seems to be too sanguine about catastrophic risk" (S089)

## 10. Bubbles, big markets and the market level
Sources: 9 (S011, S013, S049, S060, S086, S087, S089, S094, S079).
- 2015-03: No collective tech bubble; at most a young-company effect in private markets. "I like Mark Cuban but I think that he is wrong on all four counts." (S011)
- 2015-08: Overpricing of big-market stories is "a feature of investing in young, start-up businesses in big markets, not a bug." (S013) He uses the word bubble guardedly (S013).
- 2019-12: The big market delusion means the group is overpriced even if the market is real. "it does mean that the companies will be collectively over priced" (S049)
- 2021-01: US market about 12% over his value: "certainly not bubble territory, but still richly priced" (S060)
- 2025-10: Richly priced on every metric, but calling a bubble and acting on it is harder (S086, paraphrase).
- 2025-12: On LLMs: "It is entirely plausible that a big market exists for LLMs, and that one or even two of the players in this space will be winners, but in the aggregate, the market is overreaching." (S087)
- 2026-08 (newest): "Thus, bubbles are a feature, not a bug, when revolutionary change is a possibility." (S094)

## 11. Technology and AI
Sources: 12 (S012, S015, S049, S072, S081, S084, S087, S088, S091, S093, S094, S097).
- 2015-10: No tech background needed. "I don't have a tech background, don't work on the right coast and know technology only as a user, but I don't think any of those are prerequisites for investing in technology companies." (S015)
- 2023-06: AI is in its hype phase; a portfolio of AI beneficiaries is more likely over than under priced (S072, paraphrase). "AI will make a difficult game (delivering excess returns or alpha from investing) even more so" (S072)
- 2024-08: "a few big winners, a whole host of wannabes, and many losers" (S081)
- 2025-01: He trimmed Nvidia not because he doubted AI: "was not that I had doubts about the core AI story, but that investors were overpaying for the story" (S084)
- 2026-03: "I have always been troubled by how much we have worshiped at the altar of disruption in this century" (S088)
- 2026-08: "AI has built the most expensive factory in history, and done so in hyper speed" (S094) and a hard ceiling: "AI's total addressable market, in the US, cannot be greater than $12.96 trillion" (S094)
- 2026-11: AI winners win on stickiness: "the companies that win find ways to make their products stickier to their customers" (S097)

## 12. Concentration, diversification and position size
Sources: 9 (S004, S018, S038, S080, S090, S093, S097, S103, S079).
- 2012-06: Buys one or two long-odds option stocks a year alongside dividend payers and stable growers: "I acquire one or two of these each year for my portfolio" (S004)
- 2016-05: Spreading bets is recognition of reality, not weakness; concentration needs more conviction (S018, paraphrase).
- 2018-09: "As a diversified investor, the variance in simulated values is a poor proxy for risk." (S038)
- 2026-03: Does not share concentrated investors' conviction; diversifies (S090, paraphrase).
- 2026-08: "I am not an absolutist on this front, because what you do as an investor should reflect your circumstances." (S093) and "it is unlikely that I would ever have sufficient conviction to make SpaceX the biggest or only investment in my portfolio, but I stand ready to buy the stock as part of a portfolio" (S093)
- 2026-11: "I am using the law of large numbers as an ally to not only reduce my risk exposure to individual companies" (S097)

## 13. Value investing and growth versus value (view hardened over time)
Sources: 11 (S003, S020, S025, S036, S054, S057, S058, S080, S086, S090, S106).
- 2012-06: Low PE and low price to book beat the market 1952 to 2010 before risk adjustment, with long stretches of underperformance (S003, paraphrase).
- 2016-05: "The world of old-time value investing is shrinking by the day." (S020)
- 2018-09: "I am a value investor, albeit one with perhaps a broader definition of what comprises value than some old time value investors" (S036)
- Newer view, 2020-05: "I believe that value investing has lost its way" (S054) and "much of value investing had become rigid (with meaningless rules and static metrics), ritualistic (worshiping at the altar of Buffett and Munger, and paying lip service to Ben Graham) and righteous" (S054)
- 2020-10: "the good old days were never that good" (S058)
- 2025-10: Price to book as value has largely failed this century; momentum has strengthened (S086, paraphrase).

## 14. Index funds, active versus passive
Sources: 10 (S019, S024, S054, S057, S058, S081, S090, S092, S095, S022).
- 2016-12: "active investing, as structured today, is an awful business, with little to show for all the resources that are poured into it" (S024) and he has made peace with it: "I could have done as well or better, investing in index funds" (S024)
- 2020-05: "active investing has been a loser's game, as Charley Ellis aptly described it" (S054)
- 2020-10: "titled index funds are an oxymoron" (S058)
- 2026-03: "Active investing is one of the most difficult games to win at, and one reason is that you match the average investor, effortlessly and almost costlessly, by investing in index funds." (S090)
- 2026-06 (newest): "there is not a single fund group in any style that outperforms its respective index past ten years" (S092) and passive doomsayers overreach: "Doomsday stories about how passive investing is making markets less efficient and less inclined to reflect fundamentals strike me as overwrought" (S092)

## 15. Management, founders and key people
Sources: 13 (S002, S017, S026, S027, S029, S034, S063, S064, S070, S078, S080, S087, S091).
- 2016-02: "Twitter has been far too focused on keeping Wall Street analysts happy and too little on building a business." (S017)
- 2017-02: "the quality of the management at a young company can play a significant role in how the story evolves" (S026)
- 2018-06: "With a story stock, it is imperative that you have a CEO who not only is able to get the market to buy into a big story, but one who stays focused and disciplined." (S034)
- 2021-11: Usually leaves top management out of a public valuation, except at Tesla: "you are getting a package deal, when you invest in the company" (S063)
- 2021-12: "good founders will work at making themselves less central to their company's stories over time and thus less critical to its value" (S064) and "it is possible that the quality that binds together successful CEOs the most is luck" (S064)
- 2023-12: "intrinsic valuations, done right, should incorporate the value of a key person or people in a business" (S078)
- 2025-12: "I would argue that management matters more than ever" (S087), most where breakeven revenues sit far above current revenues.

## 16. Corporate governance and dual-class shares (view softened)
Sources: 10 (S001, S026, S032, S043, S044, S064, S065, S067, S087, S096).
- Older view, 2012 to 2021: dual class is a reason to walk away even at a fair price. "You may be comfortable with Zuckerberg as CEO for life but I am not." (S001), "Snap's founders should trust markets more." (S026), "these companies want your money but they don't want your input" (S044), "Endowing CEOs with increased powers to fend off challenges seems like a particularly bad idea at tech companies" (S064)
- 2022-11: Investors who bought in gave up the right to complain: "Investing in Facebook and complaining that Mark Zuckerberg will not listen to you is like getting married to one of the Kardashians and complaining that your privacy is being invaded." (S067)
- 2025-12: "Sam Altman (at OpenAI) and Elon Musk (at xAI) are more emperors than CEOs" (S087)
- Newer view, 2026-10: holds Alphabet and Meta and draws no line in the sand on dual class, but Anthropic's five classes plus a public benefit structure is far worse: "the Anthropic structure is that structure on steroids" (S096)

## 17. Stock-based compensation and adjusted earnings
Sources: 8 (S012, S017, S027, S044, S059, S070, S095, S101).
- 2015-08: "I have always been wary about stock-based compensation" (S012); he treats it as an operating expense.
- 2017-06: Profit before SBC is "Using the same indefensible practice that other technology companies have adopted" (S027)
- 2019-09: IPOs "do the adjusted EBITDA dance" (S044)
- 2020-12: Credits Airbnb for staying "without playing the adjusted earnings game" (S059)
- 2026-10: "these are adjusted profits and the adjustments represent a patchwork of add-backs of legitimate expenses" (S095)

## 18. Accounting quality
Sources: 10 (S005, S009, S039, S043, S045, S059, S068, S076, S101, S102).
- 2013-09: R&D is misclassified capex (S005).
- 2019-02: "The only people who care about impairment charges, a pointless and delayed admission of overpayment on acquisitions, are those who use book value of equity as a proxy for overall value." (S039)
- 2019-11: "I think that fair-value accounting is neither fair nor is it about value" (S045)
- 2020-12: Restructuring charges: "it has become a convenient vehicle for companies to hide ongoing operating expenses" (S059)
- 2022-11: "accounting has a legacy problem, where almost all of the rules that underlie accounting reflect the fact that they were written for the manufacturing companies that dominated the twentieth century" (S068)
- 2023-10: "Accounting is backward-looking and rule-driven, making it ill equipped to value intangibles" (S076)
- Paper, 2009: "As a result, many of the basic inputs that we use in valuation, earnings, cash flows and return on capital, are contaminated." (S101)

## 19. IPOs
Sources: 10 (S006, S008, S026, S040, S043, S044, S059, S076, S091, S096).
- 2013-10: "the IPO process has little to do with value and everything to do with price" (S008)
- 2017-02: "your job in pricing is not to assess the fair value of a company but to decide what investors will pay for the company today." (S026)
- 2019-04: Prospectus risk sections are useless: "I view it more as evidence that lawyers should never be allowed to write about investing risk." (S040)
- 2020-12: "bankers price IPOs, not value them" (S059)
- 2026-04: "I will predict that there will be far more buy than sell or hold recommendations for SpaceX" (S091)
- 2026-10: Lock-ups matter little: "the smart money (which presumably includes all of those selling at the unlock) is not that smart" (S096)

## 20. Buybacks, dividends and cash returns
Sources: 9 (S002, S003, S021, S046, S055, S073, S080, S082, S098).
- 2012-03: "A company, whose stock is over valued, should pay a special dividend since buying back shares at the inflated price hurts the stockholders who remain after the buyback." (S002)
- 2012-03: "Apple did not get to be the largest market cap company in the world by finessing its capital structure or optimizing dividend policy. It did so by taking great investments." (S002)
- 2019-11: He does not want income he does not need: "have no desire to receive large dividends, just for the sake of reaching them, since they just create concurrent tax burdens" (S046)
- 2020-06: Dividends are stickier than buybacks, so buybacks fall more in a shock (S055, paraphrase).
- 2024-08 to 2024-09: Cash return is the main lever for declining firms; dividend initiations and big buybacks (Meta, Alphabet, Nvidia) signal middle age (S080, S082, paraphrase).
- Paper: "firms collectively returned twice as much cash in the form of buybacks than they paid out in dividends" (S098)

## 21. Venture capital and private markets
Sources: 9 (S011, S023, S035, S044, S047, S095, S097, S100, S010).
- 2016-10: "if the public market catches a cold, the venture capital market will get pneumonia" (S023)
- 2019-11: "absurdly high target rates of 40%, 50% or 60%, fiction in a world where these VCs actually deliver returns closer to 15-20%" (S047)
- 2026-11: "the numbers that they offer as target or expected returns are so staggeringly out of sync with reality that the cynic in you should wonder whether these numbers are marketing props, rather than financial metrics" (S097)

## 22. Leverage and debt
Sources: 8 (S002, S029, S041, S045, S054, S080, S093, S097).
- 2017-08: No kneejerk opposition to debt; fit matters. "Tesla is singularly unsuited to using debt" (S029)
- 2020-05: Avoiding high leverage was the only value fundamental that worked in the COVID selloff (S054).
- 2024-08: "a young firm that derives much of its value from future growth should not put that at risk by borrowing money or by buying back stock" (S080)
- 2026-08: Leverage is fine on low-risk investments, not on a risky macro bet; forced selling can kill a right thesis (S093, paraphrase).
- 2026-11: "the almost mindless use of debt in funding data centers, a practice that I have attacked as senseless for both the borrowers and for the lenders" (S097)

## 23. Emerging markets, country risk and China
Sources: 10 (S004, S013, S046, S061, S083, S084, S085, S086, S104, S106).
- 2012-06: Country of incorporation is a sloppy proxy; Coca Cola and Siemens carry emerging market risk (S004).
- 2019-11: "Russian oil companies are discounted by 50% or more, relative to their peer group" (S046)
- 2021-07: "I believe that the first principles of valuation are the same in all markets" (S061) and an Indian IPO is "a joint wager on a company, a sector and a country" (S061)
- 2024-10: China's 2019 tech crackdown was about control and hurt its own markets (S083, paraphrase).
- 2025-04: Placed a limit buy on BYD below $80 during the April selloff (S085, paraphrase).
- Paper, 2012: "Just as companies mature and become less risky over time, countries can mature and become less risky as well." (S104)

## 24. Shorting (view on his own practice changed)
Sources: 7 (S004, S035, S038, S051, S059, S063, S091).
- 2012-06: Shorting winners is not recommended without caveats (S004).
- Older practice, 2018-09: sold Apple and put a limit short on Amazon at $230 above, the first Amazon short in twenty years, with a clear warning: "I can bet on a company being over valued, be right on my thesis, but still lose money on a short sale, because I am forced to close out my position, in the absence of a catalyst." (S038)
- Newer view, 2020-12 to 2026-04: his preference is not to sell short even when overpriced (S059, S063), and never on a Musk company on valuation alone: "it makes it an even more dangerous company to sell short" (S091)

## 25. Hedging, options and derivatives
Sources: 5 (S060, S086, S093, S103, S107).
- 2021-01: Bought partial protection against a market drop for the first time in a long time (S060).
- 2025-10: Cash, hedging, options and shorting are escalating timing moves, each with tax and cost drawbacks (S086, paraphrase).
- 2026-08: Leverage hides in options; a naked call is borrowing (S093, paraphrase).
- Paper: "buyers of options can never lose more than the price they pay for them" (S107)

## 27. Following famous investors and smart money
Sources: 8 (S021, S024, S025, S039, S045, S062, S092, S090).
- 2016-06: "If you have so little faith in your reasoning that you doubt it and are ready to abandon it the moment it is contested by a big name, you should consider investing in index funds instead." (S021)
- 2016-12: "the smart money does not stay smart for very long" (S025)
- 2019-02: "The fault, in my view, is not with Buffett, but with the legions of investors, analysts and journalists who treat him as an investment deity" (S039)
