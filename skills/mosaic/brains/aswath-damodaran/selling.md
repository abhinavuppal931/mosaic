# Selling: Aswath Damodaran

Built from the verified batch notes on 100 sources (blog posts 2012-02 to 2026-11, papers 2005 to 2012). His rules for selling, trimming, adding and re-checking, then the kill criteria and re-check triggers this seat would write. Rules are ordered by how many sources carry them. Mistakes he admits about selling are dated.

## Rules

1. **Sell when price rises well above value; the same gap that justified buying justifies selling.** "Investing requires assessing the value of an asset, buying if the price is lower than that value and selling if it is higher, and waiting for the gap to close." (S090) and "if MOS is good enough to use when you buy a stock, it should be good enough to use when you sell that stock." (S019) He states it as "buying when price is less than value and by the same token, selling when price is much higher than value" (S072). Sources: 8 (S012, S019, S038, S051, S063, S072, S079, S090).

2. **Do not buy and hold forever.** "Don't just buy and hold" (S012) and "once you buy a great company, you should hold it forever, but that is not just unwise but is inconsistent with true value investing" (S019). Apple was bought and sold four times between 2012 and 2018 (S012, S020, S038). Sources: 4 (S012, S019, S020, S038).

3. **Trim in halves when a winner runs past value, rather than all at once.** Value has error, gains carry a 30% tax, and regret cuts both ways (S079, paraphrase). "I plan to repeat that process, and sell half of my remaining holdings" (S079) He admits it is not pure: "I am pragmatist, not a purist, and this works for me" (S072) and "I would be lying if I said that selling one of my biggest winners is easy" (S072). Sources: 4 (S072, S079, S082, S084).

4. **Sell on price against value, not because the story died.** He trimmed Nvidia because "was not that I had doubts about the core AI story, but that investors were overpaying for the story" (S084). The sale of Tesla in 2020 was the mirror of the buy: "I made my decision to buy, based on my story and valuation for Tesla, and my decision to sell, for the same reason, because I am an investor who believes in value, and acting on it." (S051) Sources: 3 (S051, S063, S084).

5. **A falling price is a prompt to revalue, not a signal to sell or buy.** Revalue with new cash flows and risk premiums first: "you cannot compare the post-correction price to your pre-crisis valuation of your company" (S085) and "if that value exceeds the price, you should buy the stock" (S085). After a crash that brings price to value, "selling now will accomplish little." (S039) Sources: 4 (S039, S041, S067, S085).

6. **Add when the business is intact and the price falls; doubling down needs a compelling valuation.** Held and added to Meta at about $100 in 2022: "I made an exception to my rule of not doubling down and doubled my holding of Facebook on November 4, 2022, because its valuation looks compelling" (S069) and "at today's price of just over $100/share, it should be time for regrets, but I have none" (S069). Sources: 2 (S067, S069).

7. **Buy and add with limit orders set in advance, and accept they may never fill.** "I have a limit buy at my estimated value of $180, with the acceptance that it may never hit that price in this iteration" (S077) and, in a crisis, "the crisis is young and the order is good until canceled!" (S085) Sources: 7 (S032, S039, S041, S044, S076, S077, S085).

8. **Value decides whether to sell; market triggers can decide when.** "while my primary decision of whether to buy or sell a stock is driven by value judgments, the question of when to do it can be affected by market triggers." (S036) After a sell-off he may wait for momentum to run: "I think I will wait a few weeks before putting my buy order in" (S012). Sources: 2 (S012, S036).

9. **Do not hold on hope of a buyer, and do not hold to get back to your cost.** "when the only reason you have left for holding on to a stock is the hope that someone will buy the company, you are reaching the bottom of the intrinsic value barrel" (S017) He leaned to sell Twitter below his $25 cost: "Much as I would like to hold on until the stock gets back to $25, I am inclined to sell the stock sooner, unless the narrative changes dramatically." (S017) Sources: 1 (S017).

10. **Do not sell because a famous investor sold, or a sage warned.** Icahn's exit "had no impact on my value estimate or investment judgment" (S021). On Greenspan in 1996: "any investor who sold equities right after the comments were made would have underperformed an investor who held on to equities and took the hit from the correction." (S086) Sources: 3 (S021, S062, S086).

11. **A rising market alone is not a reason to sell; check whether earnings, rates and risk premiums explain it.** (S086, paraphrase.) Selling everything leaves nowhere to go: "One is to sell all your equities, though I am not sure where you would put that money" (S097). Sources: 3 (S022, S086, S097).

12. **When growth falls short of the path the price assumed, revisit the story and admit error.** "you have to be willing to revisit your story, admit that you were wrong, and adapt" (S087) He warns against defending a story at all costs: "I do think that it is easy to get trapped in your own narrative and then get sucked into protecting it at all costs." (S018) Sources: 3 (S018, S087, S097).

13. **A short needs a catalyst inside your time horizon; a long mostly does not.** "When I sell short, my time horizon is far less under my control, exposing me to timing risk." (S038) Leverage creates the same forced-sale risk on a long (S093, paraphrase). Sources: 3 (S038, S091, S093).

14. **Contrarian buyers must hold through further drops.** "if you invest in loser stocks and lose your nerve or your faith, and sell too soon, your loser stock strategy will not pay off" (S058); capitulating turns a right long-term call into a loss (S085, paraphrase). Sources: 2 (S058, S085).

15. **Size so no single story can sink you.** He would not make SpaceX "the biggest or only investment" (S093) and leans on diversification: "I am using the law of large numbers as an ally to not only reduce my risk exposure to individual companies" (S097). Sources: 3 (S090, S093, S097).

## Selling mistakes he admits

- 2013 (written 2015-08): Sold Facebook at $45 and watched it reach $95. "I left significant profits on the table by selling too early, but better than bailing on an investment philosophy that has worked for me." (S012)
- 2020-01 (written 2020-02 to 2023-11): Sold Tesla at $640 when it passed his $427 value; it went past $900 within days and far higher later. "I sold too soon" (S077) and "I would also rather leave money on the table and have an investment philosophy, flawed though it may be, than make money, and end up without a core set of beliefs about markets" (S063). He also says the timing was wrong because COVID followed (S070).
- 2023 and 2024 (written 2024-09 to 2025-12): Sold three quarters of Nvidia in stages and left money on the table on the shares sold, while the quarter held paid off (S082, S084, S087).
- Open question he leaves unresolved (2023-06): whether to keep holding a stock you would not buy at today's price (S072).

## Kill criteria this seat would write

Inferred from S017, S027, S041, S061, S067, S087, S096, S097, unless a line cites one source.
- Price exceeds my value by more than the margin I would have demanded to buy, after re-running the valuation with the latest filing (S019, S090).
- Revenue growth falls short of the breakeven path that today's price needs for two or more reports in a row (S087).
- Target revenue, the variable the value turns on most, is cut below the level that supports the price (S097).
- The story breaks rather than shifts: a core premise of the narrative (market size, monetization, margin) is shown false (S069, S084).
- Debt rises enough that a bad year could force a sale or a raise on bad terms; a young firm borrows or buys back stock against its growth (S041, S080).
- A financing round or offering priced below the last one, for a firm that depends on fresh capital (S027, S061).
- An entrenched founder or key person keeps pouring capital into a project the data say to scale back (S067, S080).
- The only remaining reason to hold is hope of a takeover or of getting back to cost (S017).
- For an emerging-market name: a regime or policy change that cuts cash flows or raises country risk (S047, S061).

## Re-check triggers

- Every earnings report: re-read the narrative, not just the numbers, and ignore the after-hours reaction (S012, S034, S070, S077, S082).
- A big story event: a scandal, a competitor's entry, a regulatory or legal action, a new technology shock like DeepSeek; classify it as story break, change or shift (S027, S069, S082, S084).
- A price drop of 20% or more, or a price that blows through the best case: revalue before acting (S029, S041, S085).
- A CEO or key person leaves, ages or changes behaviour (S064, S078, S080).
- A prospectus, S-1 or first full financials arrive (S091, S095, S096).
- A shift in the risk-free rate or the implied equity risk premium: separate how much of the change is macro and how much is the company (S098, S106).
- At least once a year for any company he follows, as with Amazon (S033); each year of survival for a young firm, since failure risk and cost of equity fall (S100).
- Reader pushback with better facts: change the input and republish (S030, S062, S097).
