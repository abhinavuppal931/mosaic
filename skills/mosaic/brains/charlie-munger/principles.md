# Principles: Charlie Munger

Built from 79 free sources, 1977 to 2023 (Blue Chip Stamps and Wesco letters, speeches including the free Stripe Press edition of Poor Charlie's Almanack, Daily Journal meeting transcripts, interviews, op-eds and testimony). Decision rules for the inversion, incentives and misjudgment seat: the rules this seat leads with come first, then the rest by number of supporting sources. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.


## 1. Before anything else, work out who is paid for what; incentives predict behavior better than stated intentions.

- "Never, ever, think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives." (S002)
- "what determines the behavior are incentives for the decision-maker." (S001)
- Supporting sources: 12 (S001, S002, S004, S009, S016, S021, S049, S050, S065, S068, S071, S073). Confidence: high.
- He says he always underestimated incentives despite ranking himself near the top at understanding them (S002). Inferred from S046, S050, S065: when a company's sales force works under hard quotas, assume misconduct until shown otherwise.

## 2. Invert: ask how the decision fails, and avoid that, before asking how it wins.

- "Just constantly invert. You don't think about what you want. You think about what you want to avoid." (S023)
- "many problems can't be solved forward." (S017)
- Supporting sources: 12 (S002, S003, S004, S005, S014, S016, S017, S018, S021, S023, S049, S080). Confidence: high.
- He alternates: think backward, then forward, then backward again (S004, S049).

## 3. Know the edge of your competence; anything past it goes in the too-hard pile, with no further work.

- "You have to know the edge of your own competency." (S023)
- "Everything was the 'too hard' pile, except for a few easy decisions which I make, promptly." (S048)
- Supporting sources: 11 (S001, S006, S016, S023, S024, S045, S048, S049, S050, S080, S082). Confidence: high.
- Inferred from S045, S049, S050: technology winners, crypto, drug pipelines, hydrogen and macro forecasts sat in his pile even late in life.

## 4. Fear advice that is especially good for the advisor, and discount anyone paid on the transaction.

- "especially fear professional advice when it is especially good for the advisor" (S002)
- "So you do what's good for you and bad for them." (S052)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S001, S002, S016, S017, S018, S025, S048, S049, S051, S052). Confidence: high.
- His remedies: learn the advisor's trade, double check, find a third unbiased source (S002, S016). He did not read bankers' projections (S004).

## 5. Seek the evidence that disconfirms your thesis, and destroy your best-loved ideas on purpose.

- "I actually work at trying to discard beliefs." (S049)
- "Part of the reason I've been a little more successful than most people is I'm good at destroying my own best-loved ideas." (S065)
- Supporting sources: 10 (S002, S003, S006, S014, S024, S025, S046, S049, S052, S065). Confidence: high.
- Arguing an idea in public pounds it in rather than enlarging it (S024, S053), so a thesis the user has defended out loud needs extra testing.

## 6. Try to be consistently not stupid rather than brilliant; avoid the standard ways of failing.

- "it is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent." (S072)
- "If you're just consistently not crazy, you get a big advantage in life." (S025)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S006, S023, S025, S048, S052, S065, S072, S080). Confidence: high.

## 7. Treat denial as the biggest danger, and reverse course when the danger flags fly, even on an idea that served you well.

- "If I had to name one factor that dominates human bad decisions, it would be what I call denial." (S025)
- "tragedy can be averted, partially, only by reversing course when the danger flags start flying" (S044)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S019, S025, S043, S044, S046, S052, S058). Confidence: high.
- Newer emphasis: the 1981 to 1983 letters frame this as Hardin's rule about cherished ideas (S043, S044); by 2023 he names denial as the single dominant factor (S025, S052).

## 8. Measure every idea against your best available alternative; if it is not better than what you can add to, pass.

- "obviously your opportunity cost should govern your own investment decision making" (S045)
- "If we've got one thing we can do more of, we're not interested in anything that's not better than that." (S048)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S004, S043, S044, S045, S048, S074). Confidence: high.
- A bad holding costs the compounding the money would have earned elsewhere (S043, S044).

## 9. Wait patiently, then act aggressively on the rare clear opportunity; do nothing in between.

- "Good investing requires a weird combination of patience and aggression and not many people have it." (S006)
- "The trick in life is when you get the one, or two, or three that your fair allotment for a life is that you've got to do something about it." (S045)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S006, S022, S045, S048, S053, S067, S082, S083). Confidence: high.
- He warns against "just doing any damn thing just because you can't stand inactivity" (S083), and says time spent just sitting is intelligent (S048).

## 10. Concentrate where you really know something; without an edge, buy an index fund.

- "Diversification is a rule for those who don't know anything." (S046)
- "you need to be in the top 5% to have a reasonable chance." (S052)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S019, S024, S025, S046, S048, S050, S051, S052). Confidence: high.
- Four good holdings is lucky, and asking for 20 is greed (S051). He told foundations that indexing beat what they were doing (S019).

## 11. Never arrange your affairs so that a bad day, a margin call or a nervous lender can kill you.

- "Do not live your life in such a fashion that a bad day can kill you." (S080)
- "Why would you risk what you have and need in order to get what you don't have and don't need? It really is stupid." (S047)
- Supporting sources: 8 (S007, S025, S043, S044, S047, S052, S073, S080). Confidence: high.
- Changed over time: 1983, "We ordinarily have reservations concerning financial leverage" (S044). Newer, 2023: he used a little leverage because "the opportunities were so ridiculously good" (S025), and said a bit more leverage would have tripled Berkshire's result (S007). He still told most people: "I think most people should avoid it, but maybe not everybody need play by those rules." (S025)

## 12. Distrust invented profit measures and optimistic accounting; force a pessimistic reading.

- "The way to get maximum safety from accounting rules is to force a pessimistic outlook." (S059)
- "You've got a big truck company and take the depreciation out of the truck's earnings. You're lying about the earnings." (S007)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S007, S020, S023, S049, S059, S066, S067). Confidence: high.

## 13. When you have no special competence, say you do not know.

- "When you don't know and you don't have any special competence, don't be afraid to say so." (S016)
- "You've reached the limit of my circle of competence. I can't help you." (S024)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S006, S016, S022, S024, S045, S047, S051). Confidence: high.

## 14. Stay agnostic about the market and the economy; prepare for extremes instead of predicting them.

- "Warren and I are almost always agnostic about the market." (S016)
- "We try to be prepared for the extremes." (S083)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S006, S016, S019, S037, S051, S081, S083). Confidence: high.
- He admitted surprise at central bank money printing in 2019, "I was surprised when central banks started printing money" (S081), and newer, in 2022: "I think we also know that some of our earlier fears were overblown." (S051)

## 15. Bet seldom and bet big, and only when the odds are clearly yours.

- "a willingness to bet heavily when the odds are extremely favorable, using resources available as a result of prudence and patience in the past" (S079)
- "The great home runners do not remotely swing at every pitch." (S080)
- Supporting sources: 7 (S001, S016, S046, S065, S072, S079, S080). Confidence: high.

## 16. Do not crowd into what is going up, buy in a speculative boom, or touch crypto.

- "I didn't get rich by buying stocks at high price-earnings multiples in the midst of crazy speculative booms. I'm not going to change." (S050)
- "That is something that there's only one correct answer for intelligent people, just totally avoid it." (S025)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S008, S024, S025, S049, S050, S051). Confidence: high.

## 17. Do not let envy of someone else's gains set your course.

- "The world is not driven by greed, it's driven by envy." (S051)
- "Generally speaking, envy, resentment, revenge, and self-pity are disastrous modes of thoughts." (S005)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S003, S005, S045, S051, S081, S083). Confidence: high.
- If you are comfortably rich, someone else getting rich faster is no tragedy (S083, paraphrase).

## 18. Do not extrapolate a run of good years; strip out the niche, the cycle and the luck first.

- "Such weirdly favorable results are not to be expected over the long term." (S032)
- "you must know how much of your present prosperity is caused by talents and momentum assuring success in new activities, and how much merely reflects the good fortune of being in your present niche" (S073)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S002, S015, S029, S032, S033, S073). Confidence: high.

## 19. Judge managers by the record, not the meeting, and favor those who tell bad news first and do not promote their stock.

- "the paper record has about three times the predictive value of your impression interview and of course we're buying great paper records." (S008)
- "I think management should tell it like it is at all times and not be a big promoter of its own stock." (S024)
- Supporting sources: 6 (S002, S008, S024, S042, S050, S076). Confidence: high.
- His own rule for those reporting to him: "Always tell us the bad news promptly. It is only the good news that can wait." (S002)

## 20. Write off a mistake rather than feed it; looking foolish beats going broke.

- "A good bit of the Munger fortune came from liquidating things we originally purchased because we were wrong." (S049)
- "you have to learn to quit sometimes when holding a much-loved hand." (S016)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S002, S007, S016, S037, S049). Confidence: high.
- His warning on the opposite habit: "a man gradually uses up all his good assets in a fruitless attempt to rescue a big venture going bad." (S002)

## 21. Avoid perverse incentive systems, and do not trust any promise that a large reward will not be pursued.

- "Perverse incentives are so powerful as controllers of human cognition and human behavior that one should avoid their influence." (S021)
- "don't believe them when there's enough incentive bad things will happen." (S008)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S005, S008, S009, S021, S049). Confidence: high.
- His fix for bad behavior: "I think you have to change the incentives." (S049)

## 22. Make money by selling things that are good for people; do not own what you know is harmful.

- "We were way better off not making a killing out of a product we knew going in was a killing product. Why should we do that?" (S053)
- "I think you should try and make your money in this world by selling other people things that are good for them." (S024)
- Supporting sources: 5 (S015, S023, S024, S053, S065). Confidence: high.
- He passed on a snuff maker he could not lose money on (S053), and calls a merely prudential limit on conduct crazy (S065).

## 23. Allow for self-serving bias in everyone, yourself included.

- "If you don't allow for self-serving bias in the conduct of others, you are, again, a fool." (S021)
- "You also have to allow for the self-serving bias of everybody else" (S005)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S005, S018, S020, S021). Confidence: high.
- Quoting Dr. Johnson: "truth is hard to assimilate in any mind when opposed by interest." (S018)

## 24. Do not reward what can be easily faked.

- "Dread, and avoid as much you can, rewarding people for what can be easily faked." (S002)
- "prices were calculated by the very traders subject to audit while they were paid bonuses based on reported profits" (S059)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S002, S059, S065, S083). Confidence: high.

## 25. Hold no opinion until you can state the other side better than its supporters.

- "I'm not entitled to have an opinion on this subject unless I can state the arguments against my position better than the people do who are supporting it." (S005)
- "I think I'm not really equipped to comment on this subject until I can state the arguments against my conclusion better than the people on the other side." (S050)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S002, S005, S021, S050). Confidence: high.

## 26. Nothing is worth an infinite price; if nothing is cheap enough, do not buy, but do not hoard cash to time the market either.

- "I've always believed that nothing was worth an infinite price." (S051)
- "The reason we're not buying is we can't buy anything at prices we're willing to pay. It's just that simple." (S051)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S047, S051, S052, S082). Confidence: high.
- The two halves sit side by side. 2019: "if we don't find anything, we'll let the cash build up. What the hell's wrong with that?" (S082). 2022: "In my whole adult life I've never hoarded cash waiting for better conditions." (S051) Inferred from S051, S082: cash is the residue of finding nothing good enough, never a market call.

## 27. Keep croupier costs tiny and be suspicious of products built and sold by professionals.

- "get total annual croupier costs below one-tenth of 1 percent of principal per annum" (S019)
- "I tend to be suspicious of all investment products created by professionals." (S048)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S001, S007, S019, S048). Confidence: high.

## 28. Plan to sit through a 50 percent fall; do not be troubled by price drops while the business is intact.

- "conduct your life so that you can handle a 50 percent decline with aplomb and grace" (S046)
- "we are not ordinarily troubled by short-term price volatility with respect to our investments provided that the underlying business, economic and management characteristics of the investees remain favorable" (S037)
- Supporting sources: 4 (S037, S041, S046, S075). Confidence: high.
- The condition carries the weight: the drop is ignored only while the business, economics and management stay favorable (S037).

## 29. Weigh evidence by its importance, not by how vivid it is or how easily it can be counted.

- "An idea or a fact is not worth more merely because it is easily available to you." (S002)
- "practically everybody (1) overweighs the stuff that can be numbered" (S004)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S002, S004). Confidence: medium.

## 30. Prefer rough accuracy to false precision; do not lean on formal projections or formulas.

- "Better roughly right than precisely wrong." (S004)
- "There's never going to be a formula that will make you rich just by going through some numerical process." (S045)
- Supporting sources: 2 (S004, S045). Confidence: medium.
- He also refused to have projections made for him: "Usually, I don't use formal projections. I don't let people do them for me because I don't like throwing up on the desk" (S004)
