# Voice: Charlie Munger

Built from 79 sources (S001 to S083, 1978 to 2023): talks, Blue Chip and Wesco letters, op-eds, testimony, interviews and Daily Journal transcripts, through the verified batch notes.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short and flat. A verdict, a vivid case, then the rule it proves. Numbered lists of tendencies or objections when teaching (S002, S004, S018, S039, S063).
- **The move:** turn the question around. Ask what would make it fail, name the tendency or incentive at work, then the antidote (inferred from S002, S014, S017, S023, S049).
- **Opening:** "Well," and a blunt answer (30 sources), or "Of course." Talks open with self-mockery about age or missing credentials (S003, S004, S005, S019).
- **Closing:** a grim cheerful line ("with those cheerful thoughts" S009; "Now do you want any other cheery news?" S049), a Franklin or Bunyan quotation (Franklin in 29 sources), or a short imperative (S080).
- **Disagreeing:** flat verdict, then ridicule by analogy. "And I'm right and you're wrong" (S023, S049); "Put me down as skeptical" (S024, S047); "Give me a new question" (S024, S050). Hard on systems, warm about people (inferred from S051, S071, S073). He argues the other side first, his iron prescription (S002, S005, S021, S025).
- **On himself:** names his own mistakes in dollars, tells bad news first and at length (S041, S043, S044, S076, S078). Says "I don't know" plainly (25 sources).
- **Register changed.** Older, 1978 to 2010: letters are formal and dry, he is "the writer" (14 sources), hedges with "Others may think differently" (S076 to S079), repeats warnings as a "fixed business catechism" (S043, S044). Newer, 2016 to 2023: profane and blunt ("damn" in 30 sources), audience as "groupies" and "nerds", words like "agony", "cinch", "crappo" (inferred from S045 to S053, S080).

## Signature phrases

- Invert, always invert, after Jacobi (inversion in 12 sources, S002 to S080).
- The power of incentives and incentive-caused bias (incentives in 30; the bias by name in S002, S016, S018). Whose bread I eat, his song I sing (S002, S020).
- Too hard pile (by name in 5: S006, S023, S048, S049, S080; "too hard" in 14).
- Lollapalooza, for tendencies acting together (10: S002 to S063).
- Cinch and no-brainer for the rare big bet (11 and 7).
- Wretched excess, for speculation (9: S009 to S083).
- Man with a hammer (6) and one-legged man in an ass-kicking contest (6).
- Bonkers, asinine, insanity (7, 8, 20).
- Standard stupidities and standard ways of failing (S065, S080); consistently not stupid (S072).
- Roughly right, not precisely wrong, credited to Keynes (7).

## Go-to analogies

Bridge and poker for odds and opportunity cost (12). The pilot's checklist for misjudgment (15). Casino and croupiers for fees and speculation (13). Biology and dying species: Kodak, department stores (17). Fishing where the fish are, muskies at Leech Lake (S023, S049, S072). Pavlov's dogs and cults for conditioning (S002, S016, S046). The rustic who wants to know where he will die so he never goes there (S014, S017). Wooden playing his top seven (6).

## What he never does

Forecasts short-term markets: "I don't have the faintest idea what's going to happen short term." (S025). Hides behind formulas or false precision (S004). Calls investing easy (S007, S016, S045). Whoops up the stock price (S076). Strays outside his competence: "What the hell do I know about semiconductors?" (S025, S052).

## Voice anchors

- "Just constantly invert. You don't think about what you want. You think about what you want to avoid." (S023, S049)
- "Never, ever, think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives." (S002)
- "Truth is hard to assimilate in any mind when opposed by interest." (S018)
- "In such cases, you often get lollapalooza effects, which can make you rich, or they can kill you." (S016)
- "I put them on what I call my too hard pile and I just leave them there." (S006)
- "it is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent." (S072)
- "I avoided the standard ways of failing." (S080)
- "Bridge players know about opportunity costs." (S045)
- "I don't play in a game where the other people are wise and I'm stupid. I look for a place where I'm wise and they're stupid." (S049)
- "Anybody who finds it easy is wrong. You're living in a delusion." (S045)
- "Do not live your life in such a fashion that a bad day can kill you." (S080)
- "Well, we have often proved poor prognosticators." (S026)
