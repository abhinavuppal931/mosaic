# Damodaran: Valuation
STANCE: A great company priced as great; I would wait for a price that needs only a probable story.
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10, B 5/10, C 7/10, D 4/10
PILLAR SCORE (Value vs price): 4/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: "I have neither a crystal ball nor the monopoly on the truth." (S005) That said, I would not buy a full position at $500 (F001): the business is superb, but the price already assumes a decade of growth near today's pace, with margins intact through the biggest capital build in its history. I would put in a limit order and wait (C), and a starter (B) is defensible only as a fairly priced holding, not a bargain.
WHY:
1. Read the price backwards (Breakeven revenue, S087). The fixture has no cost of capital or tax rate, so I assume 8% to 10% and about 20%; change them and the answer moves. Holding the 45% margin (F005) and 30% return on capital (F011), a $3.7 trillion value (F002) needs revenue of roughly $0.9 to $1.5 trillion in ten years, 12% to 18% a year off $290 billion (F003), and well past the $400 billion that only five companies had reached in January 2023 (S071). Put simply, the price needs consensus (F013) or better for a decade.
2. The 3P test (S034). That path is possible and, with cloud up 35% (F004), plausible, but not yet probable, and I "treat the possible as icing on the cake (great if it happens, but you are not paying for it up front)" (S012). At this price you pay for the icing.
3. Clean the cash flows (Accounting cleanup, S044): "adding back any expense that will persist as part of regular operations is bad practice" (S044). Take the $12 billion of stock compensation (F009) off $57 billion of free cash flow (F008) and the yield is near 1.2%, against a 4.2% Treasury (F016). Capital spending has nearly doubled (F007); "AI has built the most expensive factory in history, and done so in hyper speed" (S094), and the return on that factory is the one number I cannot yet see.
4. Good company versus good investment (S085): "most great companies are neutral or even bad investments, because the market prices them to be great" (S085). With 95% of analysts at buy (F014), the market already agrees with you.
THE RISK I SEE: I have sold great compounders too early (S084); if the new AI capacity earns 30% like the rest of the business, my distribution is too narrow. The AI lab concentration (F021) cuts the other way. Time will tell.
I'M WRONG IF: revenue grows 15% or more (F003) for the next eight quarters with the operating margin holding at 45% (F005) as the new capacity starts to depreciate.
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: At 6% of the S&P 500 (F015), how much Microsoft do you already own through index funds, and what story would you be adding on top of it?
