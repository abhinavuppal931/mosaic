# Damodaran: Valuation
STANCE: A great company is only half the case, and with no price, multiple or cleaned-up margins in hand, I cannot say what today's price assumes, so I value first and wait.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 5/10, C 7/10, D 4/10
PILLAR SCORE (Value vs price): 5/10
CONVICTION: 2 | EVIDENCE: thin | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: I have no crystal ball and, here, no price either. As general knowledge, Microsoft is a very profitable software and cloud business the market has long priced as great, and a great company priced as great is a neutral investment, not a bargain. Put simply, I'd take C with a limit order at my value, and B only if the price, read backwards, needs nothing more than probable outcomes.

WHY:
- Good company versus good investment (S079, S085). "most great companies are neutral or even bad investments, because the market prices them to be great" (S085). Missing: price, market cap and net debt. If the price needs only today's businesses growing at mature rates, my pillar score moves toward 8; if it needs the full AI story delivered, toward 2.
- Breakeven revenue (S087) and the 3P test (S012, S034). Missing: market cap and target margin. I would solve for the year-five revenue the price needs, check it against the handful of firms that ever reached that size, and "treat the possible as icing on the cake (great if it happens, but you are not paying for it up front)" (S012).
- Accounting cleanup (S044, S068). Missing: stock compensation, capitalized R&D and the split of data centre spending. "adding back any expense that will persist as part of regular operations is bad practice" (S044). The heavy AI capex, general knowledge, is my one number (S031): if returns on it drift toward the cost of capital, growth adds revenue but not value.
- Life cycle and margin of safety (S095, S038). Missing: revenue growth. If it is single digits with stable margins, this is a mature company whatever it says about itself, the numbers constrain the story, and the value distribution is tight, so a modest margin of safety will do. If growth is well above that, the distribution widens and so should my margin.

THE RISK I SEE: I have sold compounders too soon and underrated what does not yet show in margins (S063, S084); waiting for a price can mean never owning a business that keeps reinvesting well.

I'M WRONG IF: the revenue that today's market cap needs, at current margins, is reachable by the existing businesses alone at single-digit growth, which would make the price a probable story and B or A the better call.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: What revenue and operating margin do you expect Microsoft to earn in year five, and does today's price need more than that? Time will tell.
