# Burry: Contrarian bear case (the bear guard)
STANCE: Wonderful business, no floor at this price; wait for a lower price or for the AI spend to show up as cash.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 4/10, C 7/10, D 6/10
PILLAR SCORE (Bear case): 3/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: I would not buy a full position at $500 (F001). The quality is real, but "wonderful businesses make wonderful investments only at wonderful prices" (S022), and this price needs 13% a year growth (F013) for five years. Wait; a small starter at most.
WHY:
- Rebuild the cash. Free cash flow is $57 billion after all capex, a 1.5% yield (F008), against a 4.2% Treasury (F016). Stock pay is $12 billion (F009), and $40 billion of payouts cut the share count only 0.5% a year (F012), so buybacks mostly mop up dilution: "And you can just take that amount and deduct it from cash flow." (S005) I get about $45 billion, near 1.2% of $3.7 trillion (F002). The buyback split is missing; more buyback makes it worse.
- No floor. Net cash of $52 billion (F010) is under 2% of the value. "I like it when my margin of safety does not require a calculator." (S039) The floor here is a 31x forward multiple (F006); the stock fell 37% in 2022 (F020). A 33% loss needs a 50% gain to repair (S020).
- The capex tell. Capex nearly doubled to $88 billion in two years (F007), and the market pays for it: "if you announce a dollar of CapEx on AI, your market cap will go up $3 for every dollar you have." (S005) My test is capex less depreciation (S005); depreciation is missing from the pack, and when it arrives the 45% margin (F005) absorbs it. A big AI customer Microsoft also owns a stake in (F021) is where I follow the incentives (S026).
- Crowding. 95% buy ratings (F014), 6% of the S&P 500 (F015), near-record tight spreads (F017), market CAPE 38 (F018). "The theater keeps getting more crowded, but the exit door is the same as it always was." (S003) Fairly, shorts are under 1% (F022) and 30% ROIC (F011) is real.
THE RISK I SEE: Depreciation squeezes margin as growth cools and the multiple derates. I can't date it.
I'M WRONG IF: Over the next four quarterly cash flow statements, free cash flow after capex and stock pay rises above about $45 billion while capex growth flattens.
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If it fell 37% again (F020), would you add or sell, and what in the filings would tell you which?
