# Burry: Contrarian bear case (the bear guard)
STANCE: A real business at a price I have not seen, so I would not pay up for it today; wait for a price or a trigger.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 5/10, C 7/10, D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE (Bear case): 4/10
CONVICTION: 2 | EVIDENCE: thin | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: I'd take C. As general knowledge, Microsoft is a quality franchise with real cash flow; my objection is the price, which I do not have. Until I see free cash flow after stock pay against enterprise value, I wait. "Don't worry about missing a rally. Worry about losing your money." (S041)

WHY:
- Quality is not a price (Three kinds of business, S022): "fully aware that wonderful businesses make wonderful investments only at wonderful prices" (S022). Missing: price, enterprise value and free cash flow. At about a 10% annual return after dilution, my fair-value line (S041), I move to B. If the multiple needs decades of growth, C or D.
- Stock pay is a cost (Shell game, S005, S021). Missing: the buyback spend that only offsets dilution. "you can just take that amount and deduct it from cash flow." (S005) If that cut is large, owner cash flow is well below the headline.
- Capex mania test (Bubble tells, S005). As general knowledge, Microsoft is spending heavily on data centres for AI. Missing: capex less depreciation and the return on it. "you can look at net investment, which is capital expenditures less depreciation over time." (S005) If depreciation catches up while AI revenue lags, earnings fall. One bubble tell, no clock.
- Where does the price stop? (Margin of safety, S020, S038). No hard-asset floor; the floor is earnings holding up, a 5 at best. And a rich multiple is a rate bet: "investment in a stock with a price/cash earnings multiple of 25 will be much more sensitive to interest rates than investment in a stock with a price/cash earnings multiple of 5." (S041)

THE RISK I SEE: An index heavyweight held by flows that must leave together: "The theater keeps getting more crowded, but the exit door is the same as it always was." (S003) And my misfire: I sold Apple too early (S006).

I'M WRONG IF: the next two annual 10-Ks show free cash flow, after deducting buybacks spent to offset dilution, still rising while depreciation from the data-centre build catches up with capex.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: What is Microsoft's free cash flow after stock pay, and have you rebuilt it from the 10-K yourself?
