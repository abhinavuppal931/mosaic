# Burry: Contrarian bear case (the bear guard)
STANCE: Wonderful business, no floor at this price; wait for a lower price or for the AI spend to show up as cash.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 4/10, C 7/10, D 6/10
PILLAR SCORE (Bear case): 3/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: I would not buy a full position at $500 (F001). The quality is real, and "wonderful businesses make wonderful investments only at wonderful prices" (S022); this price rests on the AI build paying off and on 13% a year of growth (F013) holding for five years. I'd wait for a price or a cash trigger; a small starter is the most I could defend.
WHY:
- Rebuild the cash. Free cash flow is about $57 billion after all capex, a 1.5% yield (F008), against a 4.2% Treasury (F016). Stock pay runs $12 billion (F009), and $40 billion of buybacks plus dividends cuts the share count only 0.5% a year (F012), so much of the buyback just mops up dilution: "And you can just take that amount and deduct it from cash flow." (S005) Net of stock pay I get about $45 billion, near 1.2% of a $3.7 trillion value (F002). The buyback and dividend split is missing; more buyback in the mix makes my number worse.
- No floor. Net cash of $52 billion (F010) is under 2% of the market value, and no financial buyer exists at this size. "I like it when my margin of safety does not require a calculator." (S039) Here the floor is a multiple, about 31x forward (F006), and the 37% fall in 2022 (F020) shows how far a multiple travels. A 33% loss needs a 50% gain to repair (S020, S041).
- The capex tell. Capex nearly doubled to $88 billion in two years (F007), and the market pays for announcements: "if you announce a dollar of CapEx on AI, your market cap will go up $3 for every dollar you have." (S005) My test is net investment, capex less depreciation (S005). Depreciation is not in the pack; when this build reaches the income statement, the 45% margin (F005) absorbs it. A large AI customer Microsoft also holds a stake in (F021) is exactly where I follow the incentives (S026).
- Crowding. 95% buy ratings (F014), 6% of the S&P 500 (F015), credit spreads near 20-year tights (F017), a market CAPE of 38 (F018). "The theater keeps getting more crowded, but the exit door is the same as it always was." (S003) In fairness, the bear side is empty at under 1% short (F022), and 30% returns on capital (F011) with 35% cloud growth (F004) are real.
THE RISK I SEE: Rising depreciation from the $88 billion build squeezes margin just as growth cools, and a 31x multiple goes back toward its 2022 low. I can't date it, and being early here looks exactly like being wrong.
I'M WRONG IF: Over the next four quarterly cash flow statements, free cash flow after all capex and after stock pay rises above about $45 billion while capex growth flattens.
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If it fell 37% again, as in 2022 (F020), would you add or sell, and what in the filings would tell you which?
