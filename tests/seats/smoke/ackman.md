# Ackman: Catalysts, governance, asymmetric hedges
STANCE: Superb business, nothing to fix, no scare in the price; wait for the trigger.
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10, B 5/10, C 8/10, D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE (Catalyst and governance): 4/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: C. We believe Microsoft is a great business that needs no fixing, but at 31x forward earnings (F006) and 4% above its 200-day average (F019) no disappointed holder is selling, so there is no arbitrage to capture. I'd keep it in the library and let a capex scare do the work a catalyst would.

WHY:
- No fix needed, so no catalyst (S042, S011). "The best investments are ones where you don't need to join the board and do anything." (S042) A 45% operating margin (F005) and 30% return on capital (F011) leave no gap; Microsoft is the best peer. The as-optimized spread is zero and no one with votes is pushing; price is the only lever.
- The library and the trigger (S004, S087). "We have always believed that a good time to buy a great business is when it is in temporary trouble." (S004) Capital spending doubled to $88 billion (F007) and cut free cash to a 1.5% yield (F008), yet 95% of the sell side says buy (F014). The 37% fall of 2022 (F020) was an entry.
- Price against the hurdle (S039, S078). "You can buy the best business in the world and if you overpay, you're not going to earn particularly attractive returns." (S039) 13% growth (F013) plus a 1.5% cash yield (F008) falls well short of my 20% hurdle (S078), even at a steady multiple.
- Governance and incentives (S041). "just look at the proxy and see how they're being compensated" (S041) Pay data is missing. $12 billion of stock pay (F009) against a share count falling only 0.5% a year (F012) means the buyback mostly offsets dilution, and the AI lab that is customer and investee (F021) is a concentration I'd want sized publicly. Pay on per-share free cash flow would lift my pillar score toward 6.

THE RISK I SEE: If the AI spend earns its return, the scare never comes and waiting costs you a compounder. Protection is cheap at tight spreads (F017), but I have no variant view and you are unlevered. "Without extraordinary opportunities, we sit tight." (S064)

I'M WRONG IF: the next annual report shows return on invested capital (F011) holding near 30% after another year of rising capital spending (F007): growth spend, not maintenance (S010).

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If an AI spending scare cut the price hard, would you buy more or sell?
