# Lee: Thematic and secular trends
STANCE: Two live themes and income still keeping up with the spending, but the whole Street already agrees, so I'd own a starter and earn the rest.
OPTION SCORES: A 6/10, B 8/10, C 4/10, D 2/10
PILLAR SCORE (Thematic tailwind): 7/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: Think of wireless in the 1990s: the value went to the one or two groups that pulled away, and Microsoft looks like a survivor, not a hopeful. The theme is early; the crowd in this stock is not. So I'd take B: a starter now, and add on proof that the capex keeps turning into income, or on a waterfall decline while credit stays calm.
WHY:
1. Granny Shots says "we only add a stock that appears in more than one theme" (S007). It sits on AI, with cloud up about 35% (F004), and on the labor shortage, selling software that stands in for scarce workers. Both have a decade to run.
2. My bubble test: "it's only a bubble if spending is not driving income growth." (S002) Capex roughly doubled, from about $45 billion to $88 billion (F007), while revenue grew 15% (F003) at a 45% operating margin (F005) and a 30% return on capital (F011). Income is keeping up.
3. Sentiment fails, you know what I mean? About 95% of analysts rate it a buy (F014) and short interest is under 1% (F022). "people are not bearish at the tops, they're bullish at the tops" (S001). On this name they are, so I won't size it in one go.
4. This is the frontier name, the one supplying the bottleneck, and "it was better to feed the worker than to supply the worker." (S003) That spending leaves a 1.5% free cash flow yield (F008). The pack has no ISM, margin debt or IPO count. If those showed late-cycle excess, I'd move to C. This is not evidence-based, just an opinion: I lean that they would come back clean.
THE RISK I SEE: Credit is calm at about 0.8 points (F017), and "I'd be watching credit before I watched equities crack" (S011). A fall like the 37% in 2022 (F020) can happen again with the theme intact, so the starter has to be a size you can sit through.
I'M WRONG IF: operating margin falls below about 45% (F005) while capital spending keeps climbing past $88 billion (F007), which would mean the spending has stopped driving income.
