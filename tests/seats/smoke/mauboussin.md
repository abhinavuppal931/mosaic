# Mauboussin: Expectations, base rates, sizing
STANCE: A great business priced for more greatness; too thin a discount to expected value for a full bet.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 5/10, C 7/10, D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE (Base rates and sizing): 4/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: "The most basic question you must always answer: what's priced in?" (S130) Here, a lot. I'd wait for a better price or a passed signpost (C); a starter (B) is a defensible small bet, and a full position (A) overbets an edge I can't find.
WHY:
1. Expectations investing (S114, S130). Assuming an 8% cost of capital, missing from the pack, a business that creates no value earns about 12.5 times earnings (S114). At 31 times forward (F006), roughly 60% of the price is future value creation. Free cash flow net of stock pay (F008, F009) yields near 1.2%, under a 4.2% Treasury (F016). In other words, the price needs the 13% consensus (F013) and more after it.
2. Base rates (S045, S055, S060). Almost no company with $100 billion or more of sales has grown at a mid-teens rate for long, and Microsoft sits at $290 billion growing 15% (F003). A base rate is a guide, not a prediction.
3. Spread first, growth second (S061). Capital spending nearly doubled (F007). "Growth creates a lot of value only when the spread is positive and large" (S061). The pack lacks the return on that capital: at the base's 30% (F011), I tilt toward B; near the cost of capital, toward D.
4. Expected value and the other side (S197, S059). My scenarios: 30% the build earns like the core and growth beats consensus; 45% consensus arrives as the multiple drifts lower; 25% the build earns its cost of capital and the multiple compresses. Expected value lands near the price, the $52 against $50 case. "Margin of safety can be restated as a discount to expected value." (S197) With 95% buy ratings (F014), who is selling to me, and why? "Greater opportunity suggests a larger bet." (S167) Small opportunity, small bet; unlevered and diversified, even a 37% fall (F020) ruins nothing.
THE RISK I SEE: Full expectations can disappoint for years: Microsoft's "total return was -69 percent over 111 months from January 2000 to March 2009." (S027) The AI lab tie (F021) concentrates the payoff.
I'M WRONG IF: free cash flow after all capital spending (F008) grows faster than revenue (F003) over the next two years while capital spending stays at or above $88 billion (F007).
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: What return must the new data centres earn for today's price to make sense?
