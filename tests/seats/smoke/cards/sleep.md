THE CALL: C, wait for a lower price, and do the destination work now so the order is ready when the renters lose their nerve.
WHY:
1. Read the price backwards and the whole runway is already paid for: 21.1% a year free cash flow growth for a decade [F108], 6.4 points above the company's own record [F111], at 1.6 times base value [F107]. This is Amazon in 2006 turned inside out, and "as prices rise prospective returns go down." (S005) (What the price implies, S011) [F108]
2. The scale is going to the margin line, not the customer: operating margin up 16.7 points over the record [F067], gross margin up 3.4 [F065]. "Most companies pursue scale efficiencies, but few share them." (S007) A superb business, but a harvester, so the moat does not widen by itself as it grows. (Scale economics shared, S007) [F067]
3. Split the spending and there is a cash cow underneath: operating cash flow of 195.88B [F013] less D&A of 38.30B [F015] as a rough maintenance proxy leaves about 158B, near 4% of the market cap [F028] (my arithmetic). The other 98B or so of capex [F014] is growth spend that so far earns little: sales to capital 0.5x [F081], free cash flow up 3.6% a year over five years [F072]. "there is no guarantee that investment spending will always work." (S011) (Capitalise the investment the accounts expense, S011) [F081]
THE RISK I SEE: the capex is the industry's capital cycle, not Microsoft's moat; everyone builds the same capacity, returns on new capital fall to rivals' level, and a price assuming 21% growth [F108] has nowhere soft to land.
I'M WRONG IF: the 5-year FCF CAGR [F072] rises from 3.6% to 12% or more while revenue growth holds above 15% [F075], proving the spend is growth investment at the start of a long runway.
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: Where is Microsoft in twenty years if the AI spending works, and who gets the savings, the customer or you?
