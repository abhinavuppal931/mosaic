# Kantesaria: Toll-booth quality gate
STANCE: TOO HARD

THE CALL: Microsoft sits outside my pond today, not because the business is weak but because I cannot underwrite its next ten years with the predictability my 8 to 12 name book needs. As general knowledge, Windows and Office are toll-like franchises, but the cloud and AI build-out turns it into a heavy capital spender whose payoff I cannot forecast. I abstain rather than score it down, and I say openly that this exclusion has cost me before.

WHY:
- Exclusion list (Exclusion list, S019; Compounding machines, S019). I want a capital-light toll collector, ideally with no reinvestment. "we avoid things that have high R&D risk." (S019) As general knowledge, Microsoft now spends heavily on data centres and AI research. Missing: the capex and R&D split and the free cash flow after stock compensation. Without them I cannot even run my yield against the 10-year Treasury, so I have no price to wait for.
- Predictability over ten years (Quality is the intersection of growth and predictability, S018). My newest view on big tech is that AI helps for a few years, then turns into a capex race: "it's our view that AI becomes commoditized, and then it becomes difficult for these companies to monetize their AI offerings." (S018) And plainly, "It doesn't have the predictability that we like to see." (S021) If I cannot say who wins in ten years, it fails my bar.
- I concede the cost, then restate the rule (The big tech omission, S018). "For me, a big omission was the big tech companies." (S018) That is a learning lesson I take seriously. But conceding an omission is not the same as relaxing the gate on a name whose economics have changed since. When I wanted AI exposure, I said "we wanted the most predictable way to participate in AI." (S021) and that pointed me to a monopoly supplier, not the contenders.

WHAT WOULD BRING IT INSIDE MY CIRCLE: two facts, not a lower price alone. First, evidence that AI capital spending is peaking and free cash flow after stock compensation is growing again, so the business looks capital light once more. Second, a long record of price increases above inflation on the core software franchises with volume holding, which would show the toll survives the AI race. With both in hand, I would then need the price: a free cash flow yield 30% to 60% below what I think it deserves against the 10-year Treasury (S019), most likely on a regulatory scare or neglect that leaves the toll intact.
