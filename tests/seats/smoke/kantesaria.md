# Kantesaria: Toll-booth quality gate

STANCE: TOO HARD

THE CALL: Microsoft is a fine business, but it sits outside my pond today, so I abstain rather than score it down. The thing I cannot predict is whether roughly $88 billion a year of AI data-centre spending, nearly double two years ago (F007), earns a toll or just buys a seat in a race. I concede openly that excluding big tech has cost me before, and I still restate the rule: if I cannot see the 10-year economics, I do not own it in an 8 to 12 name book.

WHY:
- Compounding machines need to be capital light (Compounding machines, S018, S020). Here capital spending eats most of $145 billion of operating cash flow (F007), leaving about $57 billion of free cash flow and a 1.5% yield (F008), and that is before I net out $12 billion of stock pay (F009). My current read on big tech is that "AI becomes commoditized, and then it becomes difficult for these companies to monetize their AI offerings." (S018) Of this group: "It doesn't have the predictability that we like to see." (S021)
- It fails the exclusion list on big upfront bets with unknown payoff (Exclusion list, S019): "we avoid things that have high R&D risk." (S019) Azure growing about 35% (F004) is real volume, but I cannot say who dominates AI cloud in 10 years, and a major AI lab being both a large customer and a partner Microsoft holds a stake in (F021) is exactly the kind of concentration I cannot underwrite.
- When I wanted AI exposure, I chose the monopoly supplier over the contenders, Microsoft among them: "we wanted the most predictable way to participate in AI." (S021) I also own the cost of this rule: "For me, a big omission was the big tech companies." (S018)

WHAT WOULD BRING IT INSIDE MY CIRCLE: a few years of evidence that the build is done, meaning capital spending falling back toward the earlier level (F007) while free cash flow after stock pay grows again and the share count keeps shrinking (F012). Add proof of pricing above inflation in cloud with volume holding, which the pack does not give me; it has growth (F003, F004) but no price-versus-volume split. Even then I would need the trading yield after stock pay to sit well above the 4.2% Treasury (F016) and 30% to 60% below what I think it is worth (S019), which means a scare or neglect that leaves the toll intact. With a 1.5% yield (F008) against that Treasury, I am not close.
