# Taleb: Fragility and ruin (the ruin guard)
STANCE: The stock cannot ruin you; your size and your margin can. Starter, unlevered, capped.
OPTION SCORES: A 5/10, B 7/10, C 4/10, D 6/10
PILLAR SCORE (Ruin and convexity): 6/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: thin | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: I am not a stock picker and I cannot tell you whether today's price is cheap; that is not my question. My question is whether this can blow you up, and an unlevered stake in an equity-financed company, sized so a halving does not force a sale, cannot. Take a starter with no margin, cap it as a share of the whole book and leave the multiple to the valuation seats.
WHY:
- Debt first (Debt fragilizes; redundancy is a long option, S031, S054). As general knowledge, Microsoft grew on equity, not borrowing, and my brain lists it as a firm not built on debt (S054). "The one who borrows will go bust if he makes a mistake." (S031) Missing: total debt plus lease and purchase commitments against cash.
- Your margin second (Ruin, the absorbing barrier and ergodicity, S001, S038). "If you are leveraged, errors blow you up; if you are not, you can enjoy life." (S029) With margin, an ordinary drawdown in a fat-tailed stock becomes the uncle point. Without it, the worst case is a loss you sit through.
- Stacking. "No risky exposure can be analyzed in isolation: risks accumulate." (S001) As general knowledge, index funds already hold this name heavily, so a full position on top of them doubles the same exposure on the same bad day. Missing: your index and tech weight.
- The hidden tail (Steady income hides a short option, S037). "Companies that have steady income are short an option somewhere." (S037) My candidate, general knowledge only: the data-center build is spending that needs demand to cooperate. Missing: capex commitments against operating cash flow. Shock that demand 10, 20 and 30 percent and check whether the damage accelerates (Fragility detection by acceleration, S002).
THE RISK I SEE: Five years of rising earnings is the turkey's record. "Lack of ruin due to several or even many trials does not indicate safety from ruin in the next one." (S003) A de-rating of a richly priced stock is a drawdown, not ruin, unless you are levered or oversized.
I'M WRONG IF: The balance sheet shows debt plus lease and purchase commitments larger than cash plus one year of operating cash flow.
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: Counting your index funds, what share of your total book would this be, and is any of it bought on margin?
