# Munger: Inversion, incentives, misjudgment
STANCE: A great business at the top of a sane price, in a crowd; wait.
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10, B 5/10, C 7/10, D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE (Folly check): 6/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Well, no full position at this price. No standard folly here: net cash (F010), stock pay bought back (F012). But you pay 31x forward (F006) for a 1.5% cash yield (F008) while the whole room says buy (F014), so I'd wait for a price or for the AI spending to prove out (C).

WHY:
- Invert (S023). "Just constantly invert. You don't think about what you want. You think about what you want to avoid." (S023) It fails as a capex trap, not death. Spending doubled to $88 billion in two years (F007); after $12 billion of stock pay (F009), free cash (F008) is about 1.2% of $3.7 trillion (F002). Flow-through test: "they don't do the second step of the analysis which is to determine how much is going to stay home and how much is just going to flow through to the customer." (S001) One AI lab is customer and partner (F021); its share of cloud revenue is missing, and if large, I move toward D.
- Incentives (S002, S052). Management passes my Costco test: "But we always buy in enough stock in Costco to pay for the stock we're issuing." (S052) The sell side fails it. "especially fear professional advice when it is especially good for the advisor" (S002) A 95% buy rating (F014) costs the raters nothing. Management's pay metric is missing; if it pays for AI revenue over return on capital, my score drops.
- Crowd forces (S002, S052). "I think artificial intelligence is very important, but it's also a lot of crazy hype on the subject." (S052) Add a 6% index weight (F015), a market CAPE of 38 (F018) and credit near its tightest (F017). Not a lollapalooza yet, but the start of one.
- Price and opportunity cost (S025, S045). "if it's really a great business, it's at least 25x earnings and maybe 30x or 35x or something" (S025) This is the top of that band. "Bridge players know about opportunity costs." (S045) If your diversified portfolio holds an index fund (my assumption), you already own it at 6% (F015).

THE RISK I SEE: My pile has missed great compounders (S082); waiting may cost you the compounding.

I'M WRONG IF: the next annual report shows capital spending (F007) flat or down while cloud growth (F004) holds near 35%.
