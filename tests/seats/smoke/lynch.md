# Lynch: Growth and ground truth
STANCE: A great company priced like the fast grower it no longer is; I'd wait, a starter at most.
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10, B 6/10, C 7/10, D 4/10
PILLAR SCORE (Growth story and PEG): 4/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: I'd wait (C). This is a stalwart with a fast grower living inside it, and you're paying a fast grower's multiple for about 13% growth. A small starter (B) is fine, adding only when the AI spending shows up in earnings.

WHY:
- What kind of stock is this (S029; the categories are from One Up on Wall Street, paraphrase). Revenue up 15% (F003) is real sales growth, not a margin rebound. But a $3.7 trillion company (F002), 6% of the S&P 500 (F015), is a stalwart. Cloud at 35% (F004) is the fast grower inside it. The fixture doesn't give cloud's share of revenue; if it's most of the company, my score goes up.
- Does growth pay for the multiple (S007; PEG is from One Up on Wall Street, paraphrase). About 31 times forward earnings (F006) against 13% expected growth (F013) is well over twice the growth rate. That's my warning zone. Coca-Cola taught me a spectacular company can have "priced in the next 10 years of growth." (S007) With 95% buy ratings (F014), I have no edge either.
- Results, not promise (S027). Capital spending went from about $45 billion to $88 billion in two years (F007), and free cash flow is down to a 1.5% yield (F008). That's a bet on AI demand, partly from one lab that is customer and investee (F021). "You want to bite on the results." (S027) And "I am the lowest tech guy ever." (S007)
- Balance sheet first (S016, S017). Net cash about $52 billion (F010) and 45% margins (F005). "It's very hard to go bankrupt if you don't have any debt." (S016) Nothing here dies in 24 bad months, so waiting costs little and a starter is safe: "you buy a little bit, and you keep checking in and tuning in with the company" (S025)

THE RISK I SEE: I skipped Wal-Mart on price and it went up 25 times (S021); if cloud is in the third inning, waiting costs you. And it has fallen 37% before (F020), which stalwart upside at 31 times rarely pays for.
I'M WRONG IF: The next two annual reports show free cash flow rising from about $57 billion (F008) with cloud growth near 35% (F004).
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: Is your own employer spending more on Microsoft cloud or AI tools this year than last?
