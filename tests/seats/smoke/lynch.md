# Lynch: Growth and ground truth
STANCE: A great company I can describe, at a price I cannot read, so I wait for two numbers before I pay.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 5/10, C 7/10, D 4/10
PILLAR SCORE (Growth story and PEG): 4/10
CONVICTION: 2 | EVIDENCE: thin | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way
THE CALL: I'd take C. Microsoft is a great company (S007), but a great company and a good stock are different things. Without the P/E and a sales-backed growth rate I can't tell if you're buying the business or its next ten years. If those two numbers line up, a starter (B) is fine.

WHY:
- Sort it first (What kind of stock is this, S029; categories from One Up on Wall Street, paraphrase). As general knowledge it's a giant: Windows, Office, a big cloud business. A conservative stock with a growth engine bolted on. "Growth stocks, by definition, are where sales have really grown." (S029) Missing: sales growth by segment. If cloud sales move most of the company, it scores as growth. If the gains are margins, it isn't.
- The two-minute story (S016). "if you can't explain to a ten year-old in two minutes or less why you own a stock, you shouldn't own it." (S016) Subscriptions and rented computer power, I can tell. The AI part I can't, and "I have zero AI stocks" (S007). If your reasons hang on AI, that's too hard for me.
- Does growth pay for the multiple (PEG, One Up on Wall Street, paraphrase). Missing: the P/E and the growth rate. P/E near the growth rate, B climbs. Near twice it, it's my Coca-Cola problem: "I mean it's the stocks, you know, priced in the next 10 years of growth." (S007) Then C or D.
- Against myself: I skipped Wal-Mart on price. "I didn't own it during this period by the way. I thought it was overpriced." (S021) So not pass, just wait: "You have plenty of time. Don't have this idea that you have to be there at the moment of conception" (S021)

THE RISK I SEE: Heavy data-centre and AI spending (general knowledge) gets sold as growth before the sales show up, and you pay for earnings that don't exist yet (S016). And it's among the most-followed stocks, so neither of us has an edge (S025).

I'M WRONG IF: the current P/E sits at or below Microsoft's reported sales growth rate over the last three years.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: Without saying AI, can you give me three reasons Microsoft earns more in three years than it does today?
