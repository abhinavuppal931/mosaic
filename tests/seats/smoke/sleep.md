# Sleep: Long runway, scale shared
STANCE: A wonderful business at a crowded price; I'd do the work now and wait for the despised moment.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 4/10, C 7/10, D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE (Runway and reinvestment): 5/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: thin | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: I'd wait (C). The runway is real, but at about $3.7 trillion (F002) this is already the elephant, and 31x forward earnings (F006) against 13% consensus growth (F013) pays for the destination everyone can see. I'd buy when the renters panic, not while 95% say buy (F014).

WHY:
- Scale economics shared (S007). "Most companies pursue scale efficiencies, but few share them. It's the sharing that makes the model so powerful." (S007) A 45% operating margin (F005) says the savings went mostly to the margin line. What cloud customers pay per unit over time is missing; if it fell as Azure grows 35% (F004), my pillar score would rise.
- Split growth from maintenance (S011). Capital spending nearly doubled to $88 billion (F007), leaving a 1.5% free cash flow yield (F008). Even if all of it were growth, the cash cow is $145 billion of operating cash flow (F007), under 4% of market value (F002). The maintenance split is missing and the AI build has no proven return (growth spending can fail, S011). Nobody is pricing this as if growth stops.
- Read the price and the crowd (S011, S005). "Good investing is a minority sport" (S011), and here the minority is the doubter. With a market CAPE of 38 (F018) and tight credit spreads (F017), I concede "as prices rise prospective returns go down." (S005)
- Do the work, then wait (S006, S014). "Even though prices are generally high, the trick is to do the work today, so that we are ready." (S006) It fell 37% in 2022 (F020) with the business intact. "When investments go from hated and reasonable to despised and cheap something is normally up in the markets." (S014)

THE RISK I SEE: Standing aside has a cost: "the biggest error an investor can make is the sale of a Wal-Mart or a Microsoft in the early stages of the company's growth." (S012) But these are not the early stages, and the destination leans on the AI build (F007) and one lab that is both customer and partner (F021).

I'M WRONG IF: the next annual report shows cloud unit prices falling for customers while operating margin holds near 45% (F005); that is scale shared, and B would beat C.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If the price fell 37% again (F020) and the business did not, would you buy more, or sell?
