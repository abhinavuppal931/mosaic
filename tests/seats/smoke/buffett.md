# Buffett: Business quality and owner economics
STANCE: A wonderful business at a price that leaves no margin of safety; I'd wait for a better pitch.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 3/10, C 7/10, D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE (Moat and owner economics): 7/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: I'd wait (C). The castle is real: about 45% operating margins (F005), about 30% on invested capital (F011) and roughly $52 billion of net cash (F010). But at about 31x forward earnings (F006), the owner earnings I can count yield less than a 10-year Treasury (F016), so this price asks me to pay today for birds still in the bush.

WHY:
- The moat comes first (S027). "A truly great business must have an enduring moat that protects excellent returns on invested capital." (S027) The margins and returns pass that test. What I cannot see yet is whether the AI build widens the moat or just keeps it level, and one lab is both a large customer and a partner (F021). I don't like a thesis that leans on one variable I can't judge.
- Owner earnings, counting every cost (S012, S035). Operating cash of $145 billion less $88 billion of capital spending (F007) less $12 billion of stock pay (F009) leaves about $45 billion, roughly 1.2% of a $3.7 trillion price (F002). "If compensation isn't an expense, what is it?" (S035) The split between maintenance and growth capex is missing. If I generously call the $45 billion of two years ago (F007) maintenance, owner earnings are about $88 billion, near 2.4%, still under 4.2% (F016).
- Growth has to be judged against the capital it eats (S017). Capital spending nearly doubled in two years (F007). "For an increase in profits to be evaluated properly, it must be compared with the incremental capital investment required to produce it." (S017) That $43 billion step-up has no earnings record yet. Not gruesome, but unproven.
- The gap is thin (S018, S012). "If we calculate the value of a common stock to be only slightly higher than its price, we're not interested in buying." (S018) To beat the bond, the 13% consensus growth (F013) has to arrive for years with no hiccup, while 95% of analysts say buy (F014). That is the greedy side of the room. A net buyer of hamburgers wants beef cheaper, and this stock fell 37% in 2022 (F020) with the business intact.

THE RISK I SEE: Waiting on a wonderful business has cost me before; I missed Google (S130), and a small overpayment for real quality held long can still work (S079). This also sits near the edge of my circle (S033), where my lens is weakest.

I'M WRONG IF: over the next two annual reports capital spending (F007) stops rising while cloud growth (F004) and the 45% margin (F005) hold, so free cash flow after stock pay grows faster than the 13% consensus (F013).

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If the market closed for five years tomorrow, how much of your net worth would you happily lock into Microsoft at this price?
