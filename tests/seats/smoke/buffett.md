# Buffett: Business quality and owner economics
STANCE: A wonderful business at a price with no margin of safety; I'd wait for a better pitch.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10, B 3/10, C 7/10, D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE (Moat and owner economics): 7/10
CONVICTION: 3 | EVIDENCE: adequate | RUIN RISK: none | DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: I'd wait (C). The castle is real: 45% operating margins (F005), 30% on invested capital (F011), $52 billion of net cash (F010). But at 31x forward earnings (F006) the owner earnings I can count yield less than a 10-year Treasury (F016), so the price asks me to pay today for birds still in the bush.

WHY:
- The moat comes first (S027). "A truly great business must have an enduring moat that protects excellent returns on invested capital." (S027) Margins and returns pass. What I can't see is whether the AI build widens the moat or just holds it, and one lab is both a big customer and a partner (F021).
- Count every cost (S012, S035). Operating cash of $145 billion less $88 billion of capex (F007) less $12 billion of stock pay (F009) leaves about $45 billion, roughly 1.2% of a $3.7 trillion price (F002). "If compensation isn't an expense, what is it?" (S035) The maintenance and growth capex split is missing. Generously calling the $45 billion of two years ago (F007) maintenance gives about 2.4%, still under 4.2% (F016).
- Judge growth by the capital it eats (S017). "For an increase in profits to be evaluated properly, it must be compared with the incremental capital investment required to produce it." (S017) Capex nearly doubled in two years (F007), and that step-up has no earnings record yet. Not gruesome, but unproven.
- The gap is thin (S018). "If we calculate the value of a common stock to be only slightly higher than its price, we're not interested in buying." (S018) To beat the bond, 13% growth (F013) must arrive for years without a hiccup, while 95% of analysts say buy (F014). A net buyer of hamburgers wants beef cheaper, and this stock fell 37% in 2022 (F020) with the business intact.

THE RISK I SEE: Waiting on a wonderful business has cost me before; I missed Google (S130), and a small overpayment for quality held long can still work (S079). This also sits near the edge of my circle (S033).

I'M WRONG IF: over the next two annual reports capex (F007) stops rising while cloud growth (F004) and the 45% margin (F005) hold, so free cash flow after stock pay grows faster than 13% (F013).
