## Buffett

SEAT: Buffett (Moat and owner economics)
STANCE: No

PILLAR SCORE: 8/10
CONVICTION: 4/5
EVIDENCE: strong
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: C. You do not sell a wonderful business because it went up.
WHY:
1. Nine in ten members renew every year [F006]. That is a moat you can count (Moats, S002).
2. Returns on capital of 21% [F008] on a business that grows by copying itself.

THE RISK I SEE: Paying 55 times earnings means years of flat returns even if the business does well.
I'M WRONG IF: growth at existing stores falls below 4% [F005].
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: What would you buy with the money?

THE CALL: B. Trim for sanity, keep the core.
WHY:

## Marks

SEAT: Marks (Risk and cycle)
STANCE: Conditional yes
OPTION SCORES: A 4/10 · B 8/10 · C 5/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10
CONVICTION: 4/5
EVIDENCE: adequate
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: B. The price of risk has moved against you.
WHY:
1. At 55 times earnings [F009] against a 10-year average of 36 [F010], the easy money has been made (Risk means more things can happen, S015).
2. A 60% year [F013] pulls future returns forward.
3. Trimming is not a forecast. It is managing what could happen.
THE RISK I SEE: Valuations stretch further and you watch from the side.
I'M WRONG IF: earnings grow fast enough that the price looks ordinary within two years.
QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: How much of your gain could you give back without regret?

WHY:
